# American Film Screenwriting Case Studies (美国电影剧作案例)

> **Output language = the language of the question**; always anchor terminology back to its original terms — see [sw-workflow/terms.md](../sw-workflow/terms.md); do not coin your own translations.

This is a **library of examples**: each case gives a logline, structural key points, and a transferable technique. The main sources are Mei Feng's 《编剧的自修课》 (The Screenwriter's Self-Study Course) (the Western / screwball comedy / Wilder / Hitchcock / Adaptation / Mildred Pierce / five Oscar-winning original screenplays), Richard Walter's 《剧本》 (Screenwriting) (his firsthand account of American Graffiti and the Creature Comforts exercise), 《故事创作大师班·好莱坞卷》 (Story Masterclass: The Hollywood Volume) (Bourne / Guardians of the Galaxy / Zootopia / Thor / Big Daddy / 41 Days), Hoxter (Winter's Bone, Jojo Rabbit), Snyder (Miss Congeniality), Field (Chinatown, Network), and McKee (Casablanca, Chinatown). For principles, see the individual skills; this file keeps only "how this particular film did it." Supplementary examples and analysis tables are in [reference.md](reference.md).

## I. Genre Study: The Hollywood Western (梅峰 / Mei Feng)

- **Genre** = a body of standardized industrial productions bearing cultural traits; "genre is not a formula but an archetype"; the study must establish a **system of conventions and a system of variations**; genres die for two reasons: failing to keep the rules, or failing to grasp the pulse of the times.
- **Value pairs**: every genre has a permanent pair of values; "the question stays the same, but the view of the question changes"; the Western = civilization vs. savagery. Values must become events and permeate character setup, relationships, scenes, and plot.
- **A close reading of 《双虎屠龙》 (The Man Who Shot Liberty Valance)**: two protagonists, Tom (the cowboy — "the West has its own law") vs. Ransom (the Eastern lawyer); the opposition is repeated again and again (strength, dress, attitude toward guns, attitude toward the sheriff, rivalry in love); the gun = a passport (learning to use a gun = overcoming an inner obstacle; losing the gun = failure); the supporting character Link applies pressure to the protagonists, recurs at the right moments, and usually does not change; a value opposition can live inside a single character (Hallie cannot read); in the restaurant scene the same value pair changes hands several times; every scene in which the cactus rose appears marks where the key value lies; "the value categories carried by characters in a traditional genre film are basically stable, but the values themselves keep moving through the film"; whichever value the ending lands on is the film's attitude (the community rejects the real lawman, the cowboy, and chooses the outsider).
- **Standard procedure**: a Westerner (an outsider with a mysterious past and special skills) enters a community and is not accepted → is drawn in against his will (friends or family threatened) → the forces of evil are far stronger → he tries to avoid involvement but is finally dragged in → the struggle ends → he is accepted or leaves. In three 1950s films (《折箭为盟》 (Broken Arrow), 《正午》 (High Noon), 《荒漠怪客》 (Johnny Guitar)) the journey begins inside the community, and **the townspeople are the greatest enemy**.
- **Four oppositions**: the Westerner vs. the community; the community vs. the forces of evil; the Westerner vs. the forces of evil; the Westerner vs. the outside world — each can push the protagonist in two directions.
- The Western hero → antihero → alternative hero: "before evil he is the spokesman of justice; before government order he is an anarchist"; the climax often falls where both relationships are strained at once; Native Americans move from villainous convention to 《与狼共舞》 (Dances with Wolves); women are the key to "activating" the Westerner (emissary of civilization / rescued / temptress); the community moves from pure small town to the most stubborn obstacle of hypocrisy and corruption.
- The two-knight mode (《虎豹小霸王》 (Butch Cassidy and the Sundance Kid)): from separation to unity, and finally to joint action against the greatest obstacle; the star system means the two are "set up before the story even begins."

## II. The Screwball Comedy Model (Mei Feng)

- Born in the Great Depression; 《一夜风流》 (It Happened One Night) set the standard; "a sex comedy without sex."
- **Social identity**: conflict is built on a battle of the sexes across a gap in status; the narrative weight is always on scenes of mutual needling and wit; the ending "completes the elimination of class opposition and an ideological reconciliation" — to keep the reconciliation from ringing false, both are made to leap out of their own class (Peter is unemployed, Ellie runs away from home), and the real driving force of the embrace is shared values (stubbornness, honesty, love of freedom). Capra's trilogy turns into bottom-up populism; 《萨利文的旅行》 (Sullivan's Travels) reduces the battle of the sexes to a minimum ("There's always a girl in the picture").
- **Character**: the heroine must be strong and never outmatched, never naive from beginning to end (occasionally switching between good and bad); the hero is childish about sex and at a disadvantage; **the father / elder figure is crucial**: both the start of the conflict and the final untangling are accomplished through the father (Andrews goes from opposing the marriage to encouraging her to run away; Harry's "Is she pretty?") — the regret is that, except for It Happened One Night, the father's change of motive never finds a logical solution.
- **Plot points = character motivation**: It Happened One Night has three motivation shifts (minute 16: Peter decides to follow; minute 29: Ellie agrees to share a room and go to New York, and the Walls of Jericho go up; minute 75: mutual transformation → misunderstanding; minute 94: Peter asks her father for the $36.90; the wedding escape). Classic Hollywood protagonists share one attribute: **desire** (in European art cinema, characters do not know what they are doing; in Hollywood that is an unforgivable technical flaw).
- **Two ways to pair two protagonists**: the buddy mode (a shared goal, as if one protagonist were split into two) and the adversary mode (very different identities, each with their own life problems, who form a bond on the "journey").
- **Dialogue**: extreme reliance on conversation, hidden sexual teasing in the subtext, very fast delivery maximized by editing, and dialogue that also sets up ("Washington and Valley Forge," "Napoleon and Josephine," "I need him like the ax needs the turkey").
- Two authors: Riskin (twenty years with Capra); Sturges (in 1939 he sold 《江湖异人传》 (The Great McGinty) for one dollar in exchange for the right to direct).

## III. Billy Wilder (Mei Feng)

- As a child he found at the front door a letter from his father's illegitimate child — "it must have been delivered to the wrong address" — **the observation of concealment and deception in people runs through his entire body of work**; his years as a reporter were raw material for life; his epitaph: "I'm a screenwriter, and then, nobody's perfect."
- **Disguise** appears in almost every film (the showgirl posing as a baroness, the 24-year-old posing as 12, Neff hiding from Keyes, Joe hiding being kept, Baxter pretending not to know); **disguise lets the audience, who occupy an omniscient position, take pleasure in watching characters on screen be fooled by appearances; when the audience's knowledge of the truth differs from the knowledge of several characters in the film, the dramatic possibilities multiply.** "A good screenwriter should grab the audience by the throat and squeeze tighter and tighter, then let go all at once at the end."
- **The Lubitsch touch**: "Give the audience a 2, then let them see another 2, and they can work out the 4 themselves" — the sleeping-car scene in 《热情似火》 (Some Like It Hot): the audience knows Jerry is a man (the first 2), knows Sugar takes him for a girlfriend (the second 2), and works out the torment on its own.
- **The ultimate purpose of disguise: when the disguise collapses, the character discovers his true inner self** (《黄昏之恋》 (Love in the Afternoon): "Throw her back in the river"; Some Like It Hot: Joe, dressed as Josephine, kisses Sugar; Keyes: "the insurance-investigator identity is an unconscious disguise," "Closer than that," "I love you, too").
- **Meet-cute**: the man and woman must not meet in an ordinary way; a whole notebook is devoted to ways of meeting (buying only the top of a pair of pajamas); complementary pairing (sheet music on the back stairs, lyrics on the front stairs) but the two protagonists are always in sharp conflict (《午夜》 (Midnight), the bar: "If you want peace of mind, get yourself a taxi" — "No woman ever found peace in a taxi"; 《异国鸳鸯》 (Ninotchka): "I kissed that Polish cavalryman too — just before he died," which slides perfectly into Act Two). The script adapts to the actors (Garbo laughs; Grant refuses a role, so 《龙凤配》 (Sabrina) is rewritten); the Lemmon vs. Matthau mismatch.
- **Realism**: "grab a piece of reality the way a newsreel does and then develop it"; not examining America from a European point of view but examining human nature from a human point of view: "People are often not as good as they think they are, nor as cowardly as they fear they are."

## IV. Hitchcock and His Writers (Mei Feng, citing Leitch)

- Of 53 films, only 10 were original; before writing began he discussed the project with the writer day and night for weeks; "design every shot in advance — once the script is finished, the fun is gone."
- The economics of rights (buy a property for £250 and sell it for twice that; use an intermediary to avoid bidding up the price); is the value of adaptation material the story itself or "a potential Hitchcock film"?
- Complex credits (collaborative outlines make contribution impossible to determine; he liked to train obedient writers with no experience to replace experienced hands; Hunter was fired for refusing to write a rape scene; Chandler's manuscript was thrown into the wastebasket while holding his nose).
- **"Every filmmaker is an adapter"**: authorship is a complex form of collaboration among all parties, not something the controlling party owns alone; Hitchcock's longest partner was his wife Alma Reville ("I work with two pairs of hands, and one pair is Alma's") — "his authorship in the auteur sense is itself collaborative."

## V. 《改编剧本》 (Adaptation) — Mei Feng, citing Tomasulo

- Bazin: adapt "for the cinema," not "for the audience." 《兰花贼》 (The Orchid Thief) "has no plot at all and no dramatic crisis"; Kaufman adds his own interrogation of himself to Orlean's self-interrogation and writes himself into the script.
- **McKee, or not McKee**: Charlie tears up the Ten Commandments but ends up using them (mixed genre; "Don't you have the courage to introduce an incident?"; "In the end you must leave the audience shaken"; "The protagonist you create cannot be without desire"); Act Three crams guns, sex, drugs, and life lessons back in — showing the screenwriter forced to abandon his original intention and compromise step by step; but it violates "no voice-over" and uses voice-over throughout.
- Charlie / Donald = two doubles inside one person (serious modernism vs. postmodern commerce); Donald's 《如此三人》 (The Three) is a commentary on the film itself; "the reality of this film is that it has only one protagonist."
- The double meaning of the title: adapting a book into a film / the adaptation and growth of humans and species (hybridization, wild orchids that cannot leave the forest, pollination); they use the words on page 41 as "the end of the book" because the real ending (escape back to civilization) leaves no room for a car chase; the song 《一起幸福》 ("Happy Together") three times; the blank wall vs. the kitchen with a window.

## VI. 《米尔德丽德·皮尔斯》 (Mildred Pierce): The Eight Drafts — Mei Feng, citing LaValley

- Seven differences between novel and film: add murder; flashback; compress the time span; change third person to first person; delete Veda's musical career; increase glamour; change the episodic structure into "a march of moments toward a decisive situation."
- **Producer Jerry Wald** determines the direction (the flashback of 《双重赔偿》 (Double Indemnity), the added murder, pushing the climax onto the murder); "realism" for him means only a fashion of speaking frankly about sex and taboo.
- Turney version: domestic melodrama, three mother-daughter confrontations, credible but not what Wald wanted; **Maltz's 62-page critique** asks three questions: is it too episodic and biographical? Is Veda credible enough that Mildred's not seeing through her does not make Mildred look like an idiot? Is the motive for the break based on a psychological collapse rather than the blackmail letter?
- Gruen / Faulkner version: baroque excess, Gothic (the black rings of mascara, poison, "a house built on sand").
- **MacDougall** solves the combination of woman's film and film noir: the present tense tells the murder, the flashback tells the struggle; the same scene (the beach house) brings together the romantic swim and death; the gun travels from the office to the murder scene; double-edged clues (Wally shudders during sex / the parental striving); supporting characters define the protagonist (Ida = the ideal and the warning, Veda = the repressed other side, Wally = comedy and rejected sex); radio-drama training — immediate impact, fast conflict, limited time, clear storytelling; homage to 《公民凯恩》 (Citizen Kane) (circular structure, missing shots). "If Mildred represents the new ideal of womanhood, she also represents the vulnerability of that role — in the end she must be punished and rebuilt by the female characters in the family."

## VII. 《塞尔玛与路易丝》 (Thelma & Louise) — Mei Feng

- **The "but" method**: two women want a weekend getaway but accidentally kill a man; they raise the money but it is stolen; they decide to rob but are stopped; they are surrounded but choose another way to end it. A plot point must extend a line of action and produce a functional change.
- Three motivation shifts (minute 29: the shared motive forks; minute 70: "I heard a call, the call of the wild," the second convergence; minute 96: the backstory is exposed and they enter the climax); from minute 70 to 96 the line splits into flight and pursuit; the police watch the video and eat hamburgers = woman as the object of the gaze; the ending's climax and resolution merge (driving out over the Grand Canyon evades the problem laid out earlier).
- **Mirror protagonists**: at the start, packing is cross-cut (meticulous vs. muddled); as the film develops each gradually becomes the other (Thelma makes the cliff jump instead of Louise); transformation symbols — cigarettes (the second cigarette exhaled the way Louise does), the driver's seat (she drives only twice), the refrigerator and chocolate three times (the third time she does not put it back); details that "show but do not develop"; Louise's backstory (Texas) is **mentioned but never developed**, conveyed only through action and dialogue.

## VIII. 《法尔戈》 (Fargo) — Mei Feng

- **Character first**: for 33 minutes (before Marge enters), everyday sketches establish Jerry's situation and his relationship with the kidnappers thoroughly, then let character drive event; the police appearing is a coincidental inciting incident, but credible because the characters are already formed; "the designs do not happen after the story acts on the characters; rather, with the characters' traits already formed, the characters drive events." Jerry is always accompanied by a car (the car = the American Dream).
- Showing supporting characters through a single trait is very appealing, but the protagonist cannot rely on that trick alone (the paradox of the model); Marge is the only normal person (a Columbo prototype); the ending lands on an everyday conversation about stamps.
- **The criminals are thrown out at the very beginning**, so suspense shifts from "who did it" to "how the plan will derail"; the detection is handled casually (it is not the focus).
- Documentary visuals ("true story" titles, location subtitles, phonetic spellings of local accents, catchphrases, static shots and long takes) paired with an absurd script — "he did not create a visual style but developed a new way of pairing"; the opposition of humor and brutality (the wood chipper); high-frequency dialogue participates in the rhythm rather than conveying information; "stumbling blocks" accumulate into deformed absurdity; money as original sin, murder must be punished, and tolerance for those who never err morally; "a compliant reformist within the Hollywood system."

## IX. 《心灵捕手》 (Good Will Hunting) — Mei Feng

- Thompson's five-part method: setup (the inciting incident is at the midpoint; the protagonist's goal is given by Lambeau, not by himself) → complex action (half the length is devoted to Sean; the suspense is "how will ordinary Sean change Will") → development (**Sean changes the main goal**: "You don't know what you want"; the subplot monologue by Chuckie, "the ten seconds before I knock on your door," is a preview of the climax) → climax (**the antagonist only appears now** — Lambeau, "a self-righteous math bastard"; the subplot is obliquely recalled a second before the climax) → coda (the three lines merge, all ending with Will on the highway).
- The actors write the script: jokes rely on action and onomatopoeia; "It's not your fault" three times can carry the climax's tension only when it is performed.
- **The director rewrites the script back toward tradition**: the Sean exposition scene is cut from its place and moved to the "introduce a new character" position; the bar main scene is cut and the supporting character is introduced for the first time instead (reading a thick book in his room); Chuckie's mother and the priest are cut (branches of reality); an empty tram shot is added; the "pickup" scene is added so the monologue has weight; one line is added to merge two goals; focus is used to adjust who holds the emphasis. The price is "escapism" (only the genius from the lower class is worth saving); a three-way game (screenwriter / director / producer) determines the shape.

## X. 《美国美人》 (American Beauty) — Mei Feng

- "Look Closer"; the protagonist is a "dead man," and the film opens with the dead man's voice-over (the screenplay's courtroom scene is replaced by a flying point of view).
- **POV strategy**: camera positions create a fence effect (overhead, behind windows, inside cars, computer data); the cheerleading performance — a zoom pulls the objective point of view into a subjective hallucination (top light, high-speed photography, four zippers, rose petals), then returns to the objective and leaves an aftertaste; in the group scene Carolyn is not given a separate shot and is blurred = forgotten; the DV point of view creates a "sudden quiet" after continuous action; Ricky uses his camera to observe the world and expose the truth; the mutual-filming passage uses three screens; the screenplay explicitly marks point of view.
- **Pseudo-multiple protagonists**: a network of characters, but every passage functionally serves Lester's awakening and death (the umbrella-handle structure); the first seven minutes set the "dead man's tone" and display every character's typical temperament, after which they only rise and fall; three acts (numbness and awakening 36 / struggle 84 / transcendence); two dinner-table scenes (the screenplay already says "the camera slowly circles them"); the ending's cross-cutting misleads the audience about the killer's identity and weakens the moral critique — "Lester, who has left the collective unconscious, ultimately cannot survive."
- **Genre mixing**: domestic melodrama + black comedy; adjacent scenes are arranged by contrast; within the same scene, contrast is created by tie angle and shot size; **red discipline** — red appears in only three places (the roses, the door, the title), and is avoided everywhere else to preserve symbolic sensitivity.
- Oscar campaign: $5 million invested, $130 million returned; DreamWorks spent $774,000 on four weeks of publicity.

## XI. 《纯洁心灵的永恒阳光》 (Eternal Sunshine of the Spotless Mind) — Mei Feng

- It begins at the end of love and ends at the beginning of love; the three acts are stretched to **1 (18) : 5 (72) : 1 (17)**; the beginning and end are continuous events in the same time sequence, while the middle section flashes back to the erasure process; the erased content is split between beginning and end to keep the middle intact.
- The fragmented narrative is held together by **image motifs / emotional valves** (the Charles River, Montauk, the necklace, the skull drawing, the beach house) recurring and recombining; the second part, the Montauk cabin (minutes 87–92), has already completed the climax, and the real-world reunion is a compromise; the ending Kaufman loves is that years later they are still repeatedly erased; the final shot is repeated three times.
- **A single protagonist, isolated from the world, acts inside his own consciousness**; the desire turns at minute 52 (looking at the stars from the river, the childhood memory under the blanket) and establishes the axis of action; the repetition of opposition is sustained by adding secondary plots (the subplot is affected) and by "each memory being erased in a different visual way"; the wooden protagonist does not change abruptly through external stimulation; his character stays consistent; what he opposes is his own closed heart; voice-over and dialogue coexist, and he talks with his memories to revise them; the supporting characters are simplified so that the point of force falls on Joel alone.
- Sound and image: subjectivized light sources, extreme shot scales, sound placed ahead of its source, dialogue hooks, the echo of metal pipes; "the most striking special effect is achieved only with the camera."

## XII. 《美国风情画》 (American Graffiti), Firsthand — Walter

- In 1971 Kurtz called with a $10,000 contract; everyone hated the title; the first draft was written in nine days (drag racing vs. car racing; "the stark opposition between surface aimlessness and inner direction and purpose"); George came back and said not a word — "today I have become very skilled in the art of rejection"; the agent: "nine days' work, $7,500 in your pocket, don't think about it anymore"; the second draft was shelved; in the arbitration he did not submit supplementary material and his name did not appear; 1.67% of net profits came to more than a million; after that he became a top writer of teen material.
- Lessons: do not write a first draft alone before the director returns; insist on the contract but understand that "net profit" is nyet; submit supplementary material in arbitration; attach no expectations to action, and write for the sake of writing.

## XIII. 《物质享受》 (Creature Comforts): A Premise Exercise — Walter

- Hotel front desk, no luggage at check-in → "American Airlines lost it" → the invented luggage actually arrives — **what goes in the suitcase is the most important choice** (tickets and passport / keys / corpse / photos and CDs / rhino horn / clothing / condoms / drugs "absolutely avoid" / empty suitcase / cannot be opened / Twilight Zone method); the author's version: expensive clothes, a tape recorder, a .45 pistol, a million dollars, a contract schedule, and a manuscript of Creature Comforts missing forty pages.
- Techniques: the character refuses → the next scene he is doing it; repeated details (Brooklyn Heights, "clumsy, awkward, bedraggled," the accent) weave a unified texture; the cautious-man principle (he does not shower but urinates, picks up the gun, pushes the luggage cart); add a small obstacle in every spot (underwear, the Russian driver, the subway ticket auction); do not fill in the middle process (《为人父母》 (Parenthood) cuts straight to the crash); plausible impossibility (the tape recorder falls and plays) but do not expose the screenwriter; printed matter is handled by description + a flash + brief dialogue; sex is kept implicit by panning to the ceiling; work backward from the ending (the prisoner / the Berkowitz law / the pen name / the impostor / the silencing); theme of identity — an imposed identity moves from resistance to joyful acceptance.

## XIV. Masterclass Cases (Hollywood Volume)

- **《谍影重重》 (The Bourne Identity)**, following Banks's parallel arc: the theme "Who am I?" is posed on page 6; page 7: "Ivy League Ollie North," four words; page 10: the microfilm in his hip → the drive "find Jason Bourne" (theme and drive united); page 29: the embassy interlude; page 45: the first revelation — he is an assassin; pages 65–67: the apartment shoot-out at the midpoint turns the direction (from searching for identity to escaping pursuit); page 81: the low point; page 84: he changes his appearance and stops chasing, fleeing his identity instead; pages 93–95: unwilling to accept it; the final irony: to survive he must become a killer against his will. Test: if you strip out the theme, are the drive beats still there?
- **《冒牌老爸》 (Big Daddy)**: theme: responsibility; page 1: "32 years old and still a child"; page 10: the failed proposal; page 26: the "hearsay" exposition is a screenwriting mistake; page 44: the girlfriend dating a reliable old man restates the theme; the sequence (26/27→44) could be a short film; midpoint page 61, meeting Layla changes the object of the drive.
- **《冒牌天神》 (Bruce Almighty) / 《总统先生》 (The American President)**: a weak drive leads to an episodic structure; "the function of drive is to push forward while restricting."
- **《疯狂动物城》 (Zootopia) / 《银河护卫队》 (Guardians of the Galaxy) / 《美人鱼》 (The Mermaid)**, following Weiss's relationship-driven approach: opening → page 10 hook → page 30 Act One turn (the real core concept); the second-act question ("Can they solve Zootopia's biggest case?" "Can they save the tribe?" "Can they make sure the ball is in the right hands?"); 30–60 obstacles use world-specific weapons (the sea urchin); minute 45 subplot (the scratched car, the petals); the midpoint hits bottom through a fierce quarrel (the fox reveals his childhood secret; "killing me won't solve your problem"); "a change in the relationship between characters resonates more than a change in a single character."
- **《雷神》 (Thor)**, from Stentz: design the costume first (the helmet is worn only at the ceremony); cut Dr. Blake (the fake passport name is an homage); "don't think too much when building a world" (no explanation of how the hammer flies); the line "your ancestors called it magic and you call it science" folds the cosmos in; Asgard and Earth are unified (strip his power and throw him to earth so he learns humility and sacrifice); S.H.I.E.L.D. replaces the brother as the blocker; shoot the first film as a Shakespeare play and the third as an outer-space comedy; Loki "always thinks he is the good guy."
- **《41 天》 (41 Days)**, from Richards: confined-space references 《活埋》 (Buried) and 《127 小时》 (127 Hours); the first 10 pages: the pizzeria / the victim's face and the match / the room breaks the "only one pipe" expectation; a three-minute gunfight; minute 4: the same face transitions to backstory; minute 11: he enters the pipe; the coat detail; the protagonist actually changes passively, long after he escapes; the board with four lines; the "bookend" ending.
- **《冬天的骨头》 (Winter's Bone)**, from Hoxter: the argument beat is the longest (turned away house after house); the rhythm of cause and effect — effort often brings no result, and turning points have no direct explanation. The nine beats of **《乔乔的异想世界》 (Jojo Rabbit)** are in `sw-story-structure`.
- **《特工佳丽》 (Miss Congeniality)**, following Snyder's BS2 beat by beat; the first ten pages of **《唐人街》 (Chinatown)** (Field) and its Act Two climax beat by beat (McKee); the linen stall of **《卡萨布兰卡》 (Casablanca)** (McKee) — see the references for `sw-story-structure` and `sw-scene-craft`.
