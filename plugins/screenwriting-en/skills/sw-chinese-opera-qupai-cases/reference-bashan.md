## §0 Edition, OCR Notes, and the Scale of Historical Change (full text of main text 5.1)

Wei Minglun and Nan Guo, 《巴山秀才》 (*The Scholar of Bashan*), Sichuan opera **高腔** (gāoqiāng — high-pitched vocal system; 曲牌体 qǔpáitǐ — the qupai system, with 帮腔 bāngqiāng — offstage answering chorus), billed as a "newly written historical play." **Ninth draft, July 30, 1983, in Zigong**; first edition, first printing, Sichuan Literature and Art Publishing House, January 1985; print run 1–3,600 copies; 83 pages in all (67 pages of play text + 8 pages of the author's talk + 4 pages of production stills). Performed by the Zigong City Sichuan Opera Troupe; 孟登科 (Meng Dengke) was played by 杨先才 (Yang Xiancai). The author's talk 《漫谈〈巴山秀才〉的创作》 ("Random Thoughts on the Creation of *The Scholar of Bashan*") is dated September 22, 1983.

**OCR notes**: The scan consistently reads 迂 (yū) as 迁 (qiān) ("迂告" → "迁告"), and 【红衲袄】 was read as "红讷袄" and "红祸袄"; all have been corrected in the notes. Other obvious typos (禀报 [bǐngbào — to report] written as "票报" [piàobào], 恍然 [huǎngrán — suddenly understanding] as "忧然" [yōurán]) were corrected at discretion and are not flagged for verification.

**The scale of historical change is a quantifiable sample**: the prototype is the "Dongxiang Massacre" (second year of the Guangxu reign, Qing dynasty; Dongxiang is present-day Xuanhan County). **Only two real names are retained** (李有恒 [Li Youheng], 张之洞 [Zhang Zhidong]), and **only two plot elements are retained** (writing the grievance on an examination paper; throwing out a scapegoat). The protagonist, Governor 恒宝 (Hengbao), County Magistrate 孙雨田 (Sun Yutian), 霓裳 (Nichang), 袁铁匠 (Yuan the Blacksmith), 孟娘子 (Meng Niangzi), 店幺姑 (Dian Yaogu), and the "poisoned wine" ending are all fictional.

## §1 Cast List, Scene Order, and the Scene-by-Scene Structural Table for All Eight Scenes

### 0. Cast List (p0006, copied verbatim)

- **孟登科 (Meng Dengke)** — a poor scholar of Bashan County.
- **孟娘子 (Meng Niangzi)** — the wife of Scholar Meng.
- **霓裳 (Nichang)** — the Governor's favorite concubine, a native of Bashan County.
- **袁铁匠 (Yuán Tiějiàng — Yuan the Blacksmith)** — a starving peasant of Bashan County, Nichang's maternal uncle.
- **恒宝 (Hengbao)** — of Eight Banners stock, acting Governor of Sichuan.
- **孙雨田 (Sun Yutian)** — County Magistrate of Bashan, later promoted to the chief magistracy of Chengdu and Huayang, the two premier counties.
- **李有恒 (Li Youheng)** — commander of the Huwei (虎威) Security Battalion, expectant Provincial Commander.
- **钦差 (qīnchāi — Imperial Commissioner)** — a Manchu noble and imperial prince, dispatched by the court to Sichuan to investigate the "Bashan wrongful case."
- **戈什哈 (gèshìhā — Manchu bodyguard)** — the Governor's personal guard.
- **随员 (suíyuán — Attendant)** — a member of the Imperial Commissioner's suite.
- **班头 (bāntóu — head constable)** — head constable of the Bashan County yamen, later a constable in the Chengdu yamen.
- **店幺姑 (Diàn Yāogū)** — the daughter of the owner of an inn on Kejia Lane, Chengdu.
- **张之洞 (Zhang Zhidong)** — the Education Commissioner sent by the court to Sichuan to preside over the provincial examination, **appearing in Meng Dengke's hallucination**.
- **考生们 (kǎoshēngmen — examination candidates)** — the licentiates who have come to the provincial capital for the examination, **appearing in Meng Dengke's hallucination**.
- The people of Bashan County / the runners of the Bashan County yamen / officers and men of the Security Battalion / the singing girls of the Governor's residence.

**Note**: The cast list explicitly says "appearing in a hallucination" — Zhang Zhidong is the man who turns the play's fate, yet **is never given a physical entrance** (the author's talk explains why; see the third set of notes, section 3.4). This is an example of the cast list itself making a structural declaration.

### 0.1 Scene Order, Time, and Place (p0007)

| | Scene title | | Scene title |
|---|---|---|---|
| Scene 1 | Begging for Relief | Scene 5 | The Clever Appeal |
| Scene 2 | The False Report | Scene 6 | Swapping the Letter |
| Scene 3 | The Massacre | Scene 7 | Searching the Inn |
| Scene 4 | The Pedantic Appeal | Scene 8 | Exposing the Truth |

- **Every scene title in the original is exactly two Chinese characters, and every one is a verb-object or modifier-head action phrase** (Begging for Relief, The False Report, The Massacre, The Pedantic Appeal, The Clever Appeal, Swapping the Letter, Searching the Inn, Exposing the Truth); read in sequence, the eight titles form a complete plot line. Scenes 4 and 5, "The Pedantic Appeal" and "The Clever Appeal," stand as a pair; scene 8, "Exposing the Truth," closes the line.
- Time: **the second year of the Guangxu reign, when the emperor was still a suckling child and the two dowager empresses held court from behind the curtain.**
- Place: **it begins in a small place tentatively called "Bashan County," reaches the provincial capital Chengdu, and finally storms into the Forbidden City in Beijing.** ("Tentatively called" is a declaration of fiction; the historical prototype is Dongxiang County, present-day Xuanhan County — see the author's talk.)
- **Prelude** (p0008, four lines before the curtain): "Open the book of modern history, / look back at old Sichuan: / the Land of Abundance is a hell, / wailing geese fill Bashan!" — four four-character lines (四言四句, sìyán sìjù), forming a head-and-tail echo with the closing chorus of scene 8.

### 1. Structural Table for the Eight Scenes

#### Scene 1: Begging for Relief (p0009–0017)
- **Characters**: Bantou, yamen runners, the common people (A, B, C), Yuan the Blacksmith, Meng Dengke, Meng Niangzi, Sun Yutian.
- **Events**: A drought; starving people kneel at the yamen begging for relief, and the head constable beats and drives them off. Yuan the Blacksmith breaks the fire-and-water staff (水火棍, shuǐhuǒgùn — the red-and-black staff of yamen runners) and proposes "appealing over the yamen's head" (越衙告状, yuèyá gàozhuàng — bypassing the county court to appeal to a higher authority); he asks the scholar to write the complaint, but the scholar refuses and instead sings of his dream of coming first in the imperial examination. Sun Yutian enters, kneels and swears, "If I harbor any secret dishonesty or line my own pockets, may heaven destroy me and earth annihilate me," then asks the scholar to read the petition aloud in public, hangs his seal from the central beam, and mounts his sedan to "go to Chengdu and plead for the people."
- **Qupai and bangqiang**: Yuan the Blacksmith, 【红衲袄】 (Hóngnà'ǎo) (6 lines); Meng Dengke, 【笛引】 (Díyǐn — entrance prelude, 4 lines); Meng Dengke, 【流水腔】 (Liúshuǐqiāng) plus **bangqiang "the slow doctor"** (a numerical parallel from one to ten, 28 lines); Yuan the Blacksmith continues with 【流水腔】 (8 lines); Meng Dengke, 【梭梭岗】 (Suōsuōgǎng) (reading the petition, 6 lines); Sun Yutian continues with 【梭梭岗】 (6 lines).
- **Song–speech balance**: More speech than song, but **every sung passage is placed at the moment a character shows his hand** — the scholar's dream of office, the blacksmith's angry denunciation, the magistrate's false oath.
- **Ending**: Sun Yutian hurries off in his sedan; Yuan the Blacksmith suspects, "Could this be the golden cicada shedding its shell?" (金蝉脱壳, jīn chán tuō qiào); the scholar rebukes him for "measuring a gentleman's heart with a petty man's yardstick"; Meng Niangzi says, "The blacksmith hammers iron, the scholar reads books"; and the scholar buries his face again in 《八股制艺》 (*The Craft of Eight-Legged Essays*). **The scene ends on a correct judgment that is rejected (a 扣子, kòuzi — a suspense device at the segment's end).**
- **Relation to the main line**: The starting point — the scholar's "pedantry" goes so far as refusing to write a single complaint for his neighbors. The distance he must travel is measured out in this scene.

#### Scene 2: The False Report (p0018–0023)
- **Characters**: Sun Yutian, Geshiha, Bantou, Hengbao, Nichang, concubines, bodyguards.
- **Events**: Sun Yutian seeks a late-night audience and is refused; he **fires the flag-gate cannon** and falsely reports a mutiny. In the Governor's flower hall, Hengbao plays mahjong with his concubines; Nichang holds a pipa and sings 李商隐 (Li Shangyin)'s 《夜雨寄北》 ("Written on a Rainy Night to a Friend in the North"); Hengbao discards a tile and a cannon sounds offstage; the court convenes, Sun Yutian falsely accuses the starving people of "cutting off Hengbao's head to answer to Sichuan," and Hengbao in a rage issues a "suppression" order.
- **Qupai and bangqiang**: Sun Yutian, 【红衲袄】 (6 lines) plus a continuation (12 lines); Nichang sings four lines of the Tang poem "Written on a Rainy Night to a Friend in the North" (**no tune title is marked; the original poem enters the play as song**). No bangqiang.
- **Song–speech balance**: One long solo for Sun Yutian (he sings out the entire conspiracy); everything else is spoken.
- **Ending**: Hengbao flicks his sleeve; an object falls and clatters — **a mahjong "two-circle" tile**; Hengbao gives an embarrassed "ah, ah" and lets out a loud sneeze. **The scene ends on a prop hitting the floor, with no dialogue.**
- **Relation to the main line**: The cause of three thousand deaths is fixed in this scene by two men's private motives, with the scholar entirely absent — **a scene without the protagonist is the hinge of the whole play's causality.**

#### Scene 3: The Massacre (p0024–0028)
- **Characters**: Li Youheng, the Security Battalion's rifle squad, starving people, Meng Dengke, Meng Niangzi, Yuan the Blacksmith, stragglers.
- **Events**: Cavalry gallops toward Bashan under the "Suppression" banner; the Mauser rifle squad shoots everyone on sight. Meng Dengke, holding up a rice sack, shouts, "We are law-abiding commoners here for relief grain!" and is shot down; he crawls out of the pile of dead. He and Meng Niangzi recognize each other in the chaos. Stragglers want to make up "a hundred heads"; Yuan the Blacksmith rushes to the rescue and perishes together with the stragglers, and with his last breath says, "Go to Chengdu and find my niece... she is now called Nichang."
- **Qupai and bangqiang**: "Yin gong and drum" (阴锣鼓, yīn luógǔ — a somber percussion pattern) opens (a percussion cue); **bangqiang "the survivor who escaped a great disaster"**; the husband and wife sing a duet, 【幽冥钟】 (Yōumíngzhōng) (**bangqiang starts the song** with "Meeting after the catastrophe, liver and gall split," then 7 lines of duet); **bangqiang 【刮骨令】 (Guāgǔlìng)** "Where will you stay, with three thousand wronged souls of Bashan?"; **bangqiang (turning to the 合同 — hétóng, the chorus tag — of 【孝顺歌】 [Xiàoshùngē])** "Husband, do not grieve; wife, do not weep; take your wife to Chengdu!"
- **Song–speech balance**: This is the scene with the densest bangqiang in the whole play; **all four bangqiang passages are in this scene**; the sung passages are mainly duets.
- **Ending**: The scholar improvises a funeral oration: "Early I hammered iron, late I hammered iron, / the heart of the iron-hammerer understands. / Early I read books, late I read books, / the reader of books is a fool. / The scholar kept himself safe, / the blacksmith gave his life to save others. / In vain I sang 'Yangchun Baixue' (阳春白雪 — 'Spring Snow,' highbrow art); / better the 'Xiali Baren' (下里巴人 — 'The Rustic Song of Xia Li,' popular art). / If the spirit is not blind, there will be successors. / Alas, alas, accept this offering!" — **a prose funeral oration replaces the exit poem** (下场诗, xiàchǎngshī), while the funeral music sounds and the couple kneels long in oath.
- **Relation to the main line**: The scholar's first awakening ("I want to ask why"), driven by someone else dying in his place.

#### Scene 4: The Pedantic Appeal (p0029–0041)
- **Characters**: Sun Yutian, Bantou, Hengbao, Li Youheng, Geshiha, Nichang, the singing girls, Meng Dengke, bodyguards.
- **Events**: At a victory banquet in Wangjiang Tower, plaques are bestowed: "The Good Official Governs the People" and "The Bold Warrior Plans the Frontier"; Nichang leads the dance and sings her homesickness with her back turned (背唱, bèichàng — singing with the back turned, an aside to the audience); Meng Dengke, looking for Nichang, runs into Sun Yutian and files his suit in open court, peeling away one layer after another until "the chief criminal" points straight at Hengbao; the three officials take turns singing asides that reveal their collusion; Hengbao misreads 负隅 (fùyú — with one's back to a corner) as 负偶 (fù'ǒu), and the scholar, even at the execution ground, still wants to correct the wrong character; Nichang claims the blacksmith as her uncle and pleads for mercy, saving the scholar, whose sentence is changed to forty strokes. At the end, word comes that "Zhang Zhidong has entered Sichuan to preside over the examination."
- **Qupai and bangqiang**: Sun Yutian, 【七句半】 (Qījùbàn) (4 lines on entrance); Hengbao, 【昆曲】 (Kūnqǔ — Kunqu) (2 lines); the singing girls **sing in chorus 【昆曲】** (four lines from 杜甫 [Du Fu]'s 《赠花卿》 ["To General Hua"]); Nichang, 【红衲袄】 plus **bangqiang "put on a smiling face,"** turning into a long back-turned song (19 lines); Meng Dengke, 【飞梆子】 (Fēibāngzi) (4 lines) plus continuation (6 lines); **three-person back-turned song** (Sun/Li/Heng alternating, 23 lines, mahjong terms strung together); **bangqiang "wait before you behead"**; Nichang, back-turned song (7 lines); Nichang, 【新水令】 (Xīnshuǐlìng) (20 lines); Sun Yutian, back-turned song (1 line).
- **Song–speech balance**: **The most song-heavy scene in the play** (more than eight qupai); it is the 重场戏 (zhòngchǎngxì — the scene with the greatest value reversal).
- **Ending**: Geshiha reports, "The court's Education Commissioner has reached Longquan Station"; Hengbao and Nichang cry out one after another, "Zhang Zhidong!" — **the scene ends on a threefold repetition of one name**, handing the key to the next scene to the audience.
- **Relation to the main line**: The second level of appeal (to the province) fails, and the scholar understands that officials shield one another; but he also meets Nichang and learns of Zhang Zhidong.

#### Scene 5: The Clever Appeal (p0042–0052)
- **Characters**: Meng Niangzi, Dian Yaogu, Meng Dengke; Zhang Zhidong and the examination candidates in hallucination.
- **Events**: At the Kejia Lane inn, "Lower Official Room No. 3" (where three people have died: one of rage, one by a fatal collision, one by hanging); the scholar returns wounded and resolves to burn his books and file his suit; Meng Niangzi pours out her grievances and hangs herself; Dian Yaogu rescues her and brings Nichang's hundred taels of silver and a letter: "The scholar will just happen to see the chief examiner"; the scholar hallucinates entering the examination shed and writes the character 冤 (yuān — injustice) into his examination paper.
- **Qupai and bangqiang**: Meng Niangzi sings (4 lines, no tune title); Meng Dengke, 【月儿高】 (Yuè'érgāo) (22 lines, burning the books); Meng Niangzi, 【香罗带】 (Xiāngluódài) (24 lines); Meng Dengke, 【华秋儿】 (Huáqiū'ér) (8 lines); husband and wife **sing in alternation**, the lines shrinking down to single-character exchanges (exam / sue / protect / burn); **bangqiang "another death in Lower Official Room No. 3!"**; Meng Dengke **recites 【韵白】** (yùnbái — rhymed speech) (Nichang's letter, 7 lines); offstage **accompaniment sings 【昆曲】** (伴唱, bànchàng — offstage accompanying voices) in two passages ("Thoughts surge; the scholar's hallucination enters the examination shed" / "A flash of inspiration... the clever appeal succeeds!").
- **Song–speech balance**: Song outweighs speech; three large qupai in a row make this the second 重场戏.
- **Ending**: The scholar shouts "Good, good, good!", wakes, and says, "I am going to take the examination"; Meng Niangzi, overjoyed, draws out her voice: "Number One in Heaven, collect your paper—" **The scene ends on a line that is misunderstood** (she thinks he is going to sit the exam; he means to file his suit).
- **Relation to the main line**: The method for the third level of appeal (to the court) is invented here — **"a grievance sheet leaping out of the examination cell" is the only strange and wonderful event retained from history.**

#### Scene 6: Swapping the Letter (p0053–0058)
- **Characters**: Imperial Commissioner, Attendant, Hengbao, Sun Yutian, Li Youheng, Geshiha, Nichang.
- **Events**: The imperial edict arrives; the Imperial Commissioner enters Sichuan to investigate. Hengbao wants to "sacrifice the chariot to save the general" (丢车保帅, diū jū bǎo shuài) by killing Sun Yutian; Sun counters, "Better to sacrifice the chariot than the cannon," writes a substitute "pacification" letter, and shifts the blame to Li Youheng; Sun gets Li Youheng drunk and completes the swap, but Nichang catches him in the act and bluffs the real letter out of him with "the Commander is urgently asking for the letter."
- **Qupai and bangqiang**: The Imperial Commissioner **sings Peking opera 【西皮二六】** (xīpí èrliù) (16 lines, in Beijing speech and Beijing accent); Hengbao, 【飞梆子】 (4 lines). No bangqiang.
- **Song–speech balance**: Speech dominates; both sung passages serve to "establish identity / pronounce judgment."
- **Ending**: Nichang "composed, drifts away"; Sun Yutian "stands stunned, half believing, half doubting." **The scene ends on the villain's suspicion.**
- **Relation to the main line**: The scholar is absent, but the physical evidence he is about to hold is swapped and seized in this scene.

#### Scene 7: Searching the Inn (p0059–0066)
- **Characters**: Attendant, Sun Yutian, Dian Yaogu, Nichang, Meng Niangzi, Meng Dengke, Bantou, yamen runners.
- **Events**: The bearer of good news and the arresting party come looking for "the Scholar of Bashan" at the same time (one asks for a man surnamed "Ke," the other for a man surnamed "Meng"); Nichang delivers the real letter; Sun Yutian searches the inn, and the scholar hides the letter inside his melon-skin cap and wears it on his head; after Sun leaves, Bantou crawls out from under the table and extorts him, but the Attendant arrives and locks Bantou in instead.
- **Qupai and bangqiang**: The Attendant and Sun Yutian each **speak two lines aside** (onstage together, occupying opposite sides); Dian Yaogu **speaks** two lines; Nichang sings (10 lines, no tune title); Meng Dengke sings (4 lines); Meng Dengke, 【流水腔】 plus **bangqiang "the slow doctor"** (counting down from ten to one, 10 lines), plus Bantou's interjected speech "One? ... what nonsense!" plus Sun Yutian's aside "I'm drawing water with a bamboo basket—" plus **bangqiang "all in vain!"** (竹篮打水一场空 — "drawing water with a bamboo basket, all in vain").
- **Song–speech balance**: Speech and stage business dominate; there are only two sung passages, but the 【流水腔】 passage is the most finely constructed loop in the play's structure.
- **Ending**: Meng Niangzi announces, "The scholar has passed!"; the scholar locks Bantou in and says, "Seven calculations, eight calculations, and up pops a ninth — locked in!" — **the scene ends on an improvised (现挂, xiànguà) numerical gag (包袱, bāofu).**
- **Relation to the main line**: The evidence is in hand, the good news is in hand; in the next scene both turn into a dead end at once.

#### Scene 8: Exposing the Truth (p0067–0075)
- **Characters**: Hengbao, Sun Yutian, Imperial Commissioner, Attendant, Li Youheng, Meng Dengke, Meng Niangzi, bodyguards; Nichang at the end.
- **Events**: At the joint trial, the three officials have unified their story as "pacification"; Meng Dengke (invited to the seat of honor under the name "Ke Dengmeng") produces the real "suppression" letter and exposes the truth in open court; the Imperial Commissioner beheads Li Youheng, dismisses Hengbao, binds Sun Yutian, and bestows on Meng Dengke a peacock feather and official cap and three cups of imperially sealed wine; the third cup is poison; the Imperial Commissioner destroys the suppression letter and, together with Hengbao and Sun Yutian, intones in unison "recorded in the history books" and leaves; the scholar plucks off the peacock feather and hat and tramples them, and dies a miserable death; Meng Niangzi laughs wildly and faints; Nichang, dressed as a common woman, carries her pipa through Bashan and the waters of Shu, singing the story.
- **Qupai and bangqiang**: The Imperial Commissioner **recites a scene-setting poem** (定场诗, dìngchǎngshī — four four-character lines); Meng Dengke, 【一枝花】 (Yīzhīhuā) (4 lines) plus continuation (2 lines, the last line repeated by **accompaniment**); backstage 【昆曲】 (2 lines) plus **chorus** (2 lines).
- **Song–speech balance**: The first two-thirds are entirely spoken (a courtroom scene); at the end there is only one 【一枝花】 — **the play's last qupai is held back until just before the character stops breathing.**
- **Ending**: The chorus sings "The Bashan tragedy wakes men up, / the Scholar of Bashan lives on in death!" and the play ends.
- **Relation to the main line**: The fourth level of appeal (to the Imperial Commissioner/court) fails utterly even in the form of "winning the case." "Awake, he dies; dying, he wakes."

## §3 Bangqiang Passage by Passage (all nine passages)

### 2. Bangqiang Passage by Passage (the text device unique to gaoqiang)

In the script, bangqiang is **set in parallel with the character's song**, with the two characters "帮腔" written at the head of the line, occupying the same position as a character name — **it is treated as a character in the script**. There are 9 instances in the play:

| # | Page | Who helps | Which lines | Division of labor with the character's song |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | p0013 | Bangqiang | "The slow doctor!" | **Supplies the second half of a xiehouyu** (歇后语 — a two-part allegorical saying): Meng Dengke sings "Pouring out my foolish heart...", Yuan the Blacksmith sings "An acute attack of convulsions meets—" (急惊风偏遇, jí jīngfēng piān yù), and bangqiang answers "the slow doctor!" (慢郎中, màn lángzhōng — a slow physician). **Commentary**, and a three-way relay completes one metaphor |
| 2 | p0025 | Bangqiang | "The survivor who escaped a great disaster," | **Narration / speaking for the character**: the scholar, in the pile of dead, doubts whether he is alive and is just trying to hum a line; bangqiang announces on his behalf that he is still alive |

| 4 | p0026 | Bangqiang | (Turns to 【刮骨令】) "Where will you stay, with three thousand wronged souls of Bashan?" | **Takes up the second line**: Meng Dengke sings "Sigh tonight, there is no inn on the road to the Yellow Springs," and bangqiang takes the parallel second line, **at the same time expanding one person's situation into the situation of three thousand** — emotional magnification |
| 5 | p0026 | Bangqiang | (Turns to the 合同 of 【孝顺歌】) "Husband, do not grieve; wife, do not weep; / take your wife to Chengdu!" | **Makes the decision for the character**. | 6 | p0030 | Bangqiang | "Put on a smiling face!" | **Exposes**: Nichang sings "Nichang the woman, softly singing—", and bangqiang cuts her off and supplies the truth. **Irony**; three characters puncture the nature of the entire victory banquet |

| 9 | p0065 | Bangqiang | "The slow doctor!" / "All in vain!" | **Loop**: scene 7 replays instance 1 exactly ("An acute attack of convulsions meets— the slow doctor"), and at the end of the passage, after Sun Yutian's aside "I'm drawing water with a bamboo basket—", bangqiang adds "all in vain!" **Twice in the same scene, bangqiang supplies the second half of a xiehouyu — once for the plaintiff, once for the pursuer** |

**The four functions of bangqiang (directly convertible into skill entries)**: ① **narration** — stating facts the character cannot conveniently say or cannot say (#2); ② **commentary** — standing outside the character to judge, expose, settle accounts (#1 #6 #8 #9); ③ **speaking for / participating** — speaking on the character's behalf, even deciding for him (#5); ④ **emotional magnification** — expanding one person into a crowd, a whisper into the whole stage (#4 #7). There are also two purely structural uses, **starting the song** and **taking up the second line** (#3 #4).

**Three formal rules of bangqiang**:
- **Extremely short**: 6 of the 9 instances are only 3–7 characters; the longest is 3 lines. **Bangqiang never carries a full passage of lyricism** — extended lyricism is always left to the characters.
- **Always placed where "the character cannot sing any further"**: the line is cut off (#6), the first line hangs waiting for its second (#4 #1 #9), the character is in a state of lost consciousness or lost judgment (#2 #5).
- **It can change the qupai**: #4 bangqiang starts a separate 【刮骨令】; #5 bangqiang "turns to the 合同 of 【孝顺歌】" — **the act of changing the tune is performed by bangqiang**, and the characters follow. This is bangqiang's structural power unique to the qupai system.
- **The boundary with "accompaniment / chorus"**: the script also has offstage **accompaniment** (two places in scene 5, the repeated last line at the end of scene 8) and **chorus** (the epilogue of scene 8, the singing girls in scene 4). Accompaniment/chorus are offstage and handle announcement and theme; bangqiang hugs the sung passage and handles the four functions above. **The typographic position is also different**: bangqiang shares the column with character names; accompaniment is marked "offstage" or "backstage."

## §4 Four Questions and Four Answers (original notes, with page numbers)

### 3. How the Qupai System Is Used in Modern Newly Written Plays: Four Questions and Four Answers (with page numbers)

#### Question 1: Is it in suites? — **No suites; the qupai suite (曲牌联套, qǔpái liántào — linking qupai into suites) is abandoned completely.**
- The play has 8 scenes; each scene uses 1–8 qupai, and **there is no modal relationship between the qupai**: scene 3 【幽冥钟】→【刮骨令】→【孝顺歌】 (p0025–0026), scene 5 【月儿高】→【香罗带】→【华秋儿】 (p0044–0047), scene 4 【七句半】→【昆曲】→【红衲袄】→【飞梆子】→【新水令】 (p0029–0039). **Not a single modal marking appears anywhere in the play.**
- **The same qupai is reused across scenes and across characters**: 【红衲袄】 goes to Yuan the Blacksmith in scene 1 (p0010), to Sun Yutian in scene 2 (p0019), and to Nichang in scene 4 (p0030) — hero, villain, and favorite concubine share one tune. 【飞梆子】 goes to Meng Dengke (p0033) and also to Hengbao (p0055). The 传奇 (chuánqí — Ming–Qing chuanqi drama) rules — "one 出 (chū — a chuanqi scene-unit), one mode; one suite to the end; sheng and dan (生旦, shēng dàn — male and female lead role types) do not share a tune" — do not apply at all.
- The only surviving relic of suite construction is **continued singing** (接唱, jiēchàng): two people sing the same qupai in succession (#3/#4, #5/#6, #29/#30), occupying the position of the 合头 (hétóu — the chorus tag at the end of a Southern tune), but used only for duet confrontation.
- → **Placed alongside 翁偶虹 (Weng Ouhong)**: Weng Ouhong said that in full-length Kunqu plays "the suite form is too constraining" and switched to "scattered gold and broken jade" (零金碎玉, líng jīn suì yù — selecting individual tunes instead of whole suites), selecting tunes in an interlocking way (`weng-bianju-shengya`, recommendation 4). Wei Minglun reached the same step in Sichuan opera's gaoqiang, and more thoroughly — he does not even write the explanation "re-set the tune, re-fix the qupai"; he simply marks the tune title and uses it. **Two genres, two authors, two eras independently reach the same conclusion: modern newly written plays do not use suites.**

#### Question 2: Is the selection of qupai "scattered gold and broken jade"? — **Yes; the basis for choosing a tune is emotion and function, not mode.**
A **qupai–emotion correspondence table** can be assembled (empirical evidence from this play, not a rule of the tune manuals):
- **【幽冥钟】 【刮骨令】**: death and parting, ghostly air, aftermath (scene 3, husband and wife recognizing each other in a mountain of corpses and a sea of blood).
- **【香罗带】**: a woman recounting half a lifetime of bitterness (scene 5, 24 lines in one rhyme to the end).
- **【月儿高】**: night, solitude, a long monologue of farewell to the past (scene 5, burning the books).
- **【一枝花】**: dying, stating the theme (scene 8, the last qupai of the play).
- **【新水令】**: long, elaborate pleading/persuasion (scene 4, 20 lines).
- **【七句半】**: a short, smug entrance (scene 4, Sun Yutian, 4 lines).
- **【流水腔】**: a headlong parallel narration (two numerical parallel passages, 28 lines and 10 lines).
- **【红衲袄】**: six-line blocks of angry denunciation / self-incrimination / opening excitation, a **general-purpose tune** that anyone can sing.
- **【梭梭岗】**: official-document style, written register (scene 1, reading the petition).
- → **Criterion**: what can be explained is "how long is this emotion, what key is it in"; what cannot be explained is "what mode was the previous tune in."

#### Question 3: Are the qupai's character counts and metrical rules observed? — **Half observed: "one rhyme to the end of a passage" is kept; character counts and tonal patterns are not.**
- **What is kept**: ① each qupai **runs one rhyme to the end (一韵到底, yī yùn dào dǐ), with no change of rhyme midway**. 【红衲袄】 uses the 中东 (zhōngdōng) rhyme (用／虫／梦／风／涌／峰, p0010); 【香罗带】's 24 lines all rhyme in 由求 (yóuqiú) (秀／愁／就／头／走／丢／咒／头／酒／飕／口／秋／手／牛／受／头／手／流／首／求, p0046–0047); 【月儿高】's 22 lines all rhyme in 姑苏 (gūsū) (釜／书／股／腹／路／书／苦／枯／赋／徒／户／颅／悟／除／腐／涂／聩／骨／府／目／固／夫, p0044–0045). ② each qupai has a **roughly fixed scale of line count**: 【红衲袄】 6 lines, 【飞梆子】 4, 【七句半】 4, 【一枝花】 4, 【梭梭岗】 6.
- **What is not kept**: ① **character counts within the same qupai are free**. 【新水令】 mixes four-character repeated lines ("Open the net on one side, / open the net on one side"), four sets of four-character parallels ("Grand Chengdu, towering Sichuan, / every yamen, every case"), seven-character lines, and ten-character lines ("It is not the dragon-tiger fight at the Chu–Han border"). 【一枝花】 is three-three-four ("Three cups of wine, three cups of wine, every cup calls death"). ② **three-character openings** (三字头, sān zì tóu — a three-character opening phrase) **are used in quantity** ("Wait till I— / go to Chengdu, / enter the examination shed"; "Startled, I turn my head"; "Vainly leaving behind"; "When I think back"), a 板腔体 (bǎnqiāngtǐ — banqiang system) habit rather than qupai metrics. ③ **line counts can be extended without limit**: 【流水腔】 28 lines, 【香罗带】 24, 【月儿高】 22, far beyond the original tune's capacity. ④ tone patterns (平仄, píngzè — level and oblique tones) are not required; colloquial speech, Sichuan dialect words (of the 拢 [lǒng — to approach], 咋个 [zǎge — how], 巴适 [bāshì — comfortable, just right] type), idioms, and xiehouyu go straight into song.
- → **One-sentence conclusion**: **tune title → emotion and scale; rhyme → strict; character count and tone → ignored.** This directly conflicts with 吴梅 (Wu Mei)'s and 王季烈 (Wang Jilie)'s demand that "to fill words one must follow the manual"; when writing the skill, the old and new positions must be placed side by side, not reduced to one.

#### Question 4: Are there any self-made qupai titles? — **No new tune titles are invented, but the "tune-title slot" is expanded.**
**Not one qupai in this play is newly invented by the author** (all 11 high-pitched qupai are ready-made Sichuan-opera tunes; 【新水令】, 【一枝花】, and 【月儿高】 are even old Northern tunes). But **in the parentheses where a tune title should go**, four kinds of things that are not qupai appear:
1. **Vocal system as tune title**: "(sings 'Kunqu')" appears 4 times (p0029 Hengbao, p0030 the singing girls, p0050 offstage accompaniment, p0074 backstage). This is Sichuan opera's "five vocal systems used together" (五腔并用, wǔqiāng bìngyòng) notation — **in the tune-title slot the author marks "this passage switches to Kunqu singing"**; which specific Kunqu qupai to use is left to the composer.
2. **Another genre's metrical pattern as tune title**: "(sings Peking opera 'Xipi Erliu')" once (p0054); the Imperial Commissioner speaks Beijing speech throughout and sings a Peking-opera metrical pattern. **The vocal system here is a character tag**: Sichuan people sing gaoqiang; the prince from Beijing sings Peking opera.
3. **Recitation method as tune title**: "(recites 'rhymed speech')" (p0049).
4. **Vocal-pattern category / loose tune**: 【笛引】 (entrance prelude), 【七句半】, 【流水腔】 — these are less qupai than categories of gaoqiang vocal patterns.
- → **Entry for the skill**: in a modern newly written qupai-system script, **the qupai-name slot is in practice a "singing-instruction slot"**, which can be filled with a qupai name, a vocal-system name, another genre's metrical-pattern name, or a recitation method. This is far more permissive than "one must fill in a metrically correct qupai," and is one of the technical reasons the qupai system can survive in modernity.

#### Appendix: One Item Beyond the Four Questions — **A Whole Tang Poem Enters the Song**
In scene 2 Nichang sings the whole of Li Shangyin's "Written on a Rainy Night to a Friend in the North" (p0020); in scene 4 the singing girls sing the whole of Du Fu's "To General Hua" (p0030). **Not a word is changed**, and neither is given a tune title. Both do double duty: in "Written on a Rainy Night to a Friend in the North," "night rain at Bashan" hints at Nichang's native place (in the next line Geshiha reports "the Bashan magistrate has fired a cannon and sent an urgent report," and Nichang blurts out "Bashan?"); "To General Hua" originally satirizes presumption, and used at the Governor's victory banquet it is ready-made irony. **The qupai system already had a "Tang-assembled" tradition (集唐, jítáng — assembling lines from Tang poems; *The Peony Pavilion*'s 55 出 each end with an exit poem assembled from Tang lines), and this play moves it from the exit poem into the sung passage.**

## §5 The Conventions Layer: How Bangqiang Is Used and the Other Conventions (full text of main text 5.4)

In the script, bangqiang is **set in parallel with the character's song**, with the two characters "帮腔" at the head of the line, occupying the same position as a character name — **it is treated as a character in the script** (all nine passages in §3).

- **Four functions, only one chosen per instance**: **narration** (stating facts the character cannot conveniently or cannot say — the scholar in the pile of dead doubts he is alive, and bangqiang announces on his behalf "the survivor who escaped a great disaster") / **commentary** (exposing, settling accounts — Nichang sings "Nichang the woman, softly singing—", and bangqiang cuts in with "Put on a smiling face!") / **speaking for / participating** (speaking for the character, even deciding for him) / **emotional magnification** ("Sigh tonight, there is no inn on the road to the Yellow Springs" answered by "Where will you stay, with three thousand wronged souls of Bashan?" — the couplet is complete while the subject shifts from "I" to "three thousand souls"; two lines complete the leap from private grief to public justice). There are also two purely structural uses, **starting the song** and **taking up the second line**.
- **Three formal rules**: ① **extremely short** — six of nine are only 3–7 characters, the longest 3 lines; **bangqiang never carries a full passage of lyricism**; ② **always placed where "the character cannot sing any further"** — the line is cut off, the first line hangs waiting for its second, the character has lost consciousness or judgment; ③ **bangqiang can perform the tune change** (starting a separate 【刮骨令】, "turning to the 合同 of 【孝顺歌】"), and the characters follow. **This is bangqiang's structural power unique to the qupai system.**
- **The boundary with accompaniment / chorus**: bangqiang hugs the sung passage and shares the column with character names; **accompaniment/chorus are offstage** (marked "offstage" or "backstage") and handle announcement and theme.
- **The lone exception: a character hears bangqiang and adopts it.** Bangqiang sings "Husband, do not grieve; wife, do not weep; / take your wife to Chengdu!", and Meng Dengke answers, "**Hey, that burst of help was good!**" and immediately does as told → bangqiang changes from "the collective voice" into "the voice of fate" and at once produces a comic beat. **This happens only once in the whole play**, and it must be placed at the point where the character has nowhere left to turn; use it a second time and it degrades into an ordinary supporting character.
- **Other conventions**: the entrance prelude replaces self-introduction (【笛引】's four lines "Office and fame are one eight-legged essay, / with my 'yea' and 'verily'"); **the scene-setting poem uses four four-character lines** (the Imperial Commissioner's "By imperial command I leave the court, / the earth shakes and the mountains sway," short and official in tone, reserving irony for the final scene's reversal); **three-person relay asides** (scene 4, Sun/Li/Heng alternating 23 lines, singing their conspiracy to the audience right in front of the plaintiff); **two asides on stage at once** (scene 7, the Attendant and Sun Yutian occupy opposite sides, their two lines of aside alternating, neither hearing the other); **face-changing** (变脸, biànliǎn) **written into the action cue** (used at the point of a personality reversal, not as a stunt); **an improvised prose funeral oration replacing the exit poem** (scene 3); **the hallucination scene marked by anachronistic costumes** (the candidates wear traditional opera dress, Meng Dengke alone wears Qing dress; no lighting, no narration); percussion cues (yin gong and drum / dao gong / hall majesty / flag-gate cannon).

### Convention Checklist (original notes)

#### 2. Convention Checklist (actual usage in this play)

1. **Entrance prelude replacing self-introduction**: Meng Dengke's 【笛引】 "Office and fame are one eight-legged essay, / with my 'yea' and 'verily.' / Both ears shut to the world outside the window, / my heart only reads the sages' books." (p0011) — four lines that establish the character's backstory, values, and the thing the whole play will break, all at once. Before his entrance there is also a long offstage cry, "The Master says..." as a sound prelude.
2. **Scene-setting poem**: the Imperial Commissioner's "By imperial command I leave the court, / the earth shakes and the mountains sway. / I investigate the wrongful case, / and prop up the people!" (p0068) — **four four-character lines**, not the usual seven-character lines; short, official self-aggrandizement, reserving irony for the reversal in scene 8.
3. **Aside / back-turned song**: in scene 4 the three officials alternate 23 lines of back-turned song ("General, advisor, elephant / one game of chess / red dragon, green dragon, white dragon / meeting the three yuan / you, me, him / holding together / hand in hand / official protects official" [帅、仕、相／棋一盘／中、发、白／会三元／你、我、他／抱成团／手牵手／官保官]), **singing their conspiracy to the audience in front of the plaintiff**; Nichang has two back-turned songs (homesickness, calculating how to save someone), used for the character's double identity. Scene 7 goes further: the Attendant and Sun Yutian **occupy opposite sides of the same stage, each "turning his back"** (背躬, bèigōng), their two lines of aside alternating, neither hearing the other.
4. **Back-turned speech**: Sun Yutian's "Blockhead, muddle-headed; the woman is formidable!" (p0040) — one line passes judgment on two characters.
5. **Face-changing**: "Sun Yutian kicks Bantou to the ground and, covering his face with his sleeve, 'changes face.' / Bantou sees Sun's ferocious features and, terrified, agrees again and again." (p0018) — **a Sichuan-opera special effect written directly into the script's action cue**, and used at the point of a personality reversal, not as a stunt.
6. **Improvised funeral oration replacing the exit poem** (scene 3, see above): prose, parallel couplets, rhyming on 白／糊涂／人／飨 (bái / hútu / rén / xiǎng), ending with "Alas, alas, accept this offering."
7. **Prelude and epilogue chorus**: four four-character lines before the curtain and a two-line chorus at the end, forming a frame.
8. **Play-within-a-play / self-mockery convention**: Dian Yaogu: "Have you gone crazy watching Sichuan opera? The young lady strolls in the garden, the young gentleman plights his troth, goes to the capital and comes first in the examination, returns for a grand reunion! There are plenty of top scholars on stage; in this world they are really rare." (p0042), and "Just like a Sichuan opera does it — (walks a stage stride) Number One in Heaven, collect your paper!" — **letting the character on stage puncture the conventions of his own genre**, both a comic device and a premonition that this play refuses the grand reunion.
9. **Hallucination scene** (scene 5): on stage the chief examiner Zhang Zhidong wears full Qing official dress, with four **costumed extras** (龙套, lóngtào) in ancient dress on either side; the candidates wear **traditional opera costume**, with the full range of 生净末丑 (shēng jìng mò chǒu — the four role types), while Meng Dengke alone remains in Qing dress — **the anachronism of costume is used to mark "this is a hallucination,"** with no lighting and no narration.
10. **Percussion cues**: "yin gong and drum" (scene 3, the scholar's corpse stirs), "dao gong" (道锣, dào luó — the gong that clears the way for an official; scene 4, Sun Yutian's entrance procession), "hall majesty" (堂威, tángwēi — the yamen runners' shout of authority; several places), "flag-gate cannon" (scene 2, both percussion and plot).

## §6 The Main Line's Four Levels of Appeal and the Foreshadowing/Payoff Checklist

### 3. Main Line: Four Levels of Appeal and the Cognitive Ascent at Each Level

Meng Dengke's line "from pedantry to awakening" is in the script **four levels of appeal = four deceptions = four cognitive ascents** (the author says so in his talk on craft; see the third set of notes, section 3.9). Each level must let him **know one more thing about the world**, otherwise it is merely a repeated setback:

| Level | Scene | Where he appeals | How it fails | What he newly learns |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 (refuses to appeal) | 1 | —— (refuses to write the complaint) | Sings his dream of office and is cursed down on the spot by the blacksmith | Still knows nothing |
| 1 | 3 | —— (killed before he can appeal) | The suppression bloodbath; the blacksmith dies in his place | "How can the authorities have any justification for suppression?" — **he begins to ask questions** |
| 2 Pedantic appeal | 4 | Hengbao, Governor of Sichuan | The chief criminal is the defendant himself; forty strokes | **Officials shield one another**; and he discovers that above Sichuan there is still the court |
| 3 Clever appeal | 5 | The court (through Zhang Zhidong's examination paper) | Apparent success; in fact it gives the court the chance to "throw out a scapegoat" | There is a road in the system that bypasses the local authorities |
| 4 Angry appeal | 6–8 | Imperial Commissioner / Empress Dowager | He wins the case, produces the evidence, and the third of three cups of imperial wine is poison | **"From the emperor above to the corrupt officials below, all crows under heaven are black"** — "The Great Qing (大清, Dà Qīng), greatly unclear (大大不清, dàdà bù qīng)!" |

**The main line's propulsion comes entirely from "other people bearing the consequences for him"**: Yuan the Blacksmith dies in his place (3), Nichang pleads for him and seizes the letter for him (4, 6), Meng Niangzi hangs herself to force him to take the examination (5). He is pushed forward at every step; this is exactly how to write a "very low starting point" — **the protagonist does not act on his own, but after every push his understanding jumps up one notch.**

### 4. Foreshadowing and Payoff Checklist (comic machinery)

1. **"负隅" read as "负偶"**: In scene 4 Hengbao misreads it (p0037), and the scholar, even at the execution ground, still wants to correct it: "My head may be cut off, my blood may flow, but a wrong character (别字, biézì) must still be corrected"; in scene 8 the scholar turns it back on him: "General Heng, do not '负偶' (fù'ǒu) and resist stubbornly!" (p0071). **One wrong character is planted for four scenes, and at the payoff the victim uses it as a weapon.**
2. **Mahjong / gambling through-line**: scene 2 Hengbao plays tiles → a concubine goads him to "fire a cannon" (放炮, fàngpào — "fire a cannon"; in mahjong, to discard a tile that lets another player win) → as he plays a tile, the flag-gate cannon sounds offstage (**"fire a cannon" is a double entendre, the most ingenious transition in the play**) → a "two-circle" tile falls from his sleeve (p0023) → scene 4 the three men's back-turned song "General, advisor, elephant / red dragon, green dragon, white dragon / meeting the three yuan" (三元, sānyuán — the three dragon tiles) → scene 6 "sacrifice the chariot to save the general," "better to sacrifice the chariot than the cannon" (p0056). **The metaphor "officialdom = a gambling game" is written as a chain of countable props.**
3. **"The golden cicada sheds its shell" used three times**: scene 1 Yuan the Blacksmith suspects it (p0017) → scene 7 Sun Yutian says it of Nichang (p0065) → the caption to production still no. 2 confirms it as Sun Yutian's tag.
4. **The inversion of name and age**: the Imperial Commissioner hears "Meng Dengke" as "Ke Dengmeng" and records fifty-one as fifteen (p0054); in scene 8, in open court, "reversing it back" (颠转来, diān zhuǎn lái) twice unravels the tangle (p0068–0069). **Mishearing becomes the ticket of admission to scene 8.**
5. **The goading trick used twice**: in scene 2 Concubine A goads Hengbao, "Of course the Commander would not dare 'fire a cannon'" → he insists on firing (p0021); in scene 4 Nichang uses the same sentence pattern to goad him, "Even Magistrate Sun says it cannot be fired; of course the Commander would not dare fire it" → "I insist on firing!" (p0040). **The same character flaw is used twice; the second time it saves a life.**
6. **"Three cups of imperial wine"**: in scene 1's dream of office, "three cups of imperial wine, the emperor's favor" (p0013) → scene 5 Meng Niangzi, "Where are your three cups of imperial wine?" (p0046) → scene 8 actually bestows three cups, and the third is poison (p0072–0073). **The prop from the dream is delivered exactly as dreamed, and the delivery kills.**
7. **"Lower Official Room No. 3"**: Dian Yaogu says of this room, "one died of rage, one of a fatal collision, one by hanging," and worries the scholar will become "No. 4" (p0042) → Meng Niangzi really does hang herself in this room (p0048) → in scene 7, "that place has already been searched; they will not search it again" turns it into a hiding place (p0062). **The same space changes its nature three times.**

## §7 Four Bangqiang Examples and Representative Lyrics

### 4. Four Bangqiang Examples (five lyric passages moved to the third set of notes, section 2)

**（1）Bangqiang makes a decision for someone, and the character thanks it on the spot** (scene 3, p0026) — the lone case of bangqiang as a "present being"
```
Meng Dengke   (sings) My wife!
Meng Niangzi  (sings) My husband!
Bangqiang     (turns to the 合同 of 【孝顺歌】) Husband, do not grieve; wife, do not weep,
                          take your wife to Chengdu!
Meng Dengke   (the scales fall from his eyes) Hey, that burst of help was good!
Meng Niangzi  Right. A husband never leaves his wife,
Meng Dengke   a steelyard never leaves its pan.
The two       The two of us set out together!
```
Demonstration: bangqiang can **drive the plot** and not merely heighten atmosphere; once the character is allowed to hear bangqiang, it changes from "the collective voice" into "the voice of fate," and at once produces a comic beat ("that burst of help was good").

**（2）Bangqiang magnifies one person into three thousand** (scene 3, p0026)
```
Meng Dengke   (sings) Sigh tonight, there is no inn on the road to the Yellow Springs—
Bangqiang     (sings 【刮骨令】) Where will you stay, with three thousand wronged souls of Bashan?
```
Demonstration: **the paired lines are split between the character and bangqiang**; the couplet is complete, but the subject changes from "I" to "three thousand souls." Two lines complete the leap from private grief to public justice; this is the first appearance of the play's theme.

**（3）Bangqiang's cool commentary** (scene 5, p0048)
```
Meng Niangzi  (sings) If you file a suit, I will hang myself!
Bangqiang     Another death in Lower Official Room No. 3!
```
Demonstration: "comedy through tears" in two lines. Bangqiang uses **the voice of the inn's accountant**, a viewpoint completely different from the emotion on stage — this is bangqiang's commentary function.

**（4）Bangqiang exposes the surface behavior** (scene 4, p0030)
```
Nichang       (sings 【红衲袄】) Nichang the woman, softly singing—
Bangqiang                        Put on a smiling face!
Nichang       (back-turned song) I turn my back, a whole heart's homesickness mourning the old garden...
```
Demonstration: three characters from bangqiang cut off the sung line and **rewrite "softly singing" as "putting on a smiling face"**; the character then turns into a back-turned song and tells the truth. Bangqiang here is the switch from "performance" to "inner life."

## §8 Transferable Techniques (full text of examples, self-checks, and failure conditions; complete version of main text 5.5)

1. **Same-qupai loop**: the same tune and the same bangqiang line each appear once early and once late in the play, with the content in symmetrical opposition. [Scene 1: 【流水腔】 + bangqiang "the slow doctor," counting up from one to ten through the dream of office ("A clap of spring thunder announces success... tenfold perfection, a hall full of red"); scene 7: the same tune and same bangqiang, counting down from ten to one through the history of appeals ("Ten years of cold study, the dream of the top scholar; nine examinations failed, an old man... both ears shut to the world outside the window"), the countdown reaching "one" and being cut off by Bantou's retort "One? ... what nonsense!", with "all in vain" supplied by bangqiang.] **Failure condition**: if the two instances are too close together (same scene or adjacent scenes), it becomes repetition; the two must sit on opposite sides of a character change, and the second must be able to negate the first. **The banqiang system cannot do this so cleanly** — it lacks the marker of "the same qupai."
2. **Bangqiang writes only extremely short lines and places them only where the character cannot sing any further; give each instance one function label, and cut any that cannot be labeled.** **Self-check**: if the line runs longer than a seven-character line, ask "why is this line not given to the character to sing?" **Failure condition**: if one passage switches between two functions, the audience no longer knows whose voice this is.
3. **Use bangqiang for cool commentary, turning tragedy into comedy within a single line.** [Meng Niangzi sings "If you file a suit, I will hang myself!" — bangqiang: "Another death in Lower Official Room No. 3!", in the voice of the inn's accountant, a viewpoint completely different from the emotion on stage.] **Failure condition**: if the play's style is not a blend of tragedy and comedy, this will look like mocking the protagonist — confirm the stylistic register before using it.
4. **Use vocal systems to tag characters.** [Sichuan officials sing gaoqiang; the prince from Beijing speaks Beijing speech and sings Peking opera 【西皮二六】; the powerful celebrate with Kunqu (elegance = falseness).] **Failure condition**: it does not work in a genre with only one vocal system (Peking opera, Henan opera, Yue opera), where 行当 (hángdang — role types) and 板式 (bǎnshì — metrical patterns) do the distinguishing. **Self-check**: remove one character's vocal-system marking; if the audience cannot tell that he is an outsider, the device is not working, and deleting the marking is cleaner.
5. **A whole existing poem can enter as a sung passage unchanged, but it must do two things at once**: fit the situation on stage and carry a second layer of meaning. **Self-check**: ask "what would the audience know less of if this poem were removed?" If you cannot answer, replace it with newly written lyrics.
6. **Use the same character flaw twice; the second time it saves a life.** [Scene 2 Concubine A goads Hengbao, "Of course the Commander would not dare 'fire a cannon'" → he insists on firing (the double entendre "fire a cannon" leads into the offstage flag-gate cannon, the most ingenious transition in the play); scene 4 Nichang uses the same sentence pattern to goad him → "I insist on firing!", saving the scholar.] A general-layer technique, cross-referenced with `sw-character-conflict`.
7. **The prop from a dream is delivered exactly as dreamed, and the delivery kills.** [Scene 1's dream of office, "three cups of imperial wine, the emperor's favor" → scene 5 Meng Niangzi, "Where are your three cups of imperial wine?" → scene 8 really bestows three cups, the third is poison.] Analogues: one misread character planted for four scenes ("负隅" read as "负偶"; in scene 8 the victim uses it as a weapon); the same space changes its nature three times ("Lower Official Room No. 3": a haunted room where three people have died → Meng Niangzi really hangs herself there → "that place has already been searched; they will not search it again" turns it into a hiding place).

**Position in the method layer**: this play does not enter the qupai skill's §11 (which is "masterworks breaking the rules") but **§10, "modern newly written qupai-system plays"** — its practice and Weng Ouhong's Kunqu practice (no suites, "scattered gold and broken jade" interlocking selection, on the grounds that "the suite form is too constraining") are **the same conclusion independently reached by two genres, two authors, and two eras**; while 罗怀臻 (Luo Huaizhen)'s 《班昭》 (*Ban Zhao*) takes the opposite road (written in modern vernacular, "yet not one character of mine breaks the rules," at the cost that "sometimes it takes two days to fill in one qupai"). **The three stand side by side, in direct conflict with Wu Mei's and Wang Jilie's "to fill words one must follow the manual"; keep the old and the new, do not merge, do not choose one.**

**Terminological conflict must be flagged**: "**背面敷粉**" (bèimiàn fūfěn — applying powder to the back) in this play's author's talk means **using the old era to set off the new era** ("borrowing the affairs of the old court to utter the voice of people today"), while in Weng Ouhong's six self-forged methods it means **using contrasting characters within one play to set off the protagonist** — same name, different meaning; they must not be merged.

## §9 Four Items That Belong to the General Layer, Not to Opera (sources in the author's talk)

- The protagonist was chosen by **the method of negating ready-made types one by one** (negate the righteous popular leader = the prototype's personality is not distinctive enough / negate the honest-official play = falls into a generic rut / negate a historically documented official = cannot carry the theme; what remains is the protagonist specific to this subject) → `sw-character-conflict`; original text in that notebook's 3.4 "The three negations in choosing the protagonist (p0078–0079)" and 3.5, 3.6.
