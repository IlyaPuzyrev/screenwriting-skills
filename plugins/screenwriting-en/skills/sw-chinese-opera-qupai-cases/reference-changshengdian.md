# reference-changshengdian.md — 洪昇 (Hong Sheng), 《長生殿》 (The Palace of Eternal Life): The Fifty-Scene Staging Table, after 王季烈 (Wang Jilie)

> **What this file contains**: the complete fifty-scene 排场 (páichǎng — staging) table of The Palace of Eternal Life (scene／title／宫调 gōngdiào — mode／role／nature of the plot), the six-way classification of Southern suites (套数, tàoshù) by emotional color, the law matching suites to roles, the rule for the entrance of sheng (生) and dan (旦), the use of the 赚 and the counterexample of 《紫釵記》 (The Purple Hairpin), and the full text of the structural-layer and convention-layer conclusions moved out of the main text.
> **Copy-text and collation status**: **there are no complete-text notes for The Palace of Eternal Life in this batch of material**—all data come from volume 2, chapter 4 of the 《螾庐曲谈》 (Yinlu's Talks on Song) by 王季烈 (Wang Jilie) (printed pp. 0160–0180, read directly from page images; quotations are transcribed from the book facsimile and **are not marked pending verification**; printed page = scan page − 22). It can therefore **discuss only staging and role allocation, not qupai (曲牌, qǔpái — tune patterns), lyrics, or the aria-to-speech ratio**.

## §2 Southern Suites Classified into Six Categories by Emotional Color (Wang Jilie's most practical look-up table in this chapter)

### 4.4 Southern Suites Classified into Six Categories by Emotional Color (p0162–p0163; the most practical "look-up table" in this book)
**Suitable for joy**: Southern Zhonglü suites 2 and 3; Southern Huangzhong suites 1 and 2; Southern Nanlü suite 4; Southern Shuangdiao suite 1; Southern Dashi suites 1 and 2
**Suitable for travel and sightseeing**: Southern Nanlü suite 4; Southern Dashi suite 1; Zhenggong North-South composite suite; Southern Zhonglü suite 2
**Suitable for grief**: Southern Yuediao suite 2; Southern Shangdiao suites 4 and 5; repeated qupai (prelude, three 三仙橋; prelude, four 風雲會四朝元; prelude, four 金絡索)
**Suitable for hidden resentment**: Southern Xiaoshi suite 1; repeated qupai (prelude, four 風雲會四朝元; prelude, four 江頭金桂; five sections of 雁魚錦)
**Suitable for action**: Southern Zhonglü suite 1; Southern Zhenggong suite 4; repeated qupai (prelude, four 甘州歌; prelude, coda, prelude, four 朝元令; prelude, two 二犯江兒水; prelude, four or six 香柳娘; prelude, four 鎖南枝)
**Suitable for feeling**: Southern Shangdiao suites 1, 2, and 3; Southern Xianlü suite 1; Southern Nanlü suites 1, 2, 3, and 5; Southern Zhenggong suite 1; Southern Huangzhong suite 5; Southern Xianlü-into-Shuangdiao suites 4, 5, and 8; repeated qupai (prelude, four 祝英臺; prelude, four 綿搭絮)

**Four categories outside the six (p0164)**:
- **For ordinary use**: Southern Xianlü suites 1 and 2; Southern Huangzhong suites 4, 5, and 6; Southern Zhenggong suites 1, 2, and 3; Southern Yuediao suites 3 and 4; all Southern Xianlü-into-Shuangdiao suites
- **For martial plays**: Huangzhong North-South composite suite, Zhenggong North-South composite suite; prelude, four 四邊靜; two each of 水底魚兒 and 包子令; four 六么令; two each of 神仗兒 and 滴溜子; prelude, two 馱環著, two 四邊靜, 清江引; two Northern 點絳唇, three 犯江兒水; Northern 點絳唇, two 四邊靜, 對玉環帶清江引
- **For short transition scenes**: four 尾犯序; four 駐雲飛; two each of 縷縷金 and 四邊靜; 縷縷金 and 剔銀燈; prelude, two 皂羅袍; four 香柳娘; prelude, two 水紅花; four 鎖南枝; four 孝順歌; prelude, two 催拍, 一撮棹; one suite of 風入松犯; 縷縷金 and 撲燈蛾
- **For quiet short scenes**: four or six 懶畫眉; four 玉芙蓉; two each of 駐馬聽 and 越恁好; prelude, two each of 勝如花 and 解三酲; prelude, four 高陽台; prelude, two each of 太師引 and 劉潑帽; prelude, two each of 針線箱 and 大迓鼓, 秋夜月

> "**An author who reads many chuanqi will naturally be able to combine them at will and vary them as the heart directs.**" (p0164)—the table is an entrance, not a cage.

## §3 The Law Matching Suites to Roles, the Rule for the Entrance of Sheng and Dan, and the Law of Alternating Staging

### 4.5 Matching suites to roles (p0166, a direct law of allocation)
- **The "feeling" category**: "all are delicate, well-fitted tunes of lingering emotion, and are mostly long suites; **the principal scenes of a chuanqi should use this kind of suite**, and they suit what **sheng (xiaosheng) and dan** sing."
- **The "joy" category** suits **unison singing**; the **travel and action** categories are also mostly suited to unison singing; the **grief and hidden resentment** categories mostly suit **dan singing**, though xiaosheng may also sing them.
- **For sheng and jing (jing here means damian) and laosheng meeting a grief play, Northern tunes are preferable**, such as the various Northern Nanlü suites, "most suited to broad-voice roles."
- Original text: "**In sum, broad-voice roles sing mostly Northern lyrics; Southern lyrics are only twenty to thirty percent of their repertory. For Southern tunes are soft and languorous with little that is vigorous, and so do not suit the voices and mettle of sheng and jing.**"
- **Short transition scenes** (commonly called "passage plays" [過脈戲]): "Though the tunes are not many, **without them the plot does not connect and the threads do not join; they are absolutely indispensable in a chuanqi**. Use short tunes and fast tunes, **and never use the slow tunes of a long suite**."
- **Coarse tunes** (such as 普賢歌 and 光光乍) **are restricted to what chou, jing, baimian, and ermian sing**.

### 4.8 Sheng and dan are the main brain of the whole play and must appear in the second and third scenes (p0167)
- Chuanqi through the ages have "mostly taken **one sheng and one dan** as the masters of the whole. The sheng may be a guansheng (冠生 — capped sheng) or a jinsheng (巾生 — scarf sheng); the dan is mostly the **wudan, that is, the guimendan (閨門旦 — boudoir dan)**"—《浣紗記》 (The Washing of the Silk Gauze): 范蠡 (Fan Li) and 西施 (Xi Shi); 《紫釵記》 (The Purple Hairpin): 李益 (Li Yi) and 霍小玉 (Huo Xiaoyu); 《牡丹亭》 (The Peony Pavilion): 柳夢梅 (Liu Mengmei) and 杜麗娘 (Du Liniang); 《紅梨記》 (The Red Pear): 趙汝舟 (Zhao Ruzhou) and 謝素秋 (Xie Suqiu); 《桃花扇》 (The Peach Blossom Fan): 侯方域 (Hou Fangyu) and 李香君 (Li Xiangjun); 《長生殿》 (The Palace of Eternal Life): 唐明皇 (Tang Minghuang) and 楊貴妃 (Yang Guifei).
- Original text: "This sheng and dan are the main brain of the whole chuanqi and **must appear in the second and third scenes**. The first scene of a chuanqi always explains the general idea, so its second scene is really the first, and its third scene is really the second. **This not only makes it easy for the audience to see at a glance, but is also the method of seizing the main point and drawing all the threads together—the way of writing should surely be like this.**"
- **Exceptions coexist**: "There are also cases where this is not so—《邯鄲夢》 (The Handan Dream) takes the laosheng (盧生, Lu Sheng) as its lead, and 《鈞天樂》 (The Music of Heaven) takes the laosheng (沈白, Shen Bai) and xiaosheng (楊, Yang) as its leads. **If an author can invent from his own heart and capture a strange realm of his own, he need not keep to the ancients' fixed methods.**"

### 4.9 The law of alternating staging (p0169, the most executable rule)
> "Anyone writing a chuanqi must first arrange the allocation of roles properly, like the work of what today is called the **stage director**. **If the first scene has the sheng sing and the second has the dan sing, then the third must use a broad-voice role or a lively ensemble play; if two or three scenes of slow tunes and long suites have passed, a short transition scene or a comic scene performed by chou or jing must be inserted.**"

## §4 Conclusions at the Convention and Structural Layers (the full text of main-text 4.2 and 4.4)

Two criteria read directly off the fifty-scene table (see the full table in §1):

- **The three non-repetitions**: no repetition of qupai throughout, no repetition of mode between adjacent scenes, no repetition of principal role between adjacent scenes. This is the **only staging table in the whole batch whose forty-nine scenes can be checked one by one**, and it is also the sample for the "law of alternating staging."
- **The law of alternating staging** (p0169, common to both systems): "If the first scene has the sheng sing and the second has the dan sing, then the third must use **a broad-voice role or a lively ensemble play**; if two or three scenes of slow tunes and long suites have passed, a **short transition scene or a comic scene performed by chou or jing** must be inserted." **Self-check**: list the scenes in a column by "lead singer／nature of the music," and see whether three consecutive scenes share the same role and the same nature. **Failure condition**: a play of ten scenes or fewer need only keep "no two consecutive scenes with the same lead singer."
- **The plot-nature column is a ready-made classifier**: lively ensemble play／ordinary tune／fine lyric of feeling／lively transition play／quiet short play／transition play／vigorous Northern tune／action with celebration／hurried transition play／fine lyric of joy／fine lyric of hidden resentment／tender Northern tune／brisk Northern tune／martial play／joy turning to grief／action with grief／sorrowful Northern tune／vehement Northern tune／bantering Northern tune／short action play／comic transition play／lamenting Northern tune／clear and lovely Northern tune／grief play. **Count how many scenes of each nature your own play has, and you will know where the pile-up is.**
- **Southern suites are classified into six categories by emotional color** (the suite-by-suite list is in §2): suitable for **joy／travel／grief／hidden resentment／action／feeling**, plus four categories outside the six: ordinary, martial play, short transition scene, and quiet short scene. The law of allocation: **the "feeling" category: "all are delicate, well-fitted tunes of lingering emotion, and are mostly long suites; the principal scenes of a chuanqi should use this kind of suite, and they suit what sheng (xiaosheng) and dan sing"**; joy suits unison singing; grief and hidden resentment mostly suit dan singing; **for sheng, jing, and laosheng meeting a grief play, Northern tunes are preferable** ("Southern tunes are soft and languorous with little that is vigorous, and so do not suit the voices and mettle of sheng and jing"); **coarse tunes (such as 普賢歌 and 光光乍) are restricted to what chou, jing, baimian, and ermian sing**.
- **Short transition scenes cannot be omitted**: "Though the tunes are not many, **without them the plot does not connect and the threads do not join; they are absolutely indispensable in a chuanqi**. Use short tunes and fast tunes, **and never use the slow tunes of a long suite**."

The convention layer (the four items this table can support):

- **The first scene, 〈傳概〉, is the "customary opening piece"**, with fumo and a Southern Zhonglü slow lyric, and **is not counted in the three non-repetitions of the forty-nine scenes**—it occupies the same functional position as 〈標目〉 in 《牡丹亭》 (The Peony Pavilion) and the trial first scene in 《桃花扇》 (The Peach Blossom Fan).
- **The final scene, 〈重圓〉, returns to the format of the second scene**: the second scene, 〈定情〉, is "sheng, dan, and all role categories／lively ensemble play," and the fiftieth scene, 〈重圓〉, is "sheng, dan, and all role categories／lively ensemble play"—**beginning and end share one shape**.
- **The limit on the "赚" (also called "不是路")** (p0179): it is used **as a joint at changes of staging and shifts of mode**, and "**in a whole chuanqi the 赚 is best used in one scene; within one scene it is best not to use more than two**." Counterexample: The Purple Hairpin uses the 赚 in four scenes of the whole play; the two consecutive scenes "Sending the Matchmaker" and "Discussing the Marriage" both use it, and the 〈釵圓〉 scene uses as many as four—"all show a lack of care for prosody and staging."
- **For transferable techniques see SKILL.md 4.5**; **where a famous work breaks the rules**: 〈打圍〉 is discussed in qupai Section 11; also, 《幽閨記·請盟》 (The Secluded Boudoir · "Seeking an Alliance") mistakes the Yuediao tune 〔看花回〕 for the Xianlü tune 〔點絳唇〕, and **The Handan Dream and The Palace of Eternal Life repeat the error in a chain**—"predecessors marked it this way" is not evidence.

## §5 The Use of the 赚, and the Critique of Staging in the Four Dreams of Yuming

### 4.14 The use of the 賺 (p0179, a precise technical rule)
- "**The 賺** exists in every mode and is also called '不是路'; **it is most fitting at changes of staging and shifts of mode.**"
  - 《金雀記·喬醋》 (The Golden Sparrow · "Feigned Jealousy"): 太師引 handles the surprise of 安仁 (Anren) at receiving the letter; 江頭金桂 is the main text of the feigned jealousy; a 赚 links the two in between.
  - 《風箏誤·逼婚》 (The Kite · "Forced Marriage"): 桂枝香 handles the first meeting of 韓琪仲 (Han Qizhong) and 戚補臣 (Qi Buchen); from 長拍 onward Han, unable to refuse, frets alone; the main text of the forced marriage uses a 赚 in between.
- **The limit**: "**In a whole chuanqi the 赚 is best used in one scene; within one scene it is best not to use more than two.**" The Purple Hairpin uses the 赚 in four scenes of the whole play; the two consecutive scenes "Sending the Matchmaker" and "Discussing the Marriage" both use it, and the 〈釵圓〉 scene uses as many as four—"**all show a lack of care for prosody and staging**."

### 4.13 The critique of staging in the Four Dreams of Yuming and Cangyuan (p0177)
> "**The staging of all four Dreams of Yuming lacks judgment**; 《邯鄲記》 (Handan) and 《南柯記》 (Nanke) are slightly better, **but The Purple Hairpin's staging is the least fitting.**"
> Cangyuan (藏園), the studio name of 蔣士銓 Jiang Shiquan, "**cared only about preserving his aria texts, so that they became mixed and piled up, the tunes many while the plot on the contrary lost its point.**"
> "Today only the one scene 'Breaking the Willow' and 'Yang Pass' (which moderns split into two) from The Purple Hairpin reaches the theater; all the rest no one sings or performs—it is in truth unperformable."
—— Wang Jilie himself took the play in hand: "**I selected fourteen scenes from The Purple Hairpin and cut and revised them; for example, the original 'Discussing the Marriage' scene…**" (pp. 0177–0178 record his cuts item by item: of the four 一江風 that open the scene, the second, third, and fourth are deleted; of the last three 三仙橋, all slow tunes, three are cut so that the singer can manage; of the sixteen through-tunes, four 不是路, the coda, and three 哭相思—such a long stretch is rarely seen in Southern drama, and cannot be sung by one person…)
—— **This is a rare first-hand working record of "adapting an old text into a performable one,"** and can be compared with the four questions of adaptation in `sw-format-adaptation`.
