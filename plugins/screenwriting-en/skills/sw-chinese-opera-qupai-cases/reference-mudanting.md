# reference-mudanting.md — Tang Xianzu's 《牡丹亭》 [The Peony Pavilion], Fifty-Five 齣 (chū — chuanqi scenes)

> **What this file contains**: the complete scene-by-scene structural table for all 55 齣 of 《牡丹亭》 [The Peony Pavilion], the full conclusions at the aria/qupai layer, the North-South composite suite of 〈圆驾〉 ("The Imperial Decree"), the full text of the conventions, a comparison with the source tale, excerpts 1–10, and the collation record.
> **Base text and collation status**: the People's Literature Publishing House edition annotated by 徐朔方 (Xu Shuofang) and 杨笑梅 (Yang Xiaomei) (first edition 1963, 24th printing 2014); the epub has no page numbers, so everything is located by scene title. **83 items marked for checking have been checked; 4 corrected; 9 textual variants retained; 25 unverifiable.** **Any entry whose mode column carries 〔拟 (nǐ — proposed)〕 may serve only as an example, never as evidence.**
> **Which section of SKILL.md it corresponds to**: Section 2 (2.1–2.5). Main text 2.1 → §7; 2.2 → §1; 2.3 → §2, §3; 2.4 → §4, §6; 2.5 → §5, §8.
> **The complete aria table of 425 entries is not copied into this library**; see the "aria tables" section of each file in `_notes/tang-mudanting/01–07`.

## §2 The Aria/Qupai Layer (Full Text of Main Section 2.3)

- **The division of labor between Northern and Southern tunes** (the hardest rule in this play): **Northern tunes for foreign kings, bandits, ghost judges, courtrooms, and the imperial court** (〈虏谍〉 and the opening tune of 〈牝贼〉, the whole Northern Xianlü suite of 〈冥判〉, the Northern Shuangdiao suite of 〈硬拷〉, the opening tune of 〈榜下〉, the Huangzhong composite suite of 〈圆驾〉); **Southern tunes for the boudoir, the family, and scholars**, which may be divided among singers and may carry a unison tail (合头, hétóu). In a Northern suite **one person sings from beginning to end and everyone else has only spoken lines** — all ten arias of 〈冥判〉 belong to the jing (Judge Hu), while the dan, mo, and chou have only speech. This is a survival of Yuan zaju's "one-person lead singing" (一人主唱, yīrén zhǔchàng) inside Ming chuanqi (传奇, chuánqí — the long-form Southern drama of the Ming and Qing).
- **The North-South composite suite writes "one person against everyone" into the metrical law**: in the Huangzhong mode of 〈圆驾〉, the Northern 【醉花阴】 suite and the Southern 【画眉序】 suite alternate aria by aria, closing with the Northern 【尾】; **all ten Northern arias are sung by the dan alone from start to finish, while the Southern arias are divided among Du Bao, Liu Mengmei, Madam Zhen, Chen Zuiliang, the Taoist nun and Chunxiang, and the company, each Southern aria carrying a unison tail**. The Northern tune is high and has no unison tail, which fits "one person defending herself before the throne"; the Southern tune is supple and can be divided, which fits "each person holding to his own account." **When it fails**: if the finale has several people sharing right and wrong equally, the composite suite will hollow out the protagonist; switch to alternating stanzas of the same tune.
- **Using modes (宫调, gōngdiào) for cross-scene echo** (impossible in the banqiang system, 板腔体, bǎnqiāngtǐ): Liniang dies in the Southern Shang mode "ying" (oriole) suite (集贤宾 — 啭林**莺** — 玉**莺**儿 — 忆**莺**儿), and Liu's address to the portrait in 〈玩真〉 still uses the same group of tunes in the Southern Shang mode (黄莺儿 — 二郎神慢 — 莺啼序 — 集贤宾 — 黄莺儿 — 啼莺序 — 簇御林). **One is used for death, the other for calling her back.** A corollary: **when choosing a qupai, choose the literal wording of its title too** — three "莺" (oriole) characters in one four-tune suite belong to the same image chain as "the oriole's song, smooth and round" (「呖呖莺歌溜的圆」) in 〈惊梦〉 and the dead being demoted to orioles, swallows, bees, and butterflies in 〈冥判〉.
- **Tang-assembled exit poems (下场诗, xiàchǎngshī), present in all 55 scenes**: **four seven-character lines, with the original author's name in small characters beneath each line**; they may be recited by one actor, divided by role type, or carry a "unison" tag (in 〈闺塾〉 the four lines go to dan, tie, dan, and all). They may also be used for **entrances** (〈道觋〉 explicitly marks them "〔集唐〕") and **mid-scene as scenic speech** (one each in 〈拾画〉, 〈欢挠〉, and 〈冥誓〉). Two exceptions: scene 1 uses a four-line plot outline, and **scene 55 uses eight lines** (the only such case in the play; moreover it does not write the joy of reunion but "I hate all the more that the fragrant soul and I never met" (「更恨香魂不相遇」) and "I have sung all the new songs and seen no joy" (「唱尽新词欢不见」), answering the opening 【蝶恋花】 line "in this world only love is hard to tell" (「世间只有情难诉」)).
- **When a qupai appears a second time, write it against the reverse of its first appearance**: 【皂罗袍】 in scene 10, "so the purple and crimson bloom everywhere" / scene 49, sung to a wanted notice, thirty-nine scenes apart; 【夜游朝】 for encouraging farming / moving the garrison; 【锦缠道】 for entering the garden / entering the city; 【金珑璁】 for writing death / writing recovery from illness. **When it fails**: if the two appearances are within ten scenes of each other or share the same emotion, the contrast becomes repetition, and a different qupai should be chosen.
- **Composite tunes** (牌名带"犯" or joined names): 【九回肠】(解三酲 + 三学士 + 急三枪), 【刷子序犯】【朱奴儿犯】【山桃犯】【尾犯序】【金落索】【金索挂梧桐】【二犯梧桐树】【二犯幺令】【二犯江儿水】【桂花锁南枝】【莺皂袍】. They are used in passages where **emotion is fragmented and the character is walking, looking, and thinking at once**.

## §3 The North-South Composite Suite in 〈圆驾〉 (Aria by Aria)

### 3. The North-South Composite Suite in 〈圆驾〉: The Qupai System's Highest Format

- **Format**: the Northern Huangzhong 【醉花阴】 suite and the Southern Huangzhong 【画眉序】 suite alternate aria by aria, North—South—North—South…, closing with the Northern 【尾】.
- **Rules of division**: **all ten Northern arias are sung by the dan (Du Liniang) alone from beginning to end; the Southern arias are divided among Du Bao, Liu Mengmei, Madam Zhen, Chen Zuiliang, the Taoist nun and Chunxiang, and the company, and every Southern aria carries a unison tail.**
- **Why it is divided this way**: the Northern tune is high, sung by one person, and has no unison tail, exactly fitting "one person defending herself before the throne"; the Southern tune is supple, can be divided, and can be sung in chorus, exactly fitting "each person holding to his own account." **The composite suite is not a display of technique; it writes the fact of "one person against everyone" into the metrical law.**
- **Self-check**: if your finale is "one person facing everyone," consider a North-South composite suite; if it is "several people sharing right and wrong," the suite will instead hollow out the protagonist.

## §4 The Convention Layer (Full Text of Main Section 2.4)

- **Six items in the self-introduction (自报家门, zìbào jiāmén)**: name / native place / family background / present circumstances / heart's wish / what one is about to do, closing with a rhymed line "It is precisely…" 〈言怀〉, 〈腐叹〉, 〈道觋〉, 〈怅眺〉, 〈肃苑〉, 〈诀谒〉, 〈虏谍〉, 〈牝贼〉, 〈谒遇〉, 〈旁疑〉, 〈秘议〉, 〈仆侦〉, and 〈淮泊〉 all follow exactly the same format. **The self-introductions of chou and tie roles may be spoken in rhymed verse**, and may conveniently recap the previous scene to bring the audience up to speed (the passage of alternating six- and four-character rhymed speech after Chunxiang's 【夜游宫】 in 〈寻梦〉).
- **Entrance poems (上场诗, shàngchǎngshī)**: either a self-composed ci tune (〔汉宫春〕〔鹧鸪天〕〔乌夜啼〕〔醉桃源〕〔行香子〕〔画堂春〕〔浣溪沙〕〔苏幕遮〕〔清平乐〕〔相思令〕, etc.) or "〔集唐〕." The collation note states plainly: **entrance poems may use earlier poets' verse or be self-composed; quotations are sometimes slightly altered.**
- **Speech inserted within an aria does not occupy a metrical slot**: the character count and line pattern of the qupai are filled entirely by the lead singer, while other characters' interjections are wedged between lines (Chunxiang's three interjections in 〈闺塾〉, the dan's three lines of denial in 〈幽媾〉). **Self-check**: delete all the interjections — do the lead singer's lines still scan and still cohere? If not, the speech has taken up metrical slots.
- **Song for emotion, speech for reasoning (唱情、白理)**: lyric feeling and bodily sensation go into song; reasoning, judgments, legal statutes, and numbers go into speech (in 〈骇变〉 Chen Zuiliang's four-step false reasoning is all in speech; he sings only a lament for the young lady). **When it fails**: if the reasoning is itself the character's emotional explosion (the Qin Hui passage in 〈圆驾〉), it may still enter song.
- **Scene-change devices**: the qupai system has no curtain; the dark transition relies on **the Flower Spirit** — in 〈惊梦〉, after the sheng and dan exit, the mo enters as the Flower Spirit, sings 【鲍老催】, throws flowers toward the ghost gate, exits with "I go now" (「吾神去也」), and immediately the sheng and dan re-enter hand in hand. Kindred devices: **going and returning** (in 〈冥誓〉, after the dan exits, "(the dan re-enters) Is the magistrate still here?" — she gives one more passage of unmeasured speech before really exiting; the most important instruction is placed at the point of "already exited, then come back"); **two codas in one scene** (〈魂游〉: one for the ghost, one for the living).
- **A ghost's entrances and exits rely on three things**: ghostly voice, sleeve over the face, and offstage wind (plus a dimmed lamp). **The Home-Gazing Terrace**: on an empty stage, "mounting the terrace + gazing + weeping" accomplishes a journey of a thousand miles.
- **Use a ready-made convention as it stands, but reverse it once**: 〈遇母〉 tests the ghost with a stock Yuan-zaju routine (throw paper money + call three times, answer three times); Tang Xianzu adds one reversal — the rule says "each call higher than the last," but the performance has "the voice growing lower," so the ghost-testing convention proves, in reverse, that she is a ghost.

## §5 The Source Tale 《杜丽娘慕色还魂》 [Du Liniang Returns to Life for Love] Compared with the Play (Primary Material for the Four Moves of Adaptation)

### 5. Appendix 2: The Source Tale 《杜丽娘慕色还魂》 Compared with the Play (Primary Material on the Method of Adaptation)

The huaben (话本, huàběn — a vernacular story) is found in juan 9 of 《重刻增补燕居笔记》 [Yanju Biji, Revised and Expanded], compiled by He Dalun 何大抡 in the Ming; the full text is in the appendix to this book. Item-by-item comparison:

| Item | Tale | 《牡丹亭》 [The Peony Pavilion] |
|---|---|---|
| The Du family | Du Bao, Prefect of Nanxiong, has **a son and a daughter** (the son Xingwen, twelve years old) | Prefect of Nan'an, with **only one daughter** — the son is removed, and "no son" becomes the psychological motive force of Du Bao's character |
| Male lead | **Liu Mengmei, son of the succeeding prefect Liu Si'en**, named because his mother dreamed of eating a plum; the Liu and Du families are successive occupants of the post | A poor scholar from Lingnan, alone and unsupported, **who renames himself because he himself dreamed of a beauty beneath a plum tree** — the two dreams interlock |
| Finding the portrait | The young Liu obtains it "**among straw and scattered papers**" | On her deathbed Liniang asks that it be hidden beneath the Lake Tai rock; Chunxiang hides it secretly; Liu **finds it in a crevice of the lake-and-mountain rock in the ruined garden** |
| The tryst | The woman calls herself "the daughter of the family west of the yamen"; after more than ten nights she is pressed into telling the truth | The same (spread across three scenes: 〈幽媾〉, 〈欢挠〉, 〈冥誓〉), **with the addition of a sworn vow, trimming the lamp, and going and returning** |
| Return to life | Prefect Liu **presides over the exhumation**, bathes her in fragrant water, and administers soul-calming and spirit-settling powders | **Liu, Sister Shi, and Laitou Yuan open the grave without authority**, violating the Great Ming Code (《大明律》): "whoever opens a coffin and sees the corpse, whether principal or accessory, shall be beheaded" — the legal act becomes illegal, and the conflict of the play's second half grows from this |
| Reunion | "A marriage of matching doors is smoothly concluded"; Liu passes the jinshi examination, is appointed assistant prefect of Lin'an, the two families exchange letters, and two sons are born | **Du Bao refuses to acknowledge daughter or son-in-law, harshly interrogates the top scholar, the case is examined before the throne, and an edict grants the reunion** |
| Subplot | None | A whole new line: Li Quan / the Jin ruler / Du Bao's defense of the Huai (scenes 15, 19, 31, 38, 42, 43, 45, 46, 47, 50), plus new characters Chen Zuiliang, Sister Shi, Laitou Yuan, Guo Tuo, Miao Shunbin, and Han Zicai |
| Original sentences retained | 「春色恼人信有之乎……可惜妾身颜色如花，岂料命如一叶耶」 ("Is it really true that spring colors vex a person?... What a pity that my face is like a flower, who would have thought my fate would be like a single leaf?"); 「忽见一书生，年方弱冠，丰姿俊秀，于园内折杨柳一枝」 ("Suddenly I saw a scholar, barely twenty, handsome and elegant, breaking a willow branch in the garden"); 「妾千金之躯，一旦付与郎矣，勿负奴心」 ("I have given my precious body to you in a single moment; do not betray my heart"); 「妾若不得复生，必痛恨君于九泉之下矣」 ("If I cannot return to life, I will hate you bitterly beneath the nine springs") | **Retained almost word for word in the spoken dialogue** (〈惊梦〉, 〈幽媾〉, 〈冥誓〉). Xu Shuofang's "Preface": "In the spoken dialogue of 〈惊梦〉, 〈寻梦〉, and 〈闹殇〉, *The Peony Pavilion* still preserves several original sentences from the tale." |
| Poem on the portrait | 「他年得傍蟾宫客，不在梅边作柳边」 ("Some year I may stand beside the guest of the Toad Palace; if not by the plum, then by the willow") | 「**不在梅边在柳边**」 ("**Not by the plum, but by the willow**") — one character changed, and it becomes the play's master mechanism |

**The method of adaptation can be reduced to four moves**: ① **delete relatives** (remove the younger brother) to isolate the protagonist; ② **turn the legal into the illegal** (the exhumation is changed from an official proceeding to a private dig) to generate the second half's conflict; ③ **change "love at first sight and a private betrothal in the rear garden" into "a dream tryst plus a second seeking of the dream"** (Xu Shuofang's "Preface"); ④ **keep the original's good sentences unchanged in the spoken dialogue and alter only the aria text**.

### 6. Selected Passages (I): What They Demonstrate

**Passage 1 — 【前腔】 (Scene 7, 〈闺塾〉; sung by the dan): Using the punishment of a maid to speak her own mind**
```
(Dan:) Your hands are not allowed to touch the swing rope; your feet are not allowed to tread the garden path. (Tie:) Just look at that. (Dan:) Talking back! This wind-inviting mouth — I will sear a scar on it with a joss stick; these flower-summoning eyes — I will stab them blind with an embroidery needle. (Tie:) What use is being blind? (Dan:) I only want you to guard the inkstone, follow the desk, keep company with "the Odes say," attend "the Master said," and make no more mistakes. (Tie:) A mistake or two... (Dan pulls Tie's hair:) How many strands of hair do you have, how many welts on your back? I daresay you are a little afraid of those family rules in my lady's hall.
[Original: 手不许把秋千索拿，脚不许把花园路踏。（贴）则瞧罢。（旦）还嘴，这招风嘴，把香头来绰疤；招花眼，把绣针儿签瞎。（贴）瞎了中甚用？（旦）则要你守砚台，跟书案，伴“诗云”，陪“子曰”，没的争差。（贴）争差些罢。（旦挦贴发介）则问你几丝儿头发，几条背花？敢也怕些些夫人堂上那些家法。]
```
- Demonstration: **Speech inserted within an aria does not break the qupai's metrical pattern** — Chunxiang's interjections "Just look at that," "What use is being blind?", and "A mistake or two..." all fall between sung lines; **the qupai's character count and line pattern are completed entirely by the dan's sung lines**, and the tie's speech takes no metrical slot. This is the qupai system's standard way of handling "one lead singer plus other characters' interjections." When writing, fill the qupai completely first, then stuff the speech into the seams.
- Demonstration: **Saying the opposite**. Every prohibition Liniang utters is precisely what she herself will do the next day (the swing, the garden); the audience hears the subtext — in the qupai system, the "subtext" of `sw-dialogue` is achieved through **parallel prohibitions**.

**Passage 2 — 【锁窗寒】 (Scene 6, 〈怅眺〉; sung by the sheng, with a unison tail): How to write an argument as an aria**
```
The First Emperor soared, the deer ran across the Central Plain; and Zhao Tuo — he leaned against the cliff and held up half the sky. To style himself "the lone one" and "the man of few virtue" was simply what a hero does. He seized rivers and mountains for nothing and threw up palaces by the hundred. But we who have read ten thousand scrolls — do we own half a clod of earth? Half the landscape cannot even be seen from here. (Unison:) Let it be as heaven wills; to invoke the present and mourn the past is all in vain — a ruined terrace, an old tree, cold mist.
[Original: 祖龙飞、鹿走中原，尉佗呵，他倚定着摩崖半壁天。称孤道寡，是他英雄本然。白占了江山，猛起些宫殿。似吾侪读尽万卷书，可有半块土么？那半部上山河不见。（合）由天，那攀今吊古也徒然，荒台古树寒烟。]
```
- Demonstration: **Lines of uneven length** (three, seven, four, six, five, seven...), completely different from the banqiang system's paired seven- or ten-character lines; **a line of colloquial unmeasured speech can be inserted directly into the aria** ("do we own half a clod of earth?") without breaking the pattern — this is exactly the dividing line between the qupai system's "tune-filling" (填词, tiáncí) and the banqiang system's "paired lines" (上下句, shàngxiàjù).
- Demonstration: **The unison tail** — three lines sung by the company turn one man's grievance into a public emotion onstage.

**Passage 3 — 【前腔】 (Scene 8, 〈劝农〉; sung by a mulberry picker): Repeating the same tune across a crowd scene**
```
Beneath the mulberry shade, twisting a willow basket, I reach back and clip a branch. Oh — what official is this here? I, Luo Fu, have a home of my own; even if Qiu Hu came, how would he know me, lifting gold and dismounting? ... (Unison:) The magistrate is drunk on flowing clouds, laughing in the wind with flowers in his hair — the mulberry picker is utterly splendid.
[Original: 那桑阴下，柳篓儿搓，顺手腰身翦一丫。呀，什么官员在此？俺罗敷自有家，便秋胡怎认他，提金下马？……（合）官里醉流霞，风前笑插花，采桑人俊煞。]
```
- Demonstration: **Four groups of characters share one 【孝白歌】 plus three 【前腔】**, each group beginning with lines of three, three, and seven characters, inserting the same fixed line of unmeasured speech — "Oh — what official is this here?" — while the unison tail changes only its last three characters (the farmer / village boy / mulberry picker / tea picker "is utterly splendid"). **A transitional crowd scene does not need new qupai; repeating a tune and changing the words of the unison tail is enough to fill a whole scene.**

### 3. Selected Passages (II)

**Passage 4 — 【皂罗袍】 (Scene 10, 〈惊梦〉; sung by the dan, company in unison): Landscape language is emotional language**
```
So the purple and crimson bloom everywhere, and all of it is given over to broken wells and crumbling walls. Fine days and beautiful scenery — what can one do with heaven? Pleasant heart and joyful things — in whose courtyard are they? (Such scenery — my lord and my lady never even mention it.) (Unison:) Morning clouds fly, evening mist rolls; rosy clouds and emerald pavilions; threads of rain, flakes of wind; misty waves and painted boats — the one behind the brocade screen thinks this glorious light too cheap!
[Original: 原来姹紫嫣红开遍，似这般都付与断井颓垣。良辰美景奈何天，赏心乐事谁家院！（恁般景致，我老爷和奶奶再不提起。）（合）朝飞暮卷，云霞翠轩；雨丝风片，烟波画船——锦屏人忒看的这韶光贱！]
```
- Demonstration: **How to fit the qupai's metre** — the first four lines of 【皂罗袍】 are full seven-character lines (seven, seven, seven, seven); the second half turns to four-character parallel pairs (morning clouds fly / evening mist rolls / rosy clouds / emerald pavilions / threads of rain / flakes of wind / misty waves / painted boats), closing with one long line. Tang Xianzu arranges **the four four-character lines into two tightly matched couplets**, landing exactly on the tune's rhythm of moving from whole lines to fragments and from fragments back to release.
- Demonstration: **Speech inserted in the aria does not break the pattern**. "Such scenery — my lord and my lady never even mention it" is unmeasured speech wedged between the sung lines and the unison tail, taking no metrical slot; the line pulls the landscape language back into the family conflict.
- Demonstration: **The unison tail reverses the meaning**. The first half describes scenery; in the unison line "the one behind the brocade screen thinks this glorious light too cheap," sung by the company, the landscape becomes a verdict on her own condition — **the unison tail is not repetition, it is conclusion.**

**Passage 5 — Two 【山桃红】 arias (Scene 10; sheng and dan in duet): How to divide a duet and use the unison tail**
```
(Sheng:) All for you — a beauty like a flower, years that flow like water; I have searched idly everywhere, and you sit pitying yourself in the secluded boudoir. Miss, let us go over there and talk. (Dan smiles and does not move.) (Sheng takes her sleeve.) (Dan asks softly:) Go where? (Sheng:) Past this peony railing, close beside the lake-and-mountain rock. (Dan asks softly:) Scholar, what for? (Sheng answers softly:) To loosen your collar and widen your clothes with you, sleeve-tips dabbing and teeth gently biting — only let me endure and be tender for one spell of sleep. (Dan looks shy.) (Sheng embraces her.) (Dan pushes him away.) (Unison:) Where have we met before? We look at each other so gravely — how could we meet in such a lovely place and say not one word?
[Original: （生）则为你如花美眷，似水流年，是答儿闲寻遍，在幽闺自怜。小姐，和你那答儿讲话去。（旦作含笑不行）（生作牵衣介）（旦低问）那边去？（生）转过这芍药栏前，紧靠着湖山石边。（旦低问）秀才，去怎的？（生低答）和你把领扣松，衣带宽，袖梢儿揾着牙儿苫也，则待你忍耐温存一晌眠。（旦作羞）（生前抱）（旦推介）（合）是那处曾相见，相看俨然，早难道这好处相逢无一言？]
```
- Demonstration: **The dan does not sing, only speaks**. All the sung lines of 【山桃红】 belong to the sheng; the dan has only two soft questions — "Go where?" and "What for?" — and four stage actions (科介, kējiè); only at **the unison tail do the two sing together**. This is the qupai system's fixed method for handling love between man and woman — **the woman's silence is carried by stage action, and when she finally opens her mouth it is in unison**, preserving her status while completing the duet.
- Demonstration: **The same qupai is used twice with a 【鲍老催】 between them**, and the second unison tail is unchanged word for word. The repeated unison tail = the boundary of the dream, and a sign the audience can recognize.

**Passage 6 — 【江儿水】 (Scene 12, 〈寻梦〉; sung by the dan): The play's value turning point in one sentence**
```
By chance my heart clings here, beside the plum tree. If flowers and grasses may be loved as one pleases, if life and death may follow one's own will, then however bitter and wretched, I would have no complaint. I shall gather my fragrant soul into one piece, and on a rainy plum-season day, keep watch until we meet at the plum root.
[Original: 偶然间心似缱，梅树边。这般花花草草由人恋，生生死死随人愿，便酸酸楚楚无人怨。待打并香魂一片，阴雨梅天，守的个梅根相见。]
```
- Demonstration: **Three parallel lines of doubled characters** (花花草草 / 生生死死 / 酸酸楚楚) land on three lines of equal length that the qupai already provides — **the metre's three equal-length lines are exactly what the parallelism needs**; the banqiang system does this with inserted short lines (垛句, duǒjù), while the qupai system relies on "the tune already having three equal-length lines."
- These three lines are the intellectual core of *The Peony Pavilion* and the same meaning in another form as the "Inscription" (〈题词〉): "the living may die, and the dead may live again" (「生者可以死，死可以生」) — **the inscription argues, the aria feels; the same idea is written twice, once outside the play and once inside it.**

**Passage 7 — 【雁过声】 (Scene 14, 〈写真〉; sung by the dan): Breaking one action into a whole aria**
```
(Looks in the mirror and sighs.) Lay out the light silk and wipe the mirror clean. With the flower-tip of the brush I sweep lightly and trace faintly. O my shadow, let me appraise you in detail: your cheeks are so full of mischief; I have only to dot the cherry lips, dye the willow brows, and wash the cloudy hair so it floats in mist; the green of the brow-tips is not yet done — the whole person lives in the wonder of the autumn waves of the eyes, just as the pale spring hills and the tiny kingfisher ornaments should be.
[Original: （照镜叹介）轻绡，把镜儿擘掠。笔花尖淡扫轻描。影儿呵，和你细评度：你腮斗儿恁喜谑，则待注樱桃，染柳条，渲云鬟烟霭飘萧；眉梢青未了，个中人全在秋波妙，可可的淡春山钿翠小。]
```
- Demonstration: **The aria carries the action**. This aria moves through laying out the silk, setting the brush, facing the mirror, dotting the lips, painting the brows, washing the hair, and touching in the eyes; when the song ends, the painting is finished — in the qupai system, "singing" can be "doing," and no separate action description is needed. When writing, divide the action according to the qupai's short lines: one line, one brushstroke.
- Demonstration: **The self as object**. Liniang addresses the reflection in the mirror as "you" ("your cheeks" / "let me appraise you"), splitting herself into painter and painted, two people — this is how a one-person singing scene manufactures an "opponent."

**Passage 8 — The poem on the portrait and what it does (Scene 14)**
```
Seen close, she is distinct and dignified; seen from afar, she is at ease, like a flying immortal. Some year, if I may stand beside the guest of the Toad Palace, it will be not by the plum but by the willow.
[Original: 近睹分明似俨然，远观自在若飞仙。他年得傍蟾宫客，不在梅边在柳边。]
```
- Demonstration: **A twenty-eight-character poem used as the play's master mechanism**. It is picked up by Liu in 〈拾画〉, read aloud by him in 〈玩真〉, used to verify identity in 〈幽媾〉, and treated as material evidence in 〈圆驾〉. **The qupai-system chuanqi's "prop chain" is often one object plus one poem**, which can travel through more scenes than a mere object.

### Selected Passages (III), 9 and 10

**Passage 9 — 【集贤宾】 + 【尾声】 (Scene 20, 〈闹殇〉; sung by the dan): How to write a person's last night**
```
【集贤宾】Sea and sky stretch far; I ask where the ice toad rises from. The jade pestle stands in the autumn void — who will steal the elixir and serve Chang'e? What west wind blows my dream away without a trace! Once gone, one is hard to meet again; it must not be gods stirring or ghosts playing. On the brow's peak, deep in the heart, there is a different kind of pain.
...
【Coda】I fear the fifth-watch wind that leaves no treetop or tree root untouched, and the heartbreak stele set beside my little grave. (Father, tonight is Mid-Autumn.) (Wai:) It is Mid-Autumn, my child. (Dan:) If only this night's rain would stop. (Sighs:) How can the moon set and be reborn, the lamp be red again!
[Original: 【集贤宾】海天悠、问冰蟾何处涌？玉杵秋空，凭谁窃药把嫦娥奉？甚西风吹梦无踪！人去难逢，须不是神挑鬼弄。在眉峰，心坎里别是一般疼痛。……【尾声】怕树头树底不到的五更风，和俺小坟边立断肠碑一统。（爹，今夜是中秋。）（外）是中秋也，儿。（旦）禁了这一夜雨。（叹介）怎能够月落重生灯再红！]
```
- Demonstration: **Two very short lines of unmeasured speech are inserted into the coda** ("Father, tonight is Mid-Autumn." "It is Mid-Autumn, my child."), cutting a single coda into two halves. **The qupai system's coda can be interrupted**, and the interruption falls exactly on the most painful point.
- Demonstration: **The dying line foreshadows the whole second half**. "How can the moon set and be reborn, the lamp be red again!" is both Liniang's despairing question and a literal preview of 〈回生〉 — **one sentence closes the first half and opens the second at the same time.**

**Passage 10 — 【红衲袄】 (Scene 20; sung by the tie): Parallel lines as an elegy**
```
Young lady, never again will you call me to burn the heart-shaped incense at the collar, never again will you call me to trim the flower lamp and drain its red tears, never again will you call me to hold a flower and, with a sidelong glance, tune the songbird, never again will you call me to turn the mirror and shift my shoulder while you dot the vermilion peach of your lips. I think of you late at night putting down the scissors, at clear dawn approaching the sketch. As for that spring portrait — the master saw it, and fearing my lady would be heartbroken, he ordered it buried with you. I think of the young lady's dying words: still leaning on the lake-and-mountain rock, she feared she would wait until a flower-gathering visitor came and the painting's powder faded.
[Original: 小姐，再不叫咱把领头香心字烧，再不叫咱把剔花灯红泪缴，再不叫咱拈花侧眼调歌鸟，再不叫咱转镜移肩和你点绛桃。想着你夜深深放剪刀，晓清清临画藁。提起那春容，被老爷看见了，怕奶奶伤情，分付殉了葬罢。俺想小姐临终之言，依旧向湖山石儿靠也，怕等得个拾翠人来把画粉销。]
```
- Demonstration: **Four neatly parallel lines of "never again will you call me" + seven characters**, landing on the four equal-length lines that 【红衲袄】 already possesses; this is how the qupai system does "stacking" — **instead of adding inserted short lines, choose a qupai that already has a run of equal-length lines.**
- Demonstration: **The aria explains where a key prop has gone** (the portrait was to be buried with her, but Chunxiang secretly hid it beneath the Lake Tai rock); the narrative task is hidden inside the elegy, with no separate spoken passage.

### 7. Play-Wide Summary

- **Scene count**: 55 scenes. **Total qupai: about 420** (including 【前腔】 repeats and composite tunes). Longest scenes: 〈寻梦〉 20, 〈幽媾〉 20, 〈冥誓〉 20; shortest: 〈慈戒〉 2, 〈诇药〉 3, 〈虏谍〉 3.
- **Scenes with Northern tunes**: 〈虏谍〉, 〈牝贼〉 (opening tune), 〈冥判〉 (whole Northern Xianlü suite), 〈硬拷〉 (Northern Shuangdiao), 〈榜下〉 (opening tune), 〈圆驾〉 (Huangzhong North-South composite suite). **Anything involving a foreign king, bandits, a ghost judge, a courtroom, or the imperial court uses Northern tunes.**
- **Exit poems**: **each scene ends with four Tang-assembled lines, with the Tang poet's name in small characters beneath each line** (scene 1 is the exception, using a four-line plot outline; scene 55 uses eight lines). They may be recited by one actor, divided among roles, or carry a "unison" tag.
- **Entrance poems**: self-composed ci tunes (〔汉宫春〕〔鹧鸪天〕〔乌夜啼〕〔醉桃源〕〔行香子〕〔画堂春〕〔昭君怨〕〔更漏子〕〔诉衷情〕〔玉楼春〕〔浣溪沙〕〔苏幕遮〕〔清平乐〕〔相思令〕〔添字昭君怨〕) or "〔集唐〕" (scenes 17, 24, 25, 27, 30, 32, 37, 41, 46, 49, 53, 55, etc.).
- **Conventions**: self-introduction (scenes 2, 3, 4, 6, 9, 13, 15, 17, 19, 21, 29, 33, 40, 49), the scene-setting poem (定场诗, dìngchǎngshī), the exit poem, the aside (背供, bèigōng; 背介, bèijiè / 背想, bèixiǎng), the holding scene (吊场, diàochǎng), the implied exit (虚下, xūxià), the offstage cue (内介, nèijiè), the sudden face-to-face encounter (打照面, dǎ zhàomiàn), scattering flowers at the ghost gate, the three-calls-three-answers ghost test, dotting the spirit tablet (点主, diǎnzhǔ), and the sentencing with kneeling confession (发落跪供, fāluò guìgòng).
- **Composite tunes** (names containing "犯" or joined names): 【九回肠】(解三酲 + 三学士 + 急三枪), 【刷子序犯】【朱奴儿犯】【山桃犯】【尾犯序】【金落索】【金索挂梧桐】【二犯梧桐树】【二犯幺令】【二犯江儿水】【桂花锁南枝】【莺皂袍】.

## §8 Transferable Techniques (Full Text of Examples, Self-Checks, and Failure Conditions; the Complete Version of Main Section 2.5)

1. **For crowd scenes and transitions, use "repeat the same tune + change the words of the unison tail"; do not create a new qupai for each group.** [〈劝农〉: one 【孝白歌】 plus three 【前腔】; the farmer, herd boy, mulberry picker, and tea picker enter in turn, sing, receive rewards, and exit; after each group sings, Du Bao recites a seven-character quatrain as commentary, sticks a flower in their hair, and rewards them with wine; **the three-line unison tail changes only its last three characters** ("the so-and-so is utterly splendid").] **What happens if you don't**: the scene's length runs away and the audience cannot remember it.
2. **For several people reacting in one scene, use "one tune, four Qianqiang stanzas, plus a shared unison tail," one emotion per person, with no repetition.** [〈遇母〉, four 【番山虎】 arias: the mother mourns / the daughter resents / the nun is ashamed / the maid is jealous.] **Self-check**: reduce the content of each of the four arias to a single word; if two are synonyms, cut one aria.
3. **The number of arias in a solo scene = the number of layers in the protagonist's understanding of the same object; it is not determined by duration.** [〈玩真〉, eight arias = suspects Guanyin / suspects Chang'e / decides it is a self-portrait / reads the poem and identifies the name / looks closely / matches the rhyme / calls out / accepts her fate.] **Self-check**: if two arias say the same layer of meaning, cut one. **When it fails**: if there is another character onstage who can take up the song, switch to divided singing (in 〈闺塾〉, dan and tie divide 【绕池游】).
4. **Cut major information into four slices, insert a question from the other party between each slice, and give a piece of stage business to carry each turn.** [〈冥誓〉, 【啄木犯】: who is your father → "trim the lamp" → name and age → admits she is a ghost.] **What happens if you don't**: the opponent becomes a listener, the audience learns the conclusion early, and the stage business has nowhere to go.
5. **Write the same space twice: once when the event happens, once after it is over.** [〈惊梦〉, "so the purple and crimson bloom everywhere" / 〈拾画〉, "through broken mist I see the water pavilion ruined" — do not write the passage of time; write two descriptions of the same place.] **When it fails**: if there is no time gap or character change between the two, the second is merely repetition.
6. **If you want a line to echo across twenty scenes, use a ready-made line the audience already knows.** [In scene 15 Wanyan Liang sings Liu Yong's "three autumns of cassia buds, ten miles of lotus" (「三秋桂子，十里荷花」) together with his own "I would rein my horse on Mount Wu" (「立马吴山」), and in scene 41 Liu Mengmei picks it up verbatim in his palace-examination paper, **26 scenes later**.] **Self-check**: does the line still stand outside this play? A self-made sentence cannot do this; beyond ten scenes, no one remembers it.
7. **The four moves for adapting an existing story** (compare the source tale 《杜丽娘慕色还魂》, full text in appendix 2 of the annotated edition and §5 of this file): ① **delete relatives** to isolate the protagonist (remove the younger brother; "no son" becomes the psychological motive force of Du Bao); ② **turn the legal into the illegal** (the exhumation is changed from an official proceeding presided over by Prefect Liu to a private dig by Liu Mengmei, violating the Great Ming Code's "whoever opens a coffin and sees the corpse, whether principal or accessory, shall be beheaded"); ③ change "love at first sight" into "a dream tryst plus a second seeking of the dream"; ④ **keep the original's good sentences unchanged in the spoken dialogue and alter only the aria text** (the poem on the portrait changes by one character: "not by the plum, then by the willow" → "**not by the plum, but by the willow**"). **Self-check**: ask of each move, "Did this change create a new opponent or a new legal risk?"; mere verbal polishing without structural change is not adaptation. See qupai section 9 and `sw-format-adaptation`.

**Where the masterpiece breaks the rules**: too many padding characters (衬字, chènzì) to the point of being unpunctuatable; 〈冥誓〉 leaves its mode and offends the key (出宫犯调, chūgōng fàndiào); the staging (排场, páichǎng) of all Four Dreams (四梦, sì mèng — Tang Xianzu's four dream plays) is insufficiently considered (Wu Mei 吴梅 and Wang Jilie 王季烈 both criticize it; 《紫钗》 [The Purple Hairpin] is the least satisfactory, "truly cannot be performed"); level, rising, and departing rhymes are mixed with the entering tone (平上去入, píng shǎng qù rù — the four tones of Middle Chinese) (Wang Jilie: "by no means to be taken as a model"). The response in one sentence: "**Learn its writing, but do not follow its method.**" See qupai section 11.
