# reference-zaju.md — Three Yuan Zaju Plays (元杂剧, Yuán zájù) by 关汉卿 (Guan Hanqing): 《窦娥冤》 (The Injustice to Dou E), 《救风尘》 (Rescuing a Courtesan), and 《金线池》 (The Golden Thread Pool)

> **What this file covers**: act-by-act structural tables for three Yuan zaju plays, a complete table of 126 qupai (曲牌, qǔpái — fixed tune models), an act-by-act comparison with the formal patterns of Wang Jilie (王季烈) for northern suites, a rhyme audit, a combined table across the three plays, the full text of the conventions, and representative aria passages.
> **Text and collation status**: the Wikisource text of the *Yuanqu xuan* (元曲选, *Anthology of Yuan Drama*) edition (captured 2026-09-12; no base edition stated; the textual features match the system of Zang Jinshu (臧晋叔) in 《元曲选》); **quotations come from the original text and are not marked as awaiting collation**; the analyst's inferences are marked 〔按〕 (àn — analyst's note).
> **Which section of SKILL.md it corresponds to**: Section 1 (1.1–1.5). Main text 1.2 → §1, §3, §4; 1.3 → §2; 1.4 → §5, §6; 1.5 → §7.

- **The play has 41 arias, 2,695 characters of aria text and 8,299 of spoken dialogue (宾白, bīnbái — the spoken passages of opera), an aria-to-speech ratio of 1:3.1.** 〔按〕This is a ratio of "speech as the bone, aria as the flesh," the reverse of chuanqi (传奇, chuánqí — the long Ming–Qing opera form; see §5).
- **Test of single-role singing (一人主唱, yīrén zhǔchàng — the rule that one role sings an entire act)**: Acts 1–4 are **sung entirely by the lead female; not one aria is given to another role**; in Act 4 the lead female changes costume to play the "ghost female" (Dou E's ghost), but it is still the same role and the same character, so the one-singer rule is not broken. **The sole exception is the prologue: 〔仙呂・賞花時〕 is sung by chōngmò Dou Tianzhang** (the lyric "I too, with no way to make a living and four bare walls, therefore had the heart to cut my own child and leave her in two places" is a father's voice and cannot be Duanyun's). → **The accurate statement of 一人主唱 is: "in the four acts one person sings; the prologue is not subject to this rule."**

- **The play has 44 arias, 2,445 characters of aria text and 4,302 of spoken dialogue, ratio 1:1.8.** Compare Dou E's 1:3.1—**same author, same system, yet the comedy's aria-to-speech ratio is almost twice as high, and it is very even across the four acts (1.6 / 1.7 / 1.7 / 2.3)**, with no lyrical peak like Act 3 of Dou E. 〔按〕This is the structural consequence of a "scheme play": the whole play moves by confrontation scenes, so lyricism has nowhere to pile up.
- **Test of single-role singing**: all four acts are sung by the lead female Zhao Pan'er, **all 44 arias without exception**; the play **has no prologue**, so it does not even have Dou E's one exception—**it is the cleanest specimen of single-role singing**.
- **The singer here is not the protagonist's opponent but "the person who comes to solve the problem."** In Act 1 Zhao Pan'er is only an outsider giving advice; Song Yinzhang (secondary female) is the party concerned. Guan Hanqing gives all the singing to Zhao Pan'er, which amounts to **using the right to sing to designate who the protagonist is**. 〔按〕This is crucial for casting zaju: whoever sings owns the play, and the title "Zhao Pan'er Rescues a Courtesan" is precisely a proclamation of that right.

- **The play has 41 arias, 2,868 characters of aria text and 5,012 of spoken dialogue, ratio 1:1.7** (Dou E 1:3.1; Rescuing a Courtesan 1:1.8).
- **Test of single-role singing**: prologue and four acts, **all 41 arias belong to the lead female Du Ruiniang**; the supporting male (外, wài — supporting male role; Prefect Shi), the male (末, mò — male role; Han Fuchen), the chádàn (the old woman), the maid (梅香, méixiāng — maid role), and the three secondary females — Aunt Zhang (张嬷嬷), Aunt Li (李妗妗), and Sister-in-law Min (闵大嫂) — **do not sing a single aria**. **This is the only one of the three plays in which even the prologue belongs to the lead female**—the exact opposite of Dou E, whose prologue belongs to the chōngmò. Put the two side by side and the rule is fixed: "**the prologue's singer is variable; the four acts' singer is fixed**."

## §2 Qupai (曲牌, qǔpái — fixed tune models) table (126 entries: act / qupai / opening line–closing line / what it carries)

### 2. Qupai table (act / qupai / opening line–closing line / what it carries)

**Prologue · Xianlü**
1. 〔賞花時 (Shǎnghuāshí)〕 "I too, with no way to make a living and four bare walls" – "and so I end up wordless, my soul darkened"｜The seller's self-justification: it calls the sale "no way out," and one aria carries an entire scene.

**Act 1 · Xianlü mode** (〔按〕the whole act has no 〔幺篇〕)
1. 〔點絳唇 (Diǎnjiàngchún)〕 "A belly full of idle sorrow, endured for years" – "I fear I shall grow thin even as heaven does"｜Sets the emotional ground bass of the play; short, the opening aria.
2. 〔混江龍 (Hùnjiānglóng)〕 "I only ask about dusk and daylight" – "the more I feel my feelings tangled and my heart restless"｜Unfolds the sorrow of widowhood in long lines.
3. 〔油葫蘆 (Yóuhúlú)〕 "Is it that my eight characters are fated to carry a lifetime of sorrow" – "who indeed asks after me, who cares?"｜**Self-narration of her life**: mother dead at three, father gone at seven, married at seventeen, widowed; it folds the thirteen years from the prologue to now into one aria.
4. 〔天下樂 (Tiānxiàlè)〕 "Is it that in a former life I burned incense without reaching the end" – "my words must be answered"｜Resolve: support her mother-in-law, keep mourning, make every word come true—the foreshadowing of the three vows.
5. 〔一半兒 (Yībànr)〕 "Why do tears stream down without stopping" – "I see him half lingering, half ugly"｜**Speech inside the aria** (the old woman interjects twice); the last line fixes the pattern "half... half..."
6. 〔後庭花 (Hòutínghuā)〕 "At the time I grieve for you" – "in vain I make people laugh their heads off"｜Urges her mother-in-law not to remarry, arguing from age.
7. 〔青哥兒 (Qīnggēr)〕 "Though you were saved by him, saved by him" – "Father-in-law, you are left to toil for nothing"｜Argues from the property the dead father-in-law built up; repeated words "saved by him, saved by him," "bamboo strip, bamboo strip."
8. 〔寄生草 (Jìshēngcǎo)〕 "You say he is hastily happy; I on the contrary worry for you in detail" – "I say this marriage will likely fall behind others"｜**Three "worry is..." parallel clauses** (the wine of union / the love-knot / the lotus cushion), the most polished aria in the act.
9. 〔賺煞 (Zhuànshā)〕 "I think these women should never trust men's mouths" – "is this not the end of a woman without a husband?"｜Coda; Zhang Lü'er drags her to bow and pushes her down; **the aria is cut into three segments by stage business (科介, kējiè — the action cues written into the script)**.

**Act 2 · Nanlü mode**
1. 〔一枝花 (Yīzhīhuā)〕 "He wants only to sleep a lifetime in the love nest" – "using the tricks of a swindler who fishes for dragons"｜Uses "women" to curse her mother-in-law without naming her.
2. 〔梁州第七 (Liángzhōu Dìqī)〕 "This one is like Zhuo Wenjun tending the wine shop and washing vessels" – "still less can one say her nature is hard to change"｜Five allusions—Zhuo Wenjun, Meng Guang, Meng Jiangnü, Xi Shi, the Waiting-for-Husband Rock—in positive and negative parallel.
3. 〔**隔尾** (Géwěi)〕 "You say it lacks salt and vinegar and has no flavor" – "to have a body safe and sound is a great joy"｜**隔尾 is not a coda but a sectional tune within the suite**: here it closes the "kitchen" section and turns to "poisoning."
4. 〔賀新郎 (Hèxīnláng)〕 "One says you eat first, one says Mother eats first" – "who would be willing to send winter clothes a thousand li?"｜Makes drama out of the single action of "offering the soup."
5. 〔斗蝦䗫 (Dòuxiámá)〕 "In vain I grieve; I cannot understand; human life and death are reincarnation" – "then thus sigh and complain, weep and wail"｜**The longest aria in the play**, with more than twenty runs of four-character lines, discussing life and death, coffins and burial, and strangers.
6. 〔**隔尾**〕 "This fellow stirs my old mother to take you in" – "yet you would have me marry another; that truly I cannot do"｜Second section: closes "at home" and turns to "the courtroom"; inside the aria Zhang Lü'er calls the neighbors, and a whole spoken passage on "settle in court or settle in private" is inserted.
7. 〔牧羊關 (Mùyángguān)〕 "Your Honor is bright as a mirror and clear as water" – "yet you would have me say what, for no reason at all?"｜Statement in court: flattery first, then self-defense.
8. 〔罵玉郎 (Mà Yùláng)〕 "These heartless rods—I cannot bear them" – "all of you take me as a local precedent"｜First segment of torture.
9. 〔感皇恩 (Gǎnhuáng'ēn)〕 "Oh! Who is that shouting so wildly" – "one stroke, one line of blood, one layer of skin"｜Second segment, short three-character lines rushing down.
10. 〔採茶歌 (Cǎichágē)〕 "You beat me till flesh flies and blood runs" – "how is it that the overturned basin does not reflect the sunlight?"｜Third segment. **〔罵玉郎〕, 〔感皇恩〕, and 〔採茶歌〕 are a fixed three-aria group in the Nanlü suite**, fitting exactly the three beatings with the "three sprinklings of water" stage business.
11. 〔**黃鐘尾** (Huángzhōngwěi)〕 "I have become a wronged ghost with no head" – "if I do not die, how can I save you?"｜**The Nanlü suite borrows the Huangzhong coda to close**; one question closes the whole act.

**Act 3 · Zhenggong mode**
1. 〔端正好 (Duānzhènghǎo)〕 "For no reason I broke the king's law" – "how can I not pour out my complaint against heaven and earth?"｜Opens with five lines; "cry out my wrong and shake earth and heaven" states the theme "moves heaven and earth."
2. 〔**滾繡球** (Gǔnxiùqiú)〕 "There are sun and moon hanging morning and evening" – "alas, I am left with two streams of tears"｜**The most famous passage in the play**. It first establishes the principle of "sun, moon, ghosts, and gods," then overturns the case with Dao Zhi and Yan Yuan; the last two lines—"Earth, if you do not distinguish good from bad, what are you? Heaven, if you misjudge the worthy and the foolish, you are heaven for nothing!"—turn invocation into accusation.
3. 〔倘秀才 (Tǎngxiùcái)〕 "Only this cangue twists me left and right" – "do not refuse because the road is far"｜She asks to take the back street; the executioner's interjection is set inside the aria.
4. 〔叨叨令 (Dāodāolìng)〕 "Pity me, alone with no kin" – "I beg you, brother, at the point of death it is kind to give a person ease"｜**The fixed "yemoge" refrain** ("in vain you angered him to death, yemoge," twice); three executioner interjections cut the aria into four segments—a textbook passage of "aria and speech alternating."
5. 〔快活三 (Kuàihuósān)〕 "Think of Dou E, muddled into crime" – "only look on Dou E, a young thing with no mother's face"｜Four lines beginning "Think of Dou E."
6. 〔鮑老兒 (Bàolǎor)〕 "Think of Dou E serving her mother-in-law these years" – "neither clear nor dark, wronged and bearing injustice"｜Entrusts the offerings; the old woman's weeping speech is inserted.
7. 〔耍孩兒 (Shuǎháir)〕 "It is not I, Dou E, who lays down such a headless vow" – "this is Chang Hong turning to jade, the Emperor Wang crying as a cuckoo"｜**Vow one**: blood flies onto white silk.
8. 〔二煞 (Èrshà)〕 "You say it is hot weather, not the time for snow" – "and send me out to the ancient road and desolate field?"｜**Vow two**: snow in June, using the Zou Yan allusion.
9. 〔一煞 (Yīshà)〕 "You say heaven's lord cannot be relied on" – "so that the common people have mouths but cannot speak"｜**Vow three**: three years of drought, using the filial widow of the Eastern Sea; the last two lines turn the wrong toward "the officials have no heart to uphold the law."
10. 〔煞尾 (Shàwěi)〕 "The floating clouds darken for me, the sad wind whirls for me" – "then only will you, Dou E, wronged to death, show yourself"｜Closes the three vows; weeping speech inserted. **〔二煞〕, 〔一煞〕, and 〔煞尾〕 close in descending order—the standard "sha" descent of northern drama.**

**Act 4 · Shuangdiao mode** (the lead female plays the ghost female)
1. 〔新水令 (Xīnshuǐlìng)〕 "Every day I weep and keep watch at the Home-Viewing Terrace" – "urging the ghost to hurry"｜Six reduplicative adverbs (anxious, slow, scurrying) describe ghost movement.
2. 〔沉醉東風 (Chénzuìdōngfēng)〕 "I am the daughter of a judge" – "how shall I escape the boundless sea of bitterness!"｜The door gods will not let her in; first establishes the obstacle "she cannot enter."
3. 〔喬牌兒 (Qiáopáir)〕 "I only see him guess wildly in his doubt" – "first receive this bow from your child Dou E"｜Father raises his sword, daughter bows: two actions in one aria.
4. 〔雁兒落 (Yàn'érluò)〕 "You look at this file and ask whether it says so or not" – "instead you wreck my remaining life"｜Two pairs: "I would not... yet you / yet you make me."
5. 〔得勝令 (Déshènglìng)〕 "Oh, today I lean on the soul-summoning terrace" – "and still say revenge is not satisfying enough"｜Asks her father to use the power of the law.
6. 〔川撥棹 (Chuānbōzhào)〕 "Suddenly I see you, you beatable wretch" – "how would you have me bear the guilt for you?"｜Questions Zhang Lü'er in court.
7. 〔七弟兄 (Qīdìxiōng)〕 "You only wanted to repudiate a debt, play clever, and court disaster" – "today the official is gone but the yamen remains"｜Questions Doctor Sai; **〔帶云〕 inserted inside the aria**.
8. 〔梅花酒 (Méihuājiǔ)〕 "You say I am not in the right" – "my vows are indeed great"｜Recounts the three vows, intertextual with Act 3.
9. 〔收江南 (Shōujiāngnán)〕 "Oh, this is the yamen that since ancient times opens south" – "only endless flowing grief like the long Huai River"｜"The yamen since ancient times opens south, and among them there is none without injustice"—the second most famous line in the play.
10. 〔鴛鴦煞尾 (Yuānyāngshàwěi)〕 "From now on I shall set the gold tablets and power swords in order" – "the wronged dead shall have their charges reversed"｜Two spoken passages inserted in the coda (entrusting the orphan, caring for the old woman); **sing—speak—sing—speak within—sing**, five turns, the density limit of a zaju ending.

### 2. Qupai table (act / qupai / opening line–closing line / what it carries)

**Act 1 · Xianlü mode**
1. 〔點絳唇〕 "Courtesans wait on men, seeking money all their lives" – "know how to prize our charm"｜Opening aria, establishes the professional situation.
2. 〔混江龍〕 "I think about this matching of marriages" – "I reckon that men's hearts cannot be asked, but heaven's principle is hard to deceive"｜On the difficulty of marrying; the couplet "with a foot on the headboard, the thing is done early / with a hand beating the chest, regret comes late."
3. 〔油葫蘆〕 "Is the marriage ledger entirely up to me and you?" – "then, eyes wide open, whom can she blame?"｜**Parallel dilemma**: marry an honest man and fear they cannot match; marry a clever one and fear being abandoned halfway.
4. 〔天下樂〕 "I think those who marry first have not been gone a few days" – "even if I sleep alone all my life, what is that to me?"｜Uses women who married first as examples, and arrives at "better to sleep alone."
5. 〔那吒令 (Nàzhàlìng)〕 "If I pretend to be honest and learn the three obediences and four virtues" – "which one is the better bargain?"｜Declares that a courtesan cannot keep the three obediences.
6. 〔鵲踏枝 (Quètàzhī)〕 "I am not putting on an act" – "even if you say a woman is out to cheat one of things"｜**Speech inside the aria** (mimicking a client's tone: "this girl is tapping the steamed bun").
7. 〔寄生草〕 "There are always men who love a prostitute" – "the south end does business while the north end opens shop; the eastbound trade never sees the westbound precedent"｜Closes with two proverbs.
8. 〔村裏迓鼓 (Cūnlǐyàgǔ)〕 "You too should think three times before acting; twice is enough" – "I fear you cannot endure a man's temper"｜**Direct persuasion**, turning to Song Yinzhang.
9. 〔元和令 (Yuánhélìng)〕 "A husband cannot do the work of a client" – "what does he know of human relations!"｜**The play's core thesis**: "A man who is a husband cannot be a client; a man who is a client cannot be a husband"; the secondary female's speech is inserted.
10. 〔上馬嬌 (Shàngmǎjiāo)〕 "I heard the inside story" – "warming my padded clothes by the fire"｜Takes up Song Yinzhang's examples of "fanning her, warming the bed."
11. 〔遊四門 (Yóusìmén)〕 "At the table, he picks the tendons and skin off the spoon" – "women never learn; the more they are infatuated"｜**Repeats each of the other's arguments and dismantles it**, four details one by one.
12. 〔勝葫蘆 (Shènghúlu)〕 "You say this client's affection is sweet as honey" – "he will beat you till you weep"｜Predicts a beating—every word fulfilled in Act 2.
13. 〔**么篇** (yāopiān — a repeat of the same qupai, also written 幺篇)〕 "Then it will be 'too late to mend the leak when the boat is mid-river'" – "get ready to rescue a Waiting-for-Husband Rock"｜The yāopiān to 〔勝葫蘆〕; **"get ready to rescue" announces the whole action of the play**, the key to its structure.
14. 〔賺煞〕 "This little slut is a fox-spirit, a female demon" – "all for three thousand tea licenses, she married Feng Kui"｜Coda, with two 〔帶云〕 insertions, using the Pei Xingnu story from 《青衫泪》 (*Tears on the Blue Gown*) as irony.

**Act 2 · Shangdiao mode**
1. 〔集賢賓 (Jíxiánbīn)〕 "These years I have had a would-be bride's thoughts" – "and to old age I have not turned back"｜Opening aria; two metaphors, "a fish that slipped the net," "a dove that dodged the pellet."
2. 〔逍遙樂 (Xiāoyáolè)〕 "Which one does not drift into completion" – "today every account is settled with one stroke"｜Three "which one does not" parallel clauses.
3. 〔金菊香 (Jīnjúxiāng)〕 "I think how in those days he secretly did the deed" – "still saying there was love and affection, a knot tied"｜Takes up Aunt Li's news: "the words I said for you in those days all come true today."
4. 〔醋葫蘆 (Cùhúlú)〕 "You spread the mandarin-duck quilt and phoenix coverlet" – "are you not ashamed before the sworn brotherhood of the Peach Garden, the white horse and black ox?"｜**Self-questioning** (〔帶云〕 "Zhao Pan'er," 〔sings〕 "you act the one who sees death and does not save"), putting "to save or not to save" on the table.
5. 〔**幺篇**〕 "Which one does not say 'I swear' as he faces death?" – "it is like autumn wind passing the ear, over and done"｜The yāopiān to 〔醋葫蘆〕; four "which one does not" on the uselessness of oaths.
6. 〔**幺篇**〕 "I think how in the beginning, when there was sorrow, we shared it" – "set up a woman's name, and as a ghost I'd still be romantic!"｜Another yāopiān; **weaves Song Yinzhang's exact words from Act 1 ("this one's a big sister, that one's a big sister, and out comes a bag of pus") into the aria as a retort**—a ready example of aria lyrics quoting earlier spoken dialogue.
7. 〔後庭花〕 "I shall write this heart-to-heart letter myself" – "in vain I expose myself to such shame"｜〔She writes, sings〕—**writes and sings at once**; the letter's content is the aria's content.
8. 〔柳葉兒 (Liǔyèr)〕 "I shall teach you how to bear it" – "a twisted body, a special softness"｜Begins to lay out the scheme: makeup, brocade clothes.
9. 〔雙雁兒 (Shuāngyànr)〕 "I use this powdered face to rescue you, a female skeleton" – "how can you escape these mist-and-moon hands of mine!"｜**"A powdered face rescues a female skeleton" is the aria's gloss on the title "风月救风尘" (Wind and Moon Rescue the Wind and Dust; i.e., Rescuing a Courtesan).**
10. 〔浪裏來煞 (Lànglǐláishà)〕 "You pack away the sorrow in your heart" – "is this not a round of wind and moon: I shall make that fellow stop at once"｜Coda, preceded by the whole spoken plan.

**Act 3 · Zhenggong mode**
1. 〔端正好〕 "Only because he is full of sorrow and his heart is pent up" – "I too can force my way into the soul-confusing array"｜"Force my way into the soul-confusing array" states the nature of this act.
2. 〔**滾繡球**〕 "Here I breathe out softly" – "I shall have to spend a little spirit"｜**Three reasons in parallel**: "First, I simply pity a helpless woman with no one to rely on; second, 'being long a traveler, I pity the guest'; third, 'I love the cup and pity the drunk'"—finding motives for her intervention.
3. 〔倘秀才〕 "A county lady is only a county lady" – "how can she withstand his servants' orders, secretly enduring in the dark"｜The difference between the respectable and the lowly.
4. 〔**滾繡球**〕 "A decent family applies powder lightly" – "yet cannot escape the wind and dust"｜**Item-by-item contrast between the decent woman and the courtesan** (powdering, combing, knowing distance), six pairs, the most polished aria in the act.
5. 〔**么篇**〕 "My little sister has seen the world" – "I have worn out my dreams and soul for you"｜The yāopiān to 〔滾繡球〕; **two long speeches by Zhou She inserted inside the aria** (pretending not to know her, then pretending to know her), one aria performing a whole attack-and-defense of "recognize or not."
6. 〔倘秀才〕 "Did I presume on my seniority to play the clever matchmaker?" – "today you marry; would I agree to call it off?"｜A counterattack in question form.
7. 〔脫布衫 (Tuōbùshān)〕 "I am even less one to spare people" – "how is it that you would beat her?"｜After Song Yinzhang bursts in, Zhao Pan'er speaks for her (actually to provoke Zhou She).
8. 〔小梁州 (Xiǎoliángzhōu)〕 "Do they not say one night of marriage is a hundred nights of grace!" – "who would beat a pretty girl to death for a pair of twin uncles?"｜Advises, but actually provokes.
9. 〔**幺篇**〕 "I only see him fierce and glowering, feeling for his heartless rod" – "little sister, just watch me save the wind and dust with wind and moon"｜The yāopiān to 〔小梁州〕 (**〔小梁州〕 always carries a yāopiān**); **marked 〔背唱〕**—"I pretend to deceive, I blow empty business, so this fellow has a home he cannot run to" is spoken to the audience; **the title's five words "wind and moon save the wind and dust" come from this aside**.
10. 〔二煞〕 "What is tight will in the end be tight" – "not in vain to be a young gentleman all one's life"｜Lowers her own price to win trust.
11. 〔**黃鐘尾**〕 "Though you are poor to death, you are content to keep your place and endure poverty" – "I shall make the divorce writ you wrote stand as my capital"｜The Zhenggong suite borrows the Huangzhong coda; the last line is a double entendre ("正了本" = made back the capital / set the book right).

**Act 4 · Shuangdiao mode**
1. 〔新水令〕 "Smiling, I write the divorce writ as if on a chopping board" – "how can I withstand that 'Victory Gourd' running to nine thousand lines"｜Opening aria; she enters smiling.
2. 〔喬牌兒〕 "Wine and mutton are things on the cart; the great red silk I take myself" – "you want to claim a person for nothing"｜**Short three-character lines in succession**, rebutting Zhou She's "you drank my wine / took my sheep / accepted my red betrothal gift" one by one.
3. 〔慶東原 (Qìngdōngyuán)〕 "We are, after all, selling emptiness" – "if one believed these oath words, one would die young and childless!"｜**Summary of the uselessness-of-oaths argument**, echoing Act 2's yāopiān; 〔帶云〕 inserted in the middle.
4. 〔落梅風 (Luòméifēng)〕 "Only because your thinking is too muddled" – "even with nine oxen, you could not drag it out"｜Speech inserted in the aria reveals "what you bit to pieces was the fake divorce writ"; **the play's knot is untied in the middle of this aria**.
5. 〔雁兒落〕 "This fellow is cruel and venomous; this fellow is rich and powerful" – "he does not tread the solid road"｜First accusation in court.
6. 〔得勝令〕 "Song Yinzhang has a husband; he forcibly keeps her as his household" – "the divorce writ is right here; I beg Your Honor to examine it clearly"｜Produces the writ.
7. 〔沽美酒 (Gūměijiǔ)〕 "He studied the classics from childhood" – "clearly she is a good man's wife"｜Testifies for An Xiushi.
8. 〔太平令 (Tàipínglìng)〕 "Here is the matchmaker, fit to be evidence" – "have pity and rule that husband and wife be reunited"｜**Asks for judgment, puts the request in one line**.
9. 〔收尾 (Shōuwěi)〕 "I shall tell Your Honor the whole cause one by one" – "no more talk of death-and-life friendship in the flour-paste basin; at the wind-and-moon house we join the swallow-and-oriole pair again"｜Four lines close the play; the last line returns to the title.

### 2. Qupai table (act / qupai / opening line–closing line / what it carries)

**Prologue · Xianlü**
1. 〔端正好〕 "Zheng Liu met a fox spirit; Cui Tao met a tigress" – "all want to be husband and wife with scholars"｜Uses two Tang tales of "fox spirit and tigress" to mock herself, saying all courtesans want to marry a scholar.
2. 〔**么篇**〕 "If not, that whole tender heart—where can it be entrusted?" – "how could I be willing to follow that tea peddler Feng Kui!"｜**The yāopiān advances the aria one step**: the main aria says "wants to marry a scholar," the yāopiān vows "will not marry the tea peddler Feng Kui"—Act 4's 〔收江南〕 last line "no more weeping as I am helped onto the tea boat" answers exactly this.

**Act 1 · Xianlü mode**
1. 〔點絳唇〕 "In this house of injustice, where is there any trade to run?" – "nothing but 'vicious, mean, clever, poisonous, ruthless'!"｜**〔帶云〕 asks "what are those five words?" then 〔sings〕 the answer**, question and answer inside one aria.
2. 〔混江龍〕 "The penniless want intimacy—only when the donkey grows a halberd and the jar grows roots" – "mostly a plank barrier for a door"｜Lists the harms of the brothel (pampering the father, harming the mother, freezing the wife, starving the child, pawning the house, selling the land).
3. 〔油葫蘆〕 "The god who presides over burning and burying on the kang (a heated brick bed)" – "driven out the door in three nights"｜**Famous passage cursing the bawd**: "she holds a string of prayer beads—that is the demon-subduing seal to frighten clients; she swings a staff—that is the heartless rod to beat the mandarin ducks"—turning beads and staff, two good things, into weapons.
4. 〔天下樂〕 "He wants to keep someone every night and have someone new every night" – "to decide on my beloved"｜Exposes the bawd's business logic.
5. 〔醉扶歸 (Zuìfúguī)〕 "There is a word I must earnestly tell you, elder" – "when you are old, no one will ask for you"｜Pleads for marriage, arguing from age.
6. 〔金盞兒 (Jīnzhǎnr)〕 "You say her nature is simple; I say your mind is rustic" – "rubbing open my tearful eyes, rousing my old spirits"｜**Parallel opening "you say... I say..."**; a 〔帶云〕 inserts a passage imitating a client haggling over travel money.
7. 〔醉中天 (Zuìzhōngtiān)〕 "It is not that I am especially angry; it is that you are not concerned" – "do they not say one dies old in the wind and dust?"｜Answers the old woman's spoken line "you are only just past twenty this year."
8. 〔寄生草〕 "I take leave of the Mingke Alley (the courtesan quarter); I shall marry that Han Fuchen" – "do not think this wild flower can be driven back into the soul-confusing array"｜Three "never again..." parallel lines (soup bottle, frying pan, millstone) swearing by objects to leave the profession.
9. 〔賺煞〕 "Ten times I wished to reform, but nine times out of ten I was not allowed" – "how could he bear to teach me 'when one spring ends, another spring comes'"｜**A descending number sequence embedded in the lines** (ten times—nine times—eight characters—seven-step talent—six relations—five-flower decree of honor—three lives' blessing—pair upon pair of good luck), the most polished aria in the play.

**Act 2 · Nanlü mode**
1. 〔一枝花〕 "The Eastern Ocean cannot wash away the shame on my face" – "still hoping for heaven-long, earth-lasting"｜Four huge-object parallels (Eastern Ocean, Western Hua Mountain, strong ghost, giant spirit god) for shame and sorrow.
2. 〔梁州第七〕 "This fellow has been lazy and scattered, though he has left my sight" – "for what reason!"｜Longest in the act; three openings "handsome brother / bad granny / good sister."
3. 〔牧羊關〕 "I do not see him thinking of the old days; rather there is some duplicity" – "how could I neglect the hand on the strings?"｜**Sings while playing the pipa** (〔lead female plays, sings〕); Han Fuchen's provoking speech "so your old nature has not changed—you still play and sing" is inserted; the last two lines answer "you will not neglect the wine in the cup; how could I neglect the hand on the strings?"
4. 〔罵玉郎〕 "This is Mother deliberately breaking the mandarin-duck pair" – "why trouble your precious feet to come to my house again?"｜Refusal.
5. 〔感皇恩〕 "I am a wild, cheap prostitute; how could I marry a handsome scholar like you?" – "the more it shows your sweet mouth, soft knees, deep feeling"｜**One aria cut into four segments by three of Han Fuchen's interjections**; the last line closes with three "儿" phrases.
6. 〔采花歌 (Cǎihuāgē)〕 "Once I waited on the bedcurtains; now all is paid to the eastward flow" – "already the pleasure tower is pulled down"｜〔罵玉郎〕, 〔感皇恩〕, and 〔采花歌〕 remain the Nanlü three-aria group, the same group as Dou E Act 2.
7. 〔三煞 (Sānshà)〕 "Since you are heartless, do not imagine my nails will touch your skin" – "I am content to be a dove that dodged the pellet"｜First of the sha group.
8. 〔二煞〕 "Where there is a place to hoe, spread out and hoe loosely" – "when sleep comes, then rest"｜Advises him to find another place.
9. 〔尾煞 (Wěishà)〕 "Even a person as high as my three-plank status cannot get a hand out" – "I beg you, sir, to pluck another willow by the Zhangtai road"｜Dismisses the guest; the last line uses the "Zhangtai willow" allusion.

**Act 3 · Zhonglü mode**
1. 〔粉蝶兒 (Fěndié'ér)〕 "I know clearly that scholars are taught by their profession to be fickle and short-lived" – "in a drafty place they play clever"｜Opening aria; admits "formerly I won a thousand battles, a thousand victories."
2. 〔醉春風 (Zuìchūnfēng)〕 "They can look after the pit before their eyes but not guard against the well behind their head" – "the matter is fixed beforehand"｜"When will the stupid cheap woman wake, wake?"—**the fixed reduplication of the Zhonglü 〔醉春風〕**.
3. 〔石榴花 (Shíliúhuā)〕 "It is just like a lotus-fiber thread breaking the mirror-flower brightness" – "in a low room wrestling with a coiled gloomy heart"｜The Golden Thread Pool scene stirs feeling.
4. 〔鬥鵪鶉 (Dòu'ānchún)〕 "I have idly spent the beautiful day and gentle wind" – "he is still away from home"｜Closes with "I am still settled at home; he is still away from home."
5. 〔普天樂 (Pǔtiānlè)〕 "Little sister is Ailian; you all respect me" – "my heart aches"｜**Names the sisters one by one** (Ailian, Cha'er, Yu Bange), then continues singing after the urging-to-drink speech of the dan (旦, dàn — female roles).
6. 〔醉高歌 (Zuìgāogē)〕 "Or sing a few flower names in the tune" – "the proper topic is to join the sheng music at the banquet"｜**One aria cut into four segments by the dan's three "I don't understand"**—the rhythm of the drinking game itself.
7. 〔十二月 (Shí'èryuè)〕 "I think that fellow has people praising him" – "the clever ones in the end are fickle"｜The aria after her first breach of the rule (saying "Han Fuchen"), judging him "insincere."
8. 〔堯民歌 (Yáomíngē)〕 "In Lichun Garden they speak only of a pretty Su Qing" – "first accepted Feng Kui's betrothal"｜Uses the story of Su Xiaoqing, Shuang Jian, and Feng Kui to compare herself; **echoes the prologue yāopiān's "tea peddler Feng Kui" from beginning to end**.
9. 〔上小樓 (Shàngxiǎolóu)〕 "You have left me all alone" – "only when drunk does wine wash out the true nature"｜States "only drunk does she tell the truth," preparing for the drunken fall in the next aria.
10. 〔**么篇**〕 "Not dead in heart, I think of the old love" – "what are the names of the young men who support me?"｜**〔lead female drunkenly falls; the dan support her〕〔Han Fuchen enters, swap cue〕〔the dan exit〕**—the sisters' support is swapped for Han Fuchen's; in her drunkenness she asks "who is supporting me?", is told "Han Fuchen," and at once says "stand back!" **The cleverest mechanism in the play, done entirely with one 〔換科〕.**
11. 〔耍孩兒〕 "For you I have pressed the official order" – "why worry about a brocade-bright future!"｜Asserts her own worth.
12. 〔二煞〕 "I am like a scorpion stinging through a wall—I must bear it myself" – "I rise like a kite with a broken string"｜〔按〕There is one missing character here in the original (Wikisource gives "□"); quotations avoid this line.
13. 〔尾煞〕 "You and I have more than half a year of bedcurtain grace, a whole tangle of affection" – "just wait slowly for this insincere heart of mine!"｜〔帶云〕 "I am old—what do you want with me?" inserted in the middle; she pushes him away and exits.

**Act 4 · Shuangdiao mode**
1. 〔新水令〕 "Suddenly the official order comes to my Lichun Garden" – "I beg you, brother, have pity"｜Summoned, startled.
2. 〔沉醉東風〕 "I thought it was a joyful banquet being laid; why the angry rows of rods and raised whips?" – "in the midst of haste I fall back in panic"｜**Peeps at the courtroom** (〔lead female peeps; shouting offstage〕), "step by step like dragging a felt through hair."
3. 〔沽美酒〕 "There is no use putting on a shy face" – "go and plead with that official"｜**Turns to Han Fuchen for help**; Han's obstructive speech inserted.
4. 〔太平令〕 "From now on I am willing to be truly his wife" – "who is like you, a wild short-lived thing, adapting to the moment"｜**Agrees in court to marry him, but the last line still curses him**—the character is not flattened.
5. 〔川撥棹〕 "With such a good marriage, everyone says it is all up to heaven" – "when will the person and the moon be round?"｜Turns to joy.
6. 〔七兄弟〕 "Already we are face to face, shoulder to shoulder, before the green window" – "one cuts red silk and green brocade, learning needlework"｜Writes the married life, two lines for husband and wife.
7. 〔梅花酒〕 "I remember our parting last year" – "thank the lord for his pity"｜Three-character lines in succession, reviewing the whole play.
8. 〔收江南〕 "Oh, it was not in vain—'one spring always spends money on flowers'" – "no more weeping as I am helped onto the tea boat!"｜**The last line answers the prologue yāopiān's "tea peddler Feng Kui"**, and the play ends here.

## §3 Act-by-act comparison with Wang Jilie's formal patterns for northern suites, and a rhyme audit

### 3. Comparison with northern-suite patterns (套数, tàoshù — suites of qupai in one mode; after Wang Jilie, 《螾庐曲谈》 [*Talks on Opera from the Yǐnlú Studio*], vol. 2, 3.2)

| Act | Common pattern listed by Wang Jilie | This play | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 · Xianlü | ② 點絳唇・混江龍・油葫蘆・天下樂・後庭花・青哥兒・賺煞 | Same as ②, with 〔一半兒〕 added in the middle and 〔寄生草〕 added after 〔青哥兒〕 | **Conforms to the pattern** (the opening and closing arias are identical; arias are added in the middle) |
| 2 · Nanlü | ② 一枝花・梁州第七・牧羊關・四塊玉・罵玉郎・元鶴鳴・烏夜啼・尾聲 | The first two arias are the same; two 〔隔尾〕 are added for sectioning, plus 〔賀新郎〕 and 〔斗蝦䗫〕; 〔罵玉郎・感皇恩・採茶歌〕 form a three-aria group; closes with 〔黃鐘尾〕 | **Conforms**, but **Wang's table omits 〔隔尾〕 and 〔黃鐘尾〕**—these two are standard items in the Nanlü suite of Yuan zaju; Wang lists the common Kunqu pattern, so **when writing the reference one must add these two lines from the play text** |
| 3 · Zhenggong | ⑤ 端正好・滾繡球・叨叨令・倘秀才・滾繡球・白鶴子・耍孩兒・三煞・二煞・一煞・煞尾 | 〔倘秀才〕 and 〔叨叨令〕 are **reversed**; 〔快活三〕 and 〔鮑老兒〕 replace 〔滾繡球〕 and 〔白鶴子〕; only two sha (二煞, 一煞) plus 煞尾 | **Conforms**—exactly Wang Jilie's ready-made evidence that "arias in the middle may be added, deleted, altered, or reversed in order" |
| 4 · Shuangdiao | ③ 新水令・駐馬聽・沈醉東風・雁兒落・得勝令・掛玉鉤・川撥棹・七弟兄・梅花酒・收江南・煞尾 | 〔駐馬聽〕 and 〔掛玉鉤〕 deleted; 〔喬牌兒〕 added after 〔沉醉東風〕; closes with the ①-form 〔鴛鴦煞尾〕 | **Conforms** (opening and closing match; middle added to and cut) |

- **Overall verdict: all four acts conform; not one act breaks the pattern.** Wang Jilie's statement—"In northern drama, though there are long suites and short suites, the opening and closing arias of each mode's suite are more or less fixed; the arias in the middle may be added, deleted, altered, or reversed"—hits the mark in all four acts of this play.
- **〔幺篇〕**: this play has **not one**. 〔按〕Compare the three-level distinction in `_notes/wu-mei-quxue-tonglun/11b-`—"the second tune of an aria: in the north it is called yao (幺); in the south it is called qianqiang (前腔), also huantou (换头)"—this play proves **zaju need not use yāopiān**; it equally proves **"qianqiang" and "huantou" never appear once in northern zaju**; wherever "qianqiang" appears it must be southern drama, which can serve as a textual test. (For examples using yāopiān see `02-jiufengchen.md`, three arias.)

#### 3.1 Rhyme: one rhyme throughout each act, four acts with four different rhymes (a line-by-line audit)

Classified according to the rhyme categories of 《中原音韻》 (*Zhongyuan Yinyun*, *Rhymes of the Central Plains*). For the rhyme feet, take all rhyming characters in each act's arias; here the first and last arias are given as evidence:

| Act | Rhyme category | Rhyme feet of the first aria | Rhyme feet of the last aria | One rhyme throughout? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Yóuhóu** | 〔點絳唇〕 愁・受・否・瘦 | 〔賺煞〕 口・守・囚・儔・羞・州・有・就・受・頭 | Yes (9 arias, none off-rhyme) |
| 2 | **Qíwēi** | 〔一枝花〕 睡・妻・隨・計・非・識 | 〔黃鐘尾〕 鬼・賊・欺・知・底・的・你 | Yes (11 arias; the entering-tone characters 識・疾・血・皮・的 are assigned to level, rising, and departing in northern drama and still count as Qíwēi) |
| 3 | **Xiāntiān** | 〔端正好〕 法・憲・天・殿・怨 | 〔煞尾〕 陰・旋・遍・顯 | Yes (10 arias; "法" is an entering-tone character used as rising, and rhymes) |
| 4 | **Jiēlái** | 〔新水令〕 臺・待・走・來・埋・快 | 〔鴛鴦煞尾〕 擺・壞・害・奶・邁・開・改 | Yes (10 arias) |

- **Four acts, four rhymes, none repeated.** 〔按〕This is a harder structural constraint in zaju than in chuanqi: when the mode changes, the rhyme category changes with it; **one act = one mode + one rhyme category**, the two bound together.
- **The prologue is not counted in "four acts, no repetition"**: the prologue's mode is always Xianlü (the same mode as Act 1), but its rhyme category starts separately (in this play the prologue is Zhēnwén, Act 1 Yóuhóu). → **The prologue is an independent unit with its own rhyme, not read together with Act 1**; for confirmation from the third sample see `03-jinxianchi.md`.
- **Repeated rhymes within a suite are allowed**: "頭" rhymes five times in Act 1 (〔混江龍〕, 〔油葫蘆〕, 〔天下樂〕, 〔青哥兒〕, 〔賺煞〕); "冤" and "天" recur in Act 3. → **This empirically confirms Wu Mei (吴梅)'s "in chuanqi the rhymes of an earlier aria and a later aria may be reused, and famous authors do not avoid it; in qingqu they may not"** (`_notes/wu-mei-quxue-tonglun/08-`, section 6)—the rule also holds for Yuan zaju drama arias, and **the switch "qingqu vs. dramatic aria" must be turned on for zaju too**.
- **The correspondence between rhyme category and emotion** is legible in this play: Act 1 Yóuhóu (closed-mouth endings: 愁, 受, 瘦, 皺), Act 3 Xiāntiān (open and resonant: 天, 怨, 冤, 願), Act 4 Jiēlái (來, 白, 壞, 改). 〔按〕This is a Yuan-era example of **choosing the rhyme category by emotion**, occupying the same functional slot as Lu Jun (陆军)'s "set the rhyme by character and by the key line," except that zaju adds a hard constraint: "one act may not change rhyme."

## §5 The convention layer (full text of main-text 1.4 and original examples)

- **Entrance poem (上场诗, shàngchǎngshī) + self-introduction (自报家门, zìbào jiāmén)** (a four-line poem + "... this is I" + native place + background + what one is about to do). Two contrasting samples side by side: **the corrupt official's entrance poem is written as a confession** (Taowu: "I am an official better than others; when a lawsuit comes, I want silver" + "whoever comes to sue is my parent and provider"); **the honest official's entrance poem describes only the realm he governs, not himself** (Li Gongbi: "My reputation and moral influence are heard through the ninefold heavens; on fine nights every house leaves its door unclosed").
- **The lead singer does not speak an entrance poem**: all three of Zhao Pan'er's entrances say only "I am Zhao Pan'er"—**the lead singer establishes herself (定场, dìngchǎng) through the first aria, not the entrance poem** (〔點絳唇〕 and 〔混江龍〕 are her establishment).
- **The exit verse is spoken by the jing who remains on stage**, while the lead singer exits as soon as the coda is sung. There is also the **split exit poem**: in Act 1 of Rescuing a Courtesan, Zhou She has two lines and the secondary female answers with two that reverse them ("No sooner out of the brothel door, than she plays the virtuous wife" / "I fear she will suffer from virtue and want to return to the brothel")—**the last line must carry a turn or an irony**.
- **〔帶云〕** = speech inserted in an aria, still belonging to the lead singer; **〔背唱〕** = sung to the audience, unheard by those on stage (only once in Rescuing a Courtesan, in 〔小梁州・幺篇〕; the Chinese title's five characters 「风月救风尘」 come from this line).
- **〔換科〕 = the special marker for "a swap in full view"**: in Act 4 of Rescuing a Courtesan the real and fake divorce writs are exchanged; in Act 3 of Golden Thread Pool the person supporting the drunk woman changes from the sisters to Han Fuchen. Not one word of explanation: the person swapped does not know, the audience does; after the swap there must be a line of "mistaken asking" to cash in the gap ("What are the names of the young men who support me?").
- **Official verdict + 〔詞云〕**: the verdict is prose sentencing item by item; the judgment poem is a rhymed summary. Dou E has eight lines; Golden Thread Pool has eight (**no punishment, no penalty—it is about bringing a couple together**—proving 〔詞云〕 is not exclusive to crime cases); Rescuing a Courtesan has four (**one line for each of four characters**).
- **Conventional exit (虚下, xūxià) / ghost gate (古门, gǔmén)**: 〔魂旦虛下〕 = exits but has not left the stage; 〔向古門道云〕 = calls toward the exit gate and the backstage answers—**one line saves a character an entrance and an exit**.

### Zaju conventions, play by play (original notes)

#### 4. Conventions and devices unique to this play (not seen in the first two files)

- **A supporting character carries the sectioning**: Shi Haowen three times remains on stage to speak a four-line exit poem (prologue, Act 3, Act 4) ("The fine banquet in the provincial office will not wait to end; in haste I take the red sleeve and go" / "Money is what the heart loves; wine is the matchmaker of lust" / the entrance poem before the prologue "Known from youth in the examination hall"), **using a non-singing supporting character to cut the play into recognizable sections**—the lead singer does not manage transitions at all.
- **Entrance verse (〔詞云〕 used as an exit)**: in Act 2 Han Fuchen enters, narrates a whole passage, and closes with four lines of 〔詞云〕: "I am a scholar with the lofty spirit of the clouds; I have met this wild bawd who has no scruples at all. If Heaven lets Shi Haowen return to the post of Jinan, I shall surely sue her and have her exiled"—**these four lines are both an exit verse and a preview of the plot of Acts 3 and 4**.
- **〔向古門道云〕**: Act 1 "〔lead female enters with the maid, calls toward the ghost gate〕 Han Xiucai, you just hide in the room and sit; do not come out" + 〔Han Fuchen answers〕 "I know"—**calling to a person not on stage, with the backstage answering**, one line saving a character an entrance and an exit.
- **〔換科〕 used as a mechanism**: in Act 3, after the drunken fall, the dan support her → Han Fuchen enters 〔換科〕 → the dan exit; **the person supporting her has changed and Du Ruiniang does not know**, hence the question "What are the names of the young men who support me?" 〔按〕This is the same stage term as Act 4 of Rescuing a Courtesan 〔lead female 換科〕 (swapping the divorce writs), used in two ways: **〔換科〕 is the special marker in Yuan zaju for writing "a swap in full view."**
- **The drinking game as scene structure**: Act 3 organizes the whole scene with "no one may say the three words 'Han Fuchen' while drinking; whoever says them drinks a great cup"—Du Ruiniang breaks the rule three times, drinks the penalty three times, sings three arias; **the rule is set by the protagonist and broken by the protagonist**, and drunkenness and true feeling are handed over together.
- **The four-line title-and-name form**: "Title: Han Jieyuan lightly breaks the flower-and-moon vow / The old bawd deliberately blocks the swallow-and-oriole tryst ｜ Name: Shi Haowen returns to the post of Jinan / Du Ruiniang wisely enjoys the Golden Thread Pool"—**four lines each marking one party (Han, the bawd, the prefect, Du Ruiniang)**, word for word identical with the text recorded in 《故事选》 (*Story Anthology*).
- **Official verdict 〔詞云〕 in eight lines**: "Han Jieyuan is a guest from the clouds; Du Ruiniang is a flower-and-moon enchantress. They were a heaven-made pair, deliberately bullied by the bawd... how could they have hoped to fulfill their happy union so early in the green tower!"—the same length as Dou E's eight lines, but **this play has no punishment and no penalty; the judgment poem is about bringing a couple together**, proving 〔詞云〕 is not exclusive to crime cases.

## §6 Representative aria passages (7 excerpts; for the rest see `_notes/guan-jiufengchen/02, 03` §5)

### 5. Representative aria passages (7, each showing what)

1. **〔滾繡球〕 (Act 3)** "There are sun and moon hanging morning and evening; there are ghosts and gods holding the power of life and death. Heaven and earth should distinguish the clear from the muddy—how then have they muddled Dao Zhi and Yan Yuan? The good suffer poverty and shorter life; the wicked enjoy wealth and long years. Heaven and earth, you act the bully who fears the hard and cheats the soft; it turns out you too push the boat with the current. Earth, if you do not distinguish good from bad, what are you? Heaven, if you misjudge the worthy and the foolish, you are heaven for nothing! Alas, I am left with two streams of tears." (「有日月朝暮懸，有鬼神掌著生死權。天地也只合把清濁分辨，可怎生糊突了盜跖顏淵：為善的受貧窮更命短，造惡的享富貴又壽延。天地也，做得個怕硬欺軟，卻元來也這般順水推船。地也，你不分好歹何為地。天也，你錯勘賢愚枉做天！哎，只落得兩淚漣漣。」)｜**Reason—example—judgment in three steps** (see instruction 12); the last two lines use the three-character phrases "Earth" and "Heaven" to call out directly, turning lyric into accusation. This is how the words of a famous northern aria work: all seven-character long lines before, then the last three lines suddenly shorten.
2. **〔叨叨令〕 (Act 3)** "Pity me, alone with no kin, left to swallow my voice and hold my breath in empty lament. 〔Executioner speaks〕 Have you no father or mother? 〔Lead female speaks〕 I have only a father... 〔sings〕 It is already more than ten years since I saw my father's face. 〔Executioner speaks〕 You just now asked me to take the back street—what was the idea? 〔Lead female sings〕 I fear only that in the front street I might be seen by my mother-in-law... In vain he was angered to death, yemoge; in vain he was angered to death, yemoge. I beg you, brother, at the point of death it is kind to give a person ease." (「可憐我孤身隻影無親眷，則落的吞聲忍氣空嗟怨。〔劊子云〕難道你爺娘家也沒的？〔正旦云〕止有個爹爹……〔唱〕早已是十年多不睹爹爹面。〔劊子云〕你適才要我往後街裏去，是什麼主意？〔正旦唱〕怕則怕前街裏被我婆婆見……枉將他氣殺也麼哥，枉將他氣殺也麼哥。告哥哥，臨危好與人行方便。」)｜**How to write dialogue under single-role singing**: the executioner interjects three times, and the lead singer catches each and sings on; "yemoge" is the fixed refrain of 〔叨叨令〕 and must be filled in as is.
3. **〔斗蝦䗫〕 (Act 2)** "In vain I grieve, I cannot understand: human life and death are reincarnation. I have caught this illness, I live in such times; is it wind-cold, summer-heat, dampness, or hunger and fullness, toil and fatigue? Each person knows his own symptoms; human life is bound up with heaven and earth... It is not that Dou E is disobedient, but I fear others will talk. Better listen to my advice, admit that you are out of luck, and set aside one coffin to keep..." (「空悲戚，沒理會，人生死是輪迴。感著這般病疾，值著這般時勢；可是風寒暑濕，或是飢飽勞役；各人證候自知，人命關天關地……不是竇娥忤逆，生怕旁人議論。不如聽咱勸你，認個自家悔氣，割捨的一具棺材停置……」)｜**Four-character lines in more than twenty runs**, the way of the northern long aria: no descriptive padding, only a list of reasons, one turn per line.
4. **〔耍孩兒〕, 〔二煞〕, 〔一煞〕 (Act 3)** The three vows in order: "I do not want a single half-star of hot blood spilled on the red dust; let it all hang on the white silk of the eight-foot flagpole" / "Have you not heard that frost flew in June because of Zou Yan? If I have a bellyful of grievance that spits out like fire, it must move the six-petaled ice flowers to roll like cotton" / "Why is there no sweet rain for three years? Only because the Eastern Sea once had a filial widow's injustice. Now it is the turn of your Shanyang County." (「我不要半星熱血紅塵灑，都只在八尺旗槍素練懸」／「豈不聞飛霜六月因鄒衍？若果有一腔怨氣噴如火，定要感得六出冰花滾似綿」／「做甚麼三年不見甘霖降？也只為東海曾經孝婦冤。如今輪到你山陽縣。」)｜**Three sha closing in sequence, one vow per aria, each with an allusion (Chang Hong turning to jade / Zou Yan's June frost / the Eastern Sea widow)**; the third vow lands private grievance on "the officials have no heart to uphold the law, so the common people have mouths but cannot speak," closing lyric on a public judgment.
5. **〔寄生草〕 (Act 1)** "You say he is hastily happy; I on the contrary worry for you in detail: worry is that when the mood flags she cannot swallow the wine of union; worry is that when her eyes swim she cannot twist the love-knot; worry is that when her senses blur she cannot sleep steady on the lotus cushion." (「你道他匆匆喜，我替你倒細細愁：愁則愁興闌刪嚥不下交歡酒，愁則愁眼昏騰扭不上同心扣，愁則愁意朦朧睡不穩芙蓉褥。」)｜**Three "worry is..." parallel clauses + three wedding objects** (wine, knot, cushion), an example of writing abstract emotion through concrete objects.
6. **〔收江南〕 (Act 4)** "Oh, this is the yamen that since ancient times opens south, and among them there is none without injustice. It pains me to death that my delicate body is shut in the spring terrace; three years and more, and I am left with endless flowing grief like the long Huai River." (「呀，這的是衙門從古向南開，就中無個不冤哉。痛殺我嬌姿弱體閉泉臺，早三年以外，則落的悠悠流恨似長淮。」)｜**Opens with a ready-made proverb** ("the yamen since ancient times opens south"), then turns to herself; five lines close a case. Northern drama does not avoid proverbs, the opposite of southern drama's elegance.
7. **〔鴛鴦煞尾〕 (Act 4)** "From now on I shall set the gold tablets and power swords in order, kill all the corrupt officials and filthy clerks, share the emperor's worries, and rid the ten thousand people of harm. 〔Speaks〕 I have forgotten one thing... 〔Sings〕 I entrust to you, Father: take in my mother-in-law; pity her, with no wife or child to care for her declining years." (「從今後把金牌勢劍從頭擺，將濫官污吏都殺壞，與天子分憂，萬民除害。〔云〕我可忘了一件……〔唱〕囑付你爹爹，收養我奶奶，可憐他無婦無兒誰管顧年衰邁。」)｜**Speech inserted in the coda then back to song**: after the big talk (eliminating evils) she suddenly remembers a small thing (entrusting the orphan, caring for the old woman), closing the play high then low—**do not leave only the large layer at the close**.

## §7 Transferable techniques (examples, self-checks, and failure conditions in full; complete version of main-text 1.5)

1. **Before writing, decide who sings; the title comes from that person's action.** 〔《赵盼儿风月救风尘》 (Zhao Pan'er Rescues a Courtesan)〕 **Self-check**: read the formal-name line aloud—is its subject the singer? If not, change the singer or change the title; choose one. **Does not hold for**: chuanqi and the banqiang system (板腔体, bǎnqiāngtǐ)—there sheng and dan may both sing, and primary/secondary status depends on stage time, not singing rights.
2. **Under single-role singing there are only two ways to write a scene with many people**: turn the other's words into interjections, and have the lead singer's next line **catch the exact words of that speech** and sing on. [Rescuing a Courtesan 〔元和令〕 takes up the secondary female's speech; Golden Thread Pool 〔感皇恩〕 is cut into four segments by three interjections, each segment catching the words of the other's previous line] **If you do not**: supporting characters go mute and the whole act collapses into a monologue recitation.
3. **An aria cut into two or three segments by stage business or another's speech is normal, not a breach.** [〔賺煞〕 cut into three by three stage actions; 〔叨叨令〕 cut into four by three executioner interjections] **Self-check**: if not one aria in the act is cut open, the sung passages are pasted on, not grown from the action.
4. **The scheme must be laid out in full before the audience in a whole passage of spoken dialogue before it is carried out**; the audience watches "will he do it as planned?" not "what does he want to do"; **and the trap must be halfway seen through and then brought back around.** [Rescuing a Courtesan Act 2, the whole plan speech before 〔浪裏來煞〕; Act 3, Song Yinzhang bursts in and makes a scene as planned, Zhou She takes up his rod, Zhao Pan'er turns and bites] **Does not hold for**: the general-layer view of suspense—opera does not keep secrets from the audience; hide the scheme the Western way and Act 3 has nothing to watch (qupai §2).
5. **A point that appears three times, each time in the same syntax, becomes a skeleton; twice is repetition.** ["Oaths are useless": Act 1 〔賺煞〕, Act 2 〔幺篇〕, Act 4 〔慶東原〕 "which one does not swear by the ghost's execution"] **Self-check**: the third must land where there are real consequences (in this play, in court, directly refuting the other side's evidence).
6. **Use a non-singing supporting character to speak an exit poem and mark the sections.** [Golden Thread Pool, Shi Haowen three times remains on stage to speak a four-line poem] **If you do not**: in a single-role-singing play, if even transitions belong to the singer, the protagonist becomes a narrator and the whole play goes flat.

**Where a famous work breaks the rules**: Act 4 of Golden Thread Pool **has no coda**—a third exit for omitting the coda, alongside "four arias of the same tune + huantou may omit the coda"; see qupai §11.
