## Sources and How the Books Relate

**范钧宏 (Fan Junhong) supplies the entries; 翁偶虹 (Weng Ouhong) supplies the examples and the conditions under which they fail. 刘吉典 (Liu Jidian) and 樊尚林 (Fan Shanglin) supply the constraints on wording and line length**, and their two books are the only ones that can answer "how many characters does this metrical pattern require?" **张庚 (Zhang Geng) and 郭汉城 (Guo Hancheng) supply the definitional layer and the grounds for the medium's boundaries. 罗怀臻 (Luo Huaizhen) supplies the topic-selection layer, the stylistic layer, and the layer of positioning within an opera genre**, and is also the only large sample of "banqiang scripts that do not mark metrical patterns." **陆军 (Lu Jun) supplies the method for aria lyrics** (the original text is preserved in 6c). **The full-script corpus supplies verifiable numbers; where the method books and the definitive texts disagree, the definitive text wins, and the author's own statement is treated as intention.**

## 0.1 Why Divide by Vocal System, Not by Opera Genre

| | **Qupai system** (Yuan zaju, chuanqi, Kunqu; Sichuan opera's high-pitched style) | **Banqiang system** (Peking opera, Henan opera, Yue opera, Qinqiang, Pingju, Shanghai opera, Huai opera; Cantonese opera's banghuang) |
|---|---|---|
| Structural unit | Zaju: **four acts and one wedge**; chuanqi: dozens of **chu (scenes)** | **Scene (场, chǎng)** |
| How lyrics are written | **Tune-filling (填词, tiáncí)**: each qupai has a fixed character count, line pattern, and tonal pattern; qupai are joined into suites | **Paired lines (上下句, shàngxiàjù)**: seven or ten characters, with the aria sustained by changes of metrical pattern; the number of lines is open |
| What the playwright must know | Qupai metre, modes (宫调, gōngdiào), Northern and Southern tunes | The correspondence between metrical patterns and emotion; rhyme categories |

**Opera genre is not the axis for dividing skills**: the difference between Peking, Henan, and Yue opera is which rhyme table and which metrical-pattern table you consult—a matter for references, not skills. But **method can cross opera genres; the temperament of a genre cannot**: "what is hard to solve is precisely the atmosphere hidden behind technique" (luo-v6 p0034). **Divide skills by vocal system; take jobs by atmosphere.**

**Two untidy edges**: **Cantonese opera's** banghuang (梆黄, bānghuáng — its two main tune systems) is banqiang while its xiaoqu (小曲, xiǎoqǔ — little tunes) are tune-filled; the two systems are mixed and use the nine tones of Cantonese, so test the material when it arrives; **Sichuan opera is an opera genre that crosses systems within a single script**: its high-pitched style (高腔, gāoqiāng) belongs to the qupai system, and **marking the vocal system is itself part of the playwright's job; read both skills**. For the evidence (the vocal-system affiliation of two 魏明伦 (Wei Minglun) plays and "vocal system treated as a character label"), see [reference.md](reference.md) §15.

## 1 First Fix the System and the Genre

**Two sets of classifications stand side by side, not merged**: Fan Junhong's four-way division (song-heavy / acting-heavy / martial / balanced, fan p0148); Weng Ouhong's three-way division (song-heavy play / speech-heavy play / martial play, weng-lunwenji p0459)—in a song-heavy play "there is little spoken dialogue (念白, niànbái), little stage business (身段, shēnduàn), little dance," and spoken dialogue is used only "to turn the plot or to end a passage of song."

**Fan's three grounds** (fan p0148–0150): **character** (a tragic character with weak action → song-heavy play; strong action and a vivid character → speech-and-business (念做, niànzuò) led; bronze-hammer (铜锤, tóngchuí) and blue-shirt (青衣, qīngyī) roles suit singing, posture painted-face (架子花, jiàzi huā) and vivacious female (花旦, huādàn) roles suit speech and business, martial sheng should sing as little as possible, martial chou does not sing), **plot** (no conditions for dance → can only be song-heavy; conditions for dance → song, speech, and dance combined; ensemble play → balanced), **style** (tragedy leans toward song, comedy toward speech and business). **Failure condition**: 《一捧雪》 (A Handful of Snow) is a great tragedy yet is not written as a song-heavy play. **Weng adds a sharp qualification**: civil versus martial plays **depend on how the main character resolves the conflict, not on the size of the event** (p0468).

**Self-check**: before writing, write down "the system of this play = ×××"; if the number of aria lines and the number of spoken lines are close, the system is unclear. Another ruler is the open form (full-length) / closed form (excerpt) of 顾仲彝 (Gu Zhongyi) (gu p0170), which likewise cannot be changed mid-way.

## 2 Concentration of the Main Line: Three-Way Comparison, No Adjudication

| | Statement | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 李渔 (Li Yu) | **Establish the main brain and cut the threads** (立主脑、减头绪, lì zhǔnǎo, jiǎn tóuxù — establish the central idea and cut the side threads; "one person, one event" is the formulation of his line, which this skill does not use and points to `sw-chinese-opera-qupai`) | quoted in weng-bianju p0352 |
| Fan Junhong | **Vertical concentration**: one central event with causes and consequences—**the opposite direction from spoken drama's "horizontal concentration"**; more layers → more characters → more entrances and exits, "and that is not the structural method of Chinese opera" | fan p0020–0022 |
| Weng Ouhong | Li Yu's formulation "**is, after all, one family's opinion**"; when writing a full-length play he uses methods such as "a band of cloud cutting across the ridge" (横云断岭), "a grass snake's gray line" (草蛇灰线), "a lantern behind a curtain and a sword in a box" (帷灯匣剑), "powder applied to the back of the painting" (背面敷粉), and "clouds supporting the moon" (烘云托月)—"**there is no need to tell the audience plainly how many people this family has**" | weng-bianju p0352 |

Weng's six methods also include separating the strands to gather the fan (隔股聚扇), choosing troops and storing momentum (选兵蓄势), different roads to the same destination (殊途同归), returning waves and whirlpools (回澜漩涡), light crimson (浅绛), and the spiral earthworm (螺旋蚓曲). Three are executable: **lantern behind a curtain, sword in a box** (帷灯匣剑, wéi dēng xiá jiàn — a character who is present offstage still counts as a scene; **failure condition**: the scene must advance the protagonist's action line, p0297); **clouds supporting the moon / powder on the back of the painting** (烘云托月／背面敷粉 — write the offstage protagonist before the protagonist enters, p0141; **terminological conflict**: Weng's sense = using a contrasting character within one play to set off the protagonist, Wei Minglun's sense = using the old era to set off the new era, wei-bashan p0077; the two cannot be merged); **choosing troops and storing momentum** (选兵蓄势, xuǎn bīng xù shì — characters you intend to use must be selected in advance and connected to the whole play, p0392).

**Self-check**: should an expository opening scene be added—does the protagonist's second entrance come **with a scene** or **waiting for a scene** (p0349); summarize one guānmù (关目 — the key action that defines a scene) per scene, and if you cannot summarize it, cut a thread (fan p0022). **Concentration ≠ merging two or three scenes into one**: "to fail to concentrate when concentration is called for is to be scattered; to force concentration when it is not called for is equally to be scattered" (p0020). **Reverse limit**: fable form may be an exception, on condition that a single prop runs through and sustains cause and effect (luo-v2 p0057)—**set the two side by side.**

## 3 Technical Structure and Staging (排场)

- **Technical structure** (Fan's own coinage, fan p0318): a complete opera structure = story structure + technical structure, that is, the layout of song, speech, business, and martial action (唱念做打, chàng niàn zuò dǎ — the four performance skills) across the scenes.
- **The law of alternating staging** (common to both systems, wang-jilie p0169): "If the first act has the sheng (生 — male role) sing and the second act has the dan (旦 — female role) sing, then the third act must use a broad-voice role or a lively ensemble scene; if two or three acts of slow tunes and long suites have passed, a short transition scene or a comic scene performed by chou or jing must be inserted." **Self-check**: list the scenes in a row by "cold / hot"; if three consecutive scenes are cold or hot, insert something.
- **Short transition scenes** "without which the plot does not connect and the threads do not join" (wang-jilie p0166). **Historical baseline of proportions in old scripts** (luo-v6 p0141): out of twenty scenes, at least fifteen transition scenes and three to five core aria scenes—**scope of application**: the scenario-table (幕表制, mùbiǎo zhì — the early scenario-table production system) period, not a target for today. **Hot-cold counterpoint is also a physical-shift schedule** (zhang-guo p0227). For a sample scene table see [reference-hangdang.md](reference-hangdang.md) §5.

## 4 Role Types Determine the Writing; Writing for the Performer

**Definitional layer** (zhang-guo p0341–0347): role types "are both an image system and a convention system," determined by **five factors** (natural attributes / social attributes [the core] / style, grave or comic / aesthetic evaluation / technical specialty); the terms **běngōng (本工 — the role type one is primarily trained in) / yìnggōng (应工 — the role type one can also perform) / liǎngménbào (两门抱 — spanning two role types)**; a role type can be changed mid-play but an event must serve as the fastening. Bottom line: "seek individuality within the type."

1. **Singing has only two types**: the big-voice type (大嗓, dàsǎng — natural voice; represented by the laosheng (老生 — middle-aged male role), with painted-face (花脸, huāliǎn), chou (丑, chǒu — clown), and laodan (老旦, lǎodàn — old female role) belonging to it) and the small-voice type (小嗓, xiǎosǎng — head voice; represented by the dan (旦, dàn — female role), with xiaosheng (小生, xiǎoshēng — young male role) belonging to it)—**write lyrics for jing (净, jìng — painted-face role), laodan, and chou as you would for laosheng; write for xiaosheng as you would for dan** (liu p0015).
2. **Role-type taboos are hard**: jing does not sing 反二黄 (fǎn'èrhuáng), does not sing the traditional 二黄慢板 (èrhuáng mànbǎn — erhuang slow pattern), does not sing 四平调 (sìpíngdiào); 南梆子 (nánbāngzi) is given only to dan and xiaosheng; 唢呐二黄 (suǒnà èrhuáng) is given to hongsheng (红生, hóngshēng — red-face sheng) and hongjing (红净, hóngjìng — red-face jing); 南锣 (nánluó) is **spoken only, never sung** (liu p0272 and elsewhere; for the full table see [reference-hangdang.md](reference-hangdang.md) §4).
3. **For martial sheng (武生, wǔshēng — martial male role), write no more than six to eight lines of song, preferring 散板 (sǎnbǎn — free-rhythm pattern) and 摇板 (yáobǎn — shaking pattern); song must be inserted between martial passages** (weng-lunwenji p0033, p0038); **failure condition**: a martial sheng who also works in laosheng can be relaxed. The same tragic event must be written as different emotions for different role types—the martial sheng's grief must find an outlet within the same scene (p0085).
4. **When adding scenes for a star, first look at the gaps in his repertoire, not only at the subject matter** (weng-bianju p0137); **failure condition**: after a play becomes a great hit, the actor "puts his horse out to pasture" and the next script cannot be mounted (p0278). **Aria distribution should follow the stamina curve** (weng-lunwenji p0187); for the dossier see [reference-hangdang.md](reference-hangdang.md) §3.

**One pairing in opposite directions**: Weng's "write according to the existing role-type routes" versus Luo Huaizhen's "shape the subject according to the actor's plasticity"—in seven Yue operas, the **female xiaosheng** (女小生, nǚ xiǎoshēng — a woman playing young male roles) leads the singing in four; **one actor playing two / one actor playing many (一赶二／一赶多, yī gǎn èr / yī gǎn duō) is a structure written for her**, and **a xiaosheng may cry, show weakness, and sing children's songs** (luo-v3 p0006/p0236, p0264). **Modern-dress plays (现代戏, xiàndàixì): role types recede, functional positions remain**—《朝阳沟》 (Chaoyang Village) does not mark role types; 二大娘 (Er Daniang) = caidan (彩旦, cǎidàn — comic female role; the play's only comic bearer), 拴保娘 (Shuanbao's mother) = laodan route (yang, suggestion 6).

## 5 How to Use Conventions

- **A script may write conventional suites** (起霸 qǐbà — opening martial display, 趟马 tàngmǎ — horse-riding mime, 走边 zǒubiān — stealth travel mime), **but may not write individual convention units** (bending the waist [下腰, xiàyāo], flicking pheasant feathers [涮翎, shuànlíng], zhang-guo p0338). **The material condition for conventions failing**: once act divisions arrive, the entrance prelude, entrance poem, self-introduction, qǐbà, and transition scenes are deleted along with them (liu p0143); **self-check**: if you use acts, do not keep copying the traditional entrance and exit conventions.
- **Item 9 of the afterword to 《沙家浜》 (Shajiabang) is the skeleton**: "cut the idle text of entrances and exits" and "abolish the gongs and drums that play characters on and off and the 'generals out, ministers in' stage doors" are the same thing—**the abolition of conventions went hand in hand with the abolition of the scene format**; in seven of ten scenes, "the curtain rises and they are already present" (wang §1.5).
- **Gu Zhongyi's list of conventions** (gu p0189–0193) can serve as a checklist, but he is rooted in spoken drama: three times he judges song-and-dance and martial action as "**theatricality**" rather than drama (p0110, p0214), and then himself cites 《周成献嫂》 (Zhou Cheng Offers His Sister-in-Law) and 《庵堂认母》 (Recognizing Mother in the Nunnery) as counterexamples (p0147)—**set the two views side by side, do not adjudicate**.
- **The chorus layer** (luo-v2 §1.7): all seven of Luo Huaizhen's plays after 1992 **have a chorus layer** (offstage solo / chorus / 帮腔 bāngqiāng — offstage answering chorus / 莲花落 liánhuālào — a folk narrative song form), with three functions: **establish time and place, translate inner feeling, comment on events**, and with 【字幕：】 (surtitles) added, **it collectively replaces the entrance poem and exit poem**. It occupies the same functional position as the qupai system's "fùmò opening (副末开场, fùmò kāichǎng) + Tang-assembled exit poem (集唐下场诗, jítáng xiàchǎngshī)," two sets of devices (cross-reference with `sw-chinese-opera-qupai`). **Failure condition (hard rule)**: when the entrance poem has been deleted and no chorus is installed, the establishment of time and place must have two independent bearers (surtitle + song or action); if only one remains, it fails. **Self-check**: after deleting the chorus lyrics, reread—does anyone still establish time, place, and commentary?

## 6 Aria Lyrics

### 6a Paired Lines and Line Division

- **Authoritative line division** (liu p0016, p0176): seven characters = **2·2·3**, ten characters = **3·3·4**; **the first line usually ends on an oblique tone and often does not rhyme; the second line ends on a level tone and must rhyme**. Line division has nothing to do with metrical pattern, only with character count.
- **This is a default, not a hard constraint**: **more than eight lines must produce a variant** (p0177); variants have always existed—in 《捉放曹》 (Catching and Releasing Cao Cao), 陈宫 (Chen Gong) begins singing from the **second line**; in 《回荆州》 (Return to Jingzhou), 孙尚香 (Sun Shangxiang) **throws away the first line** and exits (fan p0143).
- **An aria must have an even number of lines** (p0175), **and the only legitimate exit is 【扫头】 (sǎotóu)**; **the two must be stated together**: if you write an odd number of lines without marking 【扫头】, the performer has nowhere to close the tune. A complete aria must itself have qǐ chéng zhuǎn hé (fan p0107); **self-check**: identify the line where its "turn" occurs. For the three-column metrical-pattern table see [reference.md](reference.md) §1–§2; for Henan opera's 54 patterns see [reference-yuju-difang.md](reference-yuju-difang.md) §1.

### 6b The Key Line

Four implementations of the same functional position, all called **"find that one line first"**: Li Yu's "alternative reading of the wùtóu" / 吴梅 (Wu Mei) / 王季烈 (Wang Jilie) (see the wùtóu section of `sw-chinese-opera-qupai`) / Lu Jun's "the key line fixes the rhyme." **An aria is named after its key line, not its opening line** (nine of the sixteen arias in the 《朝阳沟》 score are like this, yang item 15); **self-check**: "which line will the audience remember"? If that line is not in the most conspicuous place, rearrange.

### 6c The Three Goods of Aria Lyrics (Lu Jun, original text preserved)

- **Choose the rhyme well**: determine the rhyme by the character's personality; determine the rhyme by the key line (first write the shining key line—embodying the theme / describing the mood / revealing a change in relationships—then let it fix the rhyme); broad rhymes (人辰, 江阳, 言前—many characters, easy to write but easy to fall into sameness) versus risky rhymes (乜斜, 发花—few characters, but if apt they refresh the ear); the charm of **same-character rhymes** (dozens of "手," nearly a hundred "人," "你," "我"). For the four sets of examples see [reference.md](reference.md) §9.
- **Weave the structure well**: avoid uniform seven- and ten-character lines; borrow line patterns from different qupai ("阴阳血" [yīnyángxuè] question-and-answer, "赋子板" [fùzibǎn] narration); vary with the environment and performance style (the triple song, chorus, side song, and antiphonal song of the sedan-chair dance); palindrome can express turbulent feeling ("In my gloom I wrong others; when others wrong me, my heart has no gloom" — 闷无心我负人，人负我心无闷)—line patterns and tune patterns must avoid stiffness and uniformity.
- **Lay it out well**: avoid "not singing when one should sing, singing when one should not" and "a fragment here, a fragment there, chicken scratch"; carefully design key scenes (the emotional outpouring of the male and female leads, the moment of fierce conflict, the moment of fate's turning) as a single block of musical scene; the arias that have been handed down through the generations all stand out from heavy scenes.

**Two supplements**: Weng supplies the technical layer of rhyme: "**the first line opens the rhyme, the second line stacks the jade**" (weng-bianju p0013); rhyme categories correspond to emotions and have role-type taboos (weng-lunwenji p0310). **A first-hand disagreement**: Lu Jun's "the aria falls in the heavy scene" is the opposite of Luo Huaizhen's 《典妻》 (Wife for Hire) (luo-v2 p0146); **set the two side by side**; Luo's version holds only when the "previous scene" is the character's road toward knowing her fate.

### 6d Whole-Play Layout of Rhyme Categories and the Real Scale of Rhyming

- **Whole-play layout** (parallel to "character determines rhyme," not merged): 《锁麟囊》 (The Jewelry Purse) **changes rhyme category scene by scene, and the first and last both rhyme on 遥条 (yáotiáo)** (suo §6). **The two parties in a confrontation each occupy one rhyme category** (《断桥》 (The Broken Bridge): Bai (白) on 江阳 (jiāngyáng) / Xu (许) on 言前 (yánqián), tian item 7); **failure condition**: two people who are allies or in the same situation need not separate rhymes.
- **The real scale of rhyming** (measured in 《朝阳沟》, yang §3): **second lines rhyme 100%, first lines about 50%**; **neighboring rhyme categories may be used interchangeably, dialect may be assigned to a category, and the rhyme may change mid-passage** (only at emotional turns); **risky rhymes are not used even once**—colloquial scripts and ensemble singing default to broad rhymes. Reserve risky rhymes for the longest and most important passage (pan p0043); if you cannot assemble apt characters, fall back to a broad rhyme.
- **The three rhyme tables are not interchangeable**: **the thirteen rhyme categories are the old thirteen of Peking opera** (three differences from Northern speech: 庚青 (gēngqīng) goes into 人辰 (rénchén), entering-tone characters (入声, rùshēng) are kept as stage-read characters (上口字, shàngkǒuzì), 居鱼 (jūyú) goes into 衣期 (yīqī), liu p0169–0171); **Henan opera follows Zhongzhou phonology (中州音韵, Zhōngzhōu yīnyùn) and has no rhyme-category division table** (yuju p0014); **Sichuan opera uses Sichuan Mandarin** (pan p0040); Yue opera rhymes 人辰 interchangeably with 中东／庚青 (luo-v3 C3, "summarized from notes"). For the tables see [reference.md](reference.md) §3, the character-by-character table [reference-shisanzhe.md](reference-shisanzhe.md), [reference-yuju-difang.md](reference-yuju-difang.md) §4, §7.

### 6e Duoju and Colloquial Speech in Lyrics

**The complete specification of duoju** (垛句, duǒjù — inserted short lines; six great duoju in 《锁麟囊》, suo item 3): **only in the on-the-beat section, only in the second-line position, mixing three-character and four-character beats, with a maximum of 26 characters**; **self-check: delete all the duoju, and the remaining regular paired lines must still be complete and fluent** (liu p0217). **When the line pattern changes, the metrical pattern should turn** (yang suggestion 4): wherever the score says "turn to 【垛板】 (duǒbǎn)," the lyrics must at the same time become three-/four-character duoju—**a playwright who does not understand melody can still fix the points of metrical change**; **self-check: draw a line at every metrical-pattern change in a core aria; the line pattern on the two sides of the line (seven-character / ten-character / duoju) must change at least once; if it does not change, the metrical turn is empty**; on the Henan opera side, duoju are written only under 垛子板 (duǒzibǎn) and 流水垛板 (liúshuǐ duǒbǎn) (yuju item 5).

**Colloquial speech in lyrics** (cross-reference with Lu Jun's "plain beauty surpasses elegant beauty"): **the three-piece set = direct quotation + real numbers + dialect reduplication; if two of the three are missing, fall back to spoken dialogue**; **make an ordinary event into a big aria by means of one repeated three-character lead line** ("你写上：" ["you write down:"] leading four times = 22 lines, yang p0056), **failure condition**: more than five leads turn it into a doggerel jingle. **Symbolic couplets should choose natural phenomena whose cause and effect cannot change** (weng-bianju p0407). **Yue opera does not write this way**: among 496 aria markings there is only one true duoju; expansion relies on **same-initial alliterative parallelism in uniform lines** (six lines maximum)—**in Yue opera, duoju are the score for stage business; if no stage business accompanies them, do not use them** (luo-v3 B1–B4).

## 7 Spoken Dialogue and the Song–Speech Seam

- **Song and speech have six functions** (lyric expression, dialogue, exposition, technique, prompting, dance, fan p0096); **whether to sing or speak** depends on whether the content can bear metrical constraint + whether the other party can react (p0089). **Failure condition**: the protagonist has a long aria and the opponent is left with only limited reactions. **Self-check**: after this song begins, does the other person on stage still have a scene?
- **Ensemble scenes use speech; two-person confrontation uses song** (《朝阳沟》 scene 5 has a song-to-speech ratio of 8:92, scene 8 is 73:26); **failure condition**: ritual ensemble scenes are the opposite. **Teasing and banter use speech; when passing judgment, make the other party switch to song** (yang items 7–8).
- **Address-term interface (hard rule, common to both systems)**: call the person first, then start singing—"银环 (Yinhuan)! (sings) ..." "Co-mother (亲家母, qìngjiāmǔ — the other spouse's mother), sit down quickly. (sings) Co-mother, you sit down." Three sets of material, three vocal systems, three eras. **Self-check: is the line before every aria an address term? Failure condition**: solo song, offstage song, and behind-the-curtain song do not apply; **replacement rule**: for these three, use a **lead-in line**—the previous line must be an action or half-sentence that pushes the aria out; **self-check**: delete the line before a solo aria—can the song still start?
- **The three song-speech formats of 《沙家浜》** (wang items 6–7) **can only be done on free-rhythm metrical patterns**: yaoban cut into five segments by speech / the last line slides directly into speech / after singing, the opponent comments in speech—**the choice of metrical pattern directly determines the textual form**; **failure condition**: inserting speech into an on-the-beat (上板, shàngbǎn) pattern will break the tune. Spoken dialogue can also displace the first two clauses of a sung line (顶板, dǐngbǎn — entering on the beat, liu p0256).
- **The cause of "spoken drama plus songs" is not too much spoken dialogue but a broken rhythm** (fan p0134): "Either the arias are concentrated and continuous, or the levels of dialogue are changed and rearranged."

## 8 Duets

**Three metrical patterns used specifically for duets in Henan opera are promoted to general banqiang practice** (Peking opera duets have no special name, yuju p0212/0214/0221): **二八连弹** (èrbā liántán — starts and ends on the beat [板起板落, bǎn qǐ bǎn luò], every line connected, the paired lines split among different characters; used for reunion and celebration) / **狗撕咬** (gǒu sī yǎo — "dog bite": multiple lines → two lines → one line → half-line, diminishing; used for courtroom confrontation) / **流水留板** (liúshuǐ liúbǎn — the last line does not close the pattern but is handed to the next person to continue).

**Line-length diminution has two grades, which must not be mixed**: **the quarrel grade** (dog-bite, the mother and daughter in 《朝阳沟》 scene 8) **does not write speed-up instructions; the rhythm is self-evident**, and after diminishing to a half-line, one side must add one more line to close the passage (yang item 12); **the argument grade** (in Yue opera's 《梅龙镇》 (Meilong Town), mother and son go seven → five → four → two characters, one rhyme throughout, luo-v3 p0231)—**Yue opera has no metrical-pattern names; character count itself is Yue opera's metrical pattern**. **Four closing methods stand side by side and cannot be merged** (luo-v2 item 2): question becomes shorter while answer stays the same / answer gets two extra lines in the last round / both sides diminish to one character / chain-link continuation receives three characters; **self-check**: if the last three rounds have the same character counts as before, there is no closure.

**Syntax of conflict scenes** (fan p0116): line count and metrical pattern diminish from slow to fast, first lines odd and second lines even with the same character count, in antithesis, **the main side's lines are placed in the second-line position**—in the "Battle of Wits" (《智斗》) of 《沙家浜》, 阿庆嫂 (A Qing Sao)'s ten lines **all fall on second lines, all five pairs without exception**. **Failure condition**: if single lines are written as "2-2-3" and double lines as "3-3-4," then "the tense atmosphere will necessarily disappear"; if you are writing a scene where the protagonist is being beaten down, reverse it. **Fan also criticizes the abuse of the aside** ("falling into the trap of the new tradition," fan p0135)—**《沙家浜》 is both a model and its own counterexample; set the two side by side.**

**Three more points**: **the first line of an interface must use a tune** (weng-lunwenji p0462); the script of a duet play = **two six-step emotional staircase tables interleaved + one passage of spoken dialogue as a rhythmic turn** (《赤桑镇》 (Chisang Town) p0460); **the default form of Yue opera** is seven-character single lines cut antiphonally, one rhyme throughout, **self-check: cover the character names—do they still connect into one speech?** (luo-v3 D4) Bāngqiāng (Sichuan opera high-pitched style)—hétóu (合头 — the refrain sung by all, in Henan opera)—offstage singing (Yue opera) are three forms of the same functional position; **bāngqiāng moved to another banqiang opera genre has no equivalent** (pan item 5); for the three-column table see [reference-yuju-difang.md](reference-yuju-difang.md) §6.

## 9 What the Playwright Must Mark: Two Styles Side by Side

**"A banqiang playwright must mark metrical patterns" is not a universal rule.** Choose one house style before writing.

**Peking-opera style** (Weng Ouhong's full-play master-lecture tradition; evidence: the score edition of 《锁麟囊》, the 1955 edition of 《白蛇传》 (The White Snake), the 1965 edition of 《沙家浜》): the playwright must also **write a gong-and-drum outline for the drummer, a stage-management outline for the stage, and design costumes and makeup** (weng-bianju p0009). Mark **metrical patterns** (p0053), **opening and closing gongs and drums** (use 【扫头】 for deleted lines), **entrance and exit gongs and drums**, **scene-music qupai**, **costume-change table and prop table** (suo item 14); for the correspondence table and notation see [reference.md](reference.md) §4–§6. **When spoken dialogue is inserted into a key aria, sew it back with "continue singing" (接唱, jiēchàng)**; **self-check**: if "(sings)" appears more than four times in one key scene, you have probably broken one aria into pieces.

**Yue-opera style** (Luo Huaizhen's line; evidence: 496 aria markings in seven Yue operas and eight local operas, **zero metrical-pattern markings**): the rule is "**what has a (sings) mark is song; what has no mark is speech**"; **use 【字幕：……】 (surtitles) to establish time and place**, replacing self-introduction and entrance prose; **role types, gongs and drums, costumes, and stage management are never written** (luo-v3 E, luo-v2 §1.7). **When metrical patterns are not marked, character count is the metrical pattern**: shifting from 5 → 7 → 4 → 3 characters can produce the effect of sanban—yuanban—kuaiban—duoban (散板 free rhythm—原板 original pattern—快板 fast pattern—垛板 duoju pattern; luo-v2 p0147); the switching of long and short lines and the duoju themselves are metrical hints to the composer—the same thing as 《朝阳沟》's "when the line pattern changes, the metrical pattern should turn," seen from the other side. **Self-check**: list the character count of every line as a sequence; if it is 7, 7, 7, 7 all the way through, there is no rhythmic design. **Failure condition**: Peking-opera-style scripts still have to mark metrical patterns (《潘金莲》 (Pan Jinlian) follows the Yue-opera style, marking only "song / bāngqiāng"). **Metrical intent is issued separately in a 《唱段表》 (Aria Table) to the composer and the fiddler; the body text uses line length as a hint—that is, the structure of the 《朝阳沟》 script plus the score appended to the book; the "metrical intent" in the X4 deliverables refers to this table.**

**Three things common to both styles**: ① **Peking-opera qupai are instrumental pieces, sharing a name but not a nature with qupai-system qupai** (liu p0071); they cannot be handled by qupai-system metre, but for a few qupai that carry song, "writing words to the tune" is the playwright's job (weng-lunwenji p0482). ② **Leave the action to the actors**: the script is written up to "the fight begins"; **failure condition**: if the fight carries a plot turn, the dispositions of the two sides must be written clearly (wang item 16). ③ **The first task in organizing a traditional script is pronunciation and line breaks, not plot** (weng-bianju p0027).

## 10 Interface with Actors and Fiddlers

- **Upper bound: metrical-pattern markings are a letter of intent** (tian, preface, p0012); evidence: "Stealing the Herb" 【高拨子】 (gāobōzi) → 【唢呐二黄】, "Closing the Bowl" 【西皮】 (xīpí) → 【二黄】 (èrhuáng). **Self-check**: for every unusual metrical pattern you mark, write one sentence explaining "why this one"; if you cannot give a reason, the production will certainly change it.
- **Lower bound: the playwright's responsibility stops at level/oblique tones and line pattern** (zhang-guo p0294). **The only criterion for an inverted character** (倒字, dǎozì) is "a rising level tone may be sung high; only the yin level tone may not be brought out low" (liu p0158); self-check: read aloud the four tones (四声, sìshēng) of each line's final character and the character that carries the melisma.
- **王瑶卿 (Wang Yaoqing)'s five points on tune** (tian p0012): lyrics need not be too regular ("that way it is actually harder to vary the tune") / scenes may be cut but arias must be moved (cut 《煎药》 (Decocting Medicine) and move the 【二黄慢板】 duet into 《释疑》 (Dispelling Doubt)) / word-level colloquial revision / do not reject outside vocal systems / an entire scene may change vocal system.
- **程砚秋 (Cheng Yanqiu)'s three revisions** (weng-bianju p0141): cut 【西皮原板】 into three sections to match "three yields of the seat" (**note: the score edition has four sections**) / "write more long and short lines so I can fit the tune to the words" / change the final scene's 【南梆子】 to 【二六】 (èrliù — two-six pattern) **without changing the words**. **Self-check**: change the metrical pattern to a neighboring one—can the words still be sung?
- **Changing words necessarily changes the tune** (zhang-guo p0057). **Agree on five things before writing** (zhang-guo p0421): layout, plot development, scene interleaving, role assignment, stage handling and display of technique.

## 11 Organizing Traditional Material, Excerpts and Full-Length Plays Adapted into Each Other, and Assigned-Topic Adaptation

**Keep three routes in separate accounts** (weng-bianju p0251 and elsewhere): **organizing** (整理, zhěnglǐ) = preserve the original as much as possible; **adapting** (改编, gǎibiān) = remove the weeds and keep the essence, recut; **transplanting** (移植, yízhí) = grasp the main outline and start a new stove. Write down which category you are in before beginning; mixing them produces "neither like the original nor like a new composition."

**Organizing traditional material: affirm the essence first** (fan p0354). The wrong procedure is "first grasp the dross ... delete and add"—"in this adding and subtracting, a good play often becomes unrecognizable." The right procedure: before writing, write down "the two or three scenes this play must keep," and let all later changes be constrained by them.

For **the three-column list for 《白蛇传》** (a template for organizing: keep / cut the weak / newly add, tian §2, including the two rules "guard against mistaken borrowing" and "moving one aria to another scene will change the tone of the new scene"), see [reference.md](reference.md) §10.

**Excerpts and full-length plays adapted into each other** (gu p0318, p0170): in 《秋江》 (Autumn River) the old boatman is the protagonist in the excerpt and must be compressed when it enters the full-length play; 《徐策跑城》 (Xu Ce Runs Through the City) is a transition scene promoted to an excerpt; another route is **linking excerpts + newly writing the transitional seams** (weng-bianju p0039). **Before adapting, take four soundings**: the historical base, the performance base, the style base, and the script base (fan p0230).

**Workflow for assigned-topic adaptation** (《沙家浜》, wang §3): an outside party supplies the thematic judgment; the writer changes scenes and lyrics; **the most important finding is that "the revised script changes only words, not structure"**—between the 1965 and 1970 versions, scene names, event order, aria placement, and metrical patterns did not change for five years; **famous passages often come from places that "do not sound like revolutionary language."** **When reviving an old play, you must judge "dragging" and "jumping" again** (fan p0253): ask "from which scene does the play really enter the drama." **Choosing the base text when revising an old play** (luo-v6 p0057): small repairs will be eaten by old memories; if you want to change the theme, change the base text; **self-check**: is the base text a "source" or a "stream"? **The four adaptation questions** (the opera version of the four questions of 霍克斯特 [Huòkèsītè]; opera projects do not read `sw-format-adaptation`, so answer them directly here): ① Are there rights? Confirm with the real person first; ② Can it go on stage?—does it stand with one table and two chairs, and is there a place to sing? ③ Which specific properties of the original medium cannot be transplanted (the novel's interiority, spoken drama's dense dialogue)? ④ What can be borrowed from the thematic symbolic system? **Add another table for real-person / historical material** (`sw-workflow` X0.5): is each guānmù historical record, legend, or something I invented—**fiction occurs only in the gaps of the historical record, and does not excuse the protagonist**. For the lower bound of adaptation see [reference-common.md](reference-common.md) §5.

## 12 Three Contemporary Routes: Each with a Switch Sentence

### 12a Modern-Dress Plays: Using Conventions to Perform Contemporary Life

**Switch sentence** (开关句, kāiguān jù — the sentence that sets the route): the subject is contemporary life, and conventions must be reformed for use.

**Diagnose first** (the three contradictions in 梅兰芳 (Mei Lanfang)'s fashion plays (时装戏, shízhuāngxì — contemporary-dress Peking opera), zhang-guo p0517, his own account): dance movement "is all learned but has no use" / "first, the contradiction between music and movement ... naturally becomes **more speech and less song**" / "inserting 【慢板】 (mànbǎn — slow pattern) into a fashion play is the most headache-inducing thing." Take Luo's statement of the problem as an opening—"**what do you do about riding a bicycle?**" (luo-v6 p0116); **do not take his "re-coding" as the answer**; that is the method of newly compiled costume plays (古装新编, gǔzhuāng xīnbiān).

**The latter method**: Weng's four-step method (exaggeration → sharpening → refinement → rhythm → dance, weng-lunwenji p0517)—modern-dress scripts **do not write "which convention to use," but "what action, what rhythm, what amplitude"**; **self-check: can the action land on the gong-and-drum beat?** **Add three more to subject selection** (fan p0301): it can "become empty," it can "become mobile," and technically there is a tradition to borrow from; **failure condition**: theoretical-debate dialogue "is too out of tune with the rhythm of traditional song and speech"; **self-check**: do the protagonist's three most intense moments each have a traditional convention to borrow?

**Cut / keep / add list** (wang §1 + yang §4): **cut** self-introduction (in 《沙家浜》 it is left only to the villain and changed to singing for the people in the same scene), entrance poem, exit poem; **keep** entrances and exits, **the aside (背供, bèigōng)**, **offstage singing (幕内唱, mùnèichàng) of the daoban (导板, dǎobǎn — leading board) entrance**; **add** offstage chorus as hétóu; **keep stage business, remove props**. **Pronunciation and rhyme are another set** (liu p0169): modern plays almost never use stage-read characters; erhua (儿化, érhuà — retroflex suffixation) and Beijing minor rhyme categories (小辙口, xiǎo zhékǒu) may be written (the specific practice is limited to Peking opera; other opera genres take only the skeleton "modern play = colloquial broad rhyme," and work out the practice according to that genre's phonology, see [reference-yuju-difang.md](reference-yuju-difang.md) §4); **self-check**: is this script a traditional play or a modern play—settle this first. **One first-hand objection**: Liu Jidian p0589 objects to treating "the through-composition of characteristic melodic motifs" as the only creative method; keep it side by side.

### 12b The Absurd and Cross-Time: Modern Structure for Costume Subjects

**Switch sentence** (pan p0104): 《易胆大》 (Yi Danda) takes "**modernizing Chinese opera**," while 《四姑娘》 (Fourth Sister) takes "**making the modern play operatic**"—**a play takes only one direction**. For the rules of 《潘金莲》 (cross-time characters = **a commentary bench**, the three accompanying rules, the author and the critics covering both sides) see [reference.md](reference.md) §11.

### 12c Contemporary New Composition: Costume Subjects, All Conventions Retained, the Coding Is What Changes

**Switch sentence**: **"Modern means purification"** (luo-v6 p0045); this is **subtraction**. **The three steps of re-coding conventions** (p0072): ① **keep the component** → ② **change scale and material** → ③ **change the semantics**; **failure condition**: doing only ② is "an enlarged tradition"; doing only ③ while losing the component turns it into spoken-drama scenery; **self-check**: after the change, does the actor's stage-business route still hold? **Two places that must be read together**: Luo says to 郭小庄 (Guo Xiaozhuang) face to face, "**what you have may not be the modernization of Chinese opera but a modernized Chinese opera**" (p0045), and at the same time says, "**I very much agree with the idea of 'modernizing national theater' that Guo Xiaozhuang raises**" (p0127)—**agreeing with the direction while denying the practice; quoting only one place will misread it.**

**Bitterness play vs tragedy** (p0085, p0096)—**read the failure condition first: this is a classification, not a judgment of superior and inferior, and it does not recommend either route; writing a bitterness play (苦戏, kǔxì — a play of suffering) is not writing badly; choosing either route requires a separate reason.** A misfortune whose specific responsible person can be named is a bitterness play; only one whose responsible person cannot be found is a tragedy; **self-check**: if you can say "who should be responsible for this misfortune" and that person could have acted otherwise, it is a bitterness play. This is the same thing as Zhang Geng and Guo Hancheng's **submerged conflict** (zhang-guo p0232) in two formulations; keep both original statements.

**Urban new opera ≠ breaking the line pattern** (luo-v2 item 5): in the same author's 《金龙与蜉蝣》 (Golden Dragon and Mayfly, fable form) line lengths vary from 3 to 14, while 《典妻》 (realist form) is uniformly regular seven- and ten-character lines—**talking about "urbanization" requires giving different advice for the fable and realist routes**; the "poetic sublimation" of the ending is also tied to the subject (item 6). **Aria capacity is calculated by opera genre, and recalculated for cross-regional performance** (luo-v6 p0182 + p0131): a conventional opera genre has two or three hundred lines of lyrics in a whole play; **a Huai opera passage can reach 180 lines**, but the same block of long arias was read as "long and dragging" in Taipei; the hard constraint: "add lyrics if you must, **but they cannot affect the development of the plot and the characters' personalities**." See [reference-yuju-difang.md](reference-yuju-difang.md) §8.

**Two head-on exchanges between Luo and Wei, set side by side without adjudication** (① the debate over "absurd / absurdist" in 《潘金莲》; ② pessimism and optimism in 2003–2004), original text in [reference.md](reference.md) §14. **Scope of application of 《朝阳沟》**: all technical items are retained, but **its thematic direction has expired** ("entering the village" → today it is "entering the city," luo-v6 p0055).

## 14 Through-Line Case: The Jewelry Purse (《锁麟囊》)

The only play with **all three layers: a creation note + peer criticism + a definitive performance text**; for the scene-by-scene structure table and aria table see `sw-chinese-opera-banqiang-cases`. **Version must be noted: all numbers come from the revised score edition** (the final scene's 【南梆子】 has been changed to 【二六】, and there is not a single 【南梆子】 in the whole play).

**Four rules of metrical-pattern layout** (suo §6; for the item-by-item data and role-type distribution see [reference.md](reference.md) §8): ① **Vocal systems are used to separate people and sections, not to separate high and low emotions**; **failure condition**: it holds only in plays where "two groups of people are apart for a long time." ② **【二六】 is used only twice in the whole play, in symmetrical positions**. ③ **Sanban and yaoban occupy forty percent; there are only 20 on-the-beat sections, all concentrated in four scenes**; self-check: if on-the-beat sections are scattered across more than five scenes, the emphasis has not been established. ④ **Change rhyme category scene by scene; the first and last both rhyme on 遥条**.

**Two disagreements kept side by side** (① Weng vs Fan: the passage where 薛家 (the Xue family) becomes suspicious at the end; ② Weng vs Cheng Yanqiu: the grand closing [大收煞, dàshōushā]), plus the counterexample in the final scene of 《白蛇传》, where "reunion can be given as a single image"—original text in [reference.md](reference.md) §13.

## 15 Diagnostic Checklist

1. Are you using the branch of skill this opera genre is best at, or the one it finds hardest? (0.2, 1)
2. Have you written down the system? Are the number of aria lines and spoken lines so close that the system cannot be seen? (1)
3. Summarize one guānmù per scene—is there a scene you cannot summarize? If you list the scenes in a row by "cold / hot," are there three consecutive cold or hot scenes? (2, 3)
4. Have the main characters been assigned role types or functional positions? Has any role-type taboo been violated? (4)
5. Are you writing conventional suites or individual convention units? Having divided into acts, are you still copying the entrance prelude and entrance poem? (5)
6. Is there an aria with an odd number of lines and no 【扫头】 marking? Has an aria of more than eight lines produced a variant? (6a)
7. If you delete all the duoju, are the regular paired lines still complete and fluent? Are all the duoju in the second-line position of an on-the-beat section? (6e)
8. Copy the final characters of the second lines into one column, **check them character by character against [reference-shisanzhe.md](reference-shisanzhe.md), not by ear**—are they in the same rhyme category? Have -ei／-ui slipped into 衣期? Do the final line and key line fall out of the rhyme? Is the rhyme table you are using the one for this opera genre? (6d)
9. Is the line before every aria an address term? Is there "not singing when one should" or "singing when one should not"? (7)
10. Duets: do all the active party's lines fall on second lines? Are the last three rounds closed? If there are two or more aside arias, ask whether this is "falling into the trap of the new tradition." (8)
11. Is one house style carried through to the end? Peking-opera style or Yue-opera style—after choosing, does the whole manuscript use only one (missing gong-and-drum marks in Peking-opera style, or metrical-pattern marks mixed into Yue-opera style, both count as not having chosen)? (9)
12. Are you following Peking-opera style or Yue-opera style? If you do not mark metrical patterns, is there rhythmic design after you list the character counts as a sequence? (9)
13. Can you write one sentence "why this one" beside every unusual metrical pattern? (10)
14. Does the adaptation belong to organizing, adapting, or transplanting? Have you taken the four soundings? Have you written down "the two or three scenes that must be kept"? (11)
15. Modern-dress play: do the three most intense moments each have a traditional convention to borrow? Do "(sings aside)" and "(sings offstage)" appear? Is the pronunciation and rhyme correct? (12a)
16. Does it still stand after reducing it to one table and two chairs? After deleting the chorus lyrics, does anyone still establish time, place, and commentary? (12c, 5)
17. Is the longest aria in the scene with the greatest value reversal (Lu Jun), or is the next scene all speech (Luo Huaizhen's 《典妻》 is the exception)? (6c)

## 16 Workflow and Training

1. **Determine the system**: all banqiang opera genres use this skill; Yuan zaju, chuanqi, and Kunqu use `sw-chinese-opera-qupai`; for Sichuan opera, mark the vocal system first. **Determine the atmosphere**: if you cannot say "what this opera genre's script reads like," do not take the job, or change the viewpoint character to bypass a technique this company cannot deliver (luo-v6 p0099).
2. **Fix the system and genre** (1); write down "the system of this play = ×××."
3. Go through the general stages: xìhé (戏核 — the core premise, `sw-premise-theme`) → qǐ chéng zhuǎn hé and the eight openings (Section 6 of `sw-story-structure`) → character; **at the character step, immediately fix the role type or functional position** (4).
4. **Fix the main line and arrange the scenes** (2); draft a guānmù for each scene; run through the law of alternating staging and hot-cold counterpoint (3); mark the two or three scenes with the greatest value reversal.
5. **Make the technical structure**: write only **one line** of technical idea per scene (fan p0008); **give arias only to key scenes**, one metrical-pattern suite per scene, following the lead performer's stamina curve (4, 14).
6. **Write the lyrics**: write the key line first → fix the rhyme → spread out the paired lines → variants and duoju → check rhyme, check even-numbered lines, check the four tones (6); place an address-term interface before every aria (7).
7. **Fix the house style and mark it** (9) → **run the diagnostic checklist in Section 15** → after handing it over: metrical patterns are a letter of intent; changing words necessarily changes the tune; what may and may not change—theme, framework, main character relationships, and basic dramatic plot do not change; rhymes may change, scenes may be added or cut (luo-v6 p0123).

**Training**: ① Make a list of all the arias in a traditional play, mark the metrical pattern, line count, and rhyme category, draw two columns "on-the-beat / free-rhythm," and compare with the four rules in Section 14. ② Write the same content twice, once in Peking-opera style and once in Yue-opera style, and compare what you lost.
