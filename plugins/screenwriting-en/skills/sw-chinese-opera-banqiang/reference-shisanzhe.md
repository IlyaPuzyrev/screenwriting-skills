# Character-by-Character Table of the Thirteen Rhyme Categories (Minimal Usable Edition)

> Purpose: when writing aria lyrics, copy the final characters of the second lines into one column and **check them character by character against this table** to determine the rhyme category, not by feel (SKILL.md Section 15, item 8). For the rhyme-category names, the three differences between the old thirteen categories (十三辙, shísān zhé) and Northern speech, entering-tone characters, and stage-read characters, see [reference.md](reference.md) §3; this table solves only "which rhyme category does this character belong to, and is it level or oblique?" **This table does not come from the 25 books in this batch**; its sources and method are as follows, and every step can be checked; it is a minimal usable edition, not a rhyme dictionary.

## Sources and Method

1. **Syllable assignment**: Sohu's "Table of 402 Syllables in Peking Opera's Thirteen Rhyme Categories" (盛世梨园 Shengshi Liyuan, 2019-12-06, noted "reprinted from 京剧连载 Peking Opera Serial")—13 rhyme categories, 402 syllables, one example character per syllable. Four typos on the original page have been corrected: in the 姑苏 (gūsū) category `bu` appears twice (should be `du`), in 油求 (yóuqiú) `mou` appears twice, in 言前 (yánqián) `uan` should be `ruan`, in 中东 (zhōngdōng) `chang` should be `cheng`. That table assigns syllables by Mandarin finals, i.e., the "new thirteen categories."
2. **Characters**: Chinese characters appearing more than 8 times in the full text of the 24 source books in this batch (OCR text, including five scripts and scores), assigned to the above table's rhyme categories by the finals of their Mandarin pronunciation (pypinyin 0.55), sorted by corpus frequency; for each rhyme category, take the top 42 level-tone and top 42 oblique-tone characters; traditional characters are removed; neutral-tone characters (的 de, 了 le, 着 zhe, 呢 ne) are not listed. **The characters at the front are the most common in the corpus, not the ones most suitable for rhyming.**
3. **Level and oblique tones**: according to the four Mandarin tones, yin level and yang level = level; rising and departing = oblique. The rule that **second lines land on level tones and first lines land on oblique tones** is in SKILL.md 6a. Ancient entering-tone characters have been distributed into the four tones; the six characters still read as stage-read characters in Peking opera (学 xué, 约 yuē, 却 què, 北 běi, 贼 zéi, 黑 hēi) are marked △—§3.2 item 2 only says Peking opera "does not distribute them"; the original book does not say which rhyme category they belong to; this table places them in their Mandarin positions and only adds a mark.
4. **Peking-opera exceptions layered on** (reference.md §3.2): ① **庚青** (-eng／-ing) belongs to **人辰** in Peking opera's old thirteen categories (恒 héng read hén, 成 chéng read chén, 生 shēng read shén, 兵 bīng read bīn), while according to the new Mandarin thirteen categories it belongs to **中东**—this table **lists it on a separate row**, mixing it into neither side; when writing a play, choose one, keep it consistent throughout the play, and note on the aria table whether the old or new categories are used. ② **居鱼** (the group zhu, chu, shu, ru) was formerly read as **一七** (珠 zhū, 书 shū, 如 rú merged into 衣期 by the original rhyme), while the new categories place it in 姑苏—also listed on a separate row.
5. **Dual rhymes**: mark only characters **whose common colloquial second reading falls into another rhyme category** (得 de／děi, 谁 shuí／shéi, 血 xuè／xiě, 说 shuō／shuì ...); dictionary-style exhaustive readings are not marked. If two readings of the same character fall in the same rhyme category (长 cháng／zhǎng), do not mark.

## The Two Most Easily Confused Groups

1. **-ei／-ui belong to 灰堆 (huīduī), not 一七 (yīqī, also called 衣期)**: 碑, 悲, 杯, 辈, 泪, 贵, 内, 类, 水, 谁, 回, 非, 飞, 追, 归, 堆, 灰, 雷, 美, 味, 位, 备, 配, 退, 罪, 岁, 醉. 一七 contains only -i, -ü, -er and zhi／chi／shi／ri／zi／ci／si. In a comparison experiment, the final line and key line of a 衣期-rhyme aria landed on the character "碑," and the rhyme of the whole passage was misjudged because of this one character, while the diagnostic checklist judged it "pass" by feel.
2. **-eng／-ing (庚青)**: the old thirteen categories assign it to 人辰, the new categories to 中东, see the separate row above. Whichever side you choose, **you cannot have one passage follow the old and another the new in the same play**.

## How to Use It (Self-Check Actions)

- After finishing an aria: copy the final characters of the second lines into a column → find each character's row in this table → only if the row names are all the same is the rhyme consistent to the end; if one differs, the rhyme has broken, and **a break in the final line or key line hurts more than one in the middle**, because it is the closure of the whole passage.
- If a final character is in the "oblique tone" cell but it is a second line: change the character, or confirm that this passage intentionally uses an oblique-tone rhyme (see the exception in 6a).
- If a final character has "dual rhyme": write down which reading it is sung with and give it to the fiddler and actor.
- If a final character is not in the table: this table includes only high-frequency corpus characters; not being in the table does not mean it does not rhyme; look up its category by final in the second column "Finals," then determine level or oblique by Mandarin tone.
- For Henan opera, Sichuan opera, or Yue opera: this table does not apply (the three rhyme tables are not interchangeable, SKILL.md 6d); see [reference-yuju-difang.md](reference-yuju-difang.md) §4, §7.

> Material gaps: ① this table is a mechanical assignment by Mandarin finals plus three Peking-opera exceptions, **with no rhyme dictionary as documentary evidence**; ② for the 居鱼 group, 庚青 group, and the six entering-tone characters, "which rhyme category in Peking opera" is only as far as liu/04 p0169–0171 says; ③ the characters come from a mixed corpus of theory books and scripts, and the suitability of the rhymes has not been manually screened.
