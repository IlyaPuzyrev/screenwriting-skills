# Ozu's Six Screenplays: Scene-by-Scene Structural Tables and Original-Text Examples

> Use together with SKILL.md. Source: 《小津安二郎剧本集》 (The Ozu Yasujiro Screenplay Collection), Chinese translation by 吴菲 (Wu Fei); scene numbers are the original scene numbers in the screenplays.

## I. Late Spring (《晚春》, 1949, 103 scenes)

**Logline:** Shukichi (56), a widowed professor in Kamakura, lies that he will remarry so that his daughter Noriko (27), who refuses to marry, will leave him; after anger and loss, Noriko accepts a match and marries, and her father spends the wedding night alone peeling an apple.

| Passage | Scenes | Content | Function |
|---|---|---|---|
| Prologue | 1–8 | Kita-Kamakura Station, a tea ceremony, sunlight in the garden, a bush warbler | Pillow shots establish the season and the "stillness"; Akiko enters the frame for the first time (no dialogue) |
| Set-up | 9–18 | Shukichi and Hattori write; Noriko comes home; father and daughter ride the train together | "More tea, more tea" establishes the ease and dependence of their relationship |
| False lead A | 21–26 | A chance meeting with Onodera in Ginza → the restaurant Takigawa → "remarriage is dirty" | Plant the mine: Noriko herself rejects "remarriage" |
| False lead B | 27–30 | Cycling with Hattori; "the lotus-root pickle that stays connected by its fibers" | Make the audience think Hattori is the match |
| First push | 31–37 | The aunt urges Father to ask Noriko what she thinks of Hattori → "Hattori is getting married" | The false lead collapses; comic rhythm |
| Blank | 38–42 | At the cafe, refunding the tickets → empty theater seats → Noriko walks alone in Marunouchi | Ellipsis: express loneliness through empty seats |
| The foil enters | 43–48 | Aya visits: "returned goods"; class-reunion gossip | "I'm not getting married" said aloud for the first time |
| Second push | 49–52 | At the aunt's house: a match is proposed → refused → the aunt suggests Akiko as a second wife for Father | Midpoint: Noriko "I suppose it's all right, if Father doesn't mind" (coldly) |
| Cold war | 53–57 | Noriko ignores her father; "Going shopping..." and she leaves forlornly | Almost no dialogue |
| Corroboration | 58–61 | The neighbor receives a wedding thank-you gift from Hattori | A third-party viewpoint confirms it |
| Before the climax | 62–66 | At the Noh theater: Father nods to Akiko; Noriko grows more and more unhappy | Wordless climax |
| Explosion | 67–68 | At Aya's home: the cream cake quarrel | Displacement |
| Showdown | 69–73 | "If someone were to take care of Father" → interrogation → "Mm" → she rushes upstairs crying | The lie lands |
| Relaxation | 74 | At Hachimangu, the aunt picks up a wallet: "This marriage is sure to happen" | Comic relief + good omen |
| Decision | 75–82 | Aya urges marriage → agreement → Father asks "You're not marrying because you've given up?" | The decision is reluctant |
| Farewell trip | 83–93 | Kyoto: apology to Onodera; Ryoanji, "raising a daughter is really pointless"; night talk at the inn → the long monologue | The film's only long monologue |
| Epilogue | 94–103 | Wedding day before the mirror → kneeling farewell → empty room → Aya's kiss and "beautiful lie" → apple peel breaks → night sea | The truth is exposed by a third party |

### Example A: Three "Mm"s (scene 71)
```
Noriko: Then Father will be like Uncle Onodera...
Shukichi: (vaguely) Mm...
Noriko: Remarry?
Shukichi: Mm...
Noriko: (with growing sharpness) You will remarry, won't you?
Shukichi: Mm.
Noriko: To the person from today?
Shukichi: Mm.
Noriko: Really? ... Really?
Shukichi: Mm.
Noriko: ...... (gradually unable to bear it)
Suddenly stands up and leaves as if fleeing.
```

### Example B: The Wordless Climax (scene 62, the Noh theater)
```
Shukichi looks at the libretto, then suddenly nods to someone across the hall.
Noriko notices and looks that way—
It is Akiko Miwa, seated opposite.
So Noriko bows too.
Akiko returns an elegant, silent bow.
Shukichi goes back to the libretto and the stage. Noriko is very conscious of the exchange between her father and Akiko, and her eyes keep turning toward Akiko.
The poised, beautiful Akiko watches the stage.
Though Father does not look toward Akiko again, and Akiko does not look back at him, Noriko feels an uneasiness she cannot settle.
She looks more and more displeased.
```

### Example C: The Displaced Quarrel (scene 68)
```
Noriko, angry, sets down the plate of cake she has picked up and not touched—"Bang!"
Aya: Not eating?
Noriko: I don't want it!
Aya: Give it to me, then!
Noriko: I just don't want it!
Aya: I tell you, it's delicious!
Noriko: Enough!
Aya: What is this? For such a little thing! I made it myself—eat it!
Noriko: I won't!
Aya: Eat it! Even if you don't want to, I'll make you eat it!
Noriko: I just won't!
Aya: What's wrong with you! Fine, don't eat it!
Noriko: (speechless with anger) ......
Aya: See, I told you—you should just get married soon.
```

### Example D: The Only Long Monologue (scene 93, excerpt)
```
Shukichi: —I'm fifty-six now; my life is nearly over, but yours is just beginning. ... That is the law of human life.
... Even marriage may not be happy from the start. ... Happiness won't wait for you; you have to make it yourselves. Marriage itself is not happiness—happiness exists in the process of a newly married couple building a new life together. ... Your mother was not happy from the start either. ... I saw her crying in a corner of the kitchen more than once. But your mother held on—
```

### Example E: The Ending (scenes 100–103)
```
Aya: I couldn't tell such a beautiful lie.
Shukichi: What?
Aya: I couldn't tell such a beautiful lie.
Shukichi: Ha ha ha—(then, forlornly) There's no help for it. This is the first time in my life I've told such a big lie...
......
Shukichi alone, lonely, takes off his formal jacket and hangs it on the hook by the door. He pats the dust off it, then walks weakly to the chair and sits down. Suddenly he notices the apple on the table, picks it up, and peels it. The peel breaks before it gets very long. Shukichi sits motionless.

103　The sea at night
Great slow waves swell, rush to the shore, and recede...
```

## II. Early Summer (《麦秋》, 1951, 145 scenes)

**Logline:** In the three-generation Mamiya household in Kita-Kamakura, 28-year-old Noriko is introduced by a director to an "excellent match"; the whole family mobilizes to investigate and persuade her, but after the widowed neighbor Tami says, "How I wish you could be my daughter-in-law," Noriko suddenly decides to marry Kenkichi, a doctor with a child who is about to leave for Akita. The family is stunned, then accepts; they take a group photo, and then disperse.

| Passage | Scenes | Content | Function |
|---|---|---|---|
| Prologue | 1–19 | Early morning, the whole family eats breakfast and leaves separately; Grandfather trims his grandson's nails | One breakfast establishes three generations and seven people |
| The world | 20–25 | The director's office; Aya visits; brother, sister, and sister-in-law eat tempura | Koichi's "urges people to drink and then won't let them" is summarized to his face by his sister |
| The great-uncle's visit | 26–45 | Deaf Mokichi; the child shouts "idiot"; the Great Buddha at Hase; Kabuki | Deafness = the family member who cannot hear |
| Matchmaking | 40–46 | Takako and her husband quarrel over carrots; the director proposes a match and gives a golf photo | Married quarrel + unmarried matchmaking |
| Investigation | 47–58 | Aya's mother "announces good news"; Koichi and his wife talk by the bed; Shukichi "we must hurry" | The marriage is pushed forward while Noriko is absent |
| After the wedding banquet | 59–61 | The "happy tribe" argument; Koichi peeks from behind the paper door three times | The brother hides behind the door and directs his sister-in-law |
| Credit check | 62–64 | The credit agency has visited; Shoji "he won't come back" | From comedy it sinks into war trauma |
| Sunday | 65–75 | Carp streamers; the child lays tracks; the old couple watch a balloon with a broken string | Midpoint pillow shot: "Maybe this is the best time our family will ever have" |
| Subplot explosion | 76–105 | The cake costs 900 yen; the "tracks" are actually bread → kicks the bread → gets spanked → runs away → is found | The adults' gift is not what the child wants |
| Turning-point trigger | 106–108 | Kenkichi tells his mother he is going to Akita | Time pressure |
| Decision | 109–117 | At the Nikolai Cathedral cafe, memories of Shoji, the letter with the ear of wheat; Tami's idle wish → "If someone like me would do" | The instant of decision, Kenkichi absent |
| Family reaction | 118–121 | Late-night family meeting "Definitely not?" "No."; the parents cannot manage the stairs; Noriko applies lipstick before the mirror | Reaction queue |
| Digestion | 122–135 | Tami comes to the office to confirm; Shige complains; Shukichi sighs at the railway crossing; Aya's Akita dialect; by the sea the sisters-in-law have a "thrift contest" | "The scissors are right in front of you" |
| Farewell | 136–140 | Family group photo + parents alone; a sukiyaki farewell dinner; Noriko runs upstairs and cries | The group photo = the family's last completeness |
| Epilogue | 141–145 | The barley fields of Yamato; the old couple watch another family's wedding | The protagonist is absent; close with someone else's wedding |

### Example A: The Instant of Decision (scene 113)
```
Tami: Nothing, ha ha, it was just my own wishful thinking. I thought how nice it would be if a girl like you could be Kenkichi's wife!
Noriko: Oh.
Tami: I'm sorry—it was just a daydream of mine... Don't be angry.
Noriko: Really? Auntie—
Tami: What is it?
Noriko: Is that really what you think of me?
Tami: I'm sorry, so please don't be angry.
Noriko: Auntie, would someone like me, who can't even get married, do?
Tami: Huh? (as if doubting her own ears)
Noriko: If someone like me would do...
Tami: (involuntarily) Really? (her voice grows louder too)
Noriko: Mm.
```

### Example B: The Person Concerned Confirms It Afterward (scene 117)
```
Tami: Listen, Noriko says she'll come! She says she'll come to our house!
Kenkichi plops down; Tami sits down too—
Kenkichi: Come where—
Tami: To our house!
Kenkichi: To do what—
Tami: Not "to do what"! To you! To be your wife!
Kenkichi: My wife?
Tami: Yes! How happy I am! Thank goodness...
Kenkichi: ...... (stunned)
......
Kenkichi: (softly) I'm happy.
Tami: Then you could look a little happier. Be happy.—You strange boy...
```

### Example C: The Minimal Syllables at the Family Meeting (scene 119)
```
Koichi: Definitely not? You won't end up thinking you've made a mistake?
Noriko: No.
Koichi: Really not?
Noriko: ......
Koichi: Definitely not?
Noriko: No.
Then everyone falls silent. An awkward atmosphere—
Shige: Dad, aren't you cold?
Shukichi: Mmm...
Shige: Go and rest?
Shukichi: Mmm...
Shige: Go and rest?
Shukichi: Mmm... let's sleep...
```

### Example D: The Theme in a Pillow Shot (scene 75)
```
Shukichi: How quickly time passes... Koichi married, the grandchild was born, and now Noriko's turn. Maybe this is the happiest time of all.
Shige: Perhaps... but there is still more to come...
Shukichi: I won't think about it. People's greed has no end.—Ah, what a clear Sunday.
Shige: Oh, look—(pointing at the sky over there)
Shukichi: Hm? (looks up)
A balloon with a broken string flies up into the sky.
Shukichi: Somewhere, there must be a child crying because he let go of a balloon by mistake... Didn't Koichi have a time like that too...
```

### Example E: Ensemble Catchphrase Relay (scene 40)
```
Aya: Husbands are all like that. That's why we don't marry. Right?
Noriko: Right. You think so, right?
Takako: Nonsense! You've never even tried it!
Aya: Tried it?
Takako: You don't know until you're married!
Aya: If you only understand after you're married, it's too late! Right?
Noriko: Right?
Takako: I'm going home!
Aya: Of course you should go home. Right?
Noriko: Exactly. What else? Right?
Aya: After all, you ate the carrots, right?
Takako: I'm not leaving!
```

### Example F: The Ending (scenes 142–145)
```
Shukichi: (suddenly looking out the window, to Shige) Oh, look—someone's marrying off a daughter.
......
Shige: —I wonder where she'll go...
Shukichi: Mmm...
Shige: How is Noriko doing now...
Shukichi: Mmm... Everyone has gone their separate ways... but we're among the lucky ones...
Shige: ...So much has happened... all these years...
Shukichi: Mmm... If you're never satisfied, there's no end to it...
Shige: Yes... we really are happy...
Shukichi: Mmm...
145　Wheat field
A June breeze passes gently over the ripening ears of wheat—
```

## III. Tokyo Story (《东京物语》, 1953, 176 scenes)

**Logline:** Shukichi (70) and Tomi (67), an elderly couple in Onomichi, go to Tokyo to visit their children, are passed from one busy child to another and sent to Atami; only Noriko, the widow of their second son killed in the war, genuinely hosts them. On the way home Tomi falls ill and dies soon after returning; the children rush in for the funeral and rush away again, and only Noriko stays. The father gives her his late wife's watch and urges her to remarry.

| Passage | Scenes | Content | Function |
|---|---|---|---|
| Prologue | 1–6 | Onomichi morning market; looking for an air pillow; the neighbor "How lucky you are" | From the outside, they look very lucky |
| Arrival | 7–35 | Koichi's home: the desk is moved into the corridor; Shige brings crackers; "I thought it would be a bit livelier" | The son's home is not what they imagined |
| The wasted Sunday | 36–52 | Koichi goes out on calls; Atsushi loses his temper; "By the time you're a doctor, Grandma won't be here" | The child's anger speaks for the old people |
| Shige's home | 53–58 | Kurazo buys snacks and is told it's too expensive; Shige phones Noriko to ask a favor | The parents are "outsourced" |
| Noriko's day | 59–73 | Sightseeing bus; borrowed sake and a flask at the apartment; Shoji's photo; takeout | The only sincere hospitality |
| Atami | 74–91 | The siblings chip in 3,000 yen; mahjong, singing, insomnia; "This is a place for young people"; Tomi sways | Filial piety = sending them away; the illness is foreshadowed |
| Homeless | 92–94 | They stumble into a lecture meeting; "I've become homeless after all" (smiling); chewing peanuts in Ueno Park | Midpoint |
| Two night lines | 95–108 | Father's drinking party: Numata "calls a section chief a department head," "the selfishness of parents"; Mother at Noriko's: "I've decided not to grow any older"; Father passes out drunk and is sent back to the hair salon | Parallel montage |
| Leaving Tokyo | 109–112 | Noriko gives them spending money; "If anything happens, you don't need to rush back" | The death omen is cut off with a laugh |
| Osaka | 113–116 | Keizo "Can't put a quilt over a grave, ha ha ha"; "We're among the lucky ones" | The third "lucky" |
| Critical illness | 117–129 | Letter arrives → telegram arrives → "Do we bring mourning clothes?" | Shige's pragmatism |
| Deathbed | 130–143 | Shukichi fans himself "She'll be all right..."; Koichi "If she lasts till dawn, that's good"; "So that's the end" | The doctor son pronounces his mother's death |
| Dawn | 144 | Empty platform, deserted street, small waves | Death in a pillow shot |
| Funeral | 145–157 | Shige asks for a sash and hemp cloth; on the cliff "What a beautiful morning"; "It would be better if Father went first" | Mourning and calculation at the same table |
| Epilogue | 158–176 | Kyoko "They're too selfish" / Noriko "Everyone becomes like that"; the watch; listening to it on the train; "The days grow longer" | The summation is given to Noriko; return to the neighbor |

### Example A: Homeless (scenes 93–94)
```
Shukichi: It wouldn't be good to go and trouble Koichi again...
Tomi: That's true—shall we ask Noriko to put us up for the night?
Shukichi: No, there isn't room for both of us there. You go alone...
Tomi: And you?
Shukichi: I thought I'd call on Mr. Hattori. He should be able to put me up for the night—well, let's go.
Tomi: All right.
Shukichi: (smiling) —I've become homeless after all...
Tomi nods, smiling too.
......
Shukichi: Tokyo really is huge.
Tomi: It is. If you got lost in a place like this, you might never find your way again.
```

### Example B: The Father's Truth, Told to an Outsider (scene 101)
```
Shukichi: —But you know, Mr. Numata, before I came this time I still had some hope for my son. I never thought he'd be just a doctor in a little clinic in the suburbs. I understand how you feel. Just as you say, I'm dissatisfied too. But then again, Mr. Numata, this is the selfishness of parents everywhere; selfishness has no end. So you have to let it go. That's how I feel, anyway.
Numata: Is that how you feel?
Shukichi: That's how I feel.
Numata: So you too...
```

### Example C: The Mother and Noriko's Night Talk (scene 103)
```
Tomi: But even if things are like this now, as you get older, you'll still be lonely alone.
Noriko: It's all right. I've decided not to grow any older.
Tomi: (moved to tears) You... you really are a good person...
Noriko: (faintly) Let's sleep.
She gets up, turns off the light, and slips under the quilt. Before long, tears begin to well in Noriko's eyes.
```

### Example D: The Pillow Shot of Death (scenes 143–145)
```
143　Inner room
Shukichi silently comes to Tomi's pillow and sits down. He gazes sadly at her sleeping face and blinks from time to time.

144　Dawn
Night in Onomichi gives way to daybreak—the eastern sky is bright and dazzling; the sun is about to rise.
An empty platform—
A street with no pedestrians—
Small waves lapping the stone seawall—

145　The Hirayama house
A white cloth is laid over Tomi's face.
```

### Example E: On the Cliff (scene 149)
```
Noriko: Father—
Shukichi: (turning) Ah...
Noriko: Keizo is here.
Shukichi: Oh, is he... (with feeling) Ah, what a beautiful morning.
Noriko: ...... (a pang of grief; she lowers her head)
Shukichi: —It will surely be hot today...
He says it calmly and walks toward the house. Noriko follows silently, head lowered.
```

### Example F: Shige's Pragmatism (scene 157)
```
Shige: —But, how shall I put it. It may not be nice to say, but if one of the two old people has to go first, it would be better if Father went first.
Koichi: Mm.
Shige: As things are now, once Kyoko marries, Father alone will be a problem.
Koichi: Yes.
Shige: If it were Mother, she could come to Tokyo, and it would be easier to manage—oh, Kyoko, doesn't Mother have a summer kimono sash? The mouse-gray one with the dewdrop-and-grass pattern...
Kyoko: Yes.
Shige: I'd like to keep that as a memento. Is that all right, Brother—
```

### Example G: The Theme Conversation at the End (scene 162)
```
Kyoko: Yes. Mother has just died, and they immediately talk about wanting things as keepsakes. When I think of how Mother must have felt, I feel terribly sad. Even strangers would be kinder. I don't think parents and children should be like that.
Noriko: But Kyoko, when I was your age, I thought that too. But once children grow up, they must leave their parents. ... Everyone thinks his own life is the most important.
Kyoko: Is that so? But I don't want to become like that. If that's how it is, then parents and children are a pretty empty thing.
Noriko: Yes. But doesn't everyone become like that? Gradually.
Kyoko: Will you become like that too, Sister?
Noriko: Mm. I don't want to, but I will.
Kyoko: What a world...
Noriko: Yes... nothing but tiresome things...
```

### Example H: The Watch (scene 164)
```
Noriko: That's just selfishness. I couldn't say these things to Mother either.
Shukichi: —Don't worry about it. It's all right—you are a good person, and honest...
Noriko: Not at all.
Shukichi: No...
He stands up and takes a woman's watch from the drawer of the Buddhist altar.
Shukichi: This is your mother's watch—the style is out of fashion now, but she began wearing it when she was about your age. Please take it as a keepsake.
......
Shukichi: —It's strange. Compared with my own children, it's you, who should count as an outsider, who have been so good to us... Thank you.
He bows his head, dejected.
```

## IV. Floating Weeds (《浮草》, 1959, 121 scenes)

**Logline:** A traveling theater troupe returns to a small southern fishing port. The troupe leader Komajuro (58) secretly visits his illegitimate son Kiyoshi (21); the boy's mother, Oyoshi, runs a noodle shop and has let her son believe Komajuro is his "uncle." When his mistress Sumiko (34) finds out, she pays the young actress Kayo to seduce Kiyoshi. The troupe goes bankrupt; Kiyoshi and Kayo elope and return; Oyoshi reveals the parentage; Kiyoshi rejects his father; Komajuro leaves alone, and at the station he falls back in with Sumiko.

| Passage | Scenes | Content | Function |
|---|---|---|---|
| Prologue | 1–8 | Lighthouse, idle talk in the waiting room, the exhausted troupe in the boat | Strangers introduce the troupe |
| Street promotion | 9–17 | Street publicity; each of the clowns fixes on a woman | Setting up the subplot |
| Backstage | 18–22 | The theater owner brings sake; Sumiko and Kayo are introduced | Character registration |
| Secret | 23–29 | Turning into "Tsuruya"; reunion with Oyoshi after twelve years; arranging to go fishing | The secret is revealed to the audience |
| Opening night | 30–40 | The play "Kokutei Chūji"; peeking at the audience from behind the curtain | Play-within-the-play |
| Subplot one | 41–43 | Sentaro "feels a chill"; Yatakura is forced to shave | Comic release |
| Father and son | 44 | Fishing at the pier: "Maruhashi Chūya has no social significance" | The son does not know this is his father |
| Suspicion | 45–48 | Seen in the barbershop mirror; Sumiko interrogates | Seen through a mirror |
| Probing | 49–64 | On the dunes, pressuring Sensho; the lattice window identifies Oyoshi; the dressing room on a rainy night | The secret leaks layer by layer |
| Showdown | 65–74 | Interrupted during a game of go; in the rain, "a prostitute from a mountain hot spring" → "ties severed" | The film's longest quarrel |
| Scheme | 75–79 | Only fourteen or fifteen people left in the audience; 1,000 yen to "go seduce him" | The instrument of revenge is a third party |
| Fall | 80–91 | A kiss in the empty theater, paper snow; Kayo confesses | The tool becomes a subject |
| Collapse | 92–99 | Oyoshi "Do you think I'm jealous?"; hits Kayo → hits Sumiko; the three clowns discuss stealing money | Violence concentrated |
| Breakup | 100–104 | Selling costumes; Kichinosuke runs off with the money; farewell banquet; a pear falls to the floor | The moralist is the thief |
| Revelation | 105–117 | Elopement and return; hits Kiyoshi → Kiyoshi hits back → "He's your father!" → "I don't want a father!" | Exposure brings renunciation |
| Epilogue | 118–121 | Two matchsticks at the station; "Two tickets to Kuwana"; eating bento together on the night train | Reconciliation with almost no words |

### Example A: The Secret Revealed to the Audience (scene 27)
```
Komajuro: Tell me... how does Kiyoshi see me?
Oyoshi: ......
Komajuro: Does he still think his father is dead? Does he really think I'm your brother?
Oyoshi lowers her eyes and does not answer. She goes to fetch the sake flask.
Oyoshi: (taking up the flask) Here.
Komajuro: Oh. (takes the cup)
Oyoshi: Tell me—does it make you feel bad?
Komajuro: Why?
Oyoshi: About Kiyoshi.
Komajuro: There's no help for it, when you put it that way. Better to have no father at all than a father who's an actor.
```

### Example B: The Father-Son Quarrel over Values (scene 44)
```
Kiyoshi: But Maruhashi Chūya and the like have no social significance at all!
Komajuro: What is social significance?
Kiyoshi: It's the connection with today's society.
Komajuro: What nonsense. Maruhashi Chūya was a man of long ago!
Kiyoshi: That's why I say you're no good, Uncle—so outdated...
Komajuro: Ha, listen to you! All nonsense. Outdated isn't so bad—the audience enjoys it.
Kiyoshi: Is it enough if the audience enjoys it?
Komajuro: Enough. Don't talk about acting anymore.—(raises his fishing rod) Look, look, the bait's been eaten again, hasn't it?
```

### Example C: Seen Through a Mirror (scene 48)
```
Yatakura: Didn't the boss go fishing?
Sumiko: (doubtfully) Fishing?
Yatakura: Oh, when I was shaving at the barbershop, I saw him walking past with a young man, carrying fishing rods.
Sumiko: A young man?
Yatakura: You didn't know? They say he's from the post office—I saw it in the mirror.
```

### Example D: The Three-Stage Escalation of the Quarrel in the Rain (scene 74, excerpt)
```
Sumiko: Have you forgotten? That time in Okaya! Do you remember who came to your rescue? Wasn't it the same in Toyokawa? Whenever you were in trouble you came begging, and who was it who groveled to me!
......
Komajuro: What are you talking about! Nonsense! Who do you think you are! Think about the past! Weren't you a prostitute at a mountain hot spring! You came clinging to me and wouldn't leave, and I finally taught you enough to stand on your own.
......
Komajuro: My son is not the same breed as you people. A different breed! Remember that! You fool!
Rain pours violently down the drainpipe.
```

### Example E: The Tool's Confession (scene 90)
```
Kayo: (turning her face away sadly) I'm not a good girl. (turns back to look at Kiyoshi, eyes full of tears) I'm a worthless woman.
Kiyoshi: What are you talking about!
Kayo: I meant to deceive you at first.
Kiyoshi: —?
Kayo: I didn't know you at all. Big Sister asked me to come and see you... I thought it would be enough if I just hooked you.
Kiyoshi: That doesn't matter to me! I don't care what you thought at first! What do you think now? You, yourself!
```

### Example F: The Parentage Revealed (scene 115)
```
Oyoshi: (to Kiyoshi) Son, do you know who this man is... He's your father... your own father! How can you treat him like this!
Kiyoshi looks at Komajuro in shock.
Kiyoshi: —So that's it, after all... I wondered if it might be...
Komajuro, not knowing what to say, gives him a faint, bitter smile.
......
Kiyoshi: Why do you suddenly appear now, after all this time! (to Oyoshi) Mother, why tell me this now, after all this time! I don't need a father like this! Tell him to get out! Tell him to get out! Get out!
......
Komajuro: (sighing) The boy is right too... He's right—to appear out of nowhere and say "this is your father," of course it doesn't work.
```

### Example G: The Matchsticks (scene 119)
```
Komajuro sits down awkwardly, puts a cigarette in his mouth, but cannot find a match; he searches up and down. Sumiko silently gets up, comes over, strikes a match, and hands it to him. Komajuro gives her a surprised look, then goes on searching.
Sumiko throws away the nearly spent match and strikes a second one. Komajuro leans in to light his cigarette; Sumiko sits down beside him.
Sumiko: Boss, where are you going?
Komajuro smokes without a word.
Sumiko: (taking out a cigarette) Borrow a light.
She takes Komajuro's cigarette.
......
Komajuro: —Let's take a gamble...
Sumiko: Hm?
```

## V. Late Autumn (《秋日和》, 1960, 110 scenes)

**Logline:** At the seventh anniversary of their late friend's death, three old classmates (Mamiya, Taguchi, Hirayama) decide to arrange a match for his daughter Ayako (24). Ayako refuses on the grounds that she cannot leave her mother, so the three devise a plan to marry off the mother, Akiko (45), first, and put the widower Hirayama forward. The rumor reaches Ayako and mother and daughter fall out. In the end, Akiko lies to her daughter that she wants to remarry, sends her off, and stays alone in the apartment.

**Mirroring Late Spring:** the parent who stays, father → mother; the lie "Father will remarry" → "friends invent a remarriage for the mother / the mother herself says she does not want to remarry"; one driver → a three-man comic conspiracy; the suitor never appears → appears twice; the daughter's friend, a divorced stenographer → the sushi restaurant daughter; the ending peels an apple → folds a haori (a Japanese formal jacket); 原节子 (Setsuko Hara) plays the daughter → plays the mother.

| Passage | Scenes | Content | Function |
|---|---|---|---|
| Prologue | 1–5 | Idle talk at the steakhouse on the anniversary; a tea-leaf stem stands upright | Death is handled with a joke |
| The decision | 6–9 | The three question Ayako in person; after she leaves, they talk about "liking her mother better," a drugstore memory | Motive = secret crush on their late friend's wife |
| The wives | 10–14 | Taguchi goes home; his wife already knows about the "massage cream" | The wives know all the secrets |
| Matchmaking | 15–28 | Over eel rice, Goto is proposed; Akiko gives a pipe; Ayako "I'll refuse" | Her reason: "Mother and I are happy together" |
| Dressmaking school | 29–34 | A photo match "his nose is a little crooked" | The third proposal is refused |
| Confrontation | 35–38 | Mamiya calls Goto over: "The lady who refused you is this one" | Embarrassment creates a meeting |
| Mother-daughter night | 39–44 | Pork cutlet shop; cod roe; Shūzenji carp; "mothers and daughters are like this" | The warmest point |
| Mountain and rooftop | 45–53 | Sugiyama sees through it; on the roof, Shigeko does not wave her bouquet | Friendship "lasts until marriage" |

## VI. An Autumn Afternoon (《秋刀鱼之味》, 1962, 98 scenes, Ozu's last film)

**Logline:** The widower Hirayama (57) lives with his daughter Michiko (24) and younger son Kazuo. His old classmate Kawai urges him to marry off his daughter; at a class reunion he meets his old teacher "Hyotan" and sees that the teacher, for "convenience," kept his daughter at home, and that father and daughter now live miserably in a little noodle shop. Hirayama resolves to marry off his daughter. The man Michiko likes, Miura, already has someone; Michiko accepts an arranged match and marries. On the wedding night Hirayama goes alone to a bar and listens to the Warship March, then goes home humming "all alone."
