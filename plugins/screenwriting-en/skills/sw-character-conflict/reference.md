## I. Egri's Three-Dimensional Character Framework Checklist

| Dimension | Items |
|---|---|
| Physiology | Sex, age, height and weight, hair color, eye color, skin color, posture, appearance, defects, heredity |
| Sociology | Class, occupation (including attitude toward the union/colleagues), education (favorite and worst subjects), family life (parents' situation, marriage), religion, race and nationality, community status, political affiliation, entertainment and reading |
| Psychology | Sex life and moral standards, **personal premise and goal**, frustrations and major disappointments, temperament, attitude toward life, complexes, introversion/extroversion, abilities, qualities |

**The Eileen case** (a girl from a respectable family becomes a prostitute): nineteen, pretty, sensitive to cold; the whole family fears tuberculosis, but she believes she will die young and means to enjoy herself; her father runs a middle-class grocery store; she refuses housework, loves reading historical romances; her mother was once abandoned, attempted suicide, then married a man she did not love; the household keeps her under strict supervision; her IQ is below normal; she is admired as a dancer and wants to go to New York; she has had sex with Jimmy and is actually pleased when he refuses to marry her; "as long as you take care of yourself, any sexual relationship is all right"; she is quick-tempered and boasts of her vindictiveness, yet when her mother is ill she stays until she recovers; she is superstitious about the number 13. Thesis = her parents want her to marry well; antithesis = she wants to be a dancer; synthesis = she runs away and ends up on the street. **Why it must be a prostitute**: a hunchbacked, humble, or clever girl would not take this road; she is conceited, mature, sexually experienced, and too ashamed of failure to return to a small town — "as a playwright, your task is to eliminate the other possibilities."

**Checklist of character components** (for assembling a character): Ruthless = a pressing urgency, no way back, fixed determination, ambition, desperation, being trapped, fear of failure, intense emotion, obsession, egocentrism, single-mindedness, desire for revenge, opportunism, greed; Unambitious = wishful thinking, lack of drive, laziness, no goal; Hot-tempered = inconsiderateness, irritability, nervous tension, impatience, easy frustration, willfulness, being spoiled, quick thinking; Self-righteous = fault-finding, bigotry, unreliability, inferiority complex, domineeringness, selfishness, combativeness; Suspicious = insecurity, guilt, furtiveness, vanity, timidity, inferiority; Bigoted = a single standard, rigid conventionality, pious rectitude, obstinacy, inflexibility, prudishness, fanaticism (fanaticism is always bigoted; bigotry need not be fanatical); Rogue = egoism, unscrupulousness, envy, insecurity, vanity, fickleness, unsociability, lack of creativity; Ambitious = rebellion against the status quo, craving recognition, insistence on one's own importance, dissatisfaction, lust for power, envy, desire to control, ruthlessness.

**Checklist of two-pole growth**: 《奥赛罗》 (Othello), love → jealousy → murder → suicide; 《海达》 (Hedda Gabler), self-centeredness → suicide; 《麦克白》 (Macbeth), ambition → murder; 《樱桃园》 (The Cherry Orchard), irresponsibility → loss of property; 《哈姆雷特》 (Hamlet), doubt → murder; 《推销员之死》 (Death of a Salesman), illusion → painful truth; 《死角》 (Dead End), poverty → crime; 《等待老左》 (Waiting for Lefty), doubt → belief; 《热铁皮屋顶上的猫》 (Cat on a Hot Tin Roof), depression → hope; 《送冰人来了》 (The Iceman Cometh), hope → despair. 《马姆洛克教授》 (Professor Mamlock), ten steps: isolation → the class is affected but he still does not believe → doubt but bigotry → fear as he realizes his blindness → wanting to flee but not knowing where → despair → joining the struggle → the underground → resistance → collective action and death.

## II. Egri's Four Types of Conflict and Examples of Transition

### Static vs. Jumping vs. Rising
- **Static**: "Do you love me?" — "Oh, I don't know" — "When?" — "Soon" — "How soon?" — "I don't know either" (it can go on for a month; the two are the same type, without belief).
- **Jumping**: "Do you love me?" — "I don't know" — "Don't be a fool, make a decision" — "I'd like to slap you" (and she leaves).
- **Rising** (Manders vs. Mrs. Alving in 《群鬼》 (Ghosts)): "This is all the result of your reading bad books" → "My good pastor, you are wrong. You were the very one who first made me think for myself, and I am deeply grateful to you" → "Grateful to me?!" → "You forced me to obey duty and keep my place... one knot untied and the whole thing came loose" → "And this is the result of the hardest struggle of my life?" → "Rather, it was the greatest defeat of your life" → "It was my greatest victory, my victory over myself" → "It was a crime against us both." The counterattack must be stronger than the attack; if she only answered "Really?" the conflict would cool at once.

### The Ladder of Transition
Friendship → disappointment → annoyance → irritation → anger → attack → threat → premeditation → murder. Skipping disappointment and annoyance is "like removing the liver and lungs."

**《晚宴》 (Dinner at Eight), Kitty and Packard (irritation → rage), line by line**: "I am the boss in this house" (seeing the chaos as the other's condition) — "Your first wife in Montana?" (counterattack) — "Leave her out of this" — "A pale, flat-chested little thing" (finding the sore spot) — "Shut up" (wanting it to stop) — "No wonder she died so young" — "God damn you" (**a jump**) — "Cheap goods... a baggage checkroom" — "A drunkard father, a repeat-offender brother... and she threw herself down sixty steps" — Tina enters and is pushed out, a bag hits the floor — "Pick it up" (the transition is complete; the first real anger) — throwing the bracelet: "You gave me this bracelet only for yourself" (in a rage, the attack misses the point) — "I rescued you from the slums" — "You dressed me up like a show horse and left me alone... you never sent me a single flower" (the hound finds the scent) — "Waiting for Daddy to come home?" (crisis) — "You think you're the only man I know?" (climax) — "You don't like that, do you, Mr. Cabinet Member" — "Tell me who he is" — "I'll never tell you" — Tina: "Only the doctor came" (two birds with one stone) — "I'll hire a private detective... and get my hands around his neck" — "You couldn't even get into the Astors' bathroom" — "You slimy rattlesnake... after tonight we're through," and the door slams.

**《码头工人》 (Stevedore), diagnosing the wrong order**: Florrie slaps him → Bill: "Shut up" → she delivers a long threat (the most important line, "I wrote to Helen," comes first and gets no response = static) → Bill suddenly grabs her throat and beats her (a jump). Revised order: first "Don't think you can treat me like that and walk away" → Bill counterattacks → "I'm going to see Helen" → Bill threatens a beating → "I wrote to Helen" → the beating becomes understandable.

### The Escalating Crisis in A Doll's House
Krogstad: "If someone pushes me into the gutter a second time, I'll take you with me" (crisis) → Helmer: "I simply cannot have him working here" (climax) → Nora panics (resolution); the dismissal letter is sent — "Get the letter back at once" — "Too late"; Krogstad slips in: "I won't prosecute you for the present" — "No matter how much you pay me, I won't return the bond" — "Suppose you wanted to leave your husband and children... do something even worse" — "I haven't the courage either" — "I have a letter to your husband on me" [crisis begins] — "In less than a year the real manager will be Nils Krogstad" [crisis] — "Now I have the courage" — "Lying under the ice... so ugly no one would know me" — "Your reputation is in my hands too" — she hears the letter drop into the mailbox [climax] — "Torvald, now we are finished" [resolution].

### Examples of the Point of Attack
Oedipus decides to find the murderer; Hedda's contempt; Macbeth hears the prophecy and covets the throne; 《一生一会》 (Once in a Lifetime): quitting to go into Hollywood; 《灵魂拒葬》 (Bury the Dead): refusing to be buried; 《客房服务》 (Room Service): the manager demands that his brother-in-law settle the troupe's bills; 《浮士德》 (Faust): selling his soul; 《卫兵》 (The Guardsman): the husband disguises himself as a guard to test his wife. Diagnosis of a student's one-act play: "despair leads to success," but a shy boy is mocked, a girl takes pity on him and marries him — nothing is imminent; revise it so that the play opens with the boy waiting at the girl's house for her father, having sworn to kill himself if refused → his life hangs by a thread.

## III. Egri's Script Analysis Template (Appendix)

Evaluate item by item under thirteen headings: **premise/pivotal character/characters/orchestration/unity of opposites/point of attack/conflict/transition/growth/crisis/climax/resolution/dialogue**.

**《伪君子》 (Tartuffe)**: premise, "he who ensnares others digs his own grave"; pivotal character, Tartuffe; Orgon is domineering, stupid, and blindly credulous (without knowing why); Damis does not grow at all; Mariane is completely static; orchestration: Orgon and Tartuffe are made for each other, and Dorine is in fact Tartuffe's best match; the point of attack should be Orgon's decision to break the engagement and marry his daughter to Tartuffe (the first half of Act One is pure static presentation); the transitions are good; crisis: Elmire persuades Orgon to hide and eavesdrop; climax: Tartuffe shows his true face and throws them out; resolution: the king recognizes the fugitive — **the king's intervention weakens the premise's persuasiveness**.

**《群鬼》 (Ghosts)**: premise, "the sins of the fathers are visited on the children"; pivotal character, Pastor Manders; **everyone is three-dimensional**; orchestration is careful (Mrs. Alving's opposition to Manders's blind piety; Engstrand's scheming against Manders's firmness; Regine's independent shrewdness against Engstrand's cunning); unity of opposites: preserving the Captain's noble reputation and preventing the siblings' marriage; Act One is an excellent example of continuous presentation of character through conflict; crisis: Oswald decides to marry Regine; climax: mental collapse; resolution: Mrs. Alving looks for the morphine.

## IV. Indick's Psychology Quick Reference

### Freud
| Concept | Corresponding device in film | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Eros/Thanatos | Love and hate, sex and violence, revenge | 《阳光下的决斗》 (Duel in the Sun), 《天生杀人狂》 (Natural Born Killers) |
| Oedipal rivalry | Rivalry over love; double rivalry (the same antagonist contests both the championship and the woman) | 《毕业生》 (The Graduate), 《锡杯》 (Tin Cup), 《洛奇》 (Rocky) |
| Forbidden fruit | An illicit love ends in tragedy; only if the antagonist is unworthy can one "get the person and escape punishment" | 《罗密欧》 (Romeo and Juliet), 《泰坦尼克》 (Titanic), 《出轨》 (Unfaithful) (a loophole) |
| Powerlessness/role reversal | The caregiver becomes the dangerous one; the bed, bathtub, or sickbed is attacked | 《闪灵》 (The Shining), 《危情十日》 (Misery), 《煤气灯下》 (Gaslight) |
| Id/ego/superego | Villain/protagonist/mentor | 《恐怖角》 (Cape Fear), 《星球大战》 (Star Wars) |

| The antihero's three dimensions | Self-purpose → moral consciousness → inner conflict | 《原野奇侠》 (Shane) |
| The fallen hero | Seeking redemption leads to destruction | 《疤面煞星》 (Scarface), 《教父》 (The Godfather) |
| Guilt complex | A universal motive, must be paired with a father/role model | 《蜘蛛侠》 (Spider-Man), 《迷幻牛郎》 (Drugstore Cowboy) |
| Oral stage | Smoking = pause + inner conflict; obesity = greed; addiction = an obstacle that can be overcome | 《卡萨布兰卡》 (Casablanca), 《好家伙》 (Goodfellas), 《赤胆屠龙》 (Rio Bravo) |
| Anal stage | Meticulousness vs. slovenliness = comic pair; repression vs. release | 《单身公寓》 (The Odd Couple), 《稻草狗》 (Straw Dogs), 《叛舰喋血记》 (Mutiny on the Bounty) |
| Phallic stage | Weapon = identity symbol; penis envy = female empowerment | Lightsaber, magic sword; 《魔鬼女大兵》 (G.I. Jane) |
| Genital stage | Seducing the young girl; the virgin hero | 《洛丽塔》 (Lolita), 《美国派》 (American Pie) |

**Defense mechanisms** (the character does not know, the audience sees): repression (《告别有情天》 (The Remains of the Day)), denial (the mayor in 《大白鲨》 (Jaws); true belief = tragedy vs. knowing deception = villain), identification (《正午》 (High Noon): refusing to identify with the cowardly mentor), sublimation (《巧克力情人》 (Like Water for Chocolate); the "power of love" makes the incredible credible, but the motive must be believed first), regression (《大寒》 (The Big Chill), followed by a scene that confesses a secret), reaction formation (《蓬门今始为君开》 (The Quiet Man): the mouth says no, the eyes say yes; Fitts in 《美国美人》 (American Beauty)), displacement (《城市英雄》 (Falling Down), 《侠骨柔情》 (My Darling Clementine)), rationalization (Woody Allen), projection (《碧血金沙》 (The Treasure of the Sierra Madre), 《玫瑰舞后》 (Gypsy)), isolation (《原野奇侠》 (Shane), 《搜索者》 (The Searchers)), the slip of the tongue (used only to express emotion), the joke (placed just before or after the point of highest tension).

**Dream work**: the wish-fulfillment structure (Act One: the dream comes true; the first half of Act Two: indulgence; conflict arrives naturally and concerns "what inner conflict the new identity brings"); the incubus/succubus can represent any conflict; a surreal atmosphere (fog, smoke, soft focus, odd angles, slow motion) makes the audience believe anything can happen; write one dream for each character.

### Erikson's Eight Stages
| Stage | Conflict | Film counterpart |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Trust vs. mistrust | The suspicious protagonist (《唐人街》 (Chinatown)) → the leap of faith (《非洲女王号》 (The African Queen)); the gullible learns to say no (《绿宝石》 (Romancing the Stone)) |
| 2 | Autonomy vs. shame and doubt | The rebel hero begins in submission (《斯巴达克斯》 (Spartacus)) |
| 3 | Initiative vs. guilt | Guilt as a driving force (Scrooge) or an obstacle (Conrad in 《凡夫俗子》 (Ordinary People)) |
| 4 | Industry vs. inferiority | The underdog theme (《洛奇》 (Rocky), 《十二金刚》 (The Dirty Dozen)) — motive is the support of structure |
| 5 | Identity vs. diffusion | Act One establishes identity; the moratorium; foreclosure (《黑潮》 (Malcolm X)); send the protagonist alone into the black hole (the cave in 《帝国反击战》 (The Empire Strikes Back)) |
| 6 | Intimacy vs. isolation | Intimacy = disclosing secrets; the small romantic-comedy scene (《一夜风流》 (It Happened One Night)); confession of the heart at the end of Act Two; in Act Three solve it yourself, without a mediator |
| 7 | Generativity vs. stagnation | The stagnant mentor is persuaded by the apprentice (《龙威小子》 (The Karate Kid)); the tragedy of stolen youth (《美国美人》 (American Beauty)) |
| 8 | Integrity vs. despair | "I am the author of my own life story"; 《生之欲》 (Ikiru) |

### Jung's Archetypes and the Fourfold Whole
| Archetype | Function | Positive | Shadow |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hero | Ego | Luke | — |
| Persona | The public mask (the star) | Wayne, Gable | — |
| Shadow | Villain/second self | — | Vader, the Green Goblin, the masked killer |
| Goddess | Mother/female mentor | The fairy godmother, Galadriel | The wicked stepmother, 《亲爱的妈咪》 (Mommie Dearest) |
| Wise old man | Father/mentor | Obi-Wan | The false mentor (Vader: "I am your father") |

The fourfold whole = hero, villain, love object, mentor; plot archetypes: encounter and integration, transformation (can happen to a supporting character), synchronicity (use boldly), fate (use with caution), healing (the wounded hero faces the past), sacred marriage (the love scene completes the hero).

### Campbell's Seventeen Stages (《勇敢的心》 (Braveheart)/《角斗士》 (Gladiator))
Departure: the ordinary world (the main mentor often dies in Act One) → the call to adventure (within twenty minutes) → the herald → refusal of the call → raising the danger (the wife and children are killed) → supernatural aid (a weapon + a symbolic motif: a handkerchief/a figurine) → crossing the first threshold (the threshold guardian; gathering allies) → the belly of the whale. Initiation: the road of trials → meeting the goddess (dead/spiritual) → woman as temptress (the enemy defector, requiring a leap of faith) → atonement with the father → apotheosis (touched by death) → the ultimate boon. Return: refusal of the return → the magic flight → purification (must bear directly on the main conflict) → rescue from without → crossing the return threshold → master of two worlds (becoming the mentor who inspires the next hero) → freedom to live (the hero's death becomes legend + "a happy afterlife").

### Murdock's Ten Stages (《永不妥协》 (Erin Brockovich))
Separation from the feminine (the babysitter leaves) → identification with the masculine (the boss, Ed) → the road of trials: slaying the two-headed dragon (work/children), the ogre tyrant (the corporation + the inner "at any cost"), the knight in shining armor (the fantasy of the man who will solve everything) → the illusory boon of success (the superwoman) → the strong woman can say no (the king) → initiation and descent to the goddess (Donna in illness) → the urgent yearning to reconnect with the feminine (the wild woman) → healing the mother-daughter split (the grandmother spider) → finding the loving inner man (George) → transcending duality (fulfillment in both career and family).

### Adler
- Hamartia = the root of the inferiority complex the hero must overcome; the supervillain's deformity = inferiority → overcompensation → superiority complex; the mentor can also have a superiority complex (the coach who drives his players to make up for his own failed career).
- The child-hero formula: powerlessness → orphan-style liberation → wish fulfillment → "home is where the love is" → role reversal (the child saves the powerless parents/marriage) → the animal hero and the animal mentor who must also overcome inferiority → homecoming and reunion.
- The six elements of sibling rivalry (《伊甸园之东》 (East of Eden), 《阳光下的决斗》 (Duel in the Sun)): the good/bad binary, parental favoritism (which can cross over), romantic rivalry, the need for recognition, an external goal, and moral conflict. The antagonist and the mentor swap roles (the five Rocky films).
- Four lifestyles: ruling (Crassus), getting (Batiatus), avoiding, socially useful; the team-film arrangement = hero useful/ally avoiding/villain ruling/henchman getting (《七武士》 (Seven Samurai)); the antihero's arc = getting → avoiding → useful (《用心棒》 (Yojimbo)); one man goes through all four (《圣袍千秋》 (The Robe)).

### Rollo May
- The four existential stages: innocence (the emerging hero) → rebellion (the death of innocence: the aunt and uncle are killed; the power of hatred; the "demon" takes possession and must be driven out) → ordinary consciousness (Act Two: commitment to an external goal; the catharsis coincides with killing the Act One murderer) → creative consciousness (insight into his true identity, then dedication to a higher goal).
- Resolution answers "what happened"; the ending answers "what happens next."
- Archetypes of the narcissistic era: the American hero, the cowboy (Shane, Ethan), the lone crusader (《正午》 (High Noon)), the rebel cop (now nearly stale), the outlaw (the trapped feeling of film noir), the private detective (two-thirds rebel cop + one-third gangster), the oppositional cop (《冲突》 (Serpico), 《坏中尉》 (Bad Lieutenant)), the mad scientist/solitary genius (the existential reversal: sacrificing his work for the one he loves, destroying his creation), the willing lamb (the mentor sacrifices himself first).

## VI. Hicks's Character Tools

- **Stanley and the dog** (minimum action): the shy accountant's neighbor's dog barks all night → pillow over the head → call the police/animal control → an anonymous note → after a week without sleep he throws on his bathrobe and rushes to knock on the door → the door is opened by the scantily clad beauty Susie, who invites him in — now he must make a dramatic choice; the smart writer schedules an important career-making meeting for the next day.
- The general formula of a love story: Stanley and Susie fall passionately in love (the market, a bicycle built for two, the zoo), but the audience notices that their self-concepts are fundamentally different (classical music vs. Elvis) — they can only be together after both self-concepts have been aligned.
- Examples of the five foci of conflict: personal (《潮浪王子》 (The Prince of Tides)); interpersonal (《凡夫俗子》 (Ordinary People), 《为黛西小姐开车》 (Driving Miss Daisy), the one-room format); situational (the core of 《龙卷风》 (Twister) is the relationship between the man and the woman; in 《燕雀香巢》 (Mr. Blandings Builds His Dream House), the well-digging quickly loses dramatic value); social (in 《全金属外壳》 (Full Metal Jacket), Joker shoots the female sniper); vested-interest (Strasser in 《卡萨布兰卡》 (Casablanca); 《终结者 2》 (Terminator 2): "Now I know why you cry").
- The antagonist's-point-of-view test: seen from Concannon's point of view, 《大审判》 (The Verdict) is hardly remarkable — his goal, "win the case," is exactly the same as Galvin's. Who is the antagonist in 《沉默的羔羊》 (The Silence of the Lambs), 《泰坦尼克号》 (Titanic), or 《钢琴课》 (The Piano)?
- Maslow's hierarchy: physiological (survival films, money); safety (thrillers, the easiest to identify with); social (interpersonal drama and comedy); esteem (《盗火线》 (Heat), 《莫扎特传》 (Amadeus)); self-actualization (the hardest to dramatize; it needs a functional antagonist: the nun in 《死囚漫步》 (Dead Man Walking)).

## VII. Snyder's Rules for the Protagonist and the Villain

- Five rules for the protagonist: make me identify; let me learn something from him; be charming enough that I want to keep watching; make me believe he deserves a happy ending; have a primal drive.
- The character-swap experiment: in 《佐州》 (Ride Along), change the teacher into a retired special-forces soldier → a different movie; in 《度假》 (The Retreat), change the new employee into a sixty-five-year-old retiree → "a Jack Lemmon indie, and he's already dead."
- Write to actor types, not stars: the struggling young man, the good girl on the brink of love, the clever kid, the sex goddess, the charming leading man, the wounded soldier, the beauty in trouble, the lovable playboy, the court jester, the wise old man.
- Save the Cat: in 《午夜惊情》 (Sea of Love), Pacino lets the father with his child go; Aladdin steals bread for hungry children; the antihero uses humor (the hamburger conversation in 《低俗小说》 (Pulp Fiction)); if the protagonist has a flaw, have the boss throw someone out the window.
- The villain must be worse; the protagonist and the antagonist are the two sides of one person (Batman/the Joker); everyone has an arc except the villain; supporting characters need a "limp and an eye patch" (a persistent visual memory hook).
