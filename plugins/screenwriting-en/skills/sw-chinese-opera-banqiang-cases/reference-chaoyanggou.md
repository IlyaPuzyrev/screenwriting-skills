## §1 Nine-Scene Structure Table for Chaoyang Village

**Format**: The script has only "Scene One … Scene Nine"—**no scene titles, no location headings**; the location is given in the first stage direction. Each scene ends with a line reading "——幕闭" ("—curtain"); the last scene reads "——剧终" ("—end of play"). **Four components**: character names are set flush left; 宾白 (bīnbái — spoken dialogue) follows the name without a colon; song is marked "(sings)" and each line begins on a new line; stage directions begin with "［" on a separate line. **Metrical patterns are not marked, nor are gongs and drums (锣鼓, luógǔ), nor is stage management (检场, jiǎnchǎng).** Offstage singing (内唱, nèichàng) has three markings: "女声内唱：" ("female voice offstage:"), "幕后合唱：" ("offstage chorus:"), and "幕后银环妈唱：" ("Yinhuan's mother [银环妈] sings offstage:"). **The lyrics follow directly after the colon, without "(sings)."** There are only two special markings in the whole play: "(**背唱**)" (bèichàng — sings aside; scene 6, p0061) and "(**旁白**)" (pángbái — speaks aside; scene 6, p0058). The book has only one footnote (p0017, noting "您 (nen), a Henan dialect form"); all other dialect is left unannotated—**the author's tolerance for dialect is extremely high.**

**Main line distribution**: Scenes 1–3 = "going down to the countryside" (the external resistance is Yinhuan's mother); scenes 4–6 = "wavering" (the resistance shifts to Yinhuan herself: she cannot hoe, cannot carry, is laughed at, feels her talents are wasted); **scene 7 is the only turning-point scene**, and also the scene with the largest share of aria material; scenes 8–9 = "staying" + bringing Yinhuan's mother over too (the resistance is absorbed).

**Two structural criteria**: ① **Both major turns in the play occur on the mountain road** (scene 2, going up the mountain = positive; scene 7, going down = negative and then reversed), the same road walked twice, the same vocal passage ("Walk a ridge and cross a mountain" / "Walk a ridge and cross a gully") sung twice, the second time line by line against the first—**the hardest structural design in the play**; ② **Scene 5 deliberately gives no song**: Yinhuan's most aggrieved moments (dropping the bucket, being laughed at) are all carried by speech and stage action ("one stagger and one twist" [一歪一扭], "one shake and three wobbles" [一摇三晃], "'clang'—the carrying pole falls" [「咣当」一声担子掉了], "she coils her braid on top of her head" [把辫子盘在头上]). Set against scene 8's 73:26, this reveals the allocation rule: **ensemble scenes in speech, two-person confrontations in song**.

**The character list (p0004) and what it reveals about the writing**: 拴保 (Shuanbao) (male, 23, an educated youth returned to the village) / 银环 (Yinhuan) (female, 21, an educated youth sent down to the countryside) / branch secretary (male, in his fifties) / Shuanbao's mother (50) / Shuanbao's father (拴保爹) (in his fifties) / 巧真 (Qiaozhen) (female, in her teens) / 二大娘 (Er Daniang — Second Aunt) (in her forties) / Yinhuan's mother (in her forties, a city resident) / "Old Child" (male, in his sixties) / 伶歌 (Lingge) (female, 16) / 小黑旦 (Xiao Heidan) (male, 18) / several male and female commune members / several male and female educated youths. ① **Role types (行当, hángdang) are not marked**; age + identity replace them; but functional positions are still distributed by role type—Shuanbao's mother (老旦, lǎodàn — old female role; the laodan route), Er Daniang (彩旦, cǎidàn — comic female role; the play's only comic bearer), "Old Child" and Xiao Heidan (丑, chǒu — comic role; only comic business and bets), Qiaozhen (花旦, huādàn — vivacious female role; only message-bearing and backtalk). ② **All four main middle-aged and elderly characters have no names, only relational forms of address** (Shuanbao's mother / Shuanbao's father / Yinhuan's mother / Er Daniang)—this is precisely why "Mother-in-law, sit down" works in scene 9. ③ The two leads' identities are **symmetrical**: "returned to the village" (还乡) / "sent down to the countryside" (下乡)—a one-character difference in Chinese, and the whole play's conflict comes from that one character.

**Statistics**: 50 arias, of which **14 have had their metrical patterns supplied from the scores** (the score collection has 16 passages; the other 2 have only tempo markings). Frequency of metrical patterns: **the 【二八板】 family (including medium / tight / slow / Yudong / Yuxi / linked / erba-stacked) appears 11 times and is absolutely dominant**; the 【流水板】 family 5; 【垛板】 4; 【慢板】 2; 【飞板】, 【快三眼】, and 【紧打慢唱】 1 each.

**Aria count per scene**: 1: 8, 2: 3, 3: 9, 4: 5, 5: 2, 6: 9, 7: 5, 8: 4, 9: 5. **The scene with the most arias is not the turning-point scene** (scenes 3 and 6 have 9 each), **but the longest single aria is in the turning-point scene** (scene 7, #38, about 30 lines)—preparatory scenes use many short, fragmented arias to define each character in turn; the turning-point scene uses one solid block of a long aria and slams it onto the protagonist.

**One criterion**: the default metrical pattern of a Yuju modern-dress play (现代戏, xiàndàixì) is **【二八板】** (used for narrative, reasoning, and duets alike; medium / tight / slow and Yudong / Yuxi adjust the emotion); **【慢板】 is used only for the opening self-account and the offstage chorus**; 【流水板】 is used for rising emotion and rapid exposition; 【垛板】 follows duoju only; 【飞板】 is used only for scattered-singing moments such as "chasing after someone and shouting them to a stop."

**Three readings that constrain the aria lyrics**:

1. **The word "turn" (转, zhuǎn) is the signal that the lyric line pattern should change.** Wherever the score says "slightly faster, turn to 【垛板】" or "gradually faster, turn to 【垛板】," go back to the main text and you will find that **the lyrics simultaneously change from seven- or ten-character lines into three- or four-character duoju** (p0095's "Day after day we hope, night after night we think," p0121's five "You are studying…" lines, p0135's string of place names). **A playwright who does not understand melody can still fix the metrical-pattern turning points: when the line pattern changes, the metrical pattern should turn.**
2. **【二八连板】 and 【流水连板】 appear in only one place**—Shuanbao's mother's 25-line long narration in scene 3 (p0108). Linked metre (连板, liánbǎn) = singing continuously without pause; the corresponding lyric feature is **no tone pause between lines, one continuous flow** (the ten lines that narrate the dream). **Long narrative passages use linked metre.**
3. **【豫东】 and 【豫西】 follow character and emotion**: Yudong (bright, high-pitched) is given to the scene 2 road chorus and the first half of Er Daniang's letter; Yuxi (deep) is given to Shuanbao's mother's reasoning, the second half of Er Daniang's letter, and Yinhuan's long downhill passage. **A turn from Yudong to Yuxi within one passage is a turn from happiness to opening one's heart** (p0135's "You write: in three years I have never once been ill" is exactly at the turn).

If another reliable score (the *A Compendium of Xiangfu-School Yuju Aria, Bǎnshì, and Instrumental Tune Excerpts* by 樊尚林 (Fan Shanglin)) marks one, use that score and cite it. The metrical-pattern names in these notes follow the script's markings throughout and **have not been aligned with Fan's book**.

### 4.2 The Five-Person Duet "Mother-in-law, Sit Down" (Scene 9, p0083–0085)

Score p0161; **the score marks only "fast," with no metrical-pattern name (do not fill one in)**. The fahua rhyme runs from beginning to end.

> Shuanbao's mother: "Mother-in-law, sit down" (3+3 = a six-character opening phrase) / "let us have a heart-to-heart." (7)
> Yinhuan's mother: "Mother-in-law, let us both sit down" (7) / "let us just chat casually." (7)
> Er Daniang: "Sister-in-law, when you come to our home" (7) / "taste the big watermelons from our mountain gully." (9; padding "的")
> Shuanbao's mother: "Ever since Yinhuan left home" (7) / "I know you have been constantly worried." (8)
> Yinhuan's mother: "She left without taking a quilt" (7) / "she left home in a flustered hurry." (8)
> Er Daniang: "Come to the house and take a look" (7) / "what is laid underneath, what is on top?" (8; **the question breaks the rhyme**)
> Shuanbao's mother: "I made a new set of bedding" (7) / "new lining, new cover, new cotton." (7; **a duoju of three "new" words, bringing the rhyme back for the previous line**)
> Yinhuan's mother: "At home she never farmed" (8) / "she never once held a hoe handle." (8)
> Er Daniang: "Whatever she wants to learn, I will teach her" (8) / "plow, seed drill, hoe, harrow—she dares take them all in hand." (7; **a duoju of four single farm-tool words**)
> Yinhuan's mother: "It is my fault for taking the wrong road at the start" (8) / "I should not have held Yinhuan back." (8)
> Shuanbao's mother: "Yinhuan has made great progress since going down to the countryside" (7) / "she is determined to take root there." (8)
> Er Daniang: "Our Party branch has a plan" (7) / "what, what should we make them?" (7; **a repeated-word question that hands the lower line to someone else**)
> Qiaozhen: "Make them our agricultural scientists." (8)
> Er Daniang: "Right—make them our agricultural scientists!" (speech + 8; **repeats the previous line for emphasis**)
> Yinhuan's mother: "If Yinhuan has made progress" (7) / "it is thanks to your help." (7)
> Er Daniang: "Educating the next generation is a great responsibility" (7) / "our work is still far from good." (7)
> Yinhuan's mother: "We two, mother and daughter, must listen to the Party" (8)
> Yinhuan + Yinhuan's mother (together): "Chaoyang Village is our home." (8)

**Technical points**: ① **seven-character lines are the skeleton** (2-2-3), with padding stretching them to eight or nine; the opening "Mother-in-law, sit down" deliberately uses six characters (3+3) as an opening phrase, one notch shorter, immediately seizing attention; ② **one person per pair of lines, rotating**, three people cycling for two and a half rounds; Qiaozhen has only half a line, and Er Daniang repeats that half-line for her—**this is how the 合头 (hétóu — the sung section at the end of a tune) is absorbed into round singing**; ③ **a question may break the rhyme** ("what is laid underneath, what is on top?"), and the next person's lower line ("new cotton") brings the rhyme back—**legal in a duet, not in a solo**; ④ not one lyrical adjective in the whole passage, **only objects and actions** (quilt, bedding, cotton, hoe handle, plow, seed drill, hoe, harrow, watermelon); "have a heart-to-heart" is the only abstract phrase, and it is used up in the second line; ⑤ "mother-in-law" is **a form of address serving as the interface**.

### 4.3 Ten Passages of Colloquial Speech in Song (the Play's Greatest Technical Contribution)

**Example 1: A quarrel transcript enters song** (scene 1, p0012; Yinhuan; zhongdong rhyme; 13 lines)
> Last night, when I brought up going down to the countryside to work, / my mother and I had a fierce struggle. / She pointed at my forehead and glared, / shouting high and low until her throat was raw. / She scolded me for going from junior high to senior high, / and from one promotion to another, ending up in the countryside. / When she spoke lightly, I paid no attention; / when I spoke seriously, she scolded even more fiercely. / Open her mouth and she cursed me as "没有用" ("useless"), / close her mouth and she cursed me as a "糊涂虫" ("muddlehead"). / The electric light was not turned off all night; / I reasoned and pleaded, but she would not listen, / she kept it up until seven o'clock.

**Technique**: ① **direct quotation enters the song**—"没有用" ("useless") and "糊涂虫" ("muddlehead") are sung inside quotation marks; ② **real numbers for time and objects**—"seven o'clock," "did not turn off the electric light," not summarized as "sleepless all night"; ③ "open her mouth to curse me / close her mouth to curse me" is **an antithetical duoju with a four-character lead-in**.

**Example 2: The standard way to write duoju** (scene 1, p0013; Shuanbao; jiangyang rhyme; score p0095)
> …If you don't come in the morning, I wait until noon, / if you don't come by noon, I wait until the afternoon. / Day after day I hope, night after night I think, / waiting for you, hoping for you, missing you, thinking of you, / who would have thought your heart is colder than that ice lolly!

**Technique**: **three-stage acceleration in the duoju**—seven-character lines (morning / noon) → a three-character couplet (day after day, night after night) → **a string of four two-character verbs** (waiting for you, hoping for you, missing you, thinking of you) → a return to a ten-character long line to close. The score marks "slightly faster, turn to 【垛板】" here; **the duoju and 【垛板】 correspond strictly**, and after the duoju it "turns to 【二八板】." The closing metaphor uses "ice lolly"—an everyday object for city children in 1958, not "frost."

**Example 3: Reduplicated plain description** (scene 3, p0029; Shuanbao's mother; zhongdong rhyme; score p0116, 【快流水板】)
> Cotton is white, white and pure, / radishes are green, green and glossy, / every grain of wheat is plump, / the cabbages grow firm and solid. / Your father can work hard all year round, / your sister has been diligent since childhood.

**Technique**: 3+3 / 3+3 / 7 / 7, **sliding from a three-character duoju into seven-character lines**; the reduplicated adjectives (白生生 báishēngshēng, 青凌凌 qīnglínglíng, 饱盈盈 bǎoyíngyíng, 磁丁丁 cídīngdīng) are an inherent pattern of Henan colloquial speech; four lines, four crops, one crop per line, no elaboration. "磁丁丁" is dialect (sturdy); **do not change it to standard Mandarin**.

**Example 4: A dream and a husband-and-wife conversation enter song as they are** (scene 3, p0025; Shuanbao's mother; suobo rhyme)
> Last night, I, an old woman, had a good dream, / I dreamed that Yinhuan came to see me. / The moment she came in she was smiling, / first she called "Mother," then she called "Father," / she worked briskly in the fields, / she helped me, old woman, learn to read, / I was so happy I could not say what I felt. / The old man beside me nudged me: / "You old thing, you, / in all these generations you've never been a mother-in-law." / I woke with a start to hear the chicken crow three times, / and all night I never closed my eyes again.

**Technique**: ① the old woman **calls herself "我老婆" (wǒ lǎopó — "I, old woman")**, a Henan rural woman's self-reference; do not change it; ② **the husband's original words, with the address "老东西" ("old thing"), enter the song directly**, split into three short lines (4 / 4 / 7); ③ time is given by "the chicken crowed three times," not "dawn"; ④ "first she called Mother, then she called Father" is an unrhymed upper line.

**Example 5: Labor action enters song** (scene 4, p0041; Shuanbao; zhongdong rhyme)
> (He hands the hoe to Yinhuan, demonstrates, and sings while hoeing) That front leg bent, / that back leg pushed, / keep your pace steady and your strength even. / That way the weeds die, the seedlings thrive, and the soil loosens, / der yo, der yo, the soil loosens.

**Technique**: **the lyrics are the action mnemonic**, beginning with three-character lines and closing with a seven-character line; the padding "那个" ("that") leads the line three times; "得儿哟" is pure padding (a drover's cry). In the next passage Shuanbao changes the same words to the second person and sings them again ("Your front leg bent, your back leg pushed")—**the same words sung twice = demonstration and correction**, the modern play's way of turning body teaching into song.

**Example 6: A string of proverbs** (scene 6, p0052; Er Daniang; yiqi rhyme; 13 lines)
> A horse painted on the wall cannot be ridden, / a sesame flatbread in the mirror cannot fill the belly. / She is in the countryside, but her heart is in the city, / she has never looked favorably on the mountain district. … A good ox that is not trained cannot pull the plow, / a willow or poplar that is not pruned will not grow straight. / Mere accommodation cannot achieve unity, / if there are faults, they can be raised to your face.

**Technique**: **six proverbs + two judgment sentences alternating**; the proverbs all come from farm work and the kitchen (horse, flatbread, ox, plow, willow and poplar). Yiqi is a broad rhyme; the concreteness of the proverbs keeps the lines from sounding alike.

**Example 7: A city person's comments in parallel spoken lines** (scene 3, p0026; Er Daniang; **宾白**)
> If you ask him to come here to enjoy the scenery, he looks at this place and says it is wonderful, looks at that place and says it is too good for words; he picks up a little stone and puts it in his pocket, sees a yellow leaf and tucks it into his notebook… But if you ask him to come and live here for good, then he says the mountains are too high, the road too far, this place is dirty and that place stinks—everywhere has become a fault.

**Technique**: this passage **is not made into song**—it is spoken dialogue. Set against example 6 (same person, same content, made into song), the division of labor is clear: **prospective, teasing observations use speech; judgments and demands that the other change use song.**

> **Addendum on technique (Section 4, item ⑥, of SKILL.md does not cover this fully)**: **Teasing observations are written as spoken dialogue; judgments and demands that the other change are written as song.** *Failure condition*: if the character has no dramatic task in this scene, writing it as song will feel dry. *Self-check*: after this song ends, has anyone's action changed? If not, go back to spoken dialogue.

**Example 8: An oral letter enters song** (scene 6, p0056–0057; Er Daniang; renchen rhyme; score p0135)
> You write: in the mountain gully, every year is better than the last, / a thousand people rejoice, ten thousand laugh, everything is as they wish. … The apple trees at Yecaowan bloom and bear fruit, / the fish in the reservoir—one weighs five catties. / The walnut trees at Shengpoya spread over the sky and cover the ground, / at the Daqingzhuang livestock farm, cattle and sheep gather in herds. / Secretary Liu (柳书记) from the county leads the survey team, / from East Mountain to West Mountain, / from the foot of the mountain to the summit, / red flags are planted all over Yangpo Ridge, / he says our mountain gully is a treasure basin. / You write: in three years I have never once been ill, / "Old Child" says I have bones of steel and sinews of iron.

**Technique**: ① **the three-character lead "你写上：" ("You write:") repeats four times**, making the action of "dictating—writing on someone's behalf" the structural skeleton of the song; ② **a place-name duoju** (Yecaowan / Shengpoya / Daqingzhuang / East Mountain, West Mountain / foot of the mountain, summit) exactly matches the score's 【二八垛】; ③ **numbers enter the song**—"one weighs five catties," "in three years"; ④ the village nickname "Old Child" is used as a witness. **This is the play's best example of "making an ordinary event into a big aria"—the event is simply writing a letter.**

**Example 9: A scenery-inventory core passage** (scene 7, p0065–0066; Yinhuan; youqiu rhyme; score p0142; about 30 lines in one rhyme to the end)
> This is the road I walked when I first came down to the countryside, / here I learned to hoe and found my teacher. / That is the sweet-potato crop we planted by carrying water, / that is the early autumn crop I hoed with my own hands. / That is the apple-pear tree you grafted, / in a blink it has grown fat branches and thick leaves. / When I first came, the crop seedlings had only just broken the soil, / by autumn the big granary will be full and the small one overflowing. / The commune members strive to be strong and ride the wind and waves, / I am like a ship that has lost its rudder, drifting with the current. … Where am I to go? / Where am I to walk? / How hard to leave, how hard to forget, Chaoyang Village! / My mouth asks my heart, my heart asks my mouth, / with all these good crops before my eyes, I cannot bear to leave or lose them.

**Technique**: ① **four consecutive points—"this is / that is / that is / that is"**—each one an object that has appeared in earlier scenes (the road, the sweet potato, the early autumn crop, the pear tree), **recovering item by item the assets of scenes 2, 4, and 5**; ② only at the emotional peak does the passage's sole metaphor ("a ship that has lost its rudder") and its sole short-line duoju appear; ③ youqiu is the cleanest rhyme in the play, **30 lines without a single departure**—the longest passage uses the strictest rhyme.

**Example 10: Half-line rapid exchange** (scene 8, p0075–0076; Yinhuan / Yinhuan's mother; suobo rhyme)
> Yinhuan: "If you ask me—" / Yinhuan's mother: "What did you say?" / Yinhuan: "Mother, oh Mother…" / Yinhuan's mother: "Stop your chatter." / Yinhuan: "Dear Mother!" / Yinhuan's mother: "You'll be the death of me." / … Yinhuan: "You are not allowed to insult the people of our mountains!" / Yinhuan's mother: "I said it—dare you cut out my tongue!" / Yinhuan: "Mother, are you going to be reasonable or not?" / Yinhuan's mother: "And what if I am not reasonable?" / Yinhuan: "If you are not reasonable, I will leave." / Yinhuan's mother: "Then never come to see me again in your life." / Yinhuan: "Say I leave, and I leave at once!" / Yinhuan's mother: "Go—your brother will support me, / without you, girl, I can still live."

**Technique**: **line length decreases**—the first section gives each person four lines, the middle section two lines each, and here one line or even half a line each (three characters, four characters), one line at a time, with the upper and lower lines split between the two people. **At the end, Yinhuan's mother speaks one extra line (two lines) to close the passage.** This measures the rapid back-and-forth of a quarrel directly into upper and lower lines; **without any metrical-pattern annotation, one can see the rhythm accelerating.**

## §5 The Actual State of Rhyme in Chaoyang Village, the Handling of Conventions, and Version Differences

### 5.1 The True Scale of Rhyme and Rhyme Categories

- **The lower line must rhyme; the upper line is free** is the only strictly enforced rule. The upper line rhymes about fifty percent of the time in the play as a whole, and the more antithetical the couplet, the more likely it rhymes.
- **Neighboring rhyme categories may be used interchangeably, and dialect-based rhyme assignment is allowed**: in scene 1's "The motherland's great construction" passage, which is in the 一七 (yīqī) rhyme, there is one line, "I wish I could grow wings and fly" (恨不能插上翅膀飞)—in Mandarin, "飞" (fēi) belongs to the 灰堆 (huīduī) rhyme category, **but in Yuju singing and speech it is assigned to 一七; this is dialect rhyme assignment, not a rhyme error**.
- **Changing rhyme mid-passage is allowed**: in scene 6, #34, the first two lines are 一七 and the last eight are 怀来 (huáilái); the change comes when the emotion turns from guilt to resolve.
- **Adjacent arias often join on the same rhyme** (scene 2, 9→10 言前; scene 4, 22→23→24 中东; scene 9, 47→48 梭波), creating tonal unity within a single scene.
- **Broad rhyme categories are used heavily**: nine categories—中东 (zhōngdōng), 言前 (yánqián), 人辰 (rénchén), 梭波 (suōbō), 发花 (fāhuā), 一七 (yīqī), 由求 (yóuqiú), 怀来 (huáilái), and 江阳 (jiāngyáng)—cover all 50 passages of the play, and **the risky categories (乜斜 miēxié, 遥条 yáotiáo, 灰堆 huīduī, 姑苏 gūsū) are not used even once**. A modern play has a great deal of colloquial material, and only broad rhymes can hold everyday detail—**this does not conflict with "an apt risky rhyme refreshes the ear," but the premise must be stated alongside it: the default for a colloquial script is a broad rhyme.**
- **A question line may go unrhymed and be picked up by the other person's lower line** (see §4.2).

### 5.2 How Conventions Are Handled in the Modern-Dress Play (the Most Methodologically Valuable Section of This Case)

1. **There is no self-introduction (自报家门, zìbào jiāmén), no entrance poem (定场诗, dìngchǎngshī), no exit poem (下场诗, xiàchǎngshī).** A character's identity is established entirely through other people's spoken dialogue ("This is the old branch secretary," "I am Old Li," "This is your Second Aunt"). All nine scenes close with "curtain," and the last scene closes with "the main curtain slowly closes." **Conclusion: in a modern play these three were deleted, not rewritten.**
2. **The entrance-and-exit convention (上下场, shàngxiàchǎng) is retained and modified.** The four cross-pass lines (错身, cuòshēn) in scene 1, p0011, are the place where the play's conventional traces are heaviest: Shuanbao shouts "Yinhuan!" and sings one line before exiting; Yinhuan enters hurriedly, sings one line, and exits; Shuanbao re-enters, sings one line, and exits; Yinhuan re-enters and sings the last line—**four entrances and exits solely to complete a four-line rhymed passage**. This moves the traditional play's "rush scene / cross-pass" (赶场／错身, gǎnchǎng / cuòshēn) straight into a modern play, covered by a modern set (a park).
3. **The aside (背供, bèigòng) is retained and explicitly marked.** In scene 6, p0061, Yinhuan: "(**背唱** — sings aside) There are things in my heart I cannot say"; in scene 6, p0058, Er Daniang: "(**旁白** — speaks aside) I still don't understand, and I haven't united anyone either!"—**one song and one speech, two kinds of aside coexisting**.
4. **Offstage singing (内唱, nèichàng — inner singing) is retained.** Scene 2 opens with "female voice offstage: Walk a ridge and cross a mountain"; in scene 7, Yinhuan: "(sings offstage) I come running with small steps, walking with big strides"—**the voice is heard before the person appears**. Scene 6 goes further: Yinhuan's mother **does not appear; an offstage song replaces the reading of the letter**, turning a letter into an aria.
5. **A new convention: offstage chorus / accompanying chorus.** Scene 4 opens with a chorus that changes the set; in scene 2 a female classmate sings "(chorus) The Ten-Li Canal flows with clear spring water" as a 合头 (hétóu — the sung section at the end of a tune); in scene 7 the offstage chorus **inserts itself three times and repeats verbatim lines the protagonist has just sung**. Its function is equivalent to the qupai system's (曲牌体, qǔpáitǐ) **hetou** and Sichuan opera's **帮腔 (bāngqiāng — supporting chorus)**, but in the banqiang system (板腔体, bǎnqiāngtǐ) it is an invention of the modern play. Scene 9 uses a full-company chorus as the grand close, replacing the exit poem.
6. **The interface from speech to song has only one form in the whole play: an address term.** "Yinhuan! (sings) Ever since you wrote saying you wanted to go down to the countryside…" "My old sister-in-law!" "Uncle Laiqing (来清叔)! (sings) A thousand mouths cannot tell all the bitter lessons" "Mother! (sings) My mother, don't do this anymore" "Mother-in-law, sit down quickly. (sings) Mother-in-law, sit down"—**call the person first, then start singing**, plainer and steadier than a singing cue (叫板, jiàobǎn). **This occupies the same functional position as the record by 吴梅 (Wu Mei) of *The Interrupted Dream* (《惊梦》): first the spoken line "If I had not come to the garden, how would I know spring is this beautiful?", and only then [Zaoluopao] (〔皂罗袍〕)—the two systems share it.**
7. **Virtual and real props are used together.** All props are real objects (water bucket, hoe, seed sack, flashlight, rubber shoes, apples, pastries, diary, five yuan, broom, photograph), but the body movement is still conventionalized dance: carrying water "one stagger and one twist," "one shake and three wobbles," the braid wound around the carrying pole, "'clang'—the carrying pole falls," hoeing with the front leg bent and the back leg stretched. **Conclusion: what the modern play loses is the virtuality of props (砌末, qìmò); what it keeps is the dance-like body movement (身段, shēnduàn).**
8. **Time spans between scenes are stated directly in stage directions**: "immediately following the previous scene," "several days later," "months later," "the day after the previous scene"—no convention of time transition, and no avoidance of jumps. Four of the nine scenes are "immediately following," forming two continuous blocks of time (scenes 1–3 are one day, scenes 6–8 are one day); scenes 4, 5, and 9 are each an isolated point in time.

### 5.3 Version Differences and Period Phrases

- **The premiere company was different**: the premiere in Zhengzhou on May 19, 1958, was by the **Henan Yuju Theatre Troupe No. 3** (Yang Lanchun, playwright and director, with 许欣 (Xu Xin) as co-director; 魏云 (Wei Yun) as Yinhuan, 王善朴 (Wang Shanpu) as Shuanbao). This script is the post-Cultural Revolution revival by the **Henan Provincial Yuju Troupe No. 1**, with Shuanbao played by 韩玉生 (Han Yusheng).
- **Four versions of the opening prop** (the easiest version marker to check): the 1958 premiere script has Yinhuan holding *Theatre Gazette* (《戏剧报》) → the stage version changed it to *People's Daily* (《人民日报》) → the 1963 film version changed it to *China Youth Daily* (《中国青年报》) → **this 1978 edition has "a copy of *China Youth* magazine" (《中国青年》)**.
- **The history of revision during the Cultural Revolution**: the play was banned during the Cultural Revolution; "from October 1969 onward, the *Chaoyang Village* creation group, following the instructions of 江青 (Jiang Qing), wrote seven revised versions one after another and conducted five trial stagings," without a satisfactory result; it was revived in early 1977. **This script is that revival script, and therefore retains obvious period phrases**: in scene 7, the branch secretary's long speech contains "the three major differences" (三大差别) and "the Chiang bandit gang wanted it long ago, the imperialists want to seize it too; the revisionists gape their mouths"; scene 3 has the couplet "The General Line (总路线) shines ten thousand zhang / The Great Leap Forward (大跃进) advances a thousand li a day." **These are period limitations, to be used only as corpus, not as rules.** (Source: the Wikipedia entry "Chaoyang Village (Yuju).")
- **Creation talks from outside the book** (李红艳 (Li Hongyan), 《豫剧〈朝阳沟〉的经典"密码"及启示》 [The Classic "Code" of the Yuju *Chaoyang Village* and Its Lessons]): ① "When I wrote *Chaoyang Village* at the time, the first few scenes had drafts, the middle two scenes had lyrics, and the last two scenes had neither drafts nor lyrics"; the final scene was completed by **oral dictation on the spot, with the actors taking down the words on the spot and the composer setting the music on the spot**; ② "*Chaoyang Village* basically **starts from writing characters, while song is dominant**, using lyrics to express the true feelings of each character"; ③ "**How deep life is, how high art is**"—he and the Third Troupe sank roots in Dengfeng and Linxian; ④ writing it in seven days was not an accident but "a long gestation; once it is born, it is bound to be quick."
- **There are only three pieces of first-hand information**: the inside front cover (genre, playwright, year of the performance script); the copyright page (first Beijing edition, March 1978; book number 10019·2595; price 0.40 yuan; five inserted plates); and the production-photo caption ("On New Year's Day 1964, after watching the Yuju *Chaoyang Village*, the great leader and teacher Chairman Mao (毛泽东, Máo Zédōng), together with Chairman Zhu De (朱德, Zhū Dé), met the performers"; the performers are Wei Yun as Yinhuan, Han Yusheng as Shuanbao, 杨华瑞 (Yang Huarui) as Yinhuan's mother, 陈新理 (Chen Xinli) as the old branch secretary, 芦兰香 (Lu Lanxiang) as Shuanbao's mother, and 袁秀荣 (Yuan Xiurong) as Er Daniang). The score is credited to "王基笑 (Wang Jixiao), 梁思辉 (Liang Sihui), 姜宏轩 (Jiang Hongxuan), and others, who arranged the music."
