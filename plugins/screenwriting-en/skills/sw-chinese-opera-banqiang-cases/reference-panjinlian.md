> **Source text and collation status (must be cited together with the data)**: Heilongjiang Beifang Literature and Art Publishing House, "Half-Hour Book Series," 1st edition, September 1987, 120 pages; the script is signed "revised after the September 1986 Beijing–Shanghai–Nanjing performances." ⚠️ **The source PDF is physically missing pages (verified against the page images; this is not an OCR failure)**: p0010–0026, the whole of the wedge (楔子, xiēzi — prologue); p0067–0070, the poisoning of Wu Da (武大) and the killing of the sister-in-law; p0074–0077 and p0079–0085, where most of the confession survives only as a few lines printed at the top of each page. **Every affected entry is marked 〔残〕 (cán — fragmentary) and must not be treated as complete evidence**; no full text has been found online either. **The script marks no banshi (板式, bǎnshì — metrical pattern) and no qupai (曲牌, qǔpái — fixed tune pattern) at all**, only "song / bangqiang / chorus / back-sung aside / offstage song / wordless song"; the only two vocal-style labels are "sung in Henan bangzi style" and "sings an operatic aria"—**any vocal style the script does not mark carries no structural function**. The play is written in the banqiang manner (板腔体, bǎnqiāngtǐ — the paired-line system; here the tanxi [弹戏, tánxì] and huqin [胡琴, húqín] styles of Sichuan opera), not in the qupai system (曲牌体, qǔpáitǐ — the fixed-tune system). Quotations follow the OCR; obvious misprints have been corrected ("投井的金刚" ["the well-drowned guardian"] → 金钏 [Jinchuan]; "怒汁牛斗" → 怒冲牛斗 ["anger soaring to the Dipper"]; "谁是发／谁是优" → 谁是友／谁是仇 ["who is a friend / who is an enemy"]).

## §6 *Pan Jinlian* Structure Table (14 Units) and Characters

**The play has no "Scene X" at all.** Only two large-type title pages appear in the book: **楔子 (xiēzi — wedge, prologue)** (p0010) and **尾声 (wěishēng — coda, epilogue)** (p0071); between them, p0024–0070 is **one unbroken continuous performance**, with segment changes cut by lighting, the cloud terrace (云阶, yúnjiē — a permanent raised platform), gongs and drums, banners masking the stage, and cross-time characters entering. The only intermission is written into the dialogue (p0040). The "segments" in the table below are **the note-taker's divisions by event, not numbers in the book**.

**Synopsis (p0005, copied verbatim)**: "In the book *Water Margin*, Pan Jinlian is definitively judged: licentious, a murderer of her own husband, unfaithful to the end, a source of disaster and resentment, beyond pardon. The absurdist Sichuan opera *Pan Jinlian* prompts people to think about this question. **It does not force the author's view on people; it displays the several turning points in the fate of this woman's young life**, and, in a bizarre way, crosses national borders and compresses history, bringing Wu Zetian, Shi Nai'an, Anna Karenina, the Seventh-Rank Sesame Official, a contemporary judge, Shasha from Garden Street No. 5, and the delinquents together on one stage to narrate, express feeling, and compare fates…"

## §7 *Pan Jinlian* Cross-Time Character Table (10 Figures)

**The governing rule: the play's cross-time characters are not hallucinations but a "commentary bench." They share the stage with Pan Jinlian, can converse with her, can intervene, but cannot change the plot**; each intervention pulls the audience out of the story for a comparison and then puts them back. Entrances and exits are always made by means of the **cloud terrace (云阶, yúnjiē — a permanent raised platform)** and lighting, **not the entrance/exit-door convention (上下场门, shàngxiàchǎng mén — entrance and exit doors; 程式, chéngshì — a fixed performance convention) of Chinese opera**.

| Character | Source | Entrance | In what capacity they speak | Relationship to Pan Jinlian |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Shi Nai'an (施耐庵)** | The author of *Water Margin* himself | Opens the wedge, introducing himself: "My great work has been handed down for a thousand years"〔残〕 | **The original author / the author in the dock**: "A source of disaster, a source of disaster—only women and petty men are hard to deal with!" (惟女子与小人为难养也) (p0014) | Does not converse; he is the one being interrogated; Lü Shasha retorts to his face, "Sir, this is your 'subjective camera'!" |
| **Lü Shasha (吕莎莎)** | A character from the contemporary novel *Garden Street No. 5* (《花园街五号》), a modern female journalist | Enters in the wedge and moves in and out throughout the play; carries a camera and flash | **Link-narrator + control group**: both a commentator outside the "play" ("the scene just now is a sketch by a minor author") and a photojournalist inside it | **The only modern person who sings a duet with Jinlian**; says her divorce brought her happiness; "suppose I were Pan Jinlian" / "suppose Pan Jinlian were me" |
| **Jia Baoyu (贾宝玉)** | *Dream of the Red Chamber* (《红楼梦》) | Walks out on his own during the street parade, looking left and right | **Representative of another way of writing**: "If Baoyu had written *Water Margin*—over the wall and on horseback (墙头马上, qiáng tóu mǎ shàng), a red thread would tie them!" | Wants to make a match, but the tiger stops him with "You cannot break a frame thousands of years old" |
| **Anna Karenina (安娜·卡列尼娜)** | Tolstoy | Appears on the cloud terrace and **introduces herself by singing an operatic aria** ("Happy families are all alike…") | **The foreign specimen with the same fate but a different road**, entering twice: she urges Jinlian to "break out of an unhappy family," then urges, "Don't kill! If you must kill, kill yourself" | Demonstrates by lying down on the tracks on the spot, **then climbs out of the pool of blood and goes on singing**—singing after death, the hardest absurdist stroke in the play |
| **Wu Zetian (武则天)** | Tang dynasty | Court music and a phoenix-carriage honor guard file out; a voice offstage calls, "Emperor Zetian has arrived" | **The judge who crosses dynasties and ages**, singing "I come through the air across dynasties and ages" | Summons Jinlian to court to "give you justice"; Jinlian, "as if entering the Land of Illusion (太虚幻境, Tàixū Huànjìng — the otherworldly realm in *Dream of the Red Chamber*), steps backward step by step, gives a strange cry, covers her face, and flees"—**the party concerned refuses to appear, and the trial becomes a debate in absentia** |
| **Shangguan Wan'er (上官婉儿)** | Tang dynasty | Accompanies Wu Zetian | **Debater for the affirmative**: Wu Song "keeps to ethical principle and moral integrity, a credit to Chinese virtue… admirable and lovable!" | Does not speak directly to Jinlian; only comments |
| **Tang Cheng, the Seventh-Rank Sesame Official (七品芝麻官唐成)** | The Henan opera *The Seventh-Rank Sesame Official* (《七品芝麻官》) | "Your Majesty crosses dynasties to descend; your minister crosses ages to receive you" | **Living proof that an honest official is useless**; he searches the entire legal code and finds no article to cite | **Sings in Henan-opera style**, takes off his gauze cap, shows his bald head: "Not a braid on my head—not a way out (没得儿辫发／办法)!" |

| **The modern delinquent (现代阿飞)** | Contemporary | Aviator sunglasses, long curly hair, floral shirt; enters behind the three Song-dynasty ruffians | **The contemporary version of the same type**: "The ruffians have descendants, the delinquent has ancestors; five hundred years ago they were one family, birds of a feather, regardless of dynasty" | Joins the ruffians in bullying Jinlian, then is hired by Ximen Qing to stage the "bitter-flesh ruse" (苦肉计, kǔròujì — self-injury to win the opponent's trust) |

## §8 *Pan Jinlian* Aria Table and Sichuan Opera Rhyme

The book **marks only "song / bangqiang / chorus / back-sung aside / offstage song / wordless song," and uses neither banshi nor qupai**; the only two vocal-style labels are "sung in Henan bangzi style" and "sings an operatic aria." **The rhyme-category column is the note-taker's judgment, based on the fact that Sichuan opera uses Sichuan Mandarin: front and back nasal finals are not distinguished, so 人辰 (rénchén — the -en/-in rhyme category) and 中东 (zhōngdōng — the -eng/-ing/-ong rhyme category) rhyme freely together; the entering tone (入声, rùshēng) is redistributed into the rising tone (阳平, yángpíng), so "色客热洁" (sè kè rè jié) can rhyme with the 乜斜 (miēxié — the -ie/-üe rhyme category) category—these two rules are the key to the play's rhymes, and judging them by the Mandarin thirteen rhyme categories (十三辙, shísān zhé) will produce errors.**

### 8.1 Five Representative Aria Passages

**① Ruffians at the Door · Pan Jinlian's Furious Rebuke (p0031)**—rhyme defines character: her hardest passage in the play uses the 言前 rhyme; "a man who does not wear a headscarf" is her self-definition, set against her later downfall.
> A slap lands, sparks fly, / Let this teach you who Pan Jinlian is! / I am a man who does not wear a headscarf! / I am a heroine who fears no ruffian! / A man could stand on my fist—I have the nerve! / Horses could gallop on my arms—hooves churning! / The rose is scattered, but its thorns remain! / I rage at the pack of clowns—who dares come near? (耳光一记火星溅，／叫你认得潘金莲！／我是个不戴头巾的男子汉！／我是个不怕无赖的女英豪！／拳头上立人，人有胆！／胳膊上走马，马蹄翻！／玫瑰飘零刺还在！／怒斥群丑谁敢攀？)

**② Tiger-Killing and the Street Parade · Cross-Time Duet (p0036)**—**the one technique in this play most worth copying**: the Song-dynasty Pan Jinlian and the contemporary female journalist exchange one line at a time, share the 言前 rhyme, and each speaks from her own identity (one watches a hero, the other thinks up a headline), yet the two voices form one complete aria.
> Pan Jinlian (sings): Whose bridegroom is that in red satin? / Lü Shasha (sings): The cold-faced second brother mounts his carved saddle. / Pan Jinlian (sings): The whole town crowds to see the hero, / Lü Shasha (sings): A tiger-killing tale sung for a thousand years. / Pan Jinlian (sings): I admire him for ridding the people of a scourge— / His heroic figure flashes before my eyes at Jingyang Ridge— (潘金莲（唱）谁家新郎披红缎？／吕莎莎（唱）冷面二郎腾雕鞍。／潘金莲（唱）满城争看英雄汉，／吕莎莎（唱）千秋传颂打虎篇。／潘金莲（唱）钦佩他为民除祸患，／景阳冈英姿闪眼前——)

**③ The Tiger Pulls Off Its Tiger Head (p0037)**—a prop brought to life + reduplicated nursery-rhyme form, saying the heaviest thing in the lightest register.
> The Tiger: Second Lord Bao, you cannot break a frame thousands of years old! … (sings) Brother-in-law meets sister-in-law, / frames plus rules! / Don't come trying to tie threads, / listen to me state the case— / Wu Song only beats little kitty-cats, / he dare not violate the big rules and roads! > His return is far and uncertain; the whole sky is a blur of snow! / I think the bitterest thing in life is parting. / On the snowfield only the prints of wild-goose claws remain, / I see off the distant traveler and regret there is no green willow branch to break… / The horse neighs; the jade steed is fortunate to go with you! / Fierce and glorious, along the way it scatters the local bullies! / The road is long; at the cockcrow inn we pass the cold night! / My heart is anxious; in the little town the sister-in-law watches the lone moon! / And so you go— / A far peng-bird journey, heaven and earth wide, / A blaze of purple and red draws the travelers, / Zhangtai in the Eastern Capital has its "willow"! / Broken Bridge in Jiangnan has its "snow"! / The "osmanthus" of three autumns is fine! / The "lotus" of ten li is unsurpassed! (归期杳，迷迷茫茫满天雪！／想人生最苦是离别。／雪地空留鸿爪印，／送远客惜无青青柳条折……／马萧萧，玉骢有幸随君去！／威烈烈，沿途惊散地头蛇！／路漫漫，鸡声茅店度寒夜！／心切切，小城嫂嫂望孤月！／此去也——／鹏程远，天地阔，／万紫千红吸游客，／东京章台"柳"！／江南断桥"雪"！／三秋"桂子"好！／十里"荷花"绝！)

**⑤ Wu Song Turns Against Her (p0047–0048)**—**a change of rhyme is the rupture** (一七 → 由求); "I will build the chastity arch" is set beside "the iron fist that beats tigers and subdues beasts," welding ethics and violence into one sentence.

## §9 *Pan Jinlian*: Bangqiang and Wordless Song, Confession and Debate—Key Points

### 9.1 Bangqiang (a Text Device Specific to Sichuan Opera)

**The script writes bangqiang in three ways, each with its own job**:

1. `帮腔：（唱）` (Bangqiang: [sings])—**treated as a role and listed in the character column**; the first sung line of the play belongs to it (p0010);
2. `幕内：（帮腔）` (Offstage: [bangqiang])—when an offstage voice cuts into a character's aria (p0041 "A chasm was drawn long ago"; p0044 "Erlang, you—you—can you hear the tone beyond the strings?"; p0045 "Second Lord Wu, Lord Guan—but never Baoyu, the second lord who leans on red and borrows green!"; p0055 "Delivering the spring shirt, she goes to Wang's—"; p0061 "Lust is all in what is left unsaid!");
3. `〔帮腔。` (Bangqiang.)—**a cue only, with no words supplied** (p0048, where Jinlian faints), equivalent to a rest written purely as sound.

**List of functions (directly transferable when writing a modern play)**: ① say on the character's behalf what he or she will not say ("Lust is all in what is left unsaid"); ② narrate the character's action ("Delivering the spring shirt, she goes to Wang's") and sing the next line after it; ③ pass judgment and point up the subtext ("Can you hear the tone beyond the strings?"); ④ cut into spoken dialogue as a sharp interjection ("Hard to cure, hard to handle!"). **It is not backup singing; it is a second speaker on stage.**

**Failure condition**: bangqiang belongs to the **traditional apparatus of Sichuan opera's high-pitched style (高腔, gāoqiāng — a qupai-system style)**; it has no counterpart when transplanted to Peking, Henan, or Yue opera, so do not copy it across opera genres; before borrowing it, ask the orchestra whether it has a bangqiang chorus. Read together with the nine bangqiang passages of *The Scholar of Mount Ba* (《巴山秀才》) in `sw-chinese-opera-qupai-cases`.

### 9.2 Offstage Chorus and Wordless Song

Three kinds: `幕内：（无词歌骤起）` (Offstage: [wordless song suddenly rises]) (p0033, Wu Dalang crawls through the legs, Jinlian covers her face), `〔无词歌·啊……` (Wordless song · Ah…) (p0048, Wu Song pushes Jinlian down), `仪仗队：（合唱）` (Honor guard: [chorus]) (p0049, repeating Wu Zetian's last line), and the coda's `合唱：` (Chorus:). **The rule: the two most unbearable moments, where words fail most (crawling through the legs, being pushed down), are given no words at all, only a single "Ah."**

### 9.3 Other Text Devices

- **The cloud terrace (云阶, yúnjiē)—a permanent raised platform for cross-time characters**. Anna "steps down from the cloud terrace"; Lü Shasha "reappears on the cloud terrace"; "Shasha, Baoyu, and Hongniang crowd onto the cloud terrace to interject and cheer"; "Wu Zetian and Shangguan Wan'er watch from the cloud terrace." **Cross-time characters always use the cloud terrace; people of the play's own dynasty always use the platform below. Height in space is the stratification of eras, with no scene changes.**
- **Four techniques for jumping between times and spaces**: ① **freeze-frame in a picture frame**—the flag corps forms a picture frame plus a flash of the magnesium lamp, "freezing Wu Song into a still photograph" (the script uses film terminology outright; the Shi Nai'an passage is also marked "subjective camera"); ② **banners masking the stage to elide process**—"after the banners pass, Wu Dalang is already on stage holding Wu Song by the hand; when they meet Jinlian, one phrase elides the process"; ③ **gongs and drums waking someone**—Jia Baoyu shouts to the runners, "Play a 'Mountain-Crossing Tune' and shout her awake"; ④ **announcing an intermission inside the play**—writing the interval into the drama.
- **The clown's modern language**: the modern delinquent speaks English ("Hello, have a 'peace weed'!"), Japanese ("Ie!"), and lines like "Boss, this scene is going pretty well, isn't it?" "What about the bonus?" "Go collect it at my accounts office"—**the actor openly admits on the spot that he is acting and demands his pay**. The ruffians punish Wu Da by making him crawl through their legs, in Sichuan dialect: "a turtle crawling in the sand." Pan Jinlian curses the ruffians in a Sichuan-style patter rhyme: "'Head money' is like a fine-toothed comb—the poor man's hair is thin; officials comb it, soldiers comb it, the yamen runners trim it, the ruffians shave it; you've come too late—there's no hair (发) left, no way (法) out."
- **Deformation of conventions (程式, chéngshì)**: self-introduction (自报家门, zìbào jiāmén) is remade in two ways (Anna uses a famous line from a novel, and Shasha points it out on the spot: "A few famous lines—she's introducing herself"; Shi Nai'an uses his own book); **the entrance poem (定场诗, dìngchǎngshī) and exit poem (下场诗, xiàchǎngshī) are not used at all**, and exits are replaced by commentary (the wedge), self-mockery (segment E), and debate (the coda); mimed performance (虚拟表演, xūnjǐ biǎoyǎn) is kept as it is ("using traditional opera gestures, she performs 'cake-making,' miming each step"; "foreign boxing against ancient technique"; Jinlian "shakes her water sleeves [水袖, shuǐxiù — long silk sleeve extensions] wildly, hesitates again and again, then flicks her sleeve and refuses").

### 9.4 The Author's Confession (p0074–0087) and Its Own Genre

⚠️ **p0074–0077 and p0079–0085 are physically truncated, with only 1–4 lines surviving on each page**; only p0078, p0086, and p0087 survive complete. **The full text of the confession is unavailable; any item below marked 〔残〕 must not be cited as a complete argument.**

**The confession itself is a play-script dialogue**, structurally identical to *Pan Jinlian*: the author dreams, and people from ancient and modern times, Chinese and foreign, come one by one to debate with him. The interlocutors identified so far: **I** (Wei Minglun), (Kafka)〔残〕, **You / Old Master** (您／老前辈, Ouyang Yuqian [欧阳予倩]), **You** (尤, Ionesco), **A Voice** (an unnamed critic). Both the opening and the ending make clear it is a dream; the ending: "(How close! The responsibility for hastening the decline of Chinese opera will fall on our generation; hearing this, I lost my nerve, woke from the dream, and did not know that the east was already light!) January 1986, in Bashu."

**Surviving lines, item by item**: ① p0075: "…Kafka, author of 'At the Construction Site of the Great Wall of China' (《在中国万里长城工地上》), he can 'transmit…'"—**using Kafka's writing about the Chinese Great Wall as counter-evidence that "a foreigner can also write about another country across time and space"** (a guess at the direction; the original is incomplete); ② p0076: "…**Wu Song (武松) kills Pan Jinlian, Shi Xiu (石秀) kills Pan Qiaoyun (潘巧云), Song Jiang (宋江) kills Yan Xijiao (阎惜姣), Lu Junyi (卢俊义) kills Lady Jia (贾氏), Shi Jin (史进) kills Li Ruilan (李瑞兰), Lei Heng (雷横) beats Bai Xiuying (白秀英) to death**;…"—**a roll call of women killed in *Water Margin***, his hardest reason for rewriting Pan Jinlian: **not to overturn one person's case, but to point out a habitual way of writing in the original**; ③ p0079: "Valuable, Old Master, but unfortunately your answer is not accurate enough"—**to Ouyang Yuqian: affirming the direction, rejecting the answer**; ④ p0082: "…the 'backstage' that backs you up—the Marriage Law!"

**Complete page one (p0078): the criterion for choosing a subject**
> **I**　The May Fourth Movement sounded the bugle, and the question of women's liberation was put on the agenda… Your *Pan Jinlian* arose in response. Seen with a macroscopic eye, this is a play that follows the current of the times, not one that swims against it. You look back at the fate of women in antiquity, and you do not stop at artistic types like Zhu Yingtai (祝英台 — heroine of *The Butterfly Lovers*), which are "a beautiful tale of a thousand autumns" on which ancient and modern opinion is strangely unanimous. **Why is it that for anti-feudal women like Zhu Yingtai, even the most feudal moral guardians of every dynasty joined in the applause? This at least shows that Zhu Yingtai-style rebellion has not touched the foundation of the feudal marriage system, and is not yet a "flood and wild beast."**

> **Addendum on technique (not fully covered in Section 5 ⑥ of SKILL.md)**: **The criterion for choosing a subject—a rebellion that even the most conservative person applauds is not a rebellion.** This is his entire reason for choosing Pan Jinlian rather than Zhu Yingtai. *Self-check*: tell your protagonist's story to the most old-fashioned person you can find; if he nods, the subject is too shallow.

**Complete page two (p0086): self-positioning = magical realism**
> **I**　Yes, **it is a neighbor of the Absurdists while not far from realism; it is the branch of modernist literature that faces life more directly**… Magical realism is rooted in the soil of Latin American folk literature and also draws nourishment from European literature; it combines the realist tradition with modernist innovation, **habitually using absurdist means to reflect major social problems and expose sharp social contradictions. It completely breaks the boundaries between life and death, time and space, reality and dream**…

**He does not claim the "Absurdists"; what he claims is magical realism.** Anna climbing out of the pool of blood and singing on after lying on the tracks, and the tiger pulling off its tiger head and speaking human language—these are precisely "breaking life and death"; the cloud terrace and the cross-dynasty characters are precisely "breaking time and space."

**Complete page three (p0087): he writes the opposing view into the essay verbatim and gives it the last word**
> **I**　…Chinese opera is in a slump, and it fills one with anxiety. How to revive it? **In theory, let a hundred schools contend; in practice, even more, let a hundred flowers bloom.**
> **You (Ionesco)**　How will the audience in your country evaluate this wildflower of yours? … Haha, full houses every night, eager audiences, young people especially welcoming it… Who is frowning? Ah, the critics are speaking—
> **A Voice**　What kind of play is this? Its content defends adultery and encourages women to go home and kill their husbands! As for form, **it is not standard absurdist drama—bad! And if it were standard, it would be even worse**—that is an anti-Marxist dramatic school! … If everyone makes "absurd" plays like *Pan Jinlian*, it will not help opera reform; it will only hasten opera's decline!

### 9.5 The Debate: Arguments on Both Sides (from He Kaisan's critical biography, 何开三评传, p0115–0119)

**He Kaisan (何开三) describes the play's form (the factual basis for writing the skill)**: "The author breaks the time-space outlook of ancient and modern, Chinese and foreign, and brings all kinds of characters on stage to comment on the rights and wrongs of Pan Jinlian. **In the singing, besides Sichuan-opera vocal styles, he has blended in Yue opera, Henan opera, modern pop songs, and arias—a 'grand joint concert of Chinese and foreign, ancient and modern.'** Isn't that a mess, a breach of the rules? The fact is, **people have not denied that this is a Sichuan opera; in some respects the Sichuan flavor is quite authentic!**"
→ ⚠️ **The script marks only Henan opera (the Sesame Official "sings in Henan bangzi style") and the aria (Anna); Yue opera and pop songs are not marked in the script and belong to the performance level. "Mixing vocal styles without losing the genre's own flavor" is evidenced only by performance, not fully marked in the script, and must be stated when using this material.**

**The battleground and its scale**: the two sides **first opened fire in the *Chengdu Evening News* (《成都晚报》)**, then opened a second front—**the magazine *Drama and Film* (《戏剧与电影》)**—and the fire soon spread to other publications inside and outside the province. "The trend is that the controversy is narrowing while the discussion deepens." More than a hundred letters came from audience members; "all but one voted in favor of *Pan Jinlian*."

| Side | Argument |
|---|---|
| **Affirmative** | Cao Yu (曹禺), after seeing the play in Shanghai, wrote: "**Nothing is absent, nothing is excluded; truly an unprecedented liberation of thought, gathering the achievements of ancient and modern, Chinese and foreign.**" / Chen Baichen (陈白尘), who called him "Wei the Bold": "A play, whether it's a red cat or a black cat, **is a good cat if it can catch the mouse**." / Wu Zuguang (吴祖光) praised it in an article in *Literature and Art Gazette* (《文艺报》); he had earlier called him "an eccentric genius of the pear garden" (梨园怪杰, líyuán guàijié) / An unsigned review: "**the boldest and most breakthrough artistic attempt in our country's opera reform to date**" |
| **Negative** | ① The form is flashy and the content out of bounds; ② rehabilitating Pan Jinlian is not worthwhile; ③ it "promotes licentiousness" and advocates that "poisoning one's husband is justified" (He Kaisan notes that this "does not proceed from the work itself but is forced onto it"); ④ it is "child's play that fools the audience," and he is denounced as a "theater demon"; ⑤ **the form is trapped either way**: "it is not standard absurdist drama—bad! and if it were standard, it would be even worse"; ⑥ "it goes in for commercialism, which does not help reform but hastens decline" |

**His response**: "It is a good thing for a work to be controversial; what one fears is a stone dropped in water with no ripple at all"; his four-character attitude: "**neither elated by honor nor alarmed by disgrace**" (荣辱不惊, róng rǔ bù jīng). On the charge of "hodgepodge": "'**Hodgepodge**' itself is a good dish… **the key is to get the color, aroma, and taste right; as long as it is a fine dish, it can be as mixed as you like**." On the charge of "licentiousness" (the only time he showed resentment): "**Why is the notion that 'lust is the first of all evils' so deeply rooted in some people's minds, while the new notions that 'mediocrity is the first of all evils' and 'envy is the first of all evils' are so hard to form?**" **He refuses to enter the fray himself**: when an old comrade suggested, "Why don't you just go on stage yourself and argue the rights and wrongs with Shi Nai'an," he said he "really had no time to join that self-organized 'special mixed commentary team' of complicated composition"—**the author refuses to appear in the play and give an answer**, consistent with the synopsis's "it does not force the author's view on people."

### 9.6 Source Material → Dialogue Chain and Other Creation Notes (He Kaisan's critical biography, 何开三评传, p0088–0114)

- **The seed was planted in 1950**: just after liberation, a troupe performed *Pan Jinlian*, a play exposing arranged marriage; the nine-year-old Wei Minglun **played Yun Ge (郓哥 — the young fruit seller in *Water Margin*)**; while reading Guo Moruo's *Youth* (郭沫若《少年时代》), he connected it with the "Pan Jinlian secretly loves her brother-in-law" material on stage, and put a "bizarre" question to his father, who worked as a playwright. His father cut him off: "The child is letting his imagination run wild; when he grows up he will surely bring down a sky-high disaster."
- **The only fully traceable real-life utterance → dialogue chain in the whole batch** (p0092 ↔ p0064): in 1948 at the Tuo River wharf, a "muddy-water" paoge (袍哥, páogē — a member of a secret brotherhood) forced a female performer and the nine-year-old boy to perform *Upstream Nunnery* (《上游庵》); "Youngest Uncle" (么爸, yāobà) cursed at him: "**A performer turned into a loach still fears the mud?** Mind that I don't 'pluck' you."—thirty-eight years later this gangster's line entered *Pan Jinlian* verbatim: when Ximen Qing forces the poison, he says, "**Turned into a loach, still afraid of the mud?**"
- **The two directions must not be mixed** (p0104): *Yi Dadan* (《易胆大》) works on "**the modernization of Chinese opera**" (戏曲现代化), while *The Fourth Girl* (《四姑娘》) works on "**the operatization of modern drama**" (现代剧戏曲化). **This sentence is the switch that opens the section on "the absurd and the cross-time."**
- **The "three knocks at the door" (三叩门) rewrite** (p0112, the most complete account of a revision): what should Jin Dongshui (金东水) do inside when the Fourth Girl knocks a third time? "He wrote several versions of a 'reply through the door' aria, none satisfactory"—complaining was no good, saying what he did not mean was unbearable, and grand speeches were worse. Then he remembered 1958 in the countryside: a friend visited his lover at night, the girl said nothing, stood up, and with a "puff" blew out the oil lamp. So he changed it to an interior monologue: "**Words cannot express my bitter heart; with tears in my eyes I blow out the lamp as my reply!**" The diagnosis of the root problem was that the original draft "went wrong on sets of 'revolutionary grand talk,' **violating the character's nature in that specific situation**."
- **The film freeze-frame = the opera liangxiang** (p0106): Wei Minglun: "The **freeze-frame** in film, I think, is the **liangxiang (亮相, liàngxiàng — a posed stage tableau)** in Chinese opera!"—the play's "the magnesium lamp flashes… freezing Wu Song into a still photograph" is another use of this principle.
- **Keep the revision counts side by side**: *The Fourth Girl* went through 12 drafts, *The Scholar of Mount Ba* 11 (plus 6 more after publication), and *Year After Year, Chongyang* (《岁岁重阳》) left "a whole back basket of discarded drafts"; **only *Pan Jinlian* was "written at the speed of forty days"** (p0090). **This stands alongside his self-description as a "bitter-chanting school" (苦吟派, kǔyín pài) writer (p0109–0110); keep both side by side, do not take only one.**
- **Take the form, remake the content** (p0095–0096): *The Defense of Tianjing* (《天京保卫战》) draws on the banned play *The Iron Rooster* (《铁公鸡》)—the original stood on the Manchu/Qing side, but "its whole set of extraordinary martial-arts forms was quite worth taking," so he "took over the original's artistic form and thoroughly remade its content."
- **"To adapt is to conquer" (适应是为了征服)** (p0103); **"one play, one method" (一戏一招)** (p0114); **the three seeks and three abstentions (三求三戒)** (p0113: seek the new, the deep, the true; abstain from arrogance, laziness, and slapdash production—"abstaining from slapdash production is the hardest"); **the threshold of accumulated life material** ("store plenty of sesame and you can press oil" [多存芝麻好打油]; he explicitly turned down two subjects, *The Greek Coffin Mystery* [《希腊棺材之谜》] and *Wreaths at the Foot of the Mountain* [《高山下的花环》]).
