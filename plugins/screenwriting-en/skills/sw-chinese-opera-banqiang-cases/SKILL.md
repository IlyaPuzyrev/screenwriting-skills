# Banqiang Full-Script Case Studies (板腔体全本语料库, bǎnqiāngtǐ quánběn yǔliàokù)

> **The output language is the language of the question**; always anchor terms back to their originals, see [sw-workflow/terms.md](../sw-workflow/terms.md), and do not invent translations. Opera terms (板式 bǎnshì — metrical pattern; 辙口 zhékǒu — rhyme categories; 行当 hángdang — role types; 程式 chéngshì — performance conventions; 宾白 bīnbái — spoken dialogue; 垛句 duǒjù — inserted short lines; 帮腔 bāngqiāng — offstage answering chorus) have no English equivalents; in any language, keep the original term and add a one-sentence explanation.

**Method: see [sw-chinese-opera-banqiang](../sw-chinese-opera-banqiang/SKILL.md); this skill holds only definitive texts and creation records.** Every case has six fixed items: ① the work and its version, ② the structural layer, ③ the aria layer, ④ the convention layer, ⑤ the creation process, ⑥ the transferable techniques. The method is not rewritten; write only "see sw-chinese-opera-banqiang, Section N." **Use only first-hand definitive texts: where the author's own account conflicts with the definitive text, the definitive text wins and the account is treated as intention; whenever the base text is not the premiere text, has missing pages, or awaits verification, record that in ① and carry the note with the line.**

Scene-by-scene structure tables, aria tables, metrical-pattern statistics, convention lists, two-version diffs, and creation-record indexes are all in the reference files; the main text keeps only pointers: 《锁麟囊》 (The Jewelry Purse) and 《沙家浜》 (Shajiabang) [reference-jingju.md](reference-jingju.md) (§1–§7); 《白蛇传》 (The White Snake) [reference-baishezhuan.md](reference-baishezhuan.md) (§8–§12); 《朝阳沟》 (Chaoyang Village) [reference-chaoyanggou.md](reference-chaoyanggou.md) (§1–§5); 《潘金莲》 (Pan Jinlian) [reference-panjinlian.md](reference-panjinlian.md) (§6–§9); Luo Huaizhen's Yue operas [reference-luo-yueju.md](reference-luo-yueju.md) (§1–§3, §7) and local opera [reference-luo-difangxi.md](reference-luo-difangxi.md) (§4–§8); creation records [reference-chuangzuoji.md](reference-chuangzuoji.md).

## 1. 《锁麟囊》 (The Jewelry Purse): Metrical-Pattern Layout in a Song-Heavy Play, All Three Layers Present

**① Version**　Weng Ouhong, playwright; premiered by Cheng Yanqiu in 1940. Base text = 《京剧曲谱·锁麟囊》 (Peking Opera Score: The Jewelry Purse), edited by Wang Yinqiu, notation by Gu Yongxiang (Shanghai Literature and Art, 1983). **This is the revised score edition, not the premiere text** (He Man's preface records that Cheng Yanqiu "toiled tirelessly at innovation until the eve of his death"); where it does not match the author's account, assume the revised version first. **Any quotation of "cut into three sections to match the three yields of the seat" must carry the note: "the score edition has four sections; the seating order is side chair → guest seat → seat of honor → central seat."** The lyrics are transcribed from page images and are not marked for verification. **The only play in the whole batch with all three layers: a creation record + peer criticism + a definitive performance text.**

**② Structural layer**　Twelve scenes, 65 arias, about 196 lines; four scenes have no song, and four are key scenes (2, 5, 9, 12). **Scene 12 alone has 28 arias, 43% of the total**—of the four key scenes in the author's account, the last three are all compressed into the final scene, which is the biggest structural difference from the definitive text. Scene 1 gives the protagonist neither an entrance nor a song; two chou (丑, chǒu — clown) roles establish "snobbery" in spoken dialogue as the running word; **11 of the 21 characters in the cast list are chou, more than half**. See §1.

**③ Aria layer**　**Only 20 of the 65 arias are on-the-beat sections (上板段, shàngbǎn duàn); sanban and yaoban (散板 sǎnbǎn — free-rhythm pattern; 摇板 yáobǎn — shaking pattern) total 27 arias, forty percent**; all on-the-beat sections are concentrated in the four key scenes (Section 3). **〔二六〕 (èrliù — two-six pattern) occurs only twice and is symmetrical at head and tail** (scene 5, the starting point of the good deed; scene 12, the endpoint of the good reward), and both times it follows immediately after 〔流水〕 (liúshuǐ — flowing pattern). **Vocal systems separate people and sections, not degrees of emotion**: the rich side is erhuang (二黄, èrhuáng), the poor side xipi (西皮, xīpí); after they meet, only xipi remains. **The rhyme category changes scene by scene; the first and last both rhyme on 遥条 (yáotiáo)** (scene 5 has 33 lines in one rhyme throughout, §6d). **All six great duoju fall on the second line of an on-the-beat section**, the longest 26 characters (§6e). See §2.

**④ Convention layer**　Entrance poems are given only to chou; self-introductions (自报家门, zìbào jiāmén) only to chou and laosheng (老生 — middle-aged male role); **the protagonist's first "entrance" is no entrance at all** (a dozen or so "(offstage speech)" lines + two poems on embroidery patterns = what Cheng Yanqiu called "a lantern behind a curtain and a sword in a box"); **the play has no exit poem at all**; the ending = two 【散板】 lines that speak the truth outright + eight 【流水】 lines sung while walking a circular walk (圆场, yuánchǎng) + a suona 〔尾声〕 (wěishēng — epilogue tune) 牌子 (páizi — named instrumental tune) (Section 5).

**⑤ Creation process**　Cheng Yanqiu set the assignment (he wanted a comedy) and supplied the material; Weng's order was conception (**the comedy entrusted to the social gallery around the protagonist**) → title → names as character design → the four methods → drafting. **Cheng Yanqiu made three requests in person**: cut the long aria into sections to match the three yields of the seat; write more long and short lines so he could "fit the tune to the words"; change the final scene's 【南梆子】 (nánbāngzi — southern clapper) to 【二六】 without changing the words (the definitive text has no nanbangzi; fully implemented). **Peer criticism is set alongside**: Fan Junhong criticized the ending as "lower in tone and drifting from the theme"; **the revised definitive text keeps it**. The 15 checks between the author's account and the definitive text (11 match / 3 partial / 1 does not) are in §3.

**⑥ Transferable techniques**
1. **Write a comedy for a tragic role type; give all the laughs to the supporting roles, and stock the cast list with enough chou** (11/21). *Failure condition*: reverse it when the protagonist is himself the object of satire.
2. **Cut a long aria into n sections, put one change of position between sections, and let a chou get in one jab each time** (the three yields of the seat in scene 12). *Self-check*: count how many times the opponent changes position; if not once, the aria should be cut into sections.
3. **Hold one rhyme category through a key scene, and make the first key scene and the closing scene share a rhyme.** *Failure condition*: not suitable when two opposed characters each set their own rhyme within one scene.
4. **To get "retribution," have the latecomer repeat the protagonist's old words word for word** (Lin'er's "I want a green horse" = Daqi's exact words in scene 9); **a stage-business passage in the master copy is not an aria but "mute flute + interspersed speech"** (coaxing Lin'er in scene 12; the prop table lists nine props for this scene alone). *Self-check*: if the old words have been rewritten, it is mere coincidence; for a passage with zero sung lines that runs more than five minutes, you must write down what holds it up.

## 2. 《沙家浜》 (Shajiabang): Reforming Conventions in a Modern-Dress Play and the Song–Speech Seam

**① Version**　Collectively adapted by the Beijing Peking Opera Troupe; written by Wang Zengqi and Yang Yumin; adapted from the Shanghai opera 《芦荡火种》 (Fire Seeds in the Reed Marshes). Base text = **the China Theatre Press edition of August 1965, ten scenes, not the 1970 definitive text** (the standardized text was published in Red Flag, 1970, no. 6; the full text was not obtained). The names keep the 1965 forms (Hu Chuan**kui**, 胡传**葵**; Chen Tianmin, 陈天民; Qilong, 七龙). The two-version diff lists only the 12 rows already verified; **that no scenes were added or removed is a verified conclusion**. The cast list does not mark role types (that column is supplied from Fan Junhong, not from the original book).

**② Structural layer**　Ten scenes alternate song and speech, light and dark; **of the three enemy scenes, two are almost entirely without song**. **The two main lines meet only in scene 9**; before that they are stitched together only twice by Chen Tianmin—what is concentrated is the event, not the place (Section 2). **Hooks (扣子, kòuzi — the suspense device at the end of a section) are used only in scenes 4, 6, and 7**; scenes 2 and 5 close on emotion, scenes 1, 9, and 10 close on action—**not every scene leaves a suspense**. See §4.

**③ Aria layer**　36 arias. **Xipi is for action and confrontation, erhuang for memory and predicament**—none of the four erhuang passages advances the plot. **If you want to cut in interspersed speech, write yaoban or sanban**; for one continuous run, write yuanban (原板, yuánbǎn — original pattern), erniu, liushui, kuaiban (快板, kuàibǎn — fast pattern); a full suite of big song = daoban (导板, dǎobǎn — leading board) → huilong (回龙, huílóng — returning dragon) → sanyan (三眼, sānyǎn — three-eye) → yuanban → the last line called into free rhythm (叫散, jiàosàn). **The whole play uses only 7 rhyme categories**: 江阳 (jiāngyáng), with 15 arias, is the main rhyme; 言前 (yánqián) is the rhyme for "explaining the plan"; 人辰 (rénchén) is the rhyme for short transitional arias; **the only risky rhyme, 怀来 (huáilái), is given to the villain's persuasion passage, and the only 中东 (zhōngdōng) to the only ensemble song**; **one rhyme per scene; a change of rhyme = a change of scene or a change of story line; asides and duets must share the rhyme**. Of the 36 arias only 6 break the rule, 4 of them in two famous arias. See §5–§6.

**④ Convention layer**　**Self-introduction is left only to the villain, and changed so that he sings it to the people in the same scene**; the identity of positive characters is introduced by a third party in speech—**when identity is a secret, convention yields to the character's situation**. Entrance poems and exit poems: zero; the residual form is "two people recite four lines in alternation to cover the time between scenes." **The aside (背供, bèigōng) is retained and is the technical center of the whole play**; in scene 7 "the curtain rises and they are already present," **but the offstage daoban entrance is retained and strengthened**—item 9 of the afterword treats cutting idle text and abolishing the gongs and drums that play characters on and off as the same thing; **the abolition of conventions went hand in hand with the abolition of the scene format** (Section 5). **The three song–speech joins can be made only on sanban-type metrical patterns** (Section 7).

**⑤ Creation process**　Afterword (May 1965, signed "the adaptors"): the "three-in-one combination" (三结合, sān jiéhé — the policy of uniting leadership, experts, and the masses), "stress the role of armed struggle," **"Hu Chuankui's attitude toward Sister Aqing is more trust than doubt" (the technical premise that makes the three-person duet in scene 4 work)**, "ten years to polish one play." **The reading of the two-version diff is that "the revised script changes words, not structure"**: scene names, event order, aria placement, and metrical patterns did not change for five years; the direction is always to reduce coincidence and raise the credibility of cause and effect. **Fan Junhong sets positive and negative side by side**: he cites this play as a model of the aside, and also criticizes later writers for abusing the aside and "falling into the trap of the new tradition"—**the play is both a model and its own counterexample**. See §7.

**⑥ Transferable techniques**
1. **In a confrontation scene, every sung line by the side holding the initiative falls on the second line** (in the "Battle of Wits," all ten of Sister Aqing's lines, five pairs, no exception). *Failure condition*: reverse it when writing a scene where the protagonist is being beaten down.
2. **Aside duets use first and second lines of the same character count, the same syntactic breaks, and opposite meaning** ("Her manner is neither servile nor arrogant / His expression is neither gloomy nor bright"); **three-person asides are distinguished by different metrical patterns**. *Self-check*: cover the names—can you tell who is who from the metrical markings alone? Only then is it used correctly.
3. **In answer-style arias, repeat the other side's question word with one spoken word before each sung reply** (scene 4: "Yes!" "Now!"). *Failure condition*: more than three times in a row becomes mechanical.
4. **Turn political argument into a run of questions, taking the grounds from the opponent's own name** (in scene 7 the four "why"s attack the designation "Loyalty and Righteousness National Salvation Army"). *Failure condition*: when the opponent has no self-glorification you can turn back on him, switch to narrative contrast.

## 3. 《白蛇传》 (The White Snake): The Three-Column Account of an Organized Traditional Subject

**① Version**　Tian Han, playwright; **Writers Publishing House, first edition June 1955, sixteen scenes**. **The online full text is not the 1955 edition**—the scene names are all the same, but the metrical patterns have been systematically altered: every non-standard metrical pattern is pulled back to a common xipi pattern, and the part of the sheng (生, shēng — male role) is cut down; the 1955 book is the base text, the online text is used only for comparison and written up as "the revised text reads ..."; **seven differences have been verified**. **Quotations transcribed from page images are not marked for verification; those from OCR are marked `[to be verified]`, 43 places in the whole batch.** This case's tables are in [reference-baishezhuan.md](reference-baishezhuan.md); §8–§12 below refer to that file.

**② Structural layer**　The sixteen scenes are extremely uneven in length: **The Broken Bridge 15 pages, The Wine Turns 11, Strolling by the Lake 10 are the three longest, and they are exactly the three turning points in the Bai–Xu relationship; Guarding the Mountain, The Water Battle, and Crossing the River are 2 pages each**—the measured data behind "scenes built mainly on song and recitation are written full; scenes built mainly on movement and dance are written spare" (Section 3). **Two scenes have no song**: The Wedding (all spoken dialogue, yet one of the play's biggest plot turns) and The Water Battle; **The Wedding can do without song because its peak is the moment the conditions are agreed, not an emotional peak**. See §8.

**③ Aria layer**　71 lines of aria text. **Bai Suzhen's main rhyme is 江阳**; she changes rhyme only on specific occasions (遥条 for Stealing the Herb and Entrusting the Child, 怀来 for the grief of Closing the Bowl, 言前 for the brightness of Strolling by the Lake); **at The Broken Bridge Bai rhymes on 江阳 and Xu on 言前—the only time in the play that rhyme category separates two voices** (§6d). The three longest arias all belong to Bai Suzhen—a thoroughgoing dan (旦, dàn — female role) song-heavy play. **The Broken Bridge alone has nine metrical patterns and eighteen arias**; long song is divided by metrical pattern, **the slow for the past, the fast for what has just happened**. The play uses nine uncommon metrical patterns (小安板 xiǎo'ānbǎn, 梆子清板 bāngzi qīngbǎn, 拨子碰板 bōzi pèngbǎn, 回龙 huílóng, 反西皮 fǎn xīpí, 快原板 kuài yuánbǎn, 四平调 sìpíngdiào ...), **each corresponding to one specific situation**—the appendix arranges them by "situation → metrical pattern." See §9–§10.

**④ Convention layer**　**This organized version assigns conventions by character: it keeps them for supporting roles and non-human characters, and removes them from the protagonist.** Entrance 牌子 are given only to groups and non-mortals—**a 牌子 is the sound of the non-human**; mortal protagonists do not sing 牌子 (unlike the function of qupai in the qupai system [曲牌体, qǔpáitǐ]; cross-reference `sw-chinese-opera-qupai`). Self-introduction is given only to Fahai; the exit poem is changed to a couplet recited in alternation by two people. **Guard against false inference: the protagonist's not introducing herself is a choice of the organized version, not a general rule of Peking opera.**

**⑤ Creation process**　**The Preface is a first-hand creation record**: twelve or thirteen years, three stages—Guilin (original title 《金钵记》 [The Golden Bowl], banned as soon as it opened) → the opera school → the Peking Opera Company (1954). **Wang Yaoqing's five points on tune**: lyrics need not be too regular, to leave room for the tune / cut 《煎药》 (Decocting the Medicine) but keep its 【二黄慢板】 (èrhuáng mànbǎn — erhuang slow pattern) duet and move it into 《释疑》 (Dispelling Doubt) / change one word ("祖护他" → "向着他," i.e., "shield him" → "side with him") / do not reject gaobozi (高拨子, gāobōzi) / change the vocal system of an entire scene. **The original words on "metrical-pattern markings are a letter of intent"**: "Although the playwright prescribes something, he also trusts the director and actors ... to create together"—the **upper bound** of the playwright's responsibility, paired with the lower bound, "responsibility stops at level/oblique tones and line pattern" (Section 10). See §11–§12.

**⑥ Transferable techniques**
1. **When organizing an old play, keep three accounts separate: which conventions are kept, which scene is cut and where its arias are moved, which passage is added** (keep the 牌子 / entrance poem / kutou (哭头, kūtóu — weeping cadence); cut 《煎药》 and move the duet into 《释疑》; add the wind god escorting Xu Xian and Xu Xian's 【反西皮】). *Self-check*: before handing in the script, make these three lists; if you cannot, you have only tinkered with words (Section 11).
2. **When an aria is moved to another scene, its vocal system changes the keynote of the new scene as well—either accept it or rewrite the tune** (this is why the whole of 《释疑》 changes from xipi to erhuang).
3. **Two people in confrontation each occupy one rhyme category** (The Broken Bridge); **to make B entitled to reproach A, put A's situation under someone else's arrangement** (in scene 13, add the wind god escorting Xu Xian; Tian Han's own account: "this way Xiao Qing has more reason to reproach Xu Xian"). *Failure condition*: two people who are allies or in the same situation need not separate rhymes.
4. **Recapitulation must carry new information** (Xu Xian's sixteen lines at The Broken Bridge retell two scenes, and the new information is only two points, exactly answering Xiao Qing's suspicion). *Self-check*: cross out everything the audience has already seen; if fewer than two lines remain, cut the passage.
5. **Reunion can be given as a single image, without lyricism** (in the final scene the pagoda falls, Bai Suzhen appears from the colored clouds, with not one sung or spoken line). *Failure condition*: not applicable when the main singer is this character and the audience is waiting to hear an aria from her—this play can do it because all the singing in the final scene is given to Xiao Qing.

## 4. 《朝阳沟》 (Chaoyang Village): Colloquial Speech in Lyrics and the Real Scale of Rhyming

**① Version**　Yang Lanchun, playwright. Base text = **the People's Literature Publishing House edition of March 1978**, with the inside cover marked "the January 1977 performance text of the Henan Opera Troupe No. 1 of Henan Province"—**this is the post-Cultural-Revolution revival text, not the 1958 premiere text of Troupe No. 3**. **The thematic direction has expired; the technical layer is retained**: the script contains obvious period phrases ("the three major differences" — 三大差别, sān dà chābié: the gaps between workers and peasants, town and country, and mental and manual labor; "the Chiang bandit clique," etc.), **to be used only as corpus, not taken into the rules**. **The main text does not mark a single metrical pattern, only "(sings)"**; the metrical patterns are supplied from the 16 score passages appended to the book, **2 of which have only tempo markings ("Co-mother, sit down" and the closing chorus)—do not fill in metrical patterns for them**.

**② Structural layer**　Nine scenes with no scene titles: scenes 1–3 = going down to the village, scenes 4–6 = wavering, **scene 7 is the only turning scene**, scenes 8–9 = staying. **Both major turns happen on a mountain road** (scene 2 going into the mountains, scene 7 coming down), with the same tune and the same opening formula sung twice, the second time echoing the first line by line—**the play's hardest structural design**. A complete table of the sung/spoken ratio for all nine scenes (scene 5, the ensemble scene, is lowest at 8:92; scene 8, mother and daughter, highest at 73:26): **ensemble scenes use speech, two-person confrontation uses song**—the only piece of numerical evidence in the section on the division of labor between song and speech (Section 7). See §1.

**③ Aria layer**　50 arias. **Aria counts per scene: 8/3/9/5/2/9/5/4/5—the largest number is not in the turning scene; the single longest aria is in the turning scene** (scene 7, about 30 lines in one rhyme throughout): preparatory scenes use many short, fragmented arias to define each character; the turning scene uses one whole block of big song to land on the protagonist. **The 【二八板】 (èrbā bǎn) family occurs 11 times and is the absolute main body**; the 2 manban (慢板, mànbǎn — slow pattern) passages are used only for the opening self-account and the offstage chorus. **The word "turn" (转) is the signal that the line pattern should change**: wherever the score says "turn to 【垛板】 (duǒbǎn — stacked pattern)," the main text at the same moment changes from seven-/ten-character lines to three-/four-character duoju—**when the line pattern changes, the metrical pattern should turn** (§6e). Rhyming: **second lines rhyme 100%, first lines about 50%**; neighboring rhyme categories may be used interchangeably, dialect may be assigned to a category, and the rhyme may change mid-passage; **nine broad rhyme categories cover all 50 arias; a risky rhyme is not used once**—the default of a colloquial script is the broad rhyme (§6d). See §2, §3, §5.

**④ Convention layer**　**Self-introduction, entrance poem, and exit poem are cut, not rewritten**; **entrances and exits, the aside ("(sings aside)" / "(speaks aside)"), and offstage singing are kept and reworked** (scene 6 uses offstage song in place of reading a letter); **offstage chorus is added as hetou** (合头, hétóu — the sung refrain), and the whole company sings in the final scene in place of the exit poem. **What the modern-dress play loses is the virtuality of props; what it keeps is the dance-like quality of stage business** (§12a). **Throughout the play there is only one format for turning from speech into song: an address term**—set alongside 《惊梦》 (The Interrupted Dream) and seven of Luo Huaizhen's Yue operas, common to both systems (Section 7). **Role types are not marked, but functional positions are still assigned by role type** (Section 4).

**⑤ Creation process**　**This book has no preface, afterword, revision note, or performance record**, which is its biggest difference from the three Peking-opera books; first-hand information consists only of the inside cover, copyright page, production-photo captions, and score credits; everything about the creation is taken from outside the book (the final scene was completed by on-site oral instruction + the actor memorizing lines on the spot + the composer setting the tune on the spot; "start from writing the character, but song is dominant"). **The four versions of the opening prop** are a ready-made example in textual criticism.

**⑥ Transferable techniques**
1. **Colloquial speech enters lyrics with three things: direct quotation, real numbers, dialect reduplication; if two are missing, fall back to spoken dialogue and rewrite** ("no use," "muddlehead," "seven o'clock"; 白生生 bái shēngshēng / 青凌凌 qīng línglíng / 饱盈盈 bǎo yíngyíng / 磁丁丁 cí dīngdīng). *Failure condition*: a lyrical core passage may have only concrete objects and no quotation. *Self-check*: how many proper names/numbers are in this aria? If zero, it is empty.
2. **Make an ordinary event into a big aria by means of one repeated three-character lead line** (writing a family letter by proxy in scene 6, "You write down:" leads four times for about 22 lines). *Failure condition*: more than five leads turn it into a doggerel jingle.
3. **In a quarrel, line length decreases: four lines → two lines → one line → half a line, with no speed-up instruction written** (the mother-daughter duet in scene 8). *Failure condition*: after it diminishes to a half-line, one side must add one more line to close the passage.
4. **Let conflict land first on the work in hand** (hoeing up the crops in scene 4, smashing the water bucket in scene 5—both are performable technical actions). *Failure condition*: if the protagonist is an expert, switch to the second level (interpersonal mockery).
5. **Walk the same road twice, and on the second pass recover every item one by one** (the road song in scene 2 / the retrospective passage in scene 7). *Self-check*: did every thing referred to in the second pass appear in the first?

## 5. 《潘金莲》 (Pan Jinlian): The Absurd, Cross-Time, and Bangqiang Written as a Character

**① Version**　Wei Minglun's "absurdist Sichuan opera"; base text = the Northern Literature and Art Publishing House edition of September 1987, marked "revised after the September 1986 Beijing–Shanghai–Nanjing performances." ⚠️ **The source PDF is physically missing pages (checked against page images; not an OCR failure)**: the whole 楔子 (xiēzi — prologue), the poisoning of Wu Da and the killing of the sister-in-law, and most of the confession is printed only in the top few lines of the page; every affected entry is marked 〔fragmentary〕, **and must not be used as complete evidence**. **The script marks no metrical pattern and no qupai name**, only "song / bangqiang / chorus / aside song / offstage song / wordless song"; the only two vocal-system markings are "sung in Henan bangzi style" and "sung as an operatic aria"—**the production also wove in Yue opera and pop songs, but these are not in the script; any vocal system not marked in the script carries no structural function**. The play is written in the banqiang manner (tanxi huqin [弹戏胡琴, tánxì húqín]), not the qupai manner.

**② Structural layer**　**The play is not divided into scenes**, with only two title pages, prologue and epilogue; there is no break in between, and section changes are carried by lighting, the cloud stair (云阶, yúnjiē — a permanent raised platform for cross-time characters), gongs and drums, banners covering the stage, and the entrance of cross-time characters; the notes divide it by event into 14 units (see §6). **The epilogue does not judge the case; it closes with debate**: "The guitar changes key to play the ancient mode; right and wrong, let a hundred schools be heard" (吉他变调弹古音，是非且听百家鸣).

**③ Aria layer**　About 50 arias. **Sichuan opera rhyme cannot be judged by the thirteen rhyme categories (十三辙, shísān zhé) of Mandarin**: Sichuan Mandarin does not distinguish front and back nasals, so 人辰 and 中东 rhyme together; entering-tone characters are assigned to yangping, and "色客热洁" can enter the 乜斜 (miēxié) category (§6d). **The risky rhyme is reserved for the longest passage** (the snowy-night farewell, in 乜斜). **A change of rhyme is a change of face**: Wu Song turns from 一七 (yīqī) to 由求 (yóuqiú) at the same moment he flies into a rage. **Cross-time duets share one rhyme**: Pan Jinlian and Lü Shasha trade one line at a time, both on 言前; Pan Jinlian picks up Ximen Qing's rhyme and continues singing (the two have not met, yet already share a rhyme). See §8.

**④ Convention layer**　**Self-introduction is reworked in two ways**: outside characters introduce themselves in their own idiom (Anna with a famous line from a novel, Shi Nai'an with his own book), and a local character punctures it on the spot; entrance poems and exit poems are not used at all, and exits are replaced by commentary, self-mockery, or debate; virtual performance is retained as is. **The cloud stair is a permanent raised platform for crossing time: cross-time characters walk the cloud stair, people of this dynasty walk the flat platform; height in space is the layering of eras, with no scene change.** **Bangqiang is written as one character**, with four functions: say what the character will not say, narrate his action for him and take up the next line, pronounce judgment and point the subtext, and cut into spoken dialogue as a sharp rebuke—**not backing vocals, but a second speaker on stage**. See §9.

**⑤ Creation process**　**The general rule for the 9 cross-time characters: they are a commentary bench; they can converse and intervene, but they cannot change the plot** (Wu Zetian summons Jinlian to the hall "to decide for you," and Jinlian covers her face and flees—the party concerned refuses to appear). **The author's confession is itself in script form, as dialogue** (his interlocutors are Kafka, Ouyang Yuqian, Ionesco, and a critic), and **he writes the opposing view into the essay verbatim and lets it have the last word**; **his self-positioning is magical realism, not the Theater of the Absurd**; the critic blocks both exits, **and the two sides are set side by side without adjudication**. **Switch sentence (开关句, kāiguān jù — the sentence that sets the route)** (§12b): 《易胆大》 (Yi Danda) takes "modernizing Chinese opera," 《四姑娘》 (Fourth Sister) takes "making the modern play operatic," **one play takes only one direction**; this play takes the former, the opposite of 《沙家浜》 and 《朝阳沟》. See §9.

**⑥ Transferable techniques**
1. **Cross-time characters may comment, not change the plot.** *Self-check*: if you delete any one cross-time character, does the story still run? If not, you have written them as a plot function, and the absurd will collapse into time travel.
2. **Cross-time duets must share one rhyme.** *Failure condition*: if the two are supposed to "not be able to talk to each other" (the Sesame Official), then on the contrary change tune and rhyme.
3. **Bangqiang is the second speaker on stage; when writing the script, assign it its work.** *Failure condition*: bangqiang is a traditional device of Sichuan opera's high-pitched style (高腔, gāoqiāng); moved to Peking opera, Henan opera, or Yue opera, it has no equivalent.
4. **The most unbearable places are given no words** (the two places where he crawls between another's legs and is pushed down get only one "Ah"); **the intermission can be written into the plot**. *Failure condition*: the latter works only in a genre where "the actor admits he is acting." *Self-check*: more than three "wordless songs" in a play is abuse.

## 6. Luo Huaizhen · Yue Opera, 《李清照》 (Li Qingzhao) and 《真假驸马》 (The Real and the Fake Prince Consort): Scripts That Do Not Mark Metrical Patterns

**① Version**　Volume 3 of the Collected Dramatic Works of Luo Huaizhen (Shanghai People's Publishing House, 2008): seven Yue operas plus an afterword. **In the whole volume, 7 plays and 496 aria markings, with 0 metrical-pattern markings**—the rule is "**what has a (sings) mark is song; what has no mark is speech**," and metrical patterns and vocal tunes belong entirely to the composer. **This refutes the implicit premise that "a banqiang playwright must mark metrical patterns"**: that is the Peking-opera convention (Section 9 sets the two styles side by side). **All the plays collected are contemporary new compositions (1986–2003), not traditional Yue-opera core repertory**; 《真假驸马》 and 《蛇恋》 (Snake Love) are marked with revision years, but **the book does not collect the old drafts, so the two versions cannot be read against each other**; 《青衫·红袍》 (The Blue Robe and the Red Robe) has two different attributions (following the end of the play, "Gong Xiaoxiong" is kept, with the doubt noted).

**② Structural layer**　《李清照》 has seven scenes, **and its structural method is "one ci poem per scene"**: each scene takes one of her ci poems as its title, and that poem is sung offstage to open and close the scene; the first and last both use 《如梦令》 (Ru Meng Ling, "As If in a Dream") and the same swing, with fifty years between them. Aria counts for the seven scenes: 12/13/3/9/3/2/1; **scene 5 uses one super-long aria to offset the small number of arias—"many arias" and "one long aria" are two ways of writing a heavy scene (重场戏, zhòngchǎngxì — the scene with the greatest value reversal)**; the protagonist has not a single sung line in scene 7, **so the grand closing (大收煞, dàshōushā) can do without song**. 《真假驸马》 has six scenes, 18/18/7/19/5/6; **the singing is pressed into the first four scenes, and scene 6 (the climax in the imperial hall) is almost entirely speech**; the cast list notes "the twin brothers are played by the same actor"—**the subject was generated by the role-type condition of one actor playing two (一赶二, yī gǎn èr)**. See §1–§2.

**③ Aria layer**　**Seven-character lines are the backbone; ten-character lines are used for narrative; the two alternate freely within the same passage**; the full range of short-line registers is present (five-character passages, four-character couplets, three-character chain-link, two-character pauses), and long lines run to 11–13 characters and **must return to uniform line length to close**. **Yue opera expands a passage by same-initial uniform-line parallelism, not by duoju**—in the whole volume there is only 1 true duoju, and it is immediately followed by a stage-business cue: **in Yue opera, duoju are the score for stage business; if no stage business accompanies them, do not use them**; same-initial lines **2 for antiphon, 4 as the norm, 6 as the maximum**. Rhyme: one rhyme throughout a passage is the rule; **the trigger for changing rhyme mid-passage is "a change of the person addressed"**; **人辰 rhyming interchangeably with 中东／庚青 is normal** (a feature of the Wu sound system—**this is an inference in the notes, not from the original book**). **When metrical patterns are not marked, character count is the metrical pattern** (§6e). See §3.

**④ Convention layer**　**Cut**: self-introduction, exit poem, gongs and drums, costumes, stage management, metrical patterns, role types—all zero. **Keep**: entrances and exits (without gongs and drums), the circular walk, **one offstage-singing entrance** (the functional position of Peking opera's 【导板】), **two asides** (marked "(sings secretly)" [暗唱], **used only for suspicion**, a different direction from 《沙家浜》's making the aside the technical center). **Add**: **【字幕：】 (surtitles) to establish time and place** (replacing self-introduction and entrance prose), **offstage song / offstage chorus** (in all 7 plays: thematic line framing, situation exposition, psychological narration, hetou), theme song, storyteller.

**⑤ Creation process**　The 8 items of the afterword are the only first-hand creation record for the Yue-opera part: 《真假驸马》 was his first play to be staged, and on that account it was taken up by the Shanghai Yue Opera House; **《梅龙镇》 (Meilong Town) and 《青衫·红袍》 were "tailor-made for Zhang Ruihong"**—the common features (male roles, one actor playing many [一赶多, yī gǎn duō], long duets) are the concrete content of "tailor-made"; **the same material transformed through several forms** (《风月秦淮》 [Wind and Moon on the Qinhuai] → the Han opera 《柳如是》 [Liu Rushi] → the Yue-opera version in this volume); **《蛇恋》 changed its title and thereby its theme**. **The six things this volume cannot support (the reference must be left blank) are in §7**—**do not fill them in with Peking opera's thirteen rhyme categories or Henan opera's Zhongzhou phonology (中州韵, Zhōngzhōu yùn)**.

**⑥ Transferable techniques**
1. **The default form of a Yue-opera duet: seven-character single lines cut antiphonally, one line per person, one rhyme throughout, each line biting into the next.** *Failure condition*: with three or more people on stage, change to "pairwise antiphon + one unison line to close." *Self-check*: cover the character names—do the lines still connect into one complete speech? (Section 8)
2. **Diminishing line length in duet (seven → five → four → two characters) shows "the two closing in on each other"; it need not be a quarrel** (mother and son reasoning in 《梅龙镇》)—set alongside Henan opera's "dog bite" (used only for quarrels, and it has a metrical-pattern name) as two grades: **Yue opera has no metrical-pattern names, so the playwright can produce this effect only through character count**.
3. **When the person addressed changes within a big aria, change the rhyme** (kneeling to his mother uses 人辰; turning to kneel to his wife uses 言前); **same-initial lines aimed at the second person are used to settle accounts, not to lyricize** ("I ask you / I respect you / it is rare that you," each line nailing down one thing that has already happened and cannot be reversed). *Failure condition*: do not change rhyme when the person addressed stays the same and only the emotion changes.
4. **Argument over cause and effect, proof of identity, and group pressure use speech; one person opening his heart to another uses song.** *Failure condition*: if the scene is the play's only heavy scene and must have a big aria, change to "speech advances + one super-long aria closes at the end" (scene 5 of 《李清照》).
5. **When the biographical subject is a poet or ci poet, use his own works as the scene frames, and do not mix original works with newly written lyrics** (the seven scenes of 《李清照》); **when writing for a female xiaosheng, let him cry, show weakness, and sing children's songs, and give priority to one actor playing many**. *Failure condition*: the latter does not apply in opera genres that distinguish pheasant-feather (雉尾, zhìwěi), fan (扇子, shànzi), and poor-scholar (穷生, qióngshēng) routes—**the opposite direction from Weng Ouhong's "write according to the existing role-type routes"; set the two side by side** (Section 4).

## 7. Luo Huaizhen · Local Opera: 《金龙与蜉蝣》 (Golden Dragon and Mayfly), 《典妻》 (Wife for Hire), and the Other Six Plays

**① Version**　Volume 2 of the Collected Dramatic Works of Luo Huaizhen (Shanghai People's Publishing House, 2008): eight plays across six opera genres, **not one metrical-pattern marking**. **The sample is one author, one to three plays per opera genre; only Huai opera crosses three points in time—1981／1992／1994—so its reliability is higher than that of the other five genres**. **Two plays must be discounted**: 《西楚霸王》 (The Hegemon of Western Chu) was an original Peking-opera script of 1994, adapted to Huai opera in 1998, with Peking-opera convention words qǐbà (起霸 — opening martial display) and tàngmǎ (趟马 — horse-riding mime) remaining in the Huai-opera script; 《杨贵妃》 (Yang Guifei) was adapted from a musical (its four- and five-character short lines are a leftover from that source). **Rhyme categories (韵辙, yùnzhé) are all summarized by Mandarin pronunciation; before they have been through a dialect rhyme table, they can only serve as examples of "where the rhyme falls."**

**② Structural layer**　《金龙与蜉蝣》 (1992; premiered under the label "urban new Huai opera") **uses 幕 (mù — act), not 场 (chǎng — scene)**: prologue + five acts + epilogue, with the period deliberately kept outside any dynasty; three big surtitle jumps compress three generations into seven units, and every jump is filled by offstage accompaniment—**the traditional rule of one scene, one time, one place is not broken; what is broken is the time gap between scenes**. **Cyclical structure**: the prologue, the old king murdered and suspended above the throne ↔ the epilogue, Golden Dragon pierced by his grandson and falling on the throne—**the fable's closure does not depend on a character arc but on structural recurrence**. **It has no "concentration of the main line"**: each of the three generations has its own line—**fable form can be an exception, on condition that a single prop runs through and sustains cause and effect** (the helmet), which directly conflicts with Fan Junhong's "vertical concentration"; **set the two side by side** (Section 2). 《典妻》 (2000, after Rou Shi's 《为奴隶的母亲》 [A Slave Mother]) has five scenes + epilogue; **the four questions and four answers of scene 1 and the epilogue are repeated word for word, but the answers are now the opposite**. See §4–§6.

**③ Aria layer**　《金龙与蜉蝣》 has 44 arias (Golden Dragon 12, Mayfly 11, Yufeng 8, Yuqiao 5, accompaniment 7)—**the distribution of arias itself draws the character structure: two generations of men sing in duet, three generations of women sing in rotation**; **the accompaniment only covers time jumps and translates inner life that cannot be spoken, and never advances the plot**. **What "urban new Huai opera" changes is not rhyme but the variance of line length**: within the same passage the character count rises from 3 to 14, while the paired lines and rhyme endings do not move; only the middle of the line moves. 《典妻》 has 39 arias, the wife taking 20; **the three longest and heaviest are all in scene 5 (on the road home), and scene 5 has no plot at all; the epilogue (the son's death) has not one sung line**—**this is a counterexample to "leave the aria for the scene with the greatest value reversal," a first-hand disagreement; set the two side by side** (Section 3). **The closing of a duet depends on character count; four methods stand side by side**: question lines shorten while answers do not / the answering side gets two extra lines in the last round / both sides diminish to one character / chain-link continuation receives three characters (Section 8). See §6.

**④ Convention layer**　**The chorus layer (歌队, gēduì)**: all seven plays after 1992 have one (offstage accompaniment / singing within the curtain / chorus of the original poem / bangqiang + lianhualuo [莲花落, liánhuālào — a folk narrative song form] / offstage singing, the form varying by opera genre), with three functions—establish time and place, translate inner life, comment on events—**collectively replacing the entrance poem and exit poem**; **the only play without a chorus, 《韩信之死》 (The Death of Han Xin, 1981), is also the only one that still keeps the entrance poem and the "(recite)" marker—the three disappear together, and that is one and the same thing** (the same functional position as the qupai system's "fumo opening (副末开场, fùmò kāichǎng) + Tang-assembled exit poem (集唐下场诗, jítáng xiàchǎngshī)," Section 5). **Sichuan opera's bangqiang is the only one that can be embedded inside a sung line; this is the hardest textual boundary between the high-pitched style and other banqiang opera genres.** Two contemporary uses of conventions: **write the convention name directly into the stage-action cue (科介, kējiè); do not delete the convention—change who carries it** (§12c).

**⑤ Creation process**　The 10 items of the afterword: 《韩信之死》 is "my first real play," written before he was 25, **and apart from a few words it preserves its original form completely**—it is reliable as a sample for "the same author eleven years apart"; add 《西楚霸王》 (the same characters, thirteen years later) and 《金龙与蜉蝣》, **and the three Huai operas are the only time series in this batch from which the author's technical changes can be measured**. **"Urban new opera" has an exact work, year, and scene count** (written in 1992, premiered in Shanghai in May 1993, performed in Chengdu in September of the same year)—do not write it as a generality. **A concrete contrast between a bitterness play (苦戏, kǔxì — a play of suffering) and a tragedy**: at the end of 《金龙与蜉蝣》 the victim nods in approval of the perpetrator = tragedy; at the end of 《典妻》 the wife sits in the rain holding her dead child = bitterness play (§12c). See §7.

**⑥ Transferable techniques**
1. **When metrical patterns are not marked, shift gears by character count**: four changes of character count 5→7→4→3 within one aria can produce the effect of sanban—yuanban—kuaiban—duoban (scene 5 of 《典妻》). *Failure condition*: when writing for a traditional company that needs a score to shape the tune, you still must mark metrical patterns. *Self-check*: list the character count of every line as a sequence; if it is 7, 7, 7, 7 all the way through, there is no rhythmic design.
2. **Stack events in duoju, not adjectives** ("cross the Wu River, fight the Qin army, enter Xianyang, destroy Ying Zheng"); **when adapting a classical subject, give the original poem to the chorus and the new lyrics to the characters; do not mix the two registers**.
3. **"Same words, different context" is the main structural device of contemporary new composition**: one passage repeated verbatim two or three times, with the context reversed (the theme song appears three times, the four questions and four answers at head and tail of 《典妻》, the Song of Gaixia [《垓下歌》] sung three times). *Self-check*: if you change the words at the second appearance, the effect immediately drops a grade—**changing the words is an admission that the first writing was not accurate**.
4. **One prop runs through the whole play's cause and effect** (the helmet: pot → name pasted over → recognizing the father → begging → bewilderment; the old umbrella: the same one in and out for three years). *Self-check*: count how many times it appears and whether its meaning changes each time; fewer than three times, or no change of meaning, and it is only scenery.
5. **The "poetic sublimation" of the ending is bound to the subject, not a stylistic preference**: in the four fable/legend subjects, the dead invariably revive / visions fill the sky / a funeral turns into a wedding; in the four realist subjects there is not one instance of sublimation; likewise **"urban new opera" ≠ "breaking the line pattern"** (《金龙与蜉蝣》 line-length variance 3–14／《典妻》 regular seven- and ten-character lines)—**any discussion of urbanization must separate the fable and realist routes** (§12c). *Self-check*: if you want a sublimated ending for a realist subject, first ask whether any of the first four scenes laid a non-realist scene as groundwork.

## 9. Index of Creation Records (Explanation and Pointers)

**Weng Ouhong, 《翁偶虹编剧生涯》 (Weng Ouhong's Playwriting Career)** (1985) is an autobiographical memoir of creation that explains, play by play, **for whom he wrote, what structural decisions he made, how many drafts he revised, and where he failed**. The book has 42 chapters and the account touches on 60 plays; the index table follows chapter order, **and includes only the plays described chapter by chapter in the notes (about 45); those not described one by one are not listed**. Columns: play / for whom written / structural decision / revisions / success or failure / page. **The table is in [reference-chuangzuoji.md](reference-chuangzuoji.md) §1.** Use it to look up three kinds of things: **what kind of script a particular actor wanted** (Cheng Yanqiu wanted comedy to vary the repertory, Ye Shenglan wanted a full-length poor-scholar play, Tan Fuying wanted a change of rhyme category); **why a script could not be mounted** (the title loses by its generational rank, the lead actor at the height of his fame will not give "one extra measure on the old rule" (老尺加一, lǎo chǐ jiā yī — the old custom of adding one extra measure as a bonus), after one play is a great hit the actor puts his horse out to pasture, a performance ban); **which technique comes from which play** (a lantern behind a curtain and a sword in a box = 《将相和》 [The General and the Prime Minister Make Peace], clouds supporting the moon / powder on the back of the painting = 《锁麟囊》, a grass snake's gray line = 《大闹天宫》 [Havoc in Heaven], separating the strands to gather the fan and choosing troops and storing momentum = 《西门豹》 [Ximen Bao], different roads to the same destination = 《荆钗记》 [The Thorn Hairpin]).

**Fan Junhong, 《戏曲编剧论集》 (Collected Essays on Opera Playwriting), seven adaptation notes** (Women Generals of the Yang Family, Jiujiangkou, The Tiger Hunt, The Butterfly Cup, The White-Haired Girl, Tracks in the Snowy Forest, plus a summary of the failure of Sleeping on Brushwood and Tasting Gall), columns: play / base text / what was changed / basis / consequence, **the table is in §2**. The three most reusable points: **"take four soundings"** (the historical base, the performance base, the style base, and the script base—begin from the "periphery" before putting hand to work); **the self-negation of the three revisions of 《蝴蝶杯》** (at the time, "as long as it stands ideologically, it does not matter if it is a little weaker artistically"; he cut 《洞房》 [The Bridal Chamber] and with it the forced marriage, and on the third revision went back to "keep the forced marriage, drop the bridal chamber"); **the two criteria for cutting scenes in the revival of 《猎虎记》—"dragging" and "jumping."** For three examples of the collective-adaptation workflow see §3.

## 10. Index of Transferable Techniques (Look Up Cases by Technique)

Each case's ⑥ is its list of techniques; **failure conditions and self-check actions exist only in that entry—do not cite them apart from it**. Find cases by technique:

- **Metrical patterns and rhyme categories**: on-the-beat sections concentrated in key scenes (1·③) / long arias cut into n sections with changes of position (1·2) / only sanban-type metrical patterns can be cut by interspersed speech (2·③④) / long song divided by metrical pattern (3·③) / when the line pattern changes, the metrical pattern should turn (4·③) / when metrical patterns are not marked, shift gears by character count (7·1) / the lead actor has the right to change metrical patterns without changing words (1·⑤, 8) / one rhyme through a key scene and the first and last sharing a rhyme (1·3) / one rhyme per scene, asides and duets sharing the rhyme (2·③) / the two parties in confrontation each occupy one rhyme (3·3) / broad rhyme is the default of a colloquial script (4·③) / a change of rhyme is a change of face (5·③) / change rhyme when the person addressed changes (6·3).
- **Lyrics and the division of labor between song and speech**: duoju only on the second line of an on-the-beat section (1·③) / the three elements of colloquial speech in lyrics (4·1) / a three-character lead line makes a big aria (4·2) / diminishing line length (4·3, 6·2) / stack a list of events (7·2) / ensemble scenes use speech, two-person confrontation uses song (4·②) / argument uses speech, opening the heart uses song (6·4) / the address-term interface (4·④, 6) / repeat the question word in speech before singing again (2·3) / leave the aria for the turning scene (4·③) and for the scene before the turn (7·③)—two views side by side.
- **Conventions**: the protagonist's entrance is no entrance (1·④) / the modern-dress play cuts three things and keeps the aside and offstage singing (2·④, 4·④) / 牌子 only for non-human characters (3·④) / bangqiang as the second speaker (5·3) / the chorus's three functions replace entrance and exit poems; convention names written into stage-action cues (7·④).

## 11. Comparison with the Qupai-System Corpus

**《潘金莲》 (Section 5) and 《巴山秀才》 (The Scholar of Bashan; see `sw-chinese-opera-qupai-cases`) form a control pair: same author, same opera genre, two ways of writing.** 《巴山秀才》 = Sichuan opera high-pitched style = qupai system (qupai names marked, a table of 43 qupai, nine passages of bangqiang); 《潘金莲》 = tanxi huqin = banqiang system (not a single qupai name). **This is the cleanest evidence that "the division of axes is vocal system, not opera genre"; the two sides point to each other and must not be separated**: the qupai-system half (no suites, qupai selected piecemeal, one rhyme per passage with free character count) is in `sw-chinese-opera-qupai-cases`.

Two more control pairs: **bangqiang (high-pitched style)—hetou (Henan opera)—offstage singing (Yue opera) are three forms of the same functional position**; **the fumo opening + Tang-assembled exit poem and the chorus + 【字幕：】 are two sets of devices for the same function**. Full-script corpora for Yuan zaju, chuanqi, Kunqu, and Sichuan opera high-pitched style are in `sw-chinese-opera-qupai-cases`.
