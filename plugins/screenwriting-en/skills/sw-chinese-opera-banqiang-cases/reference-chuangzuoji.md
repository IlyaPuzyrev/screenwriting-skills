# Index of Composition Notes: Weng Ouhong's (翁偶虹) Play Index for 《编剧生涯》 (A Playwright's Career), Fan Junhong's (范钧宏) Adaptation Notes on Seven Plays, and Three Examples of Collective Adaptation Workflows

> **Source texts and verification status (must be cited together with the data)**
>
> - **Weng Ouhong, 《翁偶虹编剧生涯》 (Weng Ouhong: A Playwright's Career)** (completed in 1985; a first-person memoir of his creative life, 42 chapters, arranged chronologically by play). Weng Ouhong (1908–1994) was an amateur-turned-professional playwright who began as a *huāliǎn* (painted-face) actor; he wrote his first script in 1933 and worked for fifty years, until 1983, **processing, arranging, adapting, or writing 118 plays** (counted by his disciple Zhang Jingshan; posthumous manuscripts add more, bringing the total above 130; preface p0005). He himself said that "only six-tenths of them were actually realized on stage" (p0010). **Page numbers refer to the OCR scan pages, pNNNN.**
> - ⚠️ **Scope of this table**: the memoir discusses 60 plays, but **this table includes only the plays described chapter by chapter in the notes (about 50 entries); plays not individually described are omitted**. In the "Written for" column, "—" means the book does not name a patron. **Do not treat this table as a complete index of the book.**
> - **Fan Junhong, 《戏曲编剧论集》 (Collected Essays on Playwriting for Xiqu — xiqu, Chinese traditional opera)** (all 33 essays are covered): the adaptation notes discuss 7 plays, and the end of document 07 adds a summary table of changes to three plays. **Printed page number = scan page − 7**; this file gives scan pages.

| **《三妇艳》 (Three Radiant Wives)** | Zhonghua Opera School | Wrote three kinds of women, with much of the dialogue in modern language; **he proposed staging it in modern dress as an experiment in the modern play, but Jin Zhongsun rejected the idea on the grounds that "the audience will not accept it"**; it stayed in traditional costume and had to be broken up and rewritten | Dialogue forced back into period style | Placed on record: **in a modern play, language and costume are a matched set** | p0039 |

| **《美人鱼》 (The Mermaid)** | Zhonghua Opera School (completed in ten days) | **Sectional allocation**: "the first half leans on song and movement, the second half on movement and speech; the final decisive battle avoids ordinary martial combat and embeds the fighting inside dance" | — | **His verdict: "fiery and lively, but the plot is ordinary and not profound"** | p0062–0063 |
| **《凤双飞》 (Two Phoenixes Flying Together)** (adapted from the Bangzi play 《蝴蝶杯》 [The Butterfly Cup]) | Zhonghua Opera School | "Eliminate the weeds and take the essence, cut afresh" (the original had too many bridges); **his criterion for choosing a subject was "this script can open up different paths for several role types of the same trade"** — the fishing-boat *dàn* Hu Fenglian / the curtain *dàn* Lu Fengying / the official *dàn* Lady Tian | — | **Self-criticism**: he had failed to criticize and remove polygamy — "the feudal fortress still stands towering" — and said the title was "an unhealthy title taken from an unhealthy plot" | p0065–0066 |
| **《十二堑》 (Twelve Chasms)** | Li Shaochun's troupe (never went into rehearsal) | Deliberately broke with tradition by expanding four chasms into twelve; **a table allocating ten 曲牌 (qǔpái — fixed-tune units)** (each chasm conjures an ancient figure who must predate the Little Pilgrim); in two places the antagonist is made to stop singing and the protagonist sings in his place; the playwright had to be able to sing the music himself (he could not sing three of the ten and went away to learn them before teaching his students) | Two changes: in the Chasm of Breath, cut sung lines and substitute 【抽头】 (chōutóu — a percussion pattern) plus pheasant feathers and back flags; in the Chasm of Greed, enlarge the copper-coin prop (砌末, qìmò) so the movement (身段, shēnduàn — movement patterns) can hang on it | The press wrote that it "**obeys tradition, develops the ancient music, does not court the crowd with gags, does not dazzle with spectacle**"; **but the play's title lost out on seniority (the Little Pilgrim was several generations younger than the monkey characters of other troupes), and the troupe's core members refused to rehearse it** | p0080–0087 |

| **《蔷薇刺》 (The Rose Thorn)** (adapted from the Kunqu play 《开口笑》 [Open-Mouthed Laughter]) | Ruyi Society | Forced to write a farce he did not believe in | "Done hastily against my conscience" | **Failure** — "among the scripts I have written, this is a very failed one"; he likened creation to "a fully inflated ball: prick it with a needle-point hole... and it cannot bounce", **and the troupe's internal strife was that hole** | p0109–0111 |
| **《百鸟朝凤》 (A Hundred Birds Pay Homage to the Phoenix)** | Song Dezhu (a 武旦, wǔdàn — martial female role) | **Sections cut according to the sequence of role types the star could play**: first the 花旦 (huādàn — flower girl) plus 刺杀旦 (cìshādàn — assassin female) / middle the 魂子旦 (húnzidàn — ghost female) / then the 彩旦 (cǎidàn — comic female) of "Sawing the Big Vat" / finally the wǔdàn's weapon-throwing display | The play was originally called 《美人魂》 [Soul of a Beauty]; the Japanese puppet police banned it on the grounds that "beauty = American", and it could be staged only after the title was changed | Success — a textbook case of "role type determines the writing" | p0116–0127 |
| **《紫塞香云》 (Fragrant Clouds over the Purple Frontier)** | Song Dezhu | Wrapped two traditional plays, 《红逼宫》 [The Red Forced Abdication] and 《铁笼山》 [Iron Cage Mountain], joining them with "A Frontier Wedding" and "Revenge in Battle" | — | **An inverted goal**: "I sought only seamless unity, not protruding edges, so that the audience would see it as a repertory piece long in performance" — **when adding head and tail to a foundation classic (骨子老戏, gǔzi lǎoxì), the new writing succeeds only if it cannot be detected as new** | p0127–0128 |
| **《瓮头春》 (Spring in the Wine Jar)** | Cheng Yanqiu (程砚秋) | Story invented before the script; the object named in the title (a jar of spring wine) is placed **in the persecutor's hands and in the victim's workplace**, and the whole play's turn is accomplished within a single scene | — | **Shelved**: a friend of Cheng Yanqiu's said, "I have performed too many tragedies... could we rehearse a comedy suited to me?" — **however well a script fits an actor's craft, if it conflicts with the genre distribution of his existing repertoire, it still cannot be staged**; never performed | p0137–0138 |
| **《锁麟囊》 (The Jewelry Purse)** | Cheng Yanqiu (Cheng-school 青衣, qīngyī — the dignified female role; the commission was "write me a comedy") | Conception (the reversal of wealth and poverty and the fickleness of the world, with the comedy entrusted to the social panorama) → title (from the Shandong folk custom of the "precious-child bag") → names that are character designs → four methods (烘云托月 hōngyún tuōyuè, "paint the clouds to set off the moon"; 背面敷粉 bèimiàn fūfěn, "powder the back of the mirror"; 帷灯匣剑 wéidēng xiájiàn, "a lamp behind the curtain, a sword in the box"; 草蛇灰线 cǎoshé huīxiàn, "a snake in the grass, a line of ash") → writing; for the ending he found the gap in "change clothes first, reveal the name afterward" and made it a "leopard-tail strike on stone" (bàowěi jīshí) | **Cheng Yanqiu raised three points to his face** (trim the long aria into sections to match the three offers of a seat / write more lines of uneven length so the melody can follow the words / in the last scene change 【南梆子】 [Nánbāngzi] to 【二六】 [Èrliù] without changing the words) → Weng sat up two nights revising → Cheng, while composing the vocal music, went back and changed words → **every evening he went to the Horse God Temple to seek correction from Wang Yaoqing** | **A great success**: premiered on 29 April 1940 at the Golden Theater in Shanghai, "billed and wildly full", running more than a dozen performances; in Beiping it broke precedent with two consecutive performances. **Criticism from peers is recorded alongside**: Fan Junhong faulted the ending as "lower in tone and detached from the theme" (it was not cut from the final revised text) | p0138–0144 |
| **《女儿心》 (A Daughter's Heart)** (Princess Baihua) | Cheng Yanqiu (to fill the gap for "martial skill and dance together, rich costume, a happy ending, civil and martial equally weighted") | **Field research**: he first watched the Shanxi Bangzi 《百花亭》 [The Hundred-Flower Pavilion] ("some of its performance technique is finer in its delicate passages than Kunqu"); **one debate over the ending reversed the conclusion** — Weng proposed cutting the reunion and having Baihua cut her own throat; Cheng Yanqiu wanted her to live, on the grounds that it gave the play a larger line of motivation (stopping civil war, preserving unity); Weng accepted at once and added "from hundred-tempered steel to softness that wraps the finger" | Ending reversed once | Success — specially made leopard-tail double spears and twenty-four Hundred-Flower banners embroidered with flowers of the seasons; a long 【二黄反调】 (Erhuang fandiao) passage in "Deqing Nunnery", with **the entrances of four pairs of lovers packed into the interludes** to drive the protagonist's inner life. When another company mounted the same subject, Cheng promised to "perform it only in Shanghai, never in Beiping or Tianjin" and kept that promise for life | p0144–0147 |
| **《楚宫秋》 (Autumn in the Chu Palace)** | Cheng Yanqiu (suggested by Wang Yaoqing in a break while coaching the vocal music of A Daughter's Heart) | Expanded the 生 (shēng — male role) and 旦 (dàn — female role) scene 《武昭关》 [Wu Zhaoguan] by "adding joints and episodes" into a full-length play centered on Consort Ma; **distinguished from Ma Lianliang's treatment of the same subject by "who is the center" and "whether a certain scene is included"**, not by changing the plot | — | Performed later in other cities | p0148–0150 |

| **《杜鹃红》 (Red Azaleas)** | The sisters Xu Dongming and Xu Dongxia (rewritten from the Shanxi Bangzi 《女中孝》 [A Daughter's Filial Piety]) | **Novelty not sought in plot**: "seemingly new but actually old, seemingly old and yet new... | **《一字香》 (One-Character Incense)** (adapted from 《十五贯》 [Fifteen Strings of Cash]) | — | **Kept both plotlines as in the 传奇 (chuánqí — the long Ming–Qing drama form) original** (Kuang Zhong investigates the prison and tests characters by visiting a rat; the two miscarriages of justice involving Xiong Youlan and Xiong Youhui) | — | **Never performed** — the failure condition as he himself defined it: "keep both lines as in the chuanqi original and there will be too many characters to stage". | **《比翼舌》 (Wing-to-Wing Tongues)** | Wu Suqiu (she wanted a tragedy like Tears of the Mandarin Ducks) | A "stone thrown into water" model of triple conflict among mother- and daughter-in-law, sisters-in-law, and mother and daughter; the title comes from "waves beneath the tongue break the paired wings" | Cast changed at the last minute (Ge's mother "jumped into the pot" — learned the role at short notice) | **Failure**: from the first performance the audience visibly split in two; **the only thing that worked for everyone was technique visible on the spot (a painted-face actor mimicking voices and manners)**. His later verdict: "it is not so much a tragedy as merely a 'problem play'". From this he concluded that "**even a tragedy must be interspersed with comic technique in moderation**" | p0158–0164 |
| **《玉壶冰》 (Ice in a Jade Vase)** | Wu Suqiu | **The "cold reception in the bridal chamber" scene uses one prop (a brazier) plus eight verbs** (warming alone, warming in bed, beckoning the fire, avoiding the fire, crowding the fire, stealing the fire, raging at the fire, snatching the fire), threaded through a long 【南梆子】 (Nanbangzi) aria; he called this scene the play's "pig belly" | — | Success; but he judged the scene in which the wicked father is made drunk and a farce erupts to be "**cheap applause, shallow and flippant, with nothing to commend it**" — **a scene that wins cheers backstage is not necessarily a successful scene** | p0165–0169 |
| **《钟馗传》 (The Legend of Zhong Kui)** | Jin Shaoshan (a *huāliǎn* — painted-face role, with bound shoulders) | **First supplied the causes and consequences around a single scene** (retelling the whole *chuánqí* 《天下乐》 [Joy Under Heaven] on which 《嫁妹》 [Marrying Off His Sister] is based); **makeup determines scene order** — the 脸谱 (liǎnpǔ — face pattern) differs before and after "Five Demons Tease the Judge", so "two scenes must be written into the script to allow time for repainting the face, while also serving the plot; they must not go limp" (he used the scene "Zhong's Mother Watches for Her Son", in which Li Duokui sings 【二黄导·碰·原】); **the King of Hell played as a clown** to avoid "two painted-face roles in one scene" | Jin Shaoshan raised three questions and elevated the technique of the last scene (a painted ivory tablet + a spring-loaded bat + fire effects, "no need to lower a bat from the courtyard sky; the audience is guaranteed to see the bat come from heaven") | **Never staged** — 《金大力》 [Jin Dali] became a hit first and drew attention away; Jin Shaoshan wavered between "the gambler's hope for a shortcut and the entrepreneur's determination to storm the fortress", then went to Shanghai, suffered a car accident, lost heart, and died of illness in 1948 | p0170–0192 |
| **《金大力》 (Jin Dali)** | Jin Shaoshan (written in a week, from the storytelling cycle 《五女七贞》 [Five Women, Seven Chaste Ones]) | "One must gather details from this kind of life and distill character. **The language should use plain line-drawing; one must not overdo the literary flourishes**" | — | **It became a hit, but it ate The Legend of Zhong Kui** — **hand the same actor two scripts at once and the lighter one will eat the heavier** | p0186–0192 |
| **《全部周瑜》 (The Complete Zhou Yu)** | Ye Shenglan (叶盛兰; an all-round 小生, xiǎoshēng — young male role: fan xiaosheng / martial xiaosheng / pheasant-feather xiaosheng) | Opposed forcing 《临江会》 [The Meeting at Linjiang] and 《龙姑庙》 [Dragon Aunt Temple] into one continuous plot ("the events are too far apart"); the compromise was **to add a scene, "Sun Ce Angrily Executes Yu Ji", making Yu Ji an embodiment of the "narrator"** to cover the intervening process and give the star time to rest and change costume; he also added "Pointing at the Granary to Borrow Grain" to bring in Lu Su | One compromise | Success; **his self-examination**: "Peking opera audiences of the time took 'play' and 'technique' as one package... In playwrighting terms I may have had a small insight; in commercial terms I was still a thousand miles away." — **a direct disagreement with Li Yu's "structure first", and it must be preserved** | p0197–0200 |
| **《天国女儿》 (Daughter of the Heavenly Kingdom)** | Fuliancheng (Han Baoying) | **Five key points fixed for the arias first**: quarrelling in the tent / pulling out the arrow / choosing a son-in-law / the bridal chamber / breaking out | — | Success — the movement in "Pulling Out the Arrow" borrows from 《长坂坡》 [Changban Slope] yet makes something new, and "one horse, two riders" had no precedent in Peking opera; the vocal music was set to the words by Wang Shaoqing | p0201 |
| **《好姐姐》 (Good Sister)** (Guan Hanqing's 《救风尘》 [Rescue of a Fallen Woman], adapted to *píngjù*) | Xi Cailian | **Deliberately adapted the oldest Yuan drama into Pingju** — "in a plain, popular, well-loved art form, the antique flavour overflows"; **the "true color" principle for titles** ("though the wording is vulgar, it is the accurate language of true color") | — | **Banned** (on the grounds that "this play is about prostitutes and corrupts morals"); not performed. | **《千金小姐》 (The Precious Young Lady)** (Bai Renfu's 《墙头马上》 [Over the Wall and on Horseback], adapted to Pingju) | Xi Cailian | As above; the vocal music, by Li Xiaofang, absorbed the shadow-play tunes of Shenzhou and "created a brand-new yet classical-school Pingju vocal style"; the character design referred to wax figures of Tang and Song women | — | **Banned** ("a man and woman elope, corrupting morals"); not performed | p0204–0208 |
| **《白虹贯日》 (A White Rainbow Pierces the Sun)** | A joint production (written in three days) | **History altered in seven places to enlarge the theme**: Consort Mei given "twelve more years of life" to join the resistance / Xie Aman created to dance the Sword Dance / the collaborator archetype Zhang Po added (offering his wife) / Lei Haiqing's furious denunciation / the hot-blooded subordinate general Geng Yi added to break the deadlock / "Li Zhuer assassinates An Qingxu" changed to "stabs An Lushan to death" / Consort Mei added, taking her own life and offering a "plum-blossom broth" | The script was sent to Cheng Yanqiu for review before rehearsal | Success — a first-hand record of audience reaction (when Zhang Po offers his wife, someone in the audience shouted, "You bastard! ### 1.1 Three kinds of things to look up in this table

1. **What kind of script a particular actor wanted**: Cheng Yanqiu wanted comedy to vary his repertoire (Spring in the Wine Jar was shelved, The Jewelry Purse was commissioned) and wanted "martial skill and dance together, a happy ending" (A Daughter's Heart); Ye Shenglan wanted a full-length play centered on the impoverished scholar (Tears of the Mandarin Ducks) and wanted to display all his xiaosheng skills (The Complete Zhou Yu); Jin Shaoshan wanted the causes and consequences around Marrying Off His Sister and demanded filler scenes for changing his face makeup (The Legend of Zhong Kui); Yuan Shihai wanted "physiological conflict" (Li Kui Visits His Mother) and wanted to replace the fan with a hand lantern (Yunluo Mountain); Tan Fuying wanted an added passage of "Erhuang dao, peng, yuan" (The Little Lantern Mountain) and wanted a change of rhyme category (The General and the Chancellor Make Peace); Zhou Xinfang wanted an "old man play" (and invented his own method of "changing the white beard").
2. **Why a script could not be staged**: the title lost out on seniority (Twelve Chasms); the star, at his peak, did not want "one more foot on the old measure" (Yaguan Tower); after one play was a hit, "horses turned loose on the southern hills" (The Little Lantern Mountain vs. The General and the Chancellor Make Peace, Spring in the Wine Jar vs. The Jewelry Purse); a conflict of genre (Spring in the Wine Jar); the troupe's role types did not fit (The Thirteen Tai Bao Rebel at Suzhou); a ban (Good Sister, The Precious Young Lady, Ximen Bao, the retitling of Soul of a Beauty); internal strife in the troupe (The Rose Thorn).
3. **Which technique came from which play**: **帷灯匣剑** = the short scene after the road-blocking in The General and the Chancellor Make Peace ("the protagonist does not appear, yet one feels he has entered. It is like a lamp behind a curtain: the lamp is unseen, but its light shines out; a sword in a box: the sword is unseen, but its aura presses outward", p0297) / **烘云托月 · 背面敷粉** = the servants written before the protagonist's entrance in The Jewelry Purse, the offstage Li Zicheng conveyed by a 【粉蝶儿】 in City of Blood and Tears, the parting at the Long Pavilion in The Wild Boar Forest, and 《中山羹》 [The Zhongshan Broth] (it appears four times in the book with a stable meaning: **use other people's reactions to write a person who is absent or inconvenient to show directly**) / **草蛇灰线** = the monkeys mistaking Li Changgeng in Havoc in Heaven ("using the plot as it develops to lead a character on with a buried line and no visible trace") and the aria's last line transitioning between units in Li Kui Visits His Mother / **回澜漩涡 (huílán xuánwō — "returning waves and whirlpools")** = the seven-character riddle in Havoc in Heaven (**let the antagonist state the conclusion himself**) / **隔股聚扇 · 选兵蓄势** = Ximen Bao / **殊途同归 (shū tú tóng guī — "different roads, same destination")** = The Thorn Hairpin / **浅绛 · 螺旋蚓曲** = Peach Blossom Village / **尽态极妍 (jìn tài jí yán — "exhausting every state of beauty")** = the "ivory rice bucket" in Havoc in Heaven.

### 1.2 Weng Ouhong's three working methods (directly usable)

- **The playwright's scope of duty** (p0009): not only to sit at the desk and write, but "also to serve as a rehearser without the title of director, **to write the full gong-and-drum outline of the play for the drummer, the full stage-management outline for the stage hands, to design costumes and makeup for the characters, and to stand at the wings to supervise the performance**; and, as the playwright, to organize the troupe and travel with it on tour".
- **The little stage in the mind** (p0031, p0138): "A playwright has only written the words of a script; it cannot yet be called a mature work. At the very least, while writing he must **first build a little stage in his mind**... **Often, from the moment he conceives a character's name, the figure appears, and costume and makeup come with it. When he begins to write, at the same time as the dialogue and lyrics, the positions, movements, expressions, and dances of these figures on that little stage surge up, and with them the rhythm of gongs and drums**, flowing from the pen... he writes it all down in the script, not minding the trouble."
- **The late-life "silent play" method of writing** (p0366): first, on the sofa, **think silently through the scene's main plot, main conflict, and the method by which the conflict will be handled and elevated**, while also thinking through the characters' action lines and super-objective, the stage blocking, and the performance image; then organize the dialogue and structure the arias; when the whole scene is roughly complete, **strike while the iron is hot and write at the desk — at this point the writing is merely transcription**; **after transcribing, do not polish at once; set it aside for four or five hours**, then with a clear mind **review and revise objectively**, and **polish the lyrics last**. His motto: "**Make plans for success only; do not entertain thoughts of success.**"
- **The three-way distinction in adaptation (do not mix them)**: **arrangement (zhěnglǐ)** = preserve the original as far as possible (The Goddess of the Luo River); **adaptation (gǎibiān)** = remove the weeds and take the essence, recut, adding or cutting according to one's own judgment (Two Phoenixes Flying Together, Peach Blossom Village); **transplantation (yízhí)** = seize the main spine and build a new stove, wanting the performance rather than the original script (Tears of the Mandarin Ducks). There is also a category of "following the original's episodes and only polishing according to Peking opera's laws" (《孙安动本》 [Sun An Memorializes the Throne]). **Self-check**: write down which category you are in before you begin; mixing them produces something that "looks neither like the original nor like a new play".
- **A history of credits** (p0224–0225, p0253–0254): the xiqu world had "a congenital injustice — the name of the script's author was never mentioned"; because the Zhonghua Opera School students "spoke the truth with utter sincerity and made the performance of a certain play... known to society as it was", the newspapers began to print his name. His position: "Since I have opened a thorny path for xiqu writers, I have a duty to keep going and not retreat at the first difficulty." **But he was persuaded to stage A Hundred Battles to Revive the Tang under the name "A Secret Text from the Southern Palace", and for The Wild Boar Forest he voluntarily withdrew his name for friendship's sake** — together these two episodes are first-hand material for "a history of the xiqu playwright's credit".

### 2.1 "Feeling out four bottoms" (notes on adapting The Jiujiang Ford; the most directly workflow-ready section in the book)

**General principle**: "In arranging traditional xiqu plays there is one important piece of experience: before starting work, feel out the "**bottom**"... **The traditional performance text is of course an important "bottom"; but it is not the only one.** If you limit the range of your bottom-feeling to it, your field of vision narrows." His habit was, "after reading the script, always to begin from the "**periphery**""; which bottoms to feel out "**varies with the play**".

| Bottom | What to feel out | Result for The Jiujiang Ford |
|---|---|---|
| **Historical bottom** | "The play's typical environment must at least not violate basic historical truth" — **the aim is not to restore the "play" to history** | The story appears neither in the histories nor in 《明英烈》 [The Ming Heroes], only scattered material in the History of Ming and the Ping-Han Record; "although it departs from historical fact, in general it has its causes and consequences, **producing a reasonable historical association**". **The original author's partisanship is worth learning from**: he did not pour "absolute sympathy" on either Zhu or Chen, "but **offered historical lessons from the angle of gains and losses in war**... the brilliance lies in the author's having no subjective frame, while his partisanship is still quite distinct" |
| **Performance bottom** | In highly technical plays, the star's acting and movement "often exist only on stage, not in the script. **Ignore it, and an alteration of a few words may wipe out a great deal of performance**"; but one must not attend only to technique — "**when you have not clarified the performance's characteristics from the stage's point of view, even the highest ideological level often has nowhere to be applied**" | Zhang Dingbian has two ways of playing, **武二花 (wǔ'èrhuā — martial second painted-face, a rough beauty)** and **武老生 (wǔlǎoshēng — martial old man, a fierce beauty)**, both lacking "seasoned depth and steadiness"; the technical checklist: water sleeves, beard, eye focus, stage walk, movement, pose, boat-running, and combat. His evaluation of the original's playwrighting: "laid out smooth, cushioned smartly, gathered tight, released wide, 'giving a shoulder', 'leaving room', all just right"; its weakness: "it highlighted only Zhang Dingbian and ignored the other characters" |
| **Stylistic bottom** | "What are the characteristics of the original's style? Are they suited to the characters and life it portrays? Is there any need and possibility to change or raise it? How can the added episodes be brought into harmony with the original style?" | The style of The Jiujiang Ford is "**broad-line outline, legendary coloring, leaping rhythm, brisk technique, plain language, strong images**" — to be inherited and developed; the problem lay in Chen Youliang ("too much comic patter and even language and performance close to vulgarity... so this man of the hour, at the focus of both the enemy conflict and the internal conflict, appears shallow and inept") |
| **Script bottom** | "From the surface to the inner substance, consider and reconsider" | The original had twenty-three scenes, and "the "play" only begins in scene 8, when Hua Yunlong's feigned marriage is seen through by Zhang Dingbian"; the conflict "lacks rise and fall, and just when it might deepen, it tends to stop"; two examples of "trailing off" (after Zhang Dingbian's interrogation fails to extract a confession, he exits merely with a "rubbing the belly" pose; after Hua Yunlong's accusation, Chen Youliang takes his false son-in-law to stroll in the garden) |

**Synthesis of the four bottoms**: "From the performance angle it looks like a limitation of the wu'erhua or wulaosheng role type itself, **but look at the script and you find the main cause lies elsewhere** — in the flawed characterization of Zhang Dingbian." **The final decision was one of three options** (the "play" stays close to historical sources / the "play" and history are "cooked in two pots" / follow the original's own creative path); he chose the third, for the reason: "It already has a good foundation and does not violate historical truth; it has watchable performance technique and a deep popular influence; **from the standpoint of arranging the xiqu heritage, one may make small repairs or a major overhaul, but it is always best not to depart from the original foundation**."

### 2.2 Four preconditions and three gains in working with actors (The Jiujiang Ford)

**Four preconditions**: "If the collaborator — the actor — **has rich stage experience, a spirit of bold innovation, a fairly close artistic outlook to one's own, and at the same time a deep interest in this play**, then success in collaboration may be expected."

1. **Casting comes before writing**. The difficulty was "who (or what role type) should play Hua Yunlong" (formerly a second-rank *wǔshēng*; the adaptation was to strengthen his part). **A cool judgment on "breaking the role type"**: "So-called "breaking the role type" **is by no means easy; it depends greatly on the actor's foundation and creative ability; it is not the case that if you can write it, he can break through**." Yuan Shihai's answer was to invite **Ye Shenglan** (a famous xiaosheng) to play it. Fan Junhong's gain: "**casting before writing and casting after writing are two very different sciences**... **without this "role" I would not have dared to write so freely; with this "role", my imagination imperceptibly grew richer**."
2. **"Both new and old audiences must be supported"**. "In arranging and adapting traditional plays there often appears the situation of "**curing one channel and damaging another**"... in the last analysis these are mostly artistic problems. For example, **the characters' images have been "knocked out of shape"; the play's distinctive flavour has disappeared; fine passages of performance or song can no longer be seen or heard**." The dividing line: if the original's thought content is faulty and needs "recasting", then never mind; **but if the result comes from "elevating" characters or "strengthening" the theme without limit, one cannot simply blame the old audience as "the force of habit"**.
3. **Three examples of actors opening the road for the playwright**: Yuan Shihai prepared to fuse two approaches on the basis of the *jiàzi huāliǎn* and absorb nourishment from the Qi and Gai schools, "encouraging me to write boldly; so long as it starts from character, **you may even set a hard problem and 'check' him — that does not matter**"; **"Seizing the Seal" moved from offstage to onstage** (with "horse-riding gongs and drums" accompanying "wordless action", exiting finally with a "rinsing step"; the movement was "transformed" from Mei Lanfang's 《穆桂英挂帅》 [Mu Guiying Takes Command] and the film *Lin Zexu*), and it gave the "rubbing the belly" exit, which had had to be cut, a chance to "**shift position**"; **the vocal change in 《孝服拦马》 [Mourning Dress at the Pass]** (originally sung in "chuiqiang"; Yuan Shihai proposed changing it to "Erhuang sanban" and drawing on Zhou Xinfang's 《追韩信》 [Chasing Han Xin] style, writing a passage of "**Erhuang pengban sanyan**" that the traditional painted-face role did not possess).
4. **The rhythm of revision**: "He is good at listening to opinions, but he does not '**change on hearing every report**'... only last winter did he make **a comprehensive and detailed revision**. The advantage of doing it this way is that opinions are relatively concentrated, consideration is relatively mature, and **the author can keep the initiative throughout**."

## §3 Three Examples of Collective Adaptation Workflows

### 3.1 《沙家浜》 (Shajiabang): the "three-way combination" (1965)

**The three-party structure described in the afterword**: "**a three-way combination of leadership, professional workers, and the masses**", which "was throughout conducted under the direct care and instruction of the Party leadership". **The list of changes was derived backward from external opinions**: emphasize armed struggle → change the ending → change scenes 2 and 5 → add arias for Guo Jianguang → Sister Aqing yields place. The play's lineage: Wen Mu's Shanghai opera 《芦荡火种》 [Fire Seeded in the Reeds] → the Peking opera Fire Seeded in the Reeds (a separate edition whose "Preface" records the first round of adaptation, **not included in this book — a known gap**) → the August 1965 version of Shajiabang → the final text of May 1970. According to the Wikipedia entry, after seeing the play in July 1964 Mao Zedong proposed "bringing the role of armed struggle to the fore", having the ending "fight its way in", and changing the title — **corresponding exactly to item 3 of the afterword's account, so the two corroborate each other**. According to "Wang Zengqi and the Writing of Shajiabang", the lyrics of the trio and the rivers-and-lakes patter of "build up the seven-star stove" were written by Wang Zengqi, and were preserved "by outwardly obeying and inwardly defying". → **A first-hand record of the "assigned adaptation" process: the outside supplies the thematic judgment; the writer changes the scenes and lyrics.**

### 3.2 Song Jingshi: field research + separate scene-writing + a gong-and-drum read-through for consolidation (1952)

- **Scale of research**: they set out in early February 1952 by the lunar calendar and spent two months; "in all we investigated and interviewed **eight counties, 163 villages and towns, and 734 elderly people, recording 180,000 characters of raw conversation**". The research covered origins and family, motives for revolt, members, the course of battles, reasons for failure, and relations with the White Lotus, the Taiping Heavenly Kingdom, and the Nian Army.
- **The most crucial point**: the question the team cared about and doubted before setting out, "**the question of Song Jingshi's surrender**" — "**if the truth of this question were not clarified, any literary or artistic work on Song Jingshi might run aground**"; the conclusion was that "from beginning to end it was a 'false surrender', a matter of his strategy". → **An executable rule: for modern or historical-figure subjects, first find the question that "cannot be written until it is clarified", then begin.**
- **Separate scene-writing**: "**Tian Han, A Jia, Guxue, and I wrote the scenes separately**. Comrade Tian Han wrote the first two scenes, Comrade A Jia continued, I wrote the middle, Guxue the later part. After half a month we had each finished our share and assembled the whole. The content was very rich; we only felt the length was too great."
- **The method of consolidation** (very unusual): Chen Baichen proposed that "**I read the entire script aloud to the directors of the Institute in a recitation with 'gongs and drums', to solicit their opinions**"; after repeated discussion and several cuts it was compressed to four hours. → This is the same craft as "one person reads the full prompt script" in Tears of the Mandarin Ducks: **a read-through with gongs and drums is the xiqu script's "test screening".**
- **Two directing teams, revising while rehearsing**: the first team, led by A Jia, rehearsed the first half; the second, led by Xue Enhou, the second half; "each time a stage was rehearsed, Ma Shaobo called a seminar to sum up experience, and according to problems found in practice the script was again added to or cut, **with me holding the pen. Thus we rehearsed while revising and revised while rehearsing**"; it was staged after two months.
- **Appendix: the "theme review" checklist for arranging traditional plays in the same period** (the situation in 1951–52): "every time an old play was performed, its thematic meaning and artistic form had to be studied, along with errors and flaws in the lyrics and dialogue — for example, the ghost problem in 《打金砖》 [Striking the Golden Brick], the dream-telling problem in 《刺王僚》 [Assassinating King Liao], the plague-god problem in 《问樵闹府》 [Questioning the Woodcutter and Disturbing the Prefecture], the two-wives problem in 《红鬃烈马》 [The Red-Maned Steed], and the problem that Jin Wuzhu in 《挑滑车》 [Challenging the Sliding Carts] should be changed to Wanyan Zongbi... as a result many items could not be performed for a time."

### 3.3 The Battle of Red Cliffs: three sentences from the creative group + democratic casting (1958)

- The first draft followed the plan of "two scripts written separately, performed complete over two evenings" and was delivered in ten days; it was immediately overturned ("neither economical in time nor concentrated in story"), and Liu Zhiming took charge, with **Li Lun, Ren Guilin, A Jia, Ma Shaobo, and Weng Ouhong forming a creative group**, required to make it **complete in one evening**.
- **The three sentences distilled by the creative group**: the center of educational meaning, "**unite against the enemy, defeat the many with the few**"; the play's artistic counterpoint, "**the fierce conflict between the war party and the surrender party, between the consciousness of unity and the consciousness of jealousy**"; the method, the approach of "**cutting cloth to a broad measure and choosing the piece to cut; serving two chickens and taking the stopover to dine**", **boldly cutting, compressing, and merging, yet not abandoning the traditional essentials that everyone loves**.
- **Decisions on additions and cuts**: newly written "Scoffing at Jiangnan" ("the massed ranks of Cao Cao's army must be shown, **to bring out the 'many' in the theme 'defeat the many with the few'**"), "Deciding to Cross the River", "The Sun–Liu Alliance Is Made", "A Solemn Farewell and Declaration of Purpose", "Poetry with the Spear Across the Saddle", "Dragon and Tiger Winds and Clouds"; **cut** Jiang Gan's second crossing and Pang Tong's chain-link stratagem; "Observing the Winds and Falling Ill", "Writing the Prescription", "Borrowing the East Wind", and "Burning the Warships" were "**following Xiao's rules and Cao's precedents, all according to tradition**", with only a few lines polished. **The play's fifteen scenes**: Scoffing at Jiangnan / Deciding to Cross the River / The Alliance Is Made / Installing the Commander / Stealing the Letter / Falling for the Trick and Beheading the General / The Fire-Attack Plan / Borrowing Arrows / Beating Huang Gai / Dragon and Tiger Winds and Clouds / Poetry with the Spear Across the Saddle / The Solemn Farewell / Borrowing the East Wind / Burning the Red Cliffs / Defeat at Huarong.
- **Democratic consultation in casting**: the play needed four laosheng who had played leading roles (Zhuge Liang, Lu Su, Liu Bei, Zhang Zhao), three painted faces (Cao Cao, Huang Gai, Sun Quan), a pheasant-feather xiaosheng for Zhou Yu, and a square-cap clown for Jiang Gan. Ma Lianliang first claimed Zhuge Liang to the end and gave up Lu Su; Li Shaochun yielded to Tan Fuying and recommended Li Hezeng; Li Hezeng claimed Zhang Zhao; Tan Fuying **argued from the script** for the importance of the newly added Liu Bei ("Liu Bei's scenes are not many, but they bear on an important aspect of the three-way balance"); finally it was settled that **Tan Fuying and Li Shaochun would alternate as Lu Su and Liu Bei** — Li Shaochun's reason: "We will both rehearse both parts, and later we can swap; this is a fine chance to learn and a fine chance to practice."
- **A failure placed on record**: **after three performances "The Solemn Farewell" was ordered cut**, on the grounds that "Huang Gai's feigned surrender is like a soldier today blowing up a blockhouse: everything depends on the spirit of one sustained effort, and he does not need his commander to console and encourage him again; **such consolation and encouragement amounts to pulling Huang Gai's leg back, and the side effects must be guarded against**". The audience saw the lyrics of "The Solemn Farewell" in the program but not the performance; the theater answered that "the play was too long and was cut". Dissenting opinion: "Huang Gai and Zhou Yu of the Three Kingdoms had their historical and ideological limitations, unlike the consciousness of soldiers today... **this neither violates historical truth nor produces any side effect**." The published script still lacks this scene. → **A failure case of forcing a modern reading onto ancient characters, to be set beside the book's many admissions of "the limitations of the age".**

### 3.4 Appendix: two forms of collaborative playwriting

- **"Skylight" collaboration** (The Wild Boar Forest, with Li Shaochun, p0253): "I did not tire of the tedious work of patching many holes in his script... **whenever a rock blocked the way or a peak stopped us, I opened the road for him**... four or five times a day we consulted by telephone, and some lines and places he found difficult were settled on the phone. **Where some sung or spoken passage could not be completed, he first left a "skylight" for me to fill.** This form of collaborative playwriting also opened a path." Li Shaochun's confession about learning to write plays: "Playwriting is not easy! It looks simple enough, but take up the pen and you are lost. **Sometimes, for two lines, I have drunk a whole bottle of beer and finished a plate of peanuts, and still, like a pen that will not drip ink, I could not squeeze out a single line.**" His self-discipline as an "actor-playwright": "So long as I can think of an idea, I will take a seat. **If I have no fresh trick, I must not make up the numbers**... **playwriting is real skill, won spear-thrust by spear-thrust.**"
- **"One draft, several sets of options" delivery** (The Red Lantern, p0404): first write a script that is "complete and detailed in song, spoken lines, and stage directions" but **leaves two ways of doing things at every key point**, for the director to decide (the first scene's entrance sung or made to 【水底鱼】; the execution-ground entrance "dao, peng, yuan" or 【新水令】; "Bitterly Recounting the Family's Revolutionary History" using Shanghai opera's time-space transformation or Peking opera's foregrounded speech). → **Before handing an adaptation to the director, prepare two formal options at each of three to five key points and let the director decide; this is an institutionalized form of "playwright–director tacit understanding".**
