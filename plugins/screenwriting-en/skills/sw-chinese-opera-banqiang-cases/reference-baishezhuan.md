## §8 Sixteen-Scene Structure Table for *The White Snake*

| Scene | Scanned pages | Pages | Type | Arias | Close |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

| 9. 上山 (Going Up the Mountain) | p0058–0064 | 7 | A spoken-word scene (Fahai preaching), **about 80% spoken** | 3 arias (including 【四平调】 (sìpíngdiào — four-level tune), 10 lines) | Two spoken couplet lines + a great laugh to close |
| 10. 渡江 (Crossing the River) | p0064–0065 | 2 | Transition scene | 1 suite (【导板】 dǎobǎn — leading-board tempo → 【散板】 → 【流水板】 liúshuǐbǎn — flowing metre) | All exit together |
| 11. 索夫 (Demanding Her Husband) | p0065–0070 | 6 | Three-way confrontation in duet, **about 80% sung** | 9 arias (including three rounds of 【快板】 (kuàibǎn — fast tempo) mutual denunciation and the play's only unison song) | The divine generals (神将) pursue them offstage, leading into the fight |
| 12. 水斗 (The Water Battle) | p0070–0071 | 2 | **Pure 武戏 (wǔxì — martial play)**, only two qupai plus cues | 2 qupai | Curtain |
| 13. 逃山 (Fleeing the Mountain) | p0071–0075 | 5 | A 丑 (chǒu — clown) 白口 (báikǒu — spoken-word) scene, **about 90% spoken** | 2 arias | The young novice (小沙弥) runs off; **the Wind God (风神) shields Xu Xian and leads him off** |
| 14. 断桥 (The Broken Bridge) | p0075–0089 | **15** | **The longest scene in the play and the peak of song**, about 70% sung | **18 arias in nine banshi** | The three reconcile and exit together to the left |
| 15. 合钵 (Closing the Alms Bowl) | p0089–0097 | 9 | Song and acting equally weighted (tragic close), about 60% sung | 12 arias | Bai Suzhen is taken away under guard; lights dim |
| 16. 倒塔 (Toppling the Pagoda) | p0097–0099 | 3 | Martial finale | 1 aria (Xiao Qing) | The pagoda falls; Bai Suzhen appears with a radiant smile. (End) |

**Criterion**: The three longest scenes (The Broken Bridge 15, The Wine Transformation 11, Strolling by the Lake 10) are precisely the three turning points in the Bai–Xu relationship (meeting / rupture / reconciliation); the three shortest (Guarding the Mountain 2, The Water Battle 2, Crossing the River 2) are all transitions or martial scenes. **Two of the sixteen scenes have no singing**: Scene 2, The Wedding (all spoken dialogue, yet one of the play's largest plot turns) and Scene 12, The Water Battle (only ensemble qupai, with no singing by the principals).

### 10.1 The Fourteen Lines of "Have You the Heart?" (#8, printed pp. 65–66)

> Have you the heart to wound me (你忍心将我伤), / on the Duanwu festival urging me to drink realgar wine (端阳佳节劝雄黄); / have you the heart to deceive me (你忍心将我诳), / just after we swore before the twin stars (纔对双星盟誓愿), / you followed Fahai into the meditation hall (又随法海入禅堂); / have you the heart to break my heart (你忍心叫我断肠), / saying nothing of our former tenderness (平日恩情且不讲), / how could you not think of the child I carry? (怎不念我腹中怀有小儿郎？); / have you the heart to watch me defeated and destroyed (你忍心见我败亡), / pity me against the divine generals, blade against spear (可怜我与神将、刀对枪), / fought until my strength was gone, my head spun, my belly ached beyond bearing (只杀得我筋疲力尽、头晕目眩、腹痛不可当), / while you stood with folded arms on the hillside (你袖手旁观在山岗). / Lay your hand on your chest and think (手摸胸膛你想一想): / with what face do you come before your wife? (有何面目来见妻房？)

**Technique**: ① **the four "have you the heart" phrases form a four-stage escalation** (wound → deceive → break my heart → watch me destroyed), **and the line count at each stage increases** (2 / 3 / 3 / 4); the last two lines step back and ask a question. ② **Character counts**: 5, 7 / 5, 7, 7 / 5, 7, 10 / 5, 8, **17**, 7 / 7, 7 — "fought until my strength was gone, my head spun, my belly ached beyond bearing" is **a seventeen-character 垛句 (duǒjù — inserted short line), the longest in the whole passage, placed exactly on the most painful stage** (Fan Junhong (范钧宏) calls this **the beauty of irregularity**, 错落美 cuòluòměi); ③ **the banshi is 【西皮垛板】, not kuaiban** (the revised text makes it kuaiban: the speed goes up, but the layered effect of the duoju weakens); ④ the line endings rhyme on **江阳** (伤, 黄, 诳, 愿, 堂, 肠, 讲, 郎, 亡, 枪, 当, 岗, 想, 房).

### 10.2 Bai Suzhen Confesses Her Origins (#12, one aria in four banshi)

**The four-part shape of the banshi matches the four-part shape of the content**: daoban (stopping the sword) → bangzi qingban (origins + falling in love + sacrifice, the longest) → erniu (waiting; medium tempo, the most lyrical) → kuaiban (the battle at Jinshan). **The slow part is given to the past, the fast part to what has just happened.** Within the passage, three parallel "I love you" phrases (your deep and constant affection and elegant bearing / your constant remembrance of your mother / your self-reliance that accepts no one's pity) — **what she loves is three concrete things, not three adjectives**. The closing line, "Who is right and who is wrong — ask your own heart!", **does not pronounce a verdict**. 【梆子清板】 is the original marking of the 1955 text (qingban = beat only, no bridge accompaniment); the revised text has 【南梆子原板】.

### 10.3 Xu Xian's 【反西皮】 (#13, unique to the 1955 text)

**Structurally it is a line-by-line response to Bai Suzhen's passage**: she says, "I first knew you in the wind and rain on the lake" (风雨湖中识郎面); he says, "I first met you in wind and rain at West Lake" (风雨西湖初见面); she says, "For you I stole the herb on the immortal mountain and endured every hardship" (我为你仙山盗草受尽了颠连); he says, "Thanks to you for going so far to steal the herb on the sacred mountain" (多亏你灵山盗草不辞远); she says, "Every night I waited for you until the fifth watch" (哪一夜不等你到五更天); he says, "Poor you, waiting for me every night until the fifth watch" (可怜你每夜等我到五更天). **A duet need not alternate; it can also be "one whole passage repeating the other's words"; to repeat is to acknowledge.** The closing line, "Even if you are of another kind, my heart will not change" (你纵然是异类我也心不变), answers her "Even if you are of another kind, my love for you is not shallow" (纵然是异类我待你情非浅) head-on — **each uses "even if you are of another kind" once; only when it passes from her mouth to his is the misunderstanding resolved**. The revised text lacks these eighteen lines and gives Xu Xian only four yaoban lines: **the revision cuts the 生 (shēng — male role type) material to four lines, and the scene becomes completely a dan solo** (one piece of evidence that performance texts lean toward the star).

### 10.4 The Four Closing Lines and Three Discrepancies in Fan Junhong's Quotation

> How rare, in adversity, for a family to meet again (难得是患难中一家重见), / like swallows carrying mud to rebuild their home (学燕儿衔泥土重整家园). / Little sister Qing, help your sister turn toward Qingbo Gate (小青妹扶为姐清波门转). / Turning suddenly, the place where we sheltered from the rain is still the same (猛回头避雨处风景依然).

Fan Junhong uses these four lines to illustrate **symmetry**: "The first and second lines have the same linguistic rhythm; from the balance and harmony of their syllables, one feels a formal beauty of symmetry." He also gives a criterion: **the four lines of *The Broken Bridge* are symmetrical because the three have cleared up their misunderstanding and their agitated feelings have calmed; the "Have you the heart" passage is irregular because it must display violent emotion.**

| What Fan Junhong quotes | 1955 book image (p0088–0089) |
|---|---|
| Like swallows, carrying mud, **rebuild** (重建) their home | Like swallows carrying mud **restore** (重整) their home |
| **Little Qing'er** (小青儿), help your sister, turn toward Qingbo Gate | **Little sister Qing** (小青妹), help your sister turn toward Qingbo Gate |
| Turning suddenly, the rain-shelter, the scenery is still the same | Turning suddenly, the place where we sheltered from the rain, the scenery is still the same (same) |

(Fan breaks the four lines into "3-3-4" to display the syllables; this is his typesetting treatment — the original book does not break them.) **"Turning suddenly, the place where we sheltered from the rain is still the same" brings back the play's very first image** — the first scene is set exactly here, where they sheltered from the rain and borrowed an umbrella. Another demonstration that lands on the level/oblique tone (平仄, píngzè) of a single character (Fan Junhong p0060–0062): "If 'How rare, in adversity, for a family to meet again' (难得是患难中一家重见) were changed to 'What rejoices is that in adversity flesh and blood meet again' (喜的是患难里骨肉再见), all ten characters would be oblique-tone, and it would inevitably be harsh on the tongue"; "'中' is a level-tone character with an open-mouth sound, '里' is an oblique-tone character with a closed-mouth sound; 'oblique-oblique-level' and 'oblique-oblique-oblique' sound very different when sung" — **this is the only executable demonstration in the whole batch of materials that lands on the level/oblique tone of individual characters**.

### 10.5 Other Points

- **"前腔" is part of this play's annotation system**: when the next person continues in the same banshi, the text marks "(sings 前腔)" without repeating the banshi name; this is used throughout the play.
- **Nine banshi in one scene**: daoban, kutou, yaoban, sanban, duoban, nanbangzi daoban, bangzi qingban, erniu, kuaiban, fan xipi.
- **The three characters "向着他" ("toward him") were changed by Wang Yaoqing (王瑶卿)** (the original manuscript had "祖护他", "shield him") — a one-character colloquial revision, made by an actor.
- **One question from Xiao Qing ("How did you come here today?") forces out sixteen lines of song** — spoken dialogue asks, song answers; this is the clearest "spoken question, sung answer" structure in the scene.

**Script conventions**: character names begin a new line at the margin; before singing, mark "(sings 「banshi name」)"; for a continuation, mark "(sings 前腔)"; for a change of banshi, mark "(turns to 「二六」)"; spoken dialogue follows the character name directly with no mark; stage-action cues are set in parentheses between lyric lines, and there are also whole-paragraph stage directions on their own line; scene headings read "Scene X ○○", with the location on the line below. **The whole book does not use the words "科" or "介", and does not mark percussion patterns.**

### 11.2 The Three-Column Restoration Ledger (an operational definition of a "restored traditional-subject text")

- **Keep**: qupai, entrance poems, self-introduction, kutou, jiaotou, circular walk, fight scenes, spoken couplets, and the "前腔" notation.
- **Cut or weaken**: the protagonists' self-introductions (none for Bai, Xu, or Qing), the neat four-line exit poem (replaced by a two-person couplet), **the entire scene *Decocting the Medicine* (《煎药》)** (its 【二黄慢板】 duet is moved into *Dispelling Doubt*).
- **Add**: the passage in which the Wind God escorts Xu Xian (borrowed from a Guangxi Gui-opera performance text); Xu Xian's long 【反西皮】 passage in *The Broken Bridge*; and two lines of spoken dialogue written from direct observation on Mount Emei ("Idly wandering the fir path, glumly facing the cyathea flowers," 闲游冷杉径，闷对桫椤花).

### 11.3 Key Points from the *Preface* and *Author's Note* (p0010–0014, book images)

- **The three stages of "ten years to grind one sword"**: the Guilin period (first titled *The Golden Alms Bowl* 《金钵记》, premiered by Li Zigui (李紫贵) and Jin Suqiu (金素秋), "banned as soon as it opened"; only after the victory of the War of Resistance was it formally performed in Luzhou, Sichuan) → the opera-school period (Li Zigui rehearsed it at the Chinese Opera Experimental School, with Xie Ruiqing (谢锐青) and Liu Xiurong (刘秀荣) as Bai Niangzi) → the Peking Opera Troupe period (1954, directed by Lü Junqiao (吕君樵) and Zheng Yiqiu (郑亦秋), starring Ye Shenglan (叶盛兰) and Du Jinfang (杜近芳)).
- **Even a mythological play requires experiencing life**: "In the first scene, 'Strolling by the Lake', Bai Niangzi (白娘子) says to Xiao Qing… 'Idly wandering the fir path, glumly facing the cyathea flowers.' These ten short characters came from what I observed last spring after climbing, with friends, in straw sandals and leaning on rattan staffs… to the summit of Mount Emei, 3,060 meters high." — **in the script these ten characters are spoken dialogue, not lyrics**.
- **Borrowing a performance text from another region to change a scene**: the passage in which the Wind God escorts Xu Xian — "**this idea came to me during a Guangxi Gui-opera performance**… I felt that in this way **Xiao Qing has more reason to reproach Xu Xian**. Is that so? I am willing to hear everyone's opinion." — **the criterion for the revision was "who has more reason to say that line", not whether the plot runs smoothly**.
- **Wang Yaoqing's five points on tune** (p0012, the most important page in the book): ① **lyrics need not be too regular** — "He did not like our lyrics written too regularly; he said that made it harder to vary the tune. **That is why I wrote lines like Bai Niangzi's 'Have you the heart to wound me…' to Xu Xian in *The Broken Bridge***"; ② **cut a scene but keep its duet** — "After 'Stealing the Herb' there was originally a scene 'Decocting the Medicine', with a 【二黄慢板】 duet between Bai Niangzi and Qing'er (青儿). Mr. Wang firmly insisted on keeping it… **out of respect for Mr. Wang's wishes, I preserved that duet inside 'Dispelling Doubt'**"; ③ **change two characters** — "'祖护他' ('shield him') changed to '向着他' ('toward him') is so much more vivid"; ④ **do not reject outside vocal systems** — "He did not oppose the use of **gaobozi** (高拨子 — high bozi) in Peking opera; the opera school's 'Stealing the Herb' originally used gaobozi… when Comrade Du Jinfang sang it, it was changed to **suona erhuang** (唢呐二黄 — shawm erhuang), and I think that is fine too"; ⑤ **an entire scene may change vocal system** — "In the scene 'Closing the Alms Bowl', Mr. Wang… used **xipi**; when the Peking Opera Troupe performed it, it was changed to **erhuang**".
- **The boundary between the playwright and second-degree creation (二度创作, èrdù chuàngzuò — directing and performance)** (for the medium-boundary table): "Although the playwright does prescribe something about vocal style and stage treatment, he also entrusts the director and actors with a shared creation within the limits of the play's thematic requirements." — **marking a banshi in the script = "prescribing something", not a final decision; the banshi marking is the author's letter of intent, and a production may change the vocal system for an entire scene.**
- **Character prescriptions in the *Author's Note***: Bai Niangzi deserves sympathy, defined by three concrete things done "for him" (为着他) — selling medicine in Zhenjiang / stealing the herb on the immortal mountain / fighting to the death while pregnant; **Xu Xian: "If he never wavered at all, there would be no tragedy; if he wavered to the end, he would become a negative character"**; Xiao Qing "is a strong character who sets off and interacts with Bai Niangzi's beautiful character"; Fahai "should be handled more deeply and with more pressure".

### 11.4 Cross-References to Other Critics

- **Fan Junhong** uses two passages from *The Broken Bridge* for a teaching contrast between **symmetry and irregular beauty (错落美)**, and gives the criterion for choosing: "Whether lyrics are regular or irregular should proceed from the character." **Fan's quoted text differs from the 1955 edition in three places** (see §10.4); **when writing the skill, follow the book and note both versions side by side**.
- **Zhang Geng (张庚) / Guo Hancheng (郭汉城)** use this play in four places: the chain of textual evolution (**the three sections Stealing the Herb, The Water Battle, and The Broken Bridge were added in performance practice**); *Strolling by the Lake* and *The Broken Bridge* are written in full, while *Stealing the Herb* and *The Water Battle* are given only one stage direction each (two yardsticks: the abstract and the concrete in the allocation of ink); they quote Tian Han's postscript, "he did not like our lyrics written too regularly" (**these notes have verified the source in the original book, p0012**); in *Leifeng Pagoda* (《雷峰塔》), the defeat in the water battle is immediately followed by the meeting at the Broken Bridge = Li Yu's (李渔) "joy and sorrow intermingled (mutually overcoming)".
- **Gu Zhongyi (顾仲彝)** in two places: he uses this play to prove that Chinese opera has a simple through-line ("a lone paulownia and a sturdy bamboo, growing straight up with no branches"); and he lists *The Broken Bridge* among examples where "both sides are good people and their characters are not opposed" yet the play still works. **This play is thus a double sample of both "minimal main line" and "non-oppositional conflict".**
- Two works by Weng Ouhong (翁偶虹) **do not mention this play**, so there is no cross-reference to make.

**The direction is consistent**: the revised text **pulls every "non-standard" banshi in the 1955 text (小安板 xiǎo'ānbǎn, bozi pengban, bangzi qingban, fan xipi, fast yuanban, erhuang manban duet) back to the common xipi banshi, and cuts Xu Xian's long sung passages**. This is precisely the outcome Tian Han feared in the *Preface* — he wrote lines of uneven length precisely to leave room for melodic variation.

**The situational correspondences of five uncommon banshi** (the appendix should be arranged as "situation → banshi", because the playwright moves from situation to banshi): **bangzi qingban** = a long confession close to monologue; **fan xipi** = parting in life and death; **sipingdiao** = retelling something that has already happened; **fast yuanban** = lyricism attached to an action, such as breastfeeding or holding a child; **huilong** = the seam that turns from offstage song to onstage presence, from scene-painting to lyric expression.
