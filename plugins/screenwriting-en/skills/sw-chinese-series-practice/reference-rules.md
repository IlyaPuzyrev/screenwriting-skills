## 14. Zhao Binbin's Index of 26 Excerpts

Source: Zhao Binbin (赵彬彬), *A Course in Screenplay Excerpt Adaptation* (《影视剧片段改编教程》), Chapter 4 (six detailed examples, with scripts + teaching notes, pp. 0024–0083) and Chapter 5, "Practice Materials" (20 excerpts, pp. 0084–0197). **26 excerpts** in total. The direction is the exact opposite of Section 13: **finished film → an 8–20 minute stage excerpt for 3–8 actors** (rehearsal material for higher vocational acting programs). For the method, see Section 6 of [reference.md](reference.md).

### 14.3 Configuration Statistics (merged from the two statistical tables, Zhao pp. 0141 and 0197)

- **Cast size 3–7** (minimum 3: *Bodyguards and Assassins*; maximum 9: *Women of the Swordsman Clan*, *Love Lingers, Herbs Are Fragrant, Part 2*).
- **Set: one main set** (occasionally an adjoining second zone: outside the door / corridor / black room); **the only three-zone set** is *Pretty Big Feet*, and **the only contrasting two-zone set** is *Eighteen Springs*.
- **Genre distribution (the 20 examples in Chapter 5)**: Republic-era family compound / period drama 11; War of Resistance 3; contemporary family 4; rural / period youth 2; palace intrigue 1. → **Period drama overwhelmingly dominates** (8 of the 10 examples in the fourth notebook are period dramas).
- **Where the excerpt is taken from (the 20 examples in Chapter 5)**: episode 1: 7; episode 2: 4; episodes 4–7: 5; episode 20 or later: 2; unmarked: 2. → "**Strong-conflict scenes that can be rehearsed independently**" are highly concentrated in the **first five episodes**.
- **Proportion of second-stage adaptations from novels**: at least 10 of the 20 examples are based on novels (Zhang Ailing 2, Yan Geling 2, Qiong Yao 2, Feiwo Sicun, Qiu Shanshan, Liulian Zi, Dong Luming (董陆明)), meaning **most of the book consists of three-stage "novel → TV drama → stage" adaptations**; each stage compresses—the exact opposite of Zhang Wei's "novel → long-form series = expansion."
- **Rule for endings**: **there must be a visible physical event or an exit**—jumping into the pond / kneeling / weeping / legs giving way and collapsing / an elder taking control and lecturing / a farewell watched from a distance / escaping through a window / everyone dying + kneeling at the front of the stage / being carried out on someone's back / bleeding and fainting / slamming into a wall / closing and locking the door on someone.

### 14.4 Representative Dialogue Examples (transcribed from the original texts, for use as corpus)

#### Example 1: *Love in a Fallen City*—The Detour of Asking for Money (Zhao, p0111): The Standard Model of Chinese "Talking Around the Point"

```
Third Sister-in-Law: (sits down) It's all Mother—she thought it through, saw things clearly, and handed the household accounts over to me. If I didn't manage them, who knows what might happen. Oh, Sixth Sister, you've been back from your husband's family for several days now, haven't you?
Bai Liusu: (embarrassed) Yes. Thank you for your trouble, Third Sister-in-Law.
Third Sister-in-Law: Oh, no trouble at all. (Having said this, she looks at Liusu.) Think of a household this large—the seven things you open the door to: firewood, rice, oil, salt, soy sauce, vinegar, and tea. Is there one of them I have failed to take care of? At the end of every month the accounts have to balance. Sixth Sister, you know our family is not what it used to be. One more person is one more mouth. Those on the books—that is easy enough to discuss. Those not on the books—(she laughs) for me, your sister-in-law, to open my mouth and ask you for board money is truly embarrassing. But if I don't ask, there will be gossip. (She points outside the door.)
```

→ Technique: **a four-step detour**—establish authority (Mother gave me the accounts) → ask about duration (you've been back for days, haven't you?) → cite the shared hardship (the seven daily necessities) → **shift the blame for debt-collecting onto absent "others"** (she points outside the door). **The final cut is delivered without her having to say it herself.**

#### Example 2: *Mother*—The War of Forms of Address (Zhao, pp. 0104–0108): Using One Word to Complete a Positional Confrontation

```
Mancang Niang: Isn't this Jindou? You've come back from the Japanese?!
Jindou: Mancang Niang, call me by my formal name! (arrogant and overbearing)
Mancang Niang: Oh, by seniority you still have to call me Auntie.
Jindou: Hmph, the world has changed. There's no need for so many fussy old rules.
Mancang Niang: The world may change, but the ancestors don't.
  ...
Mancang Niang: Jindou...
Jindou: Address me by my formal name! Chuanshan Ichiro! (hands on hips, chin raised, swaggering)
Meng Shi: Ichiro, Jiro, whatever—call him Deyong! (turns around in shock, feeling humiliated)
Mancang Niang: Deyong, Deyong, look—my whole family and this auntie are kneeling right here before you.
```

→ Technique: a collaborator's name change is contested by **his mother, his neighbor, and himself**; the three names "Deyong / Jindou / Chuanshan Ichiro" = three claims of identity; Mancang Niang's final cry of "Deyong" is both submission and humiliation. **It is more effective than any speech about national righteousness.**

#### Example 3: *Iron Pear Blossom*—The Corridor Interrogation (Zhao, p0121): Turning "Delay" into a Standalone Scene

```
First Mistress: You walk so fast—how could I possibly see you! It's late too. What's the matter, that you're in such a hurry!
Zhang Ji'an: (hemming and hawing) Oh, I haven't seen Auntie for several days. I remember last time Auntie told me she likes Old Sun's walnut cakes. (lifting the parcel) I happened to pass by there, so I bought some for Auntie.
First Mistress: Then why are you going this way? You come and go from our house all the time—have you forgotten where the Old Lady lives?
Zhang Ji'an: (laughs) Listen to you, Sister-in-Law. How could I forget where the Old Lady lives! (looks around, leans closer to the First Mistress) I suddenly need to relieve myself. I want to use the latrine.
First Mistress: The Fourth Mistress and Fifth Mistress both live over there. Which of their courtyards did you plan to use as a latrine?!
  ...
First Mistress: Oh, right—let me hold the pastries for you.
[Zhang Ji'an freezes for a moment.]
First Mistress: I'm going to the Old Lady's anyway. You can't very well take pastries into the latrine, can you!
[Zhang Ji'an turns and exits. The First Mistress riffles through the parcel, finds nothing suspicious, and leaves.]
```

→ Technique: **every lie is dismantled on the spot with everyday common sense** (the wrong route, the location of the latrine, carrying pastries into the toilet), and finally "let me hold them for you" confiscates the evidence. In plot terms this scene is only "a reason for being late"; **on stage it is a complete attack-and-defense sequence**.

#### Example 4: *Ma Wen's War*—Seven Turns on the Function Word "Meaning" (Zhao, pp. 0064–0070): The Comic Weapon of Chinese Dialogue

```
Yang Xin: Ma Wen, tell me—is it interesting, living like this?
Ma Wen: Mm! Of course it's interesting!
  ...
Ma Wen: That would be so boring! I've thought about it too—there's no meaning in this, and it makes me look especially ungenerous! But live here long enough, and the interest arrives!! Very interesting! Incredibly interesting!
Yang Xin: Interesting, is it?! I'll make it even more interesting for you! Your ex-wife is definitely generous enough! (closes the door)
```

→ Technique: **seize on one function word and turn it over repeatedly** ("意思" yìsi—meaning/interest/generosity—seven times), flipping from "meaningless" to "generous" within a single exchange. The same excerpt also has **self-deprecating counterattack**: "Vulgar! Of course it's vulgar! **If it weren't vulgar, why would you have divorced me?!**" and **a threat about time**: "She used to be my wife, now she's your wife, but **who knows whose wife she'll be in the future—where will you live then?**"

#### Example 5: *Coming Home*—The "Organization" Three Questions (Zhao, p0059): The Most Economical "Failure to Communicate" on Stage

```
Director Li: Comrade Feng Wanyu, do you believe in the Organization?
Feng Wanyu: I do.
Director Li: Do I represent the Organization?
[Feng Wanyu nods.]
Director Li: Then shouldn't you believe me?
Feng Wanyu: I do.
Director Li: Good. (puts the official letter in his bag, stands, and pulls Lu Yanshi over) I now assure you in the name of the Organization that the man standing before you is your husband, Comrade Lu Yanshi!
Feng Wanyu: I know who he is. His surname is Fang—he is Master Fang!
```

→ Technique: **three escalating closed questions + one answer that negates everything**. Each of the three questions can only be answered "yes," backing the other person into a logical corner—and then she negates the entire premise from a completely different level. **The confrontation is not in logic; it is in perception.**

### 14.5 Delivery Format (reverse-engineered from the uniform convention of the 26 excerpts, Zhao pp. 0024–0197; format supplement on p0197)

Fixed order: **play title + "adapted from the film/TV drama of the same name by X"** → **a zoned set description** → **time / place / characters** (a one-line character sketch for each, optionally including "where the character is headed in the future") → **story summary** (3–6 lines; describing "what happened in the two minutes before the excerpt begins") → **plot** (action cues in `【】` on their own lines, `Character: (parenthetical) dialogue`) → `Reference video source: TV drama <X>, main series episode N` + `Note: … adapted from <author>'s novel <X>` or `… written by <name>`.

**No scene numbers (场号 chǎnghào), no INT./EXT. or DAY/NIGHT, no camera terminology or transitions.** Seven conventions (p0197): ① `【画外音】` (huàwàiyīn—voice-over) has two uses—offstage events (*Women of the Swordsman Clan*, the armed raid) and a character's interior monologue (*Eighteen Springs*); ② `【转场回忆】…【转场】` (zhuǎnchǎng huíyì … zhuǎnchǎng—transition to memory … transition) for stage flashback; ③ `【音乐起】/【音乐渐起】/【音乐渐弱】/【音乐渐强】` (music starts / music rises / music fades / music swells)—the book's only rhythm markers, fixed at three points: the opening, just before the emotional peak, and the ending; **nearly all 20 excerpts end on "【音乐起】/【音乐渐起】"**; ④ foreign-language dialogue = the original + a literal Chinese translation in parentheses; ⑤ breaking the fourth wall is permitted (`【从台下往台上逃跑】`—fleeing from the auditorium to the stage, `【大幕渐渐拉开】`—the main curtain slowly opens, `（对着台下一观众）`—to an audience member) to solve a shortage of supporting actors; ⑥ crowd characters are numbered (`女学生甲/乙/丙/丁`—Schoolgirl A/B/C/D, `丫鬟甲/乙`—Maid A/B); ⑦ for a multi-person brawl, write one overall staging note.

⚠️ Two conflicts with `sw-format-adaptation` are flagged at the end of Section 6 of [reference.md](reference.md) ("you must describe the story with shots" vs. "never mention the camera"; a character list with sketches is mandatory in stage excerpts / Chinese industry drafts and strictly forbidden in Anglo-American specs). A third: item 5 of the teaching notes for *Aftershock* (p0054) encourages "dialogue may be delivered in a Tangshan accent"—**but none of the 26 scripts in this book transliterates dialect**, so the two are in fact consistent: **dialect belongs to the performance layer and does not enter the script text.**

## 15. The Red-Line Classification Table from the *General Rules for Television Drama Content Production* (《电视剧内容制作通则》) (**31 December 2015 Edition**)

Source: Zhang Mingzhi (张明智) and Song Peiyi (宋培义), eds., *A Course for Television Drama Presenters and Producers* (《电视剧出品人与制片人教程》), Appendix 1 (pp. 0291–0294), transcribed in full. **Issuing bodies**: the Television Producers Committee of the China Federation of Radio and Television Associations (中广联合会电视制片委员会) and the China Television Drama Production Industry Association (中国电视剧制作产业协会). **Basis**: the State Administration of Press, Publication, Radio, Film and Television's *Provisions on the Administration of Television Content* (《电视内容管理规定》). **Nature**: an industry self-regulation code (not an administrative regulation), intended to "guide television drama production units in avoiding risk and producing soundly." **Edition year: 31 December 2015.**

⚠️ **Every item is the text of the 31 December 2015 edition.** The competent authorities have since issued several more documents (including regulations on "danmei adaptations" (耽改 dān gǎi), "idol cultivation" (偶像养成 ǒuxiàng yǎngchéng), and "fan circles" (饭圈 fànquān)), and items have been added and removed; **the ten-category framework and its phrasing remain in use, but any citation must state the year and be checked against the current documents**. The table below expands the ten categories of Article 5 into self-check items one by one—**the "Self-check question" column is not the original text; it rewrites the provision as a question to ask while reading a draft. Only the "Provision" column is the original text of the 31 December 2015 edition.**

Overview (Article 4, 31 December 2015 edition, p0291): **the eleven major prohibited areas—content that must not be produced** = (1) violating the basic principles established by the Constitution, or inciting resistance to or sabotage of the implementation of the Constitution, laws, administrative regulations, and rules; (2) endangering national unity, sovereignty, or territorial integrity; (3) divulging state secrets, endangering national security, or damaging national honor and interests; (4) inciting ethnic hatred or ethnic discrimination, violating ethnic customs and habits, wounding ethnic feelings, or undermining ethnic unity; (5) violating the state's religious policy, promoting religious extremism, cults, or superstition, or discriminating against or insulting religious belief; (6) disrupting social order or undermining social stability; (7) promoting obscenity, gambling, violence, terror, or drug use, or instigating crime or teaching criminal methods; (8) insulting or defaming others; (9) harming social morality or the nation's fine cultural traditions; (10) infringing the lawful rights and interests of minors or harming their physical and mental health; (11) other content prohibited by laws, administrative regulations, and rules.

Is their professional identity used as a negative punchline? (Note: this kind of content also triggers the expert review under Article 6; see 15.11.) |
| 1-3 | **Sensationalizing or exaggerating social problems; excessively depicting or displaying the dark side of society** | Is the series' worldview "there are problems, but they are being solved" or "rotten to the core"? Is there a "has a car and an apartment, and both parents are dead" kind of floating premise? (Compare the same criticism of idol dramas in Section 13.1, Zhang Wei p0089.) |

### 15.11 Accompanying Articles: Articles 6, 7, and 8 (31 December 2015 edition)

- **Article 6: Three categories of detail that must be vetted by experts (31 December 2015 edition, p0294, verbatim)**: "Treat the details of production with seriousness and earnestness. Where the following content is involved, **relevant experts must be engaged to vet it**: (1) content involving **the spoken and written languages of ethnic minorities, the images of revolutionary pioneers and other important figures, the use of specific symbols and marks, and graphics and charts**; (2) plot content depicting **the People's Army, the Armed Police, state security, public security, judicial personnel, doctors, lawyers, and other specific professions and groups, as well as social organizations and associations**; (3) content involving **the costumes, sets, and props of specific historical periods and professional groups**."
  → ★ **If the screenwriter writes any of the above, the script enters an "expert adviser review" stage**, and revision time must be left in the schedule; the cost appears in the budget as "script discussion and consultation fees" (Zhang Mingzhi pp. 0145–0146). Content involving law must also pass the production's legal adviser (Zhang Mingzhi p0280).
- **Article 7: Language standards (31 December 2015 edition, p0294, verbatim)**: "Television drama production institutions shall inspect the linguistic quality of television dramas in accordance with **national language and script standards**, to ensure that the characters and expressions used are correct and standard. Subtitles for television dramas **shall be in standard Chinese characters, except for titles and related text written in calligraphy**. For songs with lyrics, foreign-language titles, dialogue, terms with specific meanings, and signs, **Chinese subtitles shall be added**."
- **Article 8: Penalties (31 December 2015 edition, p0294)**: where a violation of the General Rules produces an adverse impact, the association, depending on the circumstances, "imposes the sanctions of criticism, warning, or notification to the entire industry and the public; in egregious cases, **membership shall be revoked, and the matter reported to the competent authorities for investigation and handling in accordance with laws and regulations**."

### 15.12 Article 3: The Eight Items Advocated (31 December 2015 edition, p0291)—The Positive Sell-Point (卖点 màidiǎn) List Beyond the Negative List

Uphold the "two serves" direction (二为 èrwéi—serve the people and serve socialism) and the "double hundred" policy (双百 shuāngbǎi—"let a hundred flowers bloom, let a hundred schools of thought contend"), and produce excellent works in which "**ideological content, artistic quality, and entertainment value are organically unified**": (1) "Adhere to a people-centered creative orientation, make the people the subject of representation, and reflect the people's aspirations"; (2) "Adhere to the guidance of core socialist values… guide the people to establish and uphold **a correct view of history, nation, state, and culture**"; (3) ★**"Adhere to a primary focus on contemporary subject matter, stay close to reality, to life, and to the masses, and record the development of the times and social progress."**; (4) "Vigorously promote China's excellent traditional culture. Use history as a mirror and pass on civilization"; (5) "Promote the true, the good, and the beautiful, and spread positive energy… guide people to strengthen their moral judgment and sense of moral honor"; (6) "Strive to tell China's story well, carry forward the Chinese spirit, and gather Chinese strength"; (7) "Insist on putting social benefit first… unify social benefit and economic benefit"; (8) "Firmly establish a sense of excellence… strive to produce excellent works with **profound thought, superb artistry, and refined production**."

→ Among these, **item (3), "adhere to a primary focus on contemporary subject matter," most directly affects subject selection**, and corroborates the 2012 genre-share data (major 1% / historical 43% / contemporary 56%, Zhang Mingzhi p0188, **on a 2014–2016 basis**).

### 15.13 How to Use It: Treat This Table as a Script-Reading Checklist

1. **Before 立项 (lìxiàng—the administrative project-approval stage)**, first run through 15.1 (1-5, 1-8), 15.4, 15.6 (6-2, 6-3), and 15.8 (8-2)—these five items determine **whether the subject can be made at all**; if they cannot be changed, change the subject.
2. **At the outline stage**, run through 15.5 (5-5) and 15.8 (8-3)—these two determine **the thematic yardstick and the core of the source work**, corresponding to "the three elements of the environment for thematic expression" in Section 13.5 (Zhang Wei pp. 0232–0233).
3. **At the episode-breakdown stage**, run through 15.5 (5-1/5-2/5-3/5-8), 15.6 (6-1/6-4/6-5), and 15.9—these are **scene-level** problems, most of which can be fixed with the avoidance techniques in Section 7.5 of [reference.md](reference.md), or transformed with Zhao Binbin's four-step "offstage + visible consequences" method (see Section 14, #2).
4. **Before the final draft is locked**, run through the three categories in Article 6 under 15.11—list every item in this draft that triggers expert review and hand the list, together with the list of symbols, call signs, and forms of address under 15.10 (10-3), to the producer.
5. **State the year every time you cite it**: all provisions in this table are from the **31 December 2015 edition**; current documents must be checked separately.
