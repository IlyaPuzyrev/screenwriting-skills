## I. The Nature of Television Drama and Typology (Yao Kougen, 姚扣根)

### 1.1 Threefold Nature (Yao, ch. 1)

**Dramatic art + screen art + the television medium**, "mutually dependent, mutually acting upon one another, mutually influencing, and inseparable" (p0050). The five elements of dramaticity = **action / conflict / situation / sudden reversal / suspense** (p0037). Three points to remember:

1. A TV drama "**can contain several conflicts in a single episode**; the intensification and resolution of contradiction proceed comparatively slowly, like life itself" (p0037).
2. TV drama "**identifies with unified, sympathetic, and active characters**" (p0038) — precisely the opposite of "the antihero is the mark of a golden age."
3. **Fragment structure** (片断结构, piànduàn jiégòu): "TV drama attends to the fragment and to the small climax within a single episode, whereas stage drama and film attend more to the climax of the whole work" (p0045).

(For the complete list — six differences from stage drama, ten from film, and nine differences of medium — see reference.)

### 1.2 Typology (Yao, ch. 14)

Yao states explicitly that "measuring television drama, this mass medium, by the classification standards of stage drama and film is no longer appropriate"; for the six classification systems, see reference. He himself adopts the following:

- **Single-play drama (单本剧, dānběnjù)**: 45 minutes per episode; it may run as two parts (upper and lower) or three parts (upper, middle, lower). Seven-part mnemonic: "**Select material by panning gold from sand; conceive the theme so that one leaf announces autumn; build the structure with a craft that outdoes nature; make the plot win by surprise; give the characters the finishing touch that brings them alive; use language as sparingly as gold; leave an inexhaustible aftertaste after viewing**." Current situation: "Buffeted by long-form TV drama, its creative momentum is not what it once was."
- **Episodic series (系列剧, xìlièjù)**: continuity between episodes is weak; episodes may be broadcast separately or in sequence (the first foreign series imported in 1979, 《大西洋底来的人》 (*The Man from Atlantis*), was an episodic series). **Four forms**: (1) theme/framework type (《聊斋》 (*Strange Tales from a Chinese Studio*), 《十大女杰》 (*Ten Outstanding Women*)); (2) fixed characters + standalone episodes = procedural (《神探亨特》 (*Hunter*); 《宰相刘罗锅》 (*Prime Minister Liu Luoguo*), "three men contending in wits," and 《铁嘴铜牙纪晓岚》 (*Iron Mouth, Bronze Teeth: Ji Xiaolan*), "three men contending in talk"); (3) character line + event line in two layers = hybrid (《急诊室故事》 (*Stories from the Emergency Room*)); (4) supporting-character stories generated by the series (the same event repeated N times + a reversal on the N+1st time).
- **Medium-length serial (中篇连续剧, zhōngpiān liánxùjù)**: **at least 3 and at most 8 episodes**. "It suits both one episode a day and concentrated broadcast in a single day"; "Japanese dramas usually stay at around 11 episodes for the same reason."
- **Long-form serial (长篇连续剧, chángpiān liánxùjù)**: **8 or more episodes; at present generally around 20** — "**this is determined by the close relationship between TV drama sales and advertising effects**" (p0320). Three structural features: characters as the warp and events as the weft, one main line pulling countless branches, "each episode resolves a problem on one branch and moves forward one step… and so it can continue endlessly"; each episode has a relatively concentrated set of characters and events and a relative climax; "each episode is interlocked with the next, with suspense ensuring the connection between episodes." Warning: high investment and high risk mean that "**investors in planning mostly let the dramatic structure occupy a dominant position**."
- **Documentary drama (纪实剧, jìshíjù)**: its origin is the BBC's formation of a drama-documentary unit in **1946**. Three subcategories = reportage drama / biographical drama / police drama. The criterion: "A documentary… does not permit the arrangement and reenactment of environments and events. A documentary drama, through actors… permits environments and events to be arranged and reenacted according to the director's design"; in biographical drama "**major events may not be fictionalized; fiction lies mainly in details and minor events**"; the difference between a police drama and a crime film is that it unfolds the case "in the manner of a 'news investigation,' very trivial, very slow… **rather like reading out a running account**."
- **Sitcom (情景喜剧, qíngjǐng xǐjù)**: Larry Mintz's definition — half an hour, characters unchanging, resolved within one episode, live audience or canned laughter; "**its most important feature is its cyclical narrative… in the end the conflict is resolved, the pressure disappears, and everything returns to the state it was in at the beginning**." For the six requirements, see reference. **Rhythm data: it must make the audience laugh from the opening, and thereafter "there must be a bright spot within every 3 minutes"** (p0325); the problem of the week must be resolved in that episode. For the general method of half-hour comedy, see `sw-sitcom-comedy`.
- **Interior drama (室内剧, shìnèijù)**: classified by shooting location; it may be a single play, episodic series, or serial. Three features: concentrated locations (one or two interior sets + built sets + multiple cameras + direct sound + live switching) / complex character relationships ("almost every character can play opposite every other character") / **excelling in dialogue — "a dialogue-dominated script requires about 18,000 characters per episode, while an action-heavy script runs between 12,000 and 15,000 characters"** (p0327).

## II. The Six Planning Documents: Length and Required Elements (Yao Kougen, pp. 0328–0348)

| Document | Length and required elements |
|---|---|
| **Plot summary** (logline; Yao gives three Chinese renderings: "a concise summary of the plot," "high concept," and "taximeter cord" (计程绳, jìchéngshéng)) | A single sentence expressing **the plot premise, the subject genre, and the thematic value**; three functions = summarize the story / position the genre / "**show that it possesses extraordinary plot power and is attractive without the participation of stars**." Series often need a longer version: the five actual samples Yao gives run **200–400 characters** (samples in reference). **Executable format: 3–5 paragraphs, each anchored to one time marker (a year) or one change in a relationship, and each paragraph must end with an irreversible change of identity or relationship.** |
| **Character design** | "Mainly sort out the protagonist's personality traits and the protagonist's relationships with other characters; **there should not be too many, lest confusion make them difficult to remember**." Template (reverse-engineered from an analysis of 《潜伏》 (*Lurk*)): for an existing genre, list **three points of innovation — the character's values / the relationship pattern between the male and female leads / the pattern of conflict between the protagonist and those around him**, each with one sentence contrasting it with genre convention |
| **Main locations** | List the main locations of the whole series (directly tied to "lock down the location names and concentrate the locations" in Section VIII of SKILL.md) |
| **Creative statement** | "Emphasize your own points of innovation and articulate valuable thoughts that might otherwise be overlooked"; "**Creativity is by no means just a flash of inspiration… its innovation comes from depth**." Two paths: innovation in archetype / motif / genre (there are 345 Cinderella variants worldwide, according to Cox's count); absorbing the latest practice and research (《犯罪心理》 (*Criminal Minds*) uses borderline personality disorder; 《生活大爆炸》 (*The Big Bang Theory*) uses string theory / Schrödinger's cat) |
| **Plot flow** | "**What happens, when, and where; which subsequent events these events trigger; how they are connected; what the specific motives of the characters' actions are; how the action unfolds; and what the result is**." Working method: "You can use a **story tree or flowchart** to decompose and condense the entire plot; **once the synopsis is confirmed, when you sit down to write the first page you are no longer creating — you are typing**." |
| **Sample script** | "Film and television companies need to review the script of the initial **first episode or first two episodes**. **This initial episode is like the pilot episode practiced abroad**." Responsibilities = establish the situation, create a rich world, build character relationships, introduce the inciting incident, give the action a clear direction, maintain strong suspense, comply with format / scene / dialogue conventions, display cultural literacy and authentic detail, and display your voice |

## III. Chinese Script Format: Paragraphing, Four Formats, Revision Numbering, and the Scene Breakdown Sheet

### 3.1 Paragraphing and Scene Headings (Yao, pp. 0063–0064)

- In TV drama writing, "**the most basic concept is to divide into natural paragraphs by scene (场景) and scene unit (场面)**." A **scene (场景, chǎngjǐng)** = the location where an action or event takes place ("within one scene characters may enter and exit and time may change, but the scene remains this one scene"); a **scene unit (场面, chǎngmiàn)** = the basic unit of the same time, the same place, and the same characters ("as soon as someone exits, we move to the next scene unit"). **If you use cinematic free time and space, paragraph by scene; if you unfold the action in one location like an interior drama, you must paragraph by scene unit.**
- A scene heading must specify: **location; interior or exterior; time; day or night**; "some also mark the scene number and the characters appearing in that scene."

### 3.2 Four Coexisting Formats (Yao, pp. 0064–0081, citing Zhu Baishui (朱白水), *How to Write Television Drama* (《怎样编写电视剧》), TTV Culture, 1984)

Yao states plainly: "For the format of the 'body' within a scene, **there is no unified standard**; at present the following four are common." — This is the source of the claim that Chinese scripts have "no single industry standard"; **consistency within one draft is sufficient**.

| # | Format | Method | Sample in the book | When to use |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Image/sound split** | Image and sound are written horizontally in left and right columns, "so that when the director, actors, and crew hold the script in their hands, everything is clear at a glance" | 《人间四月天》 (*April Rhapsody*), Episode 1 (screenplay by Wang Huiling (王惠玲), 2000) | Series in which sound design carries the narrative |
| 2 | **Stage-play script format** | "Basically composed of character dialogue, narration, and a small number of cue words; **there is very little careful depiction of character portraits, inner psychology, or environmental atmosphere**"; stage directions are enclosed in square brackets | 《阿信的故事》 (*Oshin*), Episode 1 (Sugako Hashida (桥田寿贺子), 1985) | **"Particularly suitable for dialogue-dominated interior drama"** |
| 3 | **Film script format** | "**Arranged with one shot or a group of shots as the sentence unit**… describing image and sound in the most concise language" | 《十六岁的花季》 (*Flowers of Sixteen*), Episode 1 | Visually oriented, action-heavy, fast-paced series |
| 4 | **TV novel format** | "Attending on one hand to the depiction of television screen images, while on the other adopting a narration close to that of a novel… **this format is currently used fairly widely; it not only provides a script for shooting but also lays the foundation for publishing a TV novel at the same time**" | 《新岸》 (*New Shore*) | The default option; projects in which a novel of the same title is published simultaneously |

For an abridged sample and a comparison of the literary script of the "visiting the patient" scene in 《新岸》 (*New Shore*) with its 10-shot storyboard breakdown, see reference.

### 3.3 Numbering Rules During Revision (Zhang Mingzhi, 张明智, p0121, quoted verbatim)

> Once a scene has been assigned a number, **that number must always represent that scene**. A scene added later therefore receives a mixed number-and-letter designation. For example, if two scenes are inserted between Scene 19 and Scene 20, they are designated **19A and 19B** respectively. If Scene 66 is deleted, a note is placed between Scene 65 and Scene 67 in the script reading "**66 deleted**." The standard adopted is to maintain consistent page numbers in the same way, **adding A or B between the added pages rather than repaginating or reprinting the script**.

The premise: "**Once a script goes into production, revision continues indefinitely.**"

### 3.4 The Nine Fields of the Scene Breakdown Sheet (Zhang Mingzhi, pp. 0112–0113)

The **scene breakdown sheet (分场景表, fēn chǎngjǐng biǎo)** is prepared by the **script supervisor (场记, chǎngjì)** after the final draft; it is the basis for scheduling the shooting plan. Nine fields: **episode-scene number / page count ("indicating the weight of the scene") / atmosphere (interior or exterior + morning · day · noon · dusk · evening · night) / location name / content / principal cast (including "characters whose names do not appear in the script but who must be present according to plot logic") / extras (type and number) / special notes for costumes, makeup, and props / remarks**. Sort keys = location name, atmosphere, episode-scene number, or actor; the purpose is "to group together scenes that share the same shooting conditions… and **shoot the whole series in blocks (套拍, tàopāi)**." Three cautions: omit no scene, "**even if it is a single line or a transition with no actors**"; omit no character or prop that the script does not mention but that must be present; location names "must use consistent designations from beginning to end."
→ **The takeaway for the screenwriter: whether location names are consistent, how many scenes one location carries, how many pages each scene occupies, the proportion of night scenes, and whether extras are needed — these five items directly determine the budget and the schedule.**

## IV. Per-Episode Quantitative Metrics, the Three Duties of a Scene Unit, and the Dual-Strand Formula

### 4.1 Per-Episode Quantitative Metrics

| Metric | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Number of scenes / scene units | **20–40 scenes** per episode, generally **25–35 scene units**; "if there are four or five major scenes that can attract an audience, the episode is basically viable" | Yao, p0227 |
| Plot points | **About 13 plot points per episode** (the requirement when screenwriter Liu Heng (刘恒) wrote 《贫嘴张大民的幸福生活》 (*The Happy Life of Chatterbox Zhang Damin*)) | Zhang Wei, p0060 |
| Main event | **At least one main event per episode**, supported by minor events and details; arrange slow-paced passages and high-capacity passages **in a staggered pattern** | Zhang Wei, pp. 0035, 0060 |
| Climax spacing | **A climax of emotion or event every 3 to 5 episodes; sometimes the two coincide** (the per-episode plot allocation pattern of 《杜拉拉升职记》 (*Go Lala Go!*)) | Zhang Wei, pp. 0113–0114 |
| Number of sudden reversals | **At least two sudden reversals** in the whole series (industry slang: "a flip," 翻番, fānfān) | Zhang Wei, p0062 |
| Character count | Dialogue-dominated: about **18,000 characters per episode**; action-heavy: **12,000–15,000 characters per episode**; a 30-episode series ≥ **400,000 characters**, dialogue ≥ **50%** | Yao, p0327; Zhang Wei, pp. 0076–0077 |

**The three duties of a scene unit** (Yao, p0252): connect what precedes and what follows / advance the plot / delay suspense — "**delay suspense, do not end it; advance the plot, do not display it**"; a scene unit that serves none of these three functions, "no matter how good or brilliant it is, is like the pair of hands on a statue of Balzac — it destroys the beauty of the whole and must be cut."

### 4.2 Career Line + Private-Life Line (Yao, p0285, dual-strand formula, quoted verbatim)

> Usually one strand is **a romance of love and family or an individual's private life**, while the other involves aspects such as **work, war, mission, or adventure**. **Each strand has a goal, an obstacle, and a climax. In most cases the two strands are mutually causal: touching one triggers the other, and unless one is resolved the other cannot be resolved, forming a double causal structure.**

Examples: 《北京人在纽约》 (*A Native of Beijing in New York*) (emotional entanglement + survival in a foreign country), 《上海一家人》 (*A Family in Shanghai*) (striving in the old Shanghai business world + romantic entanglement with three childhood friends — "it writes both the social dimension and the personal-life dimension… making the characters round and full"). In the fourth segment of 《亮剑》 (*Drawing Sword*) (episodes 26–30), this becomes an explicit main and secondary pair of strands: the main strand = integration into the military academy; the secondary strand = an extramarital affair; each line has a complete 起承转合 (qǐ chéng zhuǎn hé — the four-part pattern of setup, development, turn, and resolution), and together they point toward "**return**" and "**finding one's way back after going astray**" (Zhang Wei, pp. 0303–0306).

## V. Adapting into a Long-Form Series: Five-Source Typology, Sell-Point Migration, Theme, and Expansion

### 5.1 Five-Source Typology (Zhang Wei)

**Novel / film / life story (biography) / stage play / localization of a foreign series**:

| Source | Structural treatment | Character treatment |
|---|---|---|
| **Novel** | **Follow the original novel's framework as far as possible** (the narrative mode of the Chinese classical chapter novel "is extremely close to that of television drama"); remove large passages of psychological description; add plot threads; for novels with interwoven time frames, "in a television drama it is often more appropriate to make the interweaving of different times and spaces more frequent" | Three approaches: direct adherence ("taking over the whole pot" / "transplanting the characters," 《亮剑》 (*Drawing Sword*)) / breaking with the original characters and giving them a makeover (Huang Yiyi (黄依依) in 《暗算》 (*Plot Against*)) / adding or subtracting some elements and threads (eclectic minor adjustment, the most common) |
| **Film** | **Break the film's original structure** (a play within a play, nested layers, pure reverse chronology, and the episodic form "are mostly unsuitable for television drama"); "**it is extremely rare for a TV adaptation to value the original film's narrative structure**." Counterexample: the parallel two-line montage of 《一江春水向东流》 (*The Spring River Flows East*) "is very suitable for television use," and the TV version retained it | Extend and expand the characters' fates (and "**add events, applying resistance to their dramatic motivation and power to act**") / create new characters to build a multi-sided web of forces / refine and adjust the characters' own traits |
| **Biography** | Generally use **chronological order**; the biggest problem is the selection of material. One may borrow the "novelistic structure": a framework made of several major sections, each a stage of fate transformation and character growth; "a novelistic structure generally has more than one core dramatic conflict" | Two approaches: a panoramic presentation of a whole life (chronicle form, 《李小龙传奇》 (*The Legend of Bruce Lee*)) / highlighting one phase or one strongly plotted event (《梅艳芳菲》 (*The Anita Mui Story*) takes the end of her life) — the latter "needs, through the one event selected for this section, to deliver a final verdict on his whole life" |
| **Stage play** | In China there are only two major resources: **Lao She (老舍) and Cao Yu (曹禺)**; Xia Yan's (夏衍) criterion for adaptability: "**works with a relatively strong story (or dramatic quality), fairly distinct character personalities, and not too complicated a set of threads are relatively easy to adapt**" | Turn the stage characters' dramatic space into television space (invent living space outside the main set) / extend the fate lines and invent new supporting characters |
| **Foreign series** | **The institutional reasons the structure must change** (pp. 0042–0043): Japanese dramas run 11 episodes; American series air one episode a week and can continue indefinitely ("the direction of the story is adjusted according to its ratings" → open structure), whereas "Chinese TV dramas are basically all shot and completed in full… and broadcast in one complete run" | Replace the setting, redesign the characters, adjust the plot. Success: 《家有儿女》 (*Home with Kids*) (psychologist → children's playwright, career woman → housewife, multiple children → **a remarried blended family**). Failure: 《好想好想谈恋爱》 (*How I Long to Fall in Love*) had three causes of death: feminism "obviously has no cultural foundation in China" / "the frequency of character entrances and dialogue is very high," violating Chinese viewers' viewing habits / a loose attitude toward love and marriage "is somewhat disconnected from real life in China" |

### 5.2 Sell-Point Migration (Zhang Wei's most original concept, p0028, pp. 0082–0085)

- **Sell point (卖点, màidiǎn) = "a meaning that can circulate among the broadest possible audience"** — "a sell point is not a meaning that interests a minority, but a meaning with the widest potential circulation."
- Mechanism: the original work's audience ≠ the series' audience. The readers of 《杜拉拉升职记》 (*Go Lala Go!*) are "accurately positioned among the highly educated 20–35 age group," "while the age range of China's TV drama viewers happens to sit above that of the novel's readers"; the structure the novel builds from workplace rules "cannot be adopted by a television adaptation"; the novel's "TIPS"-style workplace maxims "can under no circumstances be copied directly into a television drama."
- **Executable actions**: list the differences in age / education / viewing mode between the original audience and the target series audience; **replace every sell point that works only for the original audience (workplace TIPS, philosophical speculation, argumentative passages, a semi-classical style) with sell points that work for the broadest audience (emotional lines, family lines), but keep the original work's keywords as window dressing** ("workplace," "white-collar," "foreign company," and "metropolis" are all retained because "they do not significantly subvert the original novel's theme or style").
- The same judgment from the platform side (Zhang Mingzhi, p0056): "In the domestic publishing industry, annual sales of more than 30,000 copies are enough to count as a bestseller… **but a viewership of one million is simply negligible in television, so do not be easily dazzled by the word 'bestseller.'**"

### 5.3 The Thematic Coordinate System and the Environment for Expressing Theme

- **Theme = the coordinate system of adaptation** (p0023): "In what form the old text will be placed within this coordinate system is the main problem the theme must solve. **Theme is a guiding principle of adaptation**… establishing the theme equals establishing the scope, the genre, and even the character relationships." On dimensions: "The coordinate system of verbal expression is a two-dimensional plane formed by a horizontal and a vertical axis, whereas the image introduces a third axis — time — to form a three-dimensional coordinate system."
- **What theme cannot be adapted** (p0025): "The choice of theme must possess a quality **common to three different texts**. The special thematic attribute of one particular text cannot serve as the expressive theme." (The Bible, 《百年孤独》 (*One Hundred Years of Solitude*))
- **Three elements of the environment for expressing theme** (pp. 0232–0233, the most reusable theoretical tool in the book): (1) **the enlightener (启悟者, qǐwùzhě)** — "**usually must not be set as the protagonist**… the protagonist often cannot accept or understand at once and must go through a series of events; only after growing does the protagonist realize / identify with / transcend / negate the values the enlightener told them back then"; (2) **the reward-and-punishment mechanism** — "once behavior that does not conform to or violates the thematic idea occurs, it will certainly receive corresponding punishment, while conforming behavior will certainly bring positive results"; (3) **a protagonist who does not wander off theme** — "the protagonist's motivation itself must be linked to the thematic values."
- **Three operational judgments about theme** (pp. 0026–0029): conformity with mainstream values ("karmic retribution," "good and evil both meet their end") / anticipating "hot topics" in order to address viewers' real-life anxieties (the long production cycle of film and television "amounts to a loss of timeliness. **This gives adaptation a very sufficient reason: the social response to the original work can provide a basis for predicting the response to the series**") / accurately grasping long-selling genres and combining multiple genre elements under one theme (according to CSM12 city data from May 2009 to May 2011, **spy drama, war drama, character legend, and family ethics are China's long-selling subject categories**; 《潜伏》 (*Lurk*) "borrows the shell of spy drama but actually tells the **office struggle** that modern people care about most," and 《借枪》 (*Borrowed Gun*) is "a **family-ethics drama** wrapped in the cloak of spy drama").

### 5.4 The Character Trinity and the Section-by-Section Method

- **Trinity** (p0050): **reference text (the original work) — adaptation (the script) — adapted product (the series)**, "like three large circles interlocking, with overlapping parts and also room for each to stand alone; **these mutually unrelated spaces are precisely the character information the adapter adds and supplements**." Throughout the book Zhang uses "reference text" rather than "original work" in order to avoid the presumption of "fidelity."
- **"Faithfulness to the original" is judged an outdated issue** (pp. 0051–0052): "**There is no character absolutely unsuitable for adaptation; there is only the feasibility and reasonableness of adaptation, the difficulty of actual operation, and the overall effect.**"
- **A counterintuitive judgment about length** (p0043): "**Even if a novel has sufficient word count, it may not have sufficiently effective brushwork in the depiction and narration of characters; a short story or a film only two hours long may not lack room for expansion — often, on the contrary, it gives the adapter more imaginative space and effective material for character construction.**" (《马文的战争》 (*Ma Wen's War*): a 30,000-character novella with 6 characters → 24 episodes)
- **The section-by-section copy / delete / adapt method** (Table 4, pp. 0191–0192): divide the original into sections by timecode or chapter, tag each section 【Copy】 / 【Delete】 / 【Adapt】 to make a comparison table, and use the table to decide the expansion points and deletion points. For an excerpt from the complete table for 《神话》 (*The Myth*), see reference.

### 5.5 Four Methods and Three Steps of Expansion (pp. 0039–0040, pp. 0213–0216)

"The length of a TV drama is generally **more than 10 times** that of a film" (《神话》 (*The Myth*) is 20 times). Four methods: (1) **increase the weight of B stories** ("if you stay confined to the main line, it is instead easy to make viewers feel monotonous and dull"); (2) **expand and complete the characters' fate lines**; (3) **increase the number of protagonists** ("a film often has only one or two main characters, whereas a TV drama very commonly has **three or four**," including consideration of the star cast); (4) **develop secondary characters** ("a film can tell the story of just one pair of male and female protagonists; a TV drama absolutely cannot").

The accompanying three steps: **increase the number of characters → complicate the character relationships → extend the development time of the characters' fates**. Formula (p0216): "TV drama running time / film running time = average character duration in TV drama / average character duration in film." **Taboo: do not build a purely friendly line between newly added characters** (it generates no conflict and shifts the center of gravity); either add a love line or turn it into conflict.

Three questions on the legitimacy of a new character (pp. 0091–0092): Does he have causal connections with two or more existing characters? Is his function continuous or a one-off action? If he is deleted, which line breaks? Three criteria for deleting a character (p0097): he does not advance the protagonist's fate; the audience must pay the cost of getting to know him but he does not run through the whole series; his contrasting function has already been absorbed by changes to the protagonist's character. **Fix loose relationships onto social attributes** (p0098, pp. 0139–0141): ex-girlfriend → ex-wife, friend → primary-school classmate, colleague → older sister and younger brother — "this can make mad behavior and sudden intimacy reasonable at the same time, and automatically increases resistance."

### 5.6 Three Disciplines for Dual-Time-Frame Series (p0034, pp. 0197–0198)

(1) Each single strand must connect completely from beginning to end without gaps; (2) a single strand should be neither too long nor intercut too frequently; (3) **switching between threads must be sewn together by "the same object / the same place / the same scene"**; arbitrary hard cuts are forbidden (the film 《神话》 (*The Myth*) uses "the same object (water splash) transition," while the TV version uses "the same object (treasure box) transition"). The iron rule: "The two time frames must be mutually related, must echo each other on the inner level of the story, and must be an organic whole."

### 5.7 IP Evaluation

- **Producer Zhao Junkai's (赵浚凯) three foundations** (《亮剑》 (*Drawing Sword*), Zhang Wei, pp. 0274–0275): "**First, theme — a pure soldier's banner; second, distinctive characters; third, a wonderful story.**"
- **Zhang Mingzhi's two judgments** (pp. 0056–0057): **judge the market space** (What are the demographic characteristics of the audience? Is there still a possibility of making the audience market larger?) + **judge the degree of visualizability** ("some works that reach the peak of literary and artistic achievement… may have a dispersed plot and lack clear causal logic… and characters who lack the power to act"). Conclusion: "Literature is first of all readable… **television drama, however, must be visual, and it must be the spiritual food of the masses, a popular and easily understood cultural product**."

## VI. Excerpt Adaptation: Four Principles, Four Techniques, and the Delivery Format

The direction of Zhao Binbin's book is **completely opposite** to the long-form adaptation approach in Section VI of SKILL.md: finished film → **8–20-minute stage excerpts for 3–8 actors** (rehearsal material for acting programs at vocational colleges). The two share the bottom line that "the core fact cannot be changed," but one expands and the other compresses; **they must not be conflated into a single procedure**. For stage-adaptation details and layout conventions, see `sw-format-adaptation`.

**Four principles** (Chapter 3, pp. 0018–0023):

1. **Rationalize the stage setting (merge into a main set)** — merge multiple locations into "**one main set + one connected transition area**" (home + doorway, room + corridor, bedroom + dark room), and place a character's first entrance directly in the transition area. Two hard rules: **important dramatic material must take place in the main set**; **a scene may be deleted only when it has no necessary connection to the core fact**, otherwise "there will be… a situation in which the content of the adapted excerpt does not match the given circumstances."
2. **Material selection (the veto gate)** — if a candidate scene consists of "two people sitting and talking + a single enclosed small space (a café / a living-room sofa) + no physical task," **veto it outright** ("**stage performance is precisely centered on action**… even if the male and female protagonists want to take action, the given circumstances prevent them from acting"). A qualified scene must have at least two of the following: **distinct character traits / strong conflict and event (confrontation) / complex character entanglement**.
3. **Event and conflict** — the excerpt must unfold around **one core event**; **the cut-in point is set at "the moment the protagonist's motivation has just come into being,"** and all prior backstory is compressed into the "story synopsis" and character biographies. Structural template (《幸福在哪里》 (*Where Is Happiness?*)): one main fact (setup section) + one "interspersed small matter" to prevent flatness + a door-kicking external conflict (climax section) + an action-driven ending. The **"double premise" test** for psychological motivation: before writing a character's decision to do something, list the two conditions that must both hold (the goal + trust in the other person); if either is missing, the action does not stand.
4. **Completeness** — "Although it is an excerpt, it still needs **a beginning and an end**. Normally the excerpt begins by establishing character relationships, sets up the plot for the event that follows, unfolds conflict, sustains confrontation and conflict, and continues to the end." The common defect is "no ending or a weak sense of ending," because "**no ending was written** in the adapted film or television work" — **the ending usually has to be written by the adapter; one cannot copy the film's or TV series' "to be continued."**

**Four specific techniques**:

- **Turn process scenes into entrances with results**: a process scene in film or television where "a character leaves to do something" is rewritten as "another character brings the result on stage" (Xiang Yuan (向远) specially makes braised pancakes for Mai Qiu (麦秋) → changed to **Aunt Wang (王大妈)** bringing the braised pancakes), for the reason that it "makes the script structure more compact and at the same time makes it easier to perform on stage."
- **Four steps for moving unperformable scenes offstage**: enter the inner stage → voice-over lines → throw a prop from offstage → a character enters with visible consequences.
- **Linking character**: when someone is needed to run errands, deliver messages, or call people over, simply add one, and merge several supporting characters with the same function from the film or TV version into him (the original character-list note for Secretary Yang (杨秘书) in 《归来》 (*Coming Home*): "This character was added as a linking character for entrances and exits, and at the same time several characters were merged into one"); a supporting character's gender may be changed according to rehearsal needs, retaining only the dramatic function (Seventh Uncle → Seventh Aunt).
- **Only two moves for changing the "act" within a single set**: a single line from a powerful character **clears the stage**; or **a knock / interruption** cuts off a situation that is about to become irreversible. Do not use a blackout to change the scene.

**Delivery format** (reverse-engineered from the uniform conventions of the 26 excerpts; see reference for details): title + "adapted from the film/television series of the same name by X" → a paragraph of zone-based set description → time / place / characters (one sentence of biography for each) → story synopsis (3–6 lines) → plot (action cues in `【】` on their own lines; `Character: (parenthetical) dialogue`) → `Reference video source` + `Note: original work / screenwriter`. **Do not write scene numbers, do not write interior/exterior or day/night, and do not write any camera terminology or transitions.**

⚠️ One conflict must be flagged: Zhao's book argues that "the montage structure of a film script requires the screenwriter **to describe the story in shots**" (p0014), whereas the hard rule of `sw-format-adaptation` is "never mention the camera." A coexisting explanation is that Zhao means "conceive with shot thinking," but the original wording is indeed as stated — this is the idiom of Chinese college acting textbooks, **do not treat it as a formatting rule**. Likewise, a character list with biographies is mandatory in excerpts and domestic Chinese industry drafts, but explicitly forbidden in Anglo-American spec scripts.

## VII. The Production Chain and Censorship: Administrative Systems, Delivery Milestones, Red Lines, Contracts, and Copyright

### 7.1 Eight Administrative Systems (Zhang Mingzhi, p0278, as of 2016, quoted verbatim)

> Market-access system, licensing system, subject-planning project-approval system, shooting-and-production filing-and-public-notice system, completed-film review system, distribution-licensing system, broadcast-management system, advertising-management system

→ The entire administrative chain a screenwriter needs to understand: **can it be made (market access/license) → can it be established (subject-planning project approval) → can it be shot (filing and public notice) → can it pass (completed-film review) → can it be sold (distribution license) → can it be broadcast (broadcast management) → can product placement be inserted (advertising management)**.

- **Project approval (立项, lìxiàng) may come before or after the script** (p0253): the process is "investment funds basically in place → complete the administrative approval or filing for subject planning → confirm the script (or a suitable screenwriter) → determine the key creative personnel → form the production unit → actual shooting and production → post-production → promotion → distribution," but the book notes that "several procedures in the preparation stage are not immutable… **for example, the script may come first and then the subject-planning filing, or the reverse**."
- **The precondition for a production unit's legal establishment** (《全国影视摄制组安全生产管理条例（试行）》 (*National Regulations on Work Safety Management of Film and Television Production Units (Trial)*), Television Producers Committee + China Film Producers Association, **2012-11-05**, p0295, quoted verbatim): "The basis for establishing a production unit is that the film or television script **has already passed government department filing and public notice (备案公示, bèi'àn gōngshì) and project approval, with sufficient funding guarantees and adequate market forecasting**." → A hard indicator for a screenwriter to judge whether a project is genuine.
- **Two reviews** (p0049): **content review** (also called artistic review) examines "the thematic idea, the internal logical relations of the plot, whether it violates conventional worldviews, value orientations, or historical facts, and whether it exceeds the limits regarding pornography, violence, and sensitive historical events"; if noncompliant, revise "**until the content review passes**"; **technical review** examines image stability, audio level, saturation, subtitle position, sound balance, and so on. Only after passing both does one obtain the **TV Drama Distribution License** (《电视剧发行许可证》, fāxíng xǔkězhèng, p0131; the names of the reviewing bodies had changed by 2016).
- **Special dual-review channel** (p0281): the **Provisions on Project Approval of Scripts and Review of Completed Films for Films and Television Dramas on Major Revolutionary and Major Historical Themes** (《重大革命和重大历史题材电影、电视剧剧本立项及完成片管理规定》) and the **Provisions on Project Approval of Scripts and Review of Completed Films for Major Theoretical Documentary Films and Television Programs** (《重大理论文献影视片剧本立项及完成片管理规定》) — these two categories require **both script project approval and completed-film review**; anyone taking on such subject matter must prepare for submission at the script stage. In addition: series involving ethnic or religious policy are reviewed by the Ethnic Affairs Commission and the Religious Affairs Bureau; co-productions or productions employing foreign actors are reported to the Foreign Affairs Department (p0174, agency names as of 2016).

### 7.2 Script Delivery Milestones and "Final Draft Before Shooting Begins"

- **Six-stage payment table** (《电视剧剧本委托创作合同》 (*TV Drama Script Commissioning Contract*), 1.5, p0318, 2016 template): within 10 working days after signing, **10%** (deposit) / **story outline** upon written approval **10%** / **per-episode outline** upon written approval **20%** / **first draft of the script** upon written approval **30%** / **second draft of the script** upon examined and approved acceptance **20%** / **final draft before shooting begins** upon final written confirmation **10%**. Two procedural clauses: milestone deliverables "are subject to the **producer's and director's unanimous signature of approval** by Party A"; "**only after receiving Party A's written confirmation may work proceed to the next stage**" (gate-style progression, 闸门式推进, zháménshì tuījìn).
- **Final draft required before shooting begins**: 《心术》 (*Angel Heart*) began shooting in 2010, and two weeks later the production unit disbanded; "the reason was that the final episode of the script was delivered only **one week before shooting began**, leaving insufficient preparation time for the shooting stage; during shooting, script revision notes from all sides kept arriving, and the production unit had to stop and revise the script" (p0076). The institutional reason (p0194): television drama "has no possibility of a trial (**Chinese TV dramas are usually distributed during or after production, unlike the American model of adjusting while shooting**)" — **China has no pilot and no broadcast-while-revising; all the risk is loaded onto the script stage.**
- **But "revising until the final wrap" remains the norm** (p0121): reasons include changes in actors, locations, and weather, budget considerations, and artistic considerations. The template assumes by default that "cooperating with revisions during shooting carries no additional payment" (2.2.5) — additional payment must be separately agreed.

### 7.3 Script Evaluation and the Presenter's Notes

- **The script evaluation meeting is a budget line item** (pp. 0145–0146): "After the producer has determined the subject or completed the first draft of the script, they often invite experts to evaluate the subject or script, or hold a seminar… **these activities also require certain expenses.**" → China's distinctive **script evaluation meeting / seminar** = a formal review gate. The contract also has an adjudication clause: "If the two parties dispute whether the script meets shooting requirements, they may commission experts acceptable to both parties to appraise it. **If the script passes review by the relevant state department, it is deemed to have met the standard**" (p0317).
- **Seven elements of a high-quality script** (pp. 0071–0074, usable directly as a checklist): subject and perspective ("**some subjects are relatively difficult to create, especially policy-restricted subjects; charging straight at them may mean certain death**") / theme (《士兵突击》 (*Soldiers Sortie*): "never abandon, never give up"; "a military drama with no women eventually swept the country… **the story is fresh, but the theme is eternal**") / style (consistent throughout, landing in character construction, narrative technique, and plot rhythm) / character ("**the driving force of a TV drama lies in the characters' pursuit of their 'desire'**"; "**the opposing character must be as strong as the key character. The so-called 'opposing character' need not be a person; it can be a predicament**") / structure (four types, see SKILL.md 5.6) / plot ("a good TV drama contains the dramatic development process of setup, development, turn, and resolution in the plot of every scene") / rhythm ("**rhythm depends on the speed of story progression and the number of events happening at the same time**").
- **A real presenter's-notes template** (《黎明之前》 (*Before Dawn*), three notes from the presenter (出品人, chūpǐnrén) Liu Dehong (刘德宏) to screenwriter Huang Ke (黄珂), p0060): (1) at the start of development, he asked that the story's environment be set on the Kuomintang side (character design would be unrestricted, the images would look good, and emotional scenes would be easier to write); (2) after the story outline emerged, **cut the explicitly dramatized "Trojan Horse operation"** — "that plot is only background… if it is written and shot explicitly, **a considerable portion of the story will depart from the protagonist's action trajectory**"; (3) after the first draft, **change the male lead from "Sailor" to Liu Xinjie (刘新杰)** — "'Sailor' has a concealed identity and cannot act openly; his scenes cannot be tightly linked in plot structure with the scenes of Bureau Eight, **which would produce a story structure in which 'two flowers bloom, each on its own branch'**." → The common logic: **the protagonist must be able to act openly; must inhabit the same plot structure as the main-battle institution; and background events must not be dramatized explicitly.**

### 7.4 Red Lines of the *General Rules for Television Drama Content Production* (《电视剧内容制作通则》, 2015-12-31)

Issuing bodies: **Television Producers Committee of the China Federation of Radio and Television Associations; China Television Drama Production Industry Association**; nature: an industry self-discipline code (not an administrative regulation), based on the *Provisions on the Administration of Television Content* (《电视内容管理规定》).

- **Article 3 advocates eight items** (a list of positive sell points): among them, "**adhere to a primary focus on realistic subject matter**, stay close to reality, close to life, and close to the masses" most directly affects subject selection.
- **Article 4 lists eleven categories that may not be produced** (major prohibited areas: the Constitution / national unity / state secrets / ethnicity / religion / social order / obscenity, gambling, and violence / insult and defamation / social morality / minors, etc.).
- **Article 5 lists ten major categories and more than sixty specific items that may not appear** (the red-line table; for the complete classification table, see reference). The ten category headings: (1) does not conform to national conditions and the social system, damages the national image, or endangers national unity and social stability; (2) damages ethnic unity; (3) violates national religious policy; (4) promotes feudal superstition and violates the scientific spirit; (5) renders horror and violence, displays ugly behavior, or may even induce crime; (6) renders obscenity and pornography and vulgar lowbrow taste; (7) insults or defames others; (8) distorts or denigrates the nation's outstanding cultural traditions; (9) endangers social morality and has a harmful effect on minors; (10) other content prohibited by laws, regulations, and state provisions. **The seven items with the greatest impact on writing**: taking negative characters as the main object of depiction; rendering or exaggerating social problems and over-displaying the dark side of society; promoting spirit possession, reincarnation, and witchcraft; **exposing investigative methods and detection details**; **making value judgments about truth and falsehood, good and evil, beauty and ugliness vague and unclear**; displaying and promoting unhealthy views and states of love and marriage (extramarital affairs, one-night stands, sexual freedom) and abnormal sexual relations; **"reversing the verdict" on historical figures and events already settled by history, tampering with famous works, and treating revolutionary history in an excessively entertaining or game-like manner**.
- **Article 6 requires expert review in three categories**: (1) minority-language writing; images of revolutionary pioneers and other important figures; specific symbols and marks; graphics and charts; (2) plots depicting specific professions, groups, and social organizations such as the People's Liberation Army, armed police, state security, public security, judicial personnel, doctors, and lawyers; (3) costumes, sets, and props of specific historical periods and professional groups. → **If you write any of these, the script enters an "expert reading" stage, and you must leave revision time in the schedule**; content involving law must also pass the production unit's legal counsel (p0280).
- **Article 7 language standards**: subtitles, except for a title written in calligraphy, should use standard Chinese characters; lyrics, foreign-language dialogue, and words and signs with specific meanings should have Chinese subtitles.
- ⚠️ **This is the 2015 version.** Since then the competent authorities have issued several more documents (including regulations on 耽改 (dāngǎi — adaptations of boys' love fiction), 偶像养成 (ǒuxiàng yǎngchéng — idol-training programs), and 饭圈 (fànquān — fandom culture)), and items have been added and removed; **the framework and wording of the ten major categories remain in use, but any citation must state the year and check the current document**.

### 7.5 Avoidance Techniques (actual practices in Zhang Wei's book)

1. **Delete extramarital affairs and "the other woman," making both parties already divorced** (in 《手机》 (*Cell Phone*), Wu Yue (伍月) is changed into a female editor who "expresses feeling but stops at propriety"; Fei Mo's (费墨) illicit affair with the female graduate student is changed to "the female graduate student falling in love with his own son," p0066). The stated cost: "Although such changes **weaken the film's sharpness and impact**."
2. **Delete incest/sexual violence and replace it with deep family affection** (《余震》 (*Aftershock*, novel) → 《唐山大地震》 (*Aftershock*, film): the "adoptive father's rape" is deleted, "replaced by an adoptive father's deep love for his adopted daughter"; the reason is that the audience had just experienced the collective trauma of the Wenchuan earthquake, p0028).
3. **Transform confrontations involving political positions into conflicts within the family** (《铁梨花》 (*Iron Pear Blossom*) uses family-ethics logic to carry four genre styles — family saga / bandit suppression / War of Resistance / spy thriller, pp. 0228–0232).
4. **Adopt a critical attitude of "punishment upon appearance" toward sensitive industries and replace scenes with dialogue** (《铁梨花》 (*Iron Pear Blossom*) avoids tomb-robbing plot, pp. 0231–0232).
5. **Let overly sharp, gray themes give way to family affection, friendship, and love** (the principle stated on p0066).
6. **Assess audience sentiment and investment risk for a tragic ending** — the complete reasoning behind cutting the "Cultural Revolution" section from 《亮剑》 (*Drawing Sword*) (pp. 0291–0292, the most candid passage of industry record): "**It involves the 'Cultural Revolution' and Taiwan Strait relations**… I am afraid it simply would not pass the SARFT review… **This was not cut because it was untrue, but because it was too true.** Although it conveyed a sense of historical truth, it was too tragic, too harrowing, over the line. **As a production entity that must first consider economic interests, we cannot take that risk.**" The solution: **distill a spiritual slogan that can run through the whole series** ("the Drawing Sword spirit"), move the narrative center of gravity to a period that can be written, and "not only preserve the essence of the characters but also shorten the length and concentrate the narrative."
7. **Costume drama as an avoidance strategy** (Zhang Mingzhi, p0069): "Some creators believe that placing a story in the historical space of 'ancient times' **can avoid many minefields of contemporary-drama creation and gain freer room to maneuver**." The candid rationale for War of Resistance dramas: "**Against the background of an era that advocates harmony, it is truly not easy to present sharp conflict on screen, so 'fighting the Japanese devils' became a dramatic conflict that ordinary people love to see and that creators find easy to present**" — ⚠️ at the same time, note that in **2013** the competent authorities explicitly moved to rectify "absurd War of Resistance dramas" (抗战雷剧, kàngzhàn léijù).

### 7.6 First-, Second-, and Third-Degree Creation and the Screenwriter's Position

- Yao Kougen (p0063): **first-degree creation (一度创作, yīdù chuàngzuò) = the script; second-degree creation (二度创作, èrdù chuàngzuò) = performance and filming; third-degree creation (三度创作, sāndù chuàngzuò) = editing and final assembly.** The screenwriter must recognize that "scriptwriting is not desk-bound literary creation but **the catalyst through which another art form comes into being**."
- Zhang Mingzhi (p0126) describes the same structure: "In completing a television drama work, **there are at least three processes of re-creation**" — the screenwriter conceives and writes → the director converts it into images → post-production editing and finishing. He also reminds us that at the "final cut" stage of editing, "**the production side and the distribution side will be invited to view the film**, and they will offer revision opinions based on their own experience and perspective" — **the story structure will be touched once more on the editing bench.**
- **The screenwriter's place in the organizational structure** (a structural fact): in organization chart 4-1 of the production unit's five major departments (production / directing / cinematography / art / sound), **there is no screenwriter** (p0079); but the remuneration table 7-1 formally lists the screenwriter among the "**key creative personnel**," and "the remuneration of cast and crew is usually **calculated by episode count**" (p0146); in the budget, the screenwriter's fee belongs to the **zero-based budget items** for which "there is no clear standard for expense expenditure," negotiated case by case according to market rates (pp. 0151–0152). The only place that puts the screenwriter into the on-set process is the rehearsal (pp. 0117–0118): "the director, actors, and screenwriter can take this opportunity to discuss the script together." For the difference between the collective creation process and the American writers' room, see `sw-writers-room`.

### 7.7 Ratings, Platforms, and Scheduling Constraints (2014–2016 data, now outdated, but the structure remains usable)

- **Platform determines content** (pp. 0021–0022): CCTV (especially the CCTV-1 prime-time slot) "must be strictly required and 'specially treated' in both subject selection and story construction"; its audience skews middle-aged and older and requires dignity and grandeur; provincial satellite channels "focus on ratings potential and are willing to choose somewhat lighter, more entertaining" material; the internet "also has relatively lower requirements for the subject matter and artistic quality of TV dramas." The platform's three-level decision (p0064): "**purchasing staff — review panel — station affairs committee**; buying a TV drama is a group decision."
- ⚠️ From New Year's Day 2015, "**one drama, two stars**" (一剧两星, yī jù liǎng xīng — each drama may air on no more than two satellite channels) and "**two episodes a night**" (一晚两集, yī wǎn liǎng jí); in 2012 the subject proportions were **major themes 1% / historical 43% / contemporary 56%** (p0188); annual output was around 15,000 episodes, "less than 80% can close a deal, and **only 30%–40% of TV dramas actually reach television stations and audiences**" (p0189).
- **The standard operation for expanding the audience** (p0063): "The target audience of a spy drama may be middle-aged and older male viewers. **If an entertaining emotional line (especially a love line) can be woven into the creation of a spy drama, the audience may be extended to female viewers; if several young, attractive characters can be created and fashionable elements introduced, the audience may be extended to younger viewers.**"
- **The ratings cost of dialect** (Zhang Wei, p0224): the humor in the dialogue of 《借枪》 (*Borrowed Gun*) "has an obvious **northern Chinese dialect character**"; the finale drew ratings of **8%–10%** in Beijing and Tianjin, while in Shanghai and Hangzhou over the same period it was **below 5%**; the background judgment: "this dialect culture produces China's **marked regional ratings divide between north and south, with the Yellow River as the dividing line**." Countermeasures: for a nationwide series, weaken the dialect flavor; for a regionally targeted series, increase the audience specificity of the dialogue. **Use dialect flavor to distinguish factions, but do not write phonetic transliteration** (Zhao Binbin's 《双面胶》 (*Double-Sided Tape*): Shanghai speech flavor vs. northern speech flavor).
- **Scheduling constrains writing in reverse** (Zhang Mingzhi, pp. 0112–0115, p0122): day and night scenes in the same location must be groupable ("shoot the day scenes together first, then the night scenes, in order to reduce the number of lighting adjustments"); deliberately write a batch of small scenes with **few actors / no special location requirements / no weather or environmental exposure** for the flexible schedule to slot in; put large-scale scenes and scenes with concentrated actors earlier ("so that as the shooting schedule goes on, the crew gets smaller and scheduling becomes more flexible"); do not schedule a 重场戏 (zhòngchǎngxì — the scene with the greatest value shift in the whole play) on the first day of shooting; **a series has dozens or even hundreds of interior and exterior locations, and generally each one must be paid for**, so "locations should not be too dispersed; concentrate them as much as possible"; "**in some TV dramas the plot requires destroying some expensive props**" — a cost the script can avoid (p0147).

### 7.8 The Screenwriter's Contract (2016 template, based on the 2010 Copyright Law 《著作权法》)

**Six clauses worth fighting for** (《聘请电影（电视剧）编剧合同》 (*Contract for Hiring a Screenwriter for Film (Television)*), pp. 0329–0338, the most screenwriter-protective of the templates):

1. **Review deadline and default clause** (1.5): "**If Party A fails to notify Party B of the review result or revision opinions within the period agreed in this clause, Party A shall be deemed to have confirmed and approved the script documents submitted by Party B.**"
2. **Restriction on major changes** (1.7, the most screenwriter-protective clause): "To protect the right of integrity of the work enjoyed by Party B from infringement, **Party A may not raise contrary revision demands regarding the creative intent, the main characters, the main events, the style, and other major aspects of the confirmed script-writing plan**. The exception is where Party A is willing to pay additional remuneration and Party B agrees." → Four protected objects: **creative intent / main characters / main events / style**. Use 1.7 to limit Article 6.1, "Party A enjoys the final revision right over the literary script."
3. **Reversion of rights** (4.11): "Within __ years from the date Party B… delivers the script, **if Party A has not shot a film (television drama) based on the script, the filming right and copyright enjoyed by Party A… shall terminate, and the aforesaid rights shall be enjoyed by Party B**."
4. **Remakes require separate permission** (4.7): "If Party A remakes the television drama under this contract, **it must separately obtain Party B's permission**."
5. **Three tiers of back-end royalties** (2.4): audio-visual product distribution revenue at __% of total list price; licensing to third parties at __% of the license contract amount; **for licensing for television broadcast, "for each instance of revenue, royalties shall be paid to Party B at __% of the license contract amount."** There is also additional payment for episodes beyond the agreed count (2.3).
6. **Credit remedies** (8.1(4)): when credit is omitted, replaced, or a co-author is added without authorization, "**immediately recall the copies already distributed, correct them, and return them**… if already broadcast or publicly screened, Party A is responsible for **eliminating the adverse effects, making a public apology, and paying compensation**."

**Four disadvantageous clauses you must understand** (《电视剧剧本委托创作合同》 (*TV Drama Script Commissioning Contract*), 2.1–2.2, pp. 0318–0319): Party A "**permanently owns the script of the series and all copyright in the series, as well as all rights in derivative products of the series**" (including character names, character designs, dialogue, music, etc.); Party A may transfer the work to others for shooting, and "Party B may not interfere"; "if the script still fails to meet Party A's requirements after revision, Party A reserves the right to **hire other personnel** to revise and create… revisions made to the script by other screenwriters **shall not be deemed an infringement of Party B's rights**"; in case of disagreement, "the script shall be completed with Party A's opinion as the final plan, **and Party B acknowledges and accepts this and may not express objections publicly**." Two clauses in the same contract favorable to the screenwriter: credit "**does not exclude Party B's right of authorship; the order of credits is determined by the proportion of the creative contribution**" (2.2.3); "**regardless of whether Party A ultimately uses Party B's script for shooting, as long as the script submitted by Party B meets the requirements agreed in the contract, Party A shall still pay the remuneration as agreed**" (2.2.8).

**Copyright framework** (as stated in the 2010 amendment of the Copyright Law; ⚠️ it was amended again in 2020: article numbers changed, and "cinematographic works and works created by a process analogous to cinematography" were renamed "audiovisual works"; **the substantive framework is unchanged**):

- Article 15: the overall copyright in a series is enjoyed by the **producer** (the entity, not the natural person "producer"); "however, authors such as the screenwriter, director, cinematographer, lyricist, and composer enjoy the **right of authorship** and have the right to **receive remuneration** in accordance with the contract signed with the producer" (p0269).
- **The screenwriter's protective clause** (pp. 0270–0272): "The author of a work **that can be used separately** in an audiovisual work (such as the script, music, or art design) **has the right to independently exercise their copyright without the producer's consent**, and the producer has no right to interfere." → The script may be published independently and adapted into a novel or stage play — **but once the contract says "all copyright belongs to Party A," this is stripped away.**
- Two written authorizations for an adaptation project (p0268): authorization of the **adaptation right** from the copyright holder of the original work; if the original is itself an adaptation (animation, stage play, film or television), **dual authorization from the original screenwriter + the original rights holder** is also required. When documentary literature, real people, or historical figures are involved, require the production side to promise in writing that it has obtained the consent and authorization of **the person concerned or their close relatives**, with the production side bearing liability for disputes over personality rights (contract 5.2.7).
- Moral rights (the right of publication, right of authorship, right of modification, and right to protect the integrity of the work) **cannot be transferred or inherited**; the right of authorship / right of modification / right to protect the integrity of the work **are not subject to a time limit** (pp. 0272–0274).
- Product placement is a contractual obligation: "Party A shall be responsible for coordinating with the series' **screenwriter**, director, lead actors, and other crew members to ensure that the embedded advertising appears in the series as agreed between Party B and the advertising client"; the creative script for product placement "**shall be submitted __ days before the series begins shooting**" (pp. 0307–0311).

| treatment | **脚本** ("script"; Douglas, Blum) | treatment | ⚠️ The Chinese translation 脚本 is easily confused with the shooting script; when citing, write "treatment (translated as 脚本, a prose narrative without dialogue)" |

| beat sheet | **节拍表** ("beat table"; Douglas) | rhythm sheet | — |
| breaking a story | **解析故事** ("analyzing the story"; Douglas) | breaking the story / building the story | — |
