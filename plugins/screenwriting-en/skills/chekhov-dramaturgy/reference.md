# Chekhov's Seven Full-Length Plays: Act-by-Act Structure Tables and Original-Text Examples

> Use together with SKILL.md. Translations: 焦菊隐 (Jiao Juyin) for Ivanov, The Seagull, Uncle Vanya, Three Sisters, and The Cherry Orchard; 童道明 (Tong Daoming) for The Fatherless One and The Wood Demon; 李健吾 (Li Jianwu) for the one-act plays.

## I. 《没有父亲的人》 (The Fatherless One, Platonov, c. 1880, Four Acts)

**Logline**: The estate of the general's widow Anna opens its doors to summer guests. The village schoolteacher Platonov (27, once "a second Byron," now "a stone lying flat") is surrounded in the course of one night by four women: his wife Sasha, Anna (who rides over at night: "Let's go"), his former lover Sofya (now the wife of Anna's stepson), who sends a telegram-style letter: "Come and take me away", and Grekova, the girl he has publicly humiliated. He goes to Sofya; Sasha tries to lie down on the rails and then to poison herself with match heads, both unsuccessfully; Sofya tells her husband the truth; Osip, the horse thief, is hired to kill him; the estate is auctioned; Platonov puts a pistol to his temple and then lowers it: "I want to live." Grekova comes to take him away; Sofya enters and fires; the second shot is fired point-blank into his chest. The last line: "Give him three roubles!"

| Act | Scene/ambient sound | Content | Function |
|---|---|---|---|
| One (22 scenes) | Drawing room, piano, violin, harmonium | Chess and talk of Grekova; old Glagolev reminisces; the Platonovs enter; to his dead father, "either tell the truth or say nothing"; he humiliates Grekova with "bedbug ether" and then apologizes; Sofya recognizes him; false teeth; "three roads"; young Vengerovich refuses to shake hands; Osip is exhibited as "a bloodthirsty animal from a museum of modern animals"; Anna: "Either the estate or my conscience... I choose the estate." | A reception day: all the characters enter in turn and are offended by the protagonist one by one |
| Two, Scene One (21 scenes) | Garden, night feast; skittles "Five good! Four bad!"; fireworks | Borrowing money and throwing it around; the colonel's drunken talk; Sofya: "Let's get away from here"; "You are afraid"; hiring Osip to "give him a beating"; Grekova is kissed and abused; "Friendship between a man and a woman is only one step from love"; "I have a heaven, but no angels"; the son lies to his father — "She wants a thousand roubles" → stroke; "Why can't we live as we ought to?!"; Sofya: "I bless you, new life!" → fireworks | In one night all four lines are ignited |
| Two, Scene Two (17 scenes) | In front of the school, railway embankment, telegraph poles; night; a train | Osip talks about loving Anna; "Why aren't you a fly?"; young Vengerovich's night talk; **Anna rides up: "Let's go!"**; Katya delivers the letter: "Come and take me away. Yours"; Voynitsev drunk: "I play Hamlet, you play Claudius"; Osip fires into the air; **Sasha lies down on the rails → is lifted up → the train passes** | The summons of three women alternate on the same stretch of embankment |
| Three (10 scenes) | The schoolroom, clutter, bottles | Three weeks later. Sofya: "We leave tomorrow"; reading Anna's letter; a summons → he writes to Grekova on the summons; Anna comes to drink: "Live like a human being, you fool"; he shuts the door and stops his ears; **Osip comes to kill him**: he tears up the banknotes — "So you won't think I'm killing you for money"; Sasha: "Is the affair over?" → leaves; "Give her back to me"; old Glagolev: "Is she an honest woman?" → faints → "Let's go to Paris" | The protagonist is tried in one room by five visitors in turn |
| Four (13 scenes) | The general's study; guns and swords cover the walls; morning; rain | "I am a cuckolded husband," on his knees, begging; Osip is beaten to death by peasants; the estate is bought; Grekova withdraws the complaint; Platonov, his hand bandaged: "Don't commit suicide... I have killed myself"; "Give me something hot"; "She has poisoned herself with match heads!" "She's not dead?" "She's alive!" (Laughter.); the colonel: "A lie can sometimes save a person"; to his temple: "I want to live"; "Where does it hurt?" "Platonov is in pain"; Sofya fires; "Give him three roubles!" | Everyone has their reckoning in the same study; death is comic |

**Example: Osip Comes to Kill (Act Three, Scene Seven)**
```
OSIP: Do you want to know?
PLATONOV: Come on! How interesting! Where to?
OSIP: To the next world.
PLATONOV: That's a long way! (Pause.) Are you the one who is to send me to the next world?
OSIP: That's right. I've brought you your passport.
...
OSIP: (Takes out some banknotes.) Look... Vengerovich gave me this. He told me to finish you off! (Tears up the banknotes.)
PLATONOV: Hmm... why did you tear up the money? To show your magnanimity, is that it?
OSIP: I don't do magnanimity. I tore up the money so you wouldn't think I'm killing you for money.
```

**Example: The Ending (Act Four)**
```
[Sofya picks up the pistol, fires at Platonov, and misses.
GREKOVA: (Standing between Platonov and Sofya.) What are you doing?! (Shouts.) Somebody! Come quickly!
SOFYA: Let me go...
[She goes around Grekova and fires point-blank into Platonov's chest.
PLATONOV: Wait, wait... why is this happening? (Falls.)
...
MARKO: (Entering.) The magistrates' court... (Pause.)
ANNA: Platonov!
[Platonov sits up slightly and looks around at everyone.
ANNA: Platonov... it's nothing... have some water!
PLATONOV: (Pointing at Marko.) Give him three roubles! (Lies down and dies.)
...
TRILETSKY: Life is a kopeck! Misha, farewell! Your kopeck is gone!
```

## II. 《伊凡诺夫》 (Ivanov, 1887, Four-Act Drama)

**Logline**: Ivanov, a country gentleman of thirty-five, once passionate, idealistic, and married for love to the Jewish woman Anna (who converted for him and was disowned by her parents), is now weary of everything and escapes every evening to the neighbouring Lebedevs' house; the Lebedevs' twenty-year-old daughter Sasha wants to save him with "active love"; his wife catches the two kissing and he loses control and shouts, "You are going to die"; a year later his wife is dead; before his wedding to Sasha he looks in the mirror, sees his white hair, asks to break off the engagement, is publicly called a scoundrel by the doctor Lvov, and shoots himself.

| Act | Scene/ambient sound | Content | Function |
|---|---|---|---|
| One | The estate garden; piano and cello duet; an owl; the watchman; an accordion in the distance | Borkin frightens him with a gun and demands eighty-two roubles; the doctor announces consumption; Ivanov confesses "there is nothing at all remarkable about me"; advises the doctor to "marry an ordinary, drab woman"; Lvov's monologue "I hate this Tartuffe"; Anna begs him to stay; he answers, "Pray for me" | In a single act, everyone's disease is laid out: weariness, consumption, stinginess, misanthropy, righteous frenzy |
| Two | The Lebedevs' drawing room; cards "Pass" "Two of spades"; the old lady's gossip | Sasha's birthday; three versions of the rumor (garlic, cattle plague, nine thousand roubles); Sasha berates the young men: "You are all worthless"; Zyuzyushka blows out the candles; a request to defer the interest is refused; Borkin proposes to Babakina on the count's behalf; **Sasha declares her love → "Life has begun again" → Anna stands frozen in the doorway → "Sara!" Curtain** | A rumor scene + a stinginess scene + a proposal farce + an act-ending discovery |
| Three | The study; papers and a revolver on the desk, vodka, pickled herring, cucumber; noon | Talk of French and German politics over cucumber and carp; Lebedev produces eleven thousand and then takes it back: "I was only joking"; "I have given myself a hernia"; "I hate my own voice, my steps, my hands"; Lvov: "Take off your mask"; Sasha: "Have you ever forged banknotes?... Smash something"; **Anna presses him → "Be quiet, you Jewish woman!... You are going to die" → "When did he say that?"** | The protagonist dissects himself twice; the cruelest line is spoken at the act ending |
| Four | A year later; wedding atmosphere; piano, violin, cello | Lvov: "I will expose you"; Kosykh is still talking about cards; Sasha: "Sometimes I even feel I don't love him as I ought to"; the count sees the cello and weeps for his dead wife; "I looked in the mirror and saw that my temples had turned white... let it end here"; "The ceiling is white, the boots are black, the sugar is sweet"; "You are a scoundrel!" — "Thank you very much"; "This is not a wedding but a debate"; "My youth has awakened in my heart" → shoots himself | The wedding turns into a debate; the suicide is the irony of "my old self has roused itself" |

**Example: The End of Act Three**
```
IVANOV: Hm, let me tell you... you... you are going to die... the doctor told me you are going to die...
ANNA: (Sits down, in a low voice.) When did he say that?
[Pause.
IVANOV: (Clutches his head with both hands.) I am a brute! My God, I am a brute! (Sobs.)
—Curtain
```

**Example: Lebedev's Common Sense (Act Four)**
```
Pull yourself together! Look at things simply, the way other people do! Everything in the world is simple. The ceiling is white, the boots are black, the sugar is sweet. You love Sasha and she loves you. If you love her, stay; if you don't, go; there's no need to make a fuss.
```

## III. 《海鸥》 (The Seagull, 1896, Four-Act Comedy)

**Logline**: A lakeside estate. The young Treplev stages his "new form" play on a homemade open-air stage, with Nina, the girl he loves, in the lead; his mother, the actress Arkadina, laughs at it, and he angrily brings down the curtain. Nina falls for Arkadina's lover, the writer Trigorin. Treplev kills a seagull and lays it at Nina's feet, then attempts suicide. Trigorin elopes with Nina and abandons her. Two years later Nina returns for one evening as a third-rate actress, recites her old monologue, and leaves; Treplev tears up his manuscript, a shot is heard off-stage, and the doctor says, "One of my bottles of ether has just exploded."

**Love chain (all one-way)**: Medvedenko → Masha → Treplev → Nina → Trigorin ⇄ Arkadina; Polina → Dorn; Sorin wanted to marry and wanted to be a writer and achieved neither ("L'homme qui a voulu").

| Act | Scene/ambient sound | Content | Function |
|---|---|---|---|
| One | A garden, an amateur stage hides the lake; hammering; a dog barks all night; a horn, the smell of sulphur, and phosphorus lights from the play-within-the-play | "Why do you always wear black?" "I am in mourning for my life"; the play-within-the-play "Men, lions, eagles, and partridges..." is interrupted by his mother with "decadent" and "the smell of sulphur" → "Curtain!"; "An angel has flown past"; Dorn praises the play when alone; Masha leans on Dorn's chest: "I love Konstantin" | The first line is the theme; the play-within-the-play is the embryo of the whole play; the act ending is changed to a confession |
| Two | A croquet lawn, noon, the lake glittering | Arkadina and Masha compare their youth; the steward quarrels about horses: "I resign"; Polina begs Dorn to take her away and ruins the flowers; **the dead seagull is laid at Nina's feet: "I shall shoot myself in the same way soon"**; Trigorin's long monologue (clouds like a grand piano, "not as good as Turgenev"); taking notes: "A lakeside... a girl like a seagull lives there... a man comes along by chance... and destroys her" | All three meanings of the seagull are planted; the subject of the story = prophecy |
| Three | The dining room, suitcases | Masha: "I am getting married"; the medallion "page 121, lines eleven and twelve"; mother and son change the bandage → quarrel → reconcile; "If ever you need my life, come and take it" → she kneels and clings to his knees: "You are Russia's only hope" → (aside) "Now I've got him"; the steward tells the "wily chicken" story; he returns for his cane, they arrange to meet at the hotel, a long kiss | Three "catches" in one act |
| Four | Two years later; the drawing room turned into a study; wind; the watchman's clapper; a waltz; lotto calls | The stage "like the skeleton of a dead man"; the magazine with his story uncut; Dorn: "The crowd in Genoa" → "Where is Zarechnaya?"; the stuffed seagull: "I don't remember"; **Nina enters: "I am a seagull... no, I said it wrong," "One must have patience, one must learn to bear one's cross, one must have faith," recites "Men, lions, eagles, and partridges" again, and runs out**; he tears up his manuscript; the card game continues; **a shot → "One of my bottles of ether has just exploded" → "Find some way to get Irina away"** | All the characters are at the card table; the protagonist's death is hidden in an off-stage lie |

**Example: The Opening**
```
MEDVEDENKO: Why do you always wear black?
MASHA: I am in mourning for my life. I am unhappy.
MEDVEDENKO: Why? (Silence.) I don't understand... You are healthy; your father, though not rich, is well off. My life is much harder than yours. I earn only twenty-three roubles a month, and even out of that they deduct a pension. Still, I don't go into mourning.
```

**Example: The Catch (Act Three)**
```
TRIGORIN: I have no will of my own... I never have had a will of my own... I am lazy, soft, always obedient; I was really born to be liked by women! Take me, carry me away, only don't let me go one step from you...
ARKADINA: (Aside.) Now I've got him. (Calmly, as if nothing had happened.) This, if you like, you may stay. I am leaving today; in a week you can come to me. Really, why should you be in such a hurry?
TRIGORIN: No, we had better go together.
```

**Example: The Ending**
```
SHAMRAYEV: (Leading Trigorin to the cupboard.) The thing I was telling you about is right here... (Takes the stuffed seagull out of the cupboard.) This is what you asked us to have made.
TRIGORIN: (Looking at the seagull.) I don't remember! (Thinks.) No, I don't remember!
[Backstage, a shot from the right; everyone jumps in fright.
ARKADINA: (Alarmed.) What was that?
DORN: Nothing. Something must have exploded in my medicine chest. Don't be alarmed. (Goes out by the right door and returns at once.) I was quite right. One of my bottles of ether has just exploded. (Hums.) "Once more I see you, enchanting woman..."
ARKADINA: (Sitting down at the card table.) It gave me such a fright! It reminded me of that time when he... (Covers her face with both hands.) It made my eyes go dark.
DORN: (Turning the pages of a magazine, to Trigorin.) About two months ago this magazine published an article... a letter from America; I wanted to ask you about it... (Puts his arm around Trigorin's waist and draws him toward the footlights.) This question is extremely interesting... (In a low voice.) Find some way to get Irina Nikolayevna away. Konstantin Gavrilovich has just shot himself...
—Curtain
```

## IV. 《林妖》 (The Wood Demon, 1889, Four-Act Comedy) → 《万尼亚舅舅》 (Uncle Vanya, 1896, "Scenes from Country Life")

**Logline for Uncle Vanya**: The retired professor Serebryakov moves with his young wife Yelena into the estate of his first wife, disrupting the arrangement by which Uncle Vanya and his niece Sonya have worked for twenty-five years to support the professor; Vanya falls for Yelena, the doctor Astrov is also drawn to her and neglects his forests and his patients, and Sonya has loved the doctor hopelessly for six years; the professor announces that he will sell the estate, Vanya explodes and fires twice at the professor and misses; the professor and his wife leave, the doctor leaves, and Vanya and Sonya return to the account book — "We shall rest."

| Act | Scene/ambient sound | Content | Function |
|---|---|---|---|
| One | A garden, a tea table under an old poplar, a swing, a guitar; a cloudy afternoon near three | "How long have we known each other?" "Eleven years"; "The professor means to stay till the end of the world"; the "famous nonentity" speech; Telegin's wife ran away; "Just the weather for hanging yourself"; cluck-cluck; the forest monologue (Sonya relays it first, the doctor tells it again, interrupted by vodka: "In short..."); Yelena: "Every one of you has a destructive nature"; Vanya's declaration: "Don't drive me away" | Everyone's "disease" is laid out at the tea table |
| Two | The dining room, night, the watchman tapping; a thunderstorm | The professor keeps watch: "I dreamed my left leg fell off" "In five or six years I shall be old too"; Vanya drunk: "Ten years ago she was seventeen and I was thirty-seven" "soap bubbles"; the doctor drunkenly sings "acquaintance → mistress → friend"; Sonya and the doctor eat cheese: the lights in the forest, "I love no one," "If I had a sister" → "I can only make her understand that I cannot love her"; "Why am I not pretty?"; they drink from the same glass and make peace; "I want to play the piano" → the watchman is told to stop → "He refuses" | Every pair tries to come close once and fails |
| Three | The drawing room, three doors; afternoon | "Idleness and slackness are contagious"; Vanya goes to get autumn roses; Sonya confesses: "I have loved him for six years"; Yelena: "I'll go and talk to him" — Sonya at the door: "No, it's better to leave it unclear"; **three maps** → "You are not in the least interested in this"; "Do you like her as a woman?" "No"; "A beautiful cat, soft as silk, cruel and fond of killing"; a kiss → **Vanya stands in the doorway with the roses** → "The weather is lovely today... only the days are getting shorter"; the family council ("The Inspector General," manet omnes una nox, selling the estate to buy a villa in Finland); "Twenty-five years like a mole"; the nurse: "Let the turkeys gobble and fight"; **a shot off-stage → "Ah, bang! Missed? Missed again?!"** | The three maps = a chart of degeneration; the bunch of roses is the cruelest prop |
| Four | Vanya's bedroom and office; account books, scales, a starling, a map of Africa; autumn night | Winding wool: "This is better"; the revolver hidden in the cellar; the doctor asks for morphine; "We have both ruined our lives beyond repair"; Yelena takes a pencil: "Serve me right! Once in a lifetime!" a kiss; "Finita la comedia"; the professor: "One must do something"; the map of Africa: "Isn't it still terribly hot?"; the nurse brings vodka; horse bells; "February the second, oil, twenty pounds..."; Sonya's monologue; the watchman, the guitar, the annotations, the wool | Everything returns to the order that preceded Act One |

**For the comparison of The Wood Demon → Uncle Vanya see Section VII of SKILL.md. The original ending of Act Two of The Wood Demon (retained as is):**
```
YELENA: I haven't played for a long time. I want to play and cry, cry like a silly woman. (Looks out of the window.) Yefim, is that you tapping?
THE WATCHMAN'S VOICE: Yes, it's me!
YELENA: Don't tap; the master is unwell.
THE WATCHMAN'S VOICE: I'm going at once! (Whistles.) Hey there, little dog Zhuchka! Zhuchka!
[A silence.
SONYA: (Returning.) Not allowed to play!
—Curtain
```

**Example: The Discovery (Act Three)**
```
ASTROV: (Putting his arm around her waist and pulling her to him.) Tomorrow at the forester's hut... about two o'clock. Will you come? Yes?
YELENA: (Seeing Voynitsky.) Let me go! (Very confused, goes to the window.) This is dreadful.
VOYNITSKY: (Lays the bunch of flowers on a chair, trembling all over with emotion, wipes his face and neck with a handkerchief.) It doesn't matter... no... it doesn't matter...
ASTROV: (In a displeased tone.) My dear Ivan Petrovich, the weather is lovely today. It was a little overcast this morning, as if it were going to rain, but now look how sunny it is. To tell the truth, the autumn weather has been splendid this year... and the harvest isn't bad either. (Rolls up the map.) Only the days are getting shorter... (Exits.)
```

**Example: The Gunshot (End of Act Three)**
```
VOYNITSKY: Let me go, Yelena, let me go! (Tears himself free, runs on stage, looks for Serebryakov with his eyes.) Where has he got to? Ah, there he is! (Fires.) Ah, bang!
[Pause.
Missed? Missed again?! (Furiously.) Ah, you... you go to the devil... (Throws the revolver on the floor and sinks exhausted into a chair...)
YELENA: Take me away! Take me away, kill me, but... I can't stay here any longer!
VOYNITSKY: (In despair.) Oh, what have I done! What have I done!
SONYA: (In a low voice.) Mama! Mama!
```

**Example: The Ending (Act Four)**
```
VOYNITSKY: (Writing.) "February the second, oil, twenty pounds... February the sixteenth, oil again, twenty pounds... buckwheat..."
[Pause. Horse bells are heard.
MARINA: He has gone.
[Pause.
SONYA: (Returning, puts the candle back on the table.) He has gone...
VOYNITSKY: (Tapping the abacus, then writing down the total.) Together that makes... fifteen... twenty-five...
...
SONYA: What can we do? We must go on living!
[Pause.
We shall go on living, Uncle Vanya. We shall live through a long, long chain of monotonous days and evenings... We shall work for others until old age... and we shall see a bright, beautiful life full of joy... (Involuntarily kneels before him and lays her head on his hands, in a low voice.) We shall rest at last!
[Telegin softly plays the guitar.
We shall rest! We shall hear the angels, we shall see the whole sky paved with diamonds...
[The watchman's tapping is heard.
[Telegin softly plays. Maria Vasilyevna writes a note in the margin of her pamphlet. Marina knits.
Ah, rest!
—The curtain falls slowly
```

## V. 《三姊妹》 (Three Sisters, 1900, Four-Act Drama)

**Logline**: In a provincial town, the general's three daughters — Olga (a teacher), Masha (married to a schoolteacher), and Irina (twenty) — and their brother Andrei dream of returning to Moscow; the officers of the artillery battery are the only people they have to talk to. Andrei marries the petty-bourgeois Natasha, who takes over the house room by room, drives out the old nurse, and has an affair with the chairman of the zemstvo board; Andrei loses money at cards and mortgages the house; Masha falls in love with Lieutenant Colonel Vershinin, who has a mad wife; Irina agrees to marry the baron she does not love, Tuzenbakh, and to go to work at the brickworks; on the day the army leaves, Solyony kills the baron in a duel; the military band fades, and the three sisters cling to one another: "We must live."

Time: Act One, noon on May 5 (the first anniversary of their father's death, Irina's name day); Act Two, 8 p.m. during Shrovetide the following February; Act Three, two years later, after two o'clock on a summer night, during a fire; Act Four, noon in autumn, as the army departs.

| Act | Content | Function |
|---|---|---|
| One | "Father has been dead a whole year now"; the three sisters in three colors; "To Moscow"; Irina: "I want to work"; Chebutykin reads the newspaper "to prevent hair loss" and gives a silver samovar; Masha recites the oak tree, wants to leave, stays; Vershinin: "People will forget us" "In two or three hundred years"; "Your clock is seven minutes fast"; thirteen people at the table; Natasha's green sash; **Andrei proposes, two officers witness it** | The opening is the theme; the "two or three hundred years from now" philosophy is stated once in Act One |
| Two | Natasha carries a candle to check the fire and demands Irina's room; Andrei complains to the deaf man; Ferapont: "ate pancakes and died" "a rope stretched across Moscow"; "Lom—ton—ton"; the "two or three hundred years from now" debate (migratory birds, cranes); "Balzac was married in Berdichev" ×3; card reading: "You will not go to Moscow"; Vershinin gets a letter: "My wife has taken poison again" and slips away; the chekhartma/cheremsha argument; Solyony: "I shall kill my rival"; Natasha rides off with Protopopov; Irina alone: "To Moscow!" | Each person's longing is pressed down by a trivial thing |
| Three | Fire bell; red glow. Anfisa: "Don't drive me away"; Natasha: "I won't have you sitting in front of me!"; Kulygin hides behind the wardrobe; Chebutykin washes his hands and monologues "Perhaps I don't exist," **smashes his mother's clock** "Smashed!" and announces the affair; Fedotik dances: "Burnt out!"; Kulygin: "I am content" ×3; "He has mortgaged the house to the bank"; Irina breaks down: "I have forgotten even the Italian for window"; **Masha confesses — Olga hides behind the screen: "I won't listen anyway"**; "I would marry him... only we must go to Moscow!" | The night of the fire = the family's night of confession |
| Four | The old garden, the long avenue ending at the river and the forest; "Hey! Halloo!" The lieutenants say goodbye: "Goodbye, echo!"; Chebutykin: "Everyone has forgotten to say goodbye to me"; Kulygin has shaved off his moustache; "What happened yesterday near the theatre?" — "Nothing much"; "Did you love my mother?" "Very much" "I don't remember"; "One baron more or less, what does it matter"; Solyony sprays perfume: "There's a smell of a corpse"; Andrei to the deaf man: "A hundred thousand people know only how to eat, drink, and sleep"; **Tuzenbakh's farewell: "This tree is dead, but it still sways in the wind like the others" "I haven't had my coffee yet today"**; Anfisa: "There is no one happier than I in the world"; a long kiss; Kulygin: "I won't say a word"; Masha misquotes "A cat, green leaves all around"; **a distant shot**; the false moustache; Natasha: "Why is this fork lying about"; the military band; a whisper: "The baron has just been killed in the duel" → "I suspected it long ago"; the three sisters cling together; "It makes no difference, it makes no difference" | Three farewells layered into the same noon |

**Example: Andrei and the Deaf Man (Act Two)**
```
ANDREI: ... I, who dream every night that I am a professor at Moscow University, a famous scholar of whom all Russia is proud, am condemned to be merely a member of the zemstvo council!
FERAPONT: I can't say anything about that... I didn't hear clearly...
ANDREI: If you could really hear clearly, perhaps I wouldn't be telling you. I very much need to talk to someone.
...
FERAPONT: In Moscow — the other day a contractor was saying at the zemstvo council — he said that in Moscow some merchants ate pancakes; well, it seems one of them ate forty and died. Whether it was forty or fifty I don't remember exactly.
```

**Example: The Smashed Clock (Act Three)**
```
CHEBUTYKIN: (Drops the wall clock and smashes it.) Smashed!
[Pause. Everyone has a grieved face; everyone is upset.
KULYGIN: (Picking up the pieces.) To smash such a precious thing — just look at you, Ivan Romanovich, Ivan Romanovich! I shall give you a zero for conduct!
IRINA: It was Mama's clock.
CHEBUTYKIN: Perhaps... if it was Mama's, then it was Mama's. Perhaps I didn't smash it, but only think I smashed it? Perhaps we only think we exist, but in reality we don't exist? I know nothing, and no one knows anything. (Goes to the door.) Why are you staring at me? You are all blind! Natasha and Protopopov are having a little affair, and you don't see anything...
```

**Example: Tuzenbakh's Farewell (Act Four)**
```
TUZENBAKH: ... I must go, it is time... Look at this tree: it is dead, but it still sways in the wind like the others. So I feel that if I die, I shall still take part in life, one way or another. Goodbye, my dear... (Kisses her hands.) The papers you gave me are on my desk, under the calendar.
IRINA: I'll come with you.
TUZENBAKH: (Alarmed.) No, no! (Goes quickly, stops in the avenue.) Irina!
IRINA: What?
TUZENBAKH: (Not knowing what to say.) I haven't had my coffee yet today. Tell them to get me some. (Goes out quickly.)
```

**Example: The Shot and the Ending (Act Four)**
```
MASHA: (Suppressing her sobs.) "By the seashore grows an oak tree, green leaves all around... and on the tree a golden chain, gleaming..." A golden chain on the tree... I am going mad... By the seashore... an oak tree, green leaves all around...
OLGA: Calm yourself, Masha... calm yourself... give her some water.
MASHA: I am not crying...
KULYGIN: She is not crying... she is so good...
[A faint shot in the distance.
MASHA: By the seashore grows an oak tree, green leaves all around, and on the tree a golden chain... A cat, green leaves all around... an oak tree, green leaves all around... I've got it wrong...
...
CHEBUTYKIN: The baron has just been killed in the duel.
IRINA: (Weeping silently.) I suspected it, I suspected it...
CHEBUTYKIN: (Goes to the far back of the stage and sits on a bench.) I am worn out... (Takes his newspaper out of his pocket.) Let her cry... (Hums.) "Tell us, what will you do? Tell us, will you play the fool?..." It's all the same anyway!
[The three sisters stand, clinging tightly to one another.
MASHA: Oh! Listen to that military band! They are leaving us, one of them is gone forever, gone and never coming back, and we shall have to begin our lives again alone... We must live... We must live...
...
OLGA: (Embracing her two sisters.) What joyful, lively music! How it makes one want to live!... We must live! How happy and joyful the music is! It makes one feel that in a little while we shall know why we live, why we suffer... We long to understand! Oh! We long to understand!
[The music fades into the distance. Kulygin, smiling happily, brings Masha's hat and shawl; Andrei pushes the pram.
CHEBUTYKIN: (Hums.) "Tell us, tell us, what will you do? Tell us, will you play the fool?..." (Reads his newspaper.) It makes no difference, it makes no difference.
OLGA: We long to understand, we long to understand!
—Curtain
```

## VI. 《樱桃园》 (The Cherry Orchard, 1903, Four-Act Comedy)

**Logline**: Lyubov, a landowner, returns from Paris to the family cherry orchard, which is about to be auctioned; Lopakhin, the son of a serf and now a merchant, repeatedly urges her to lease the land for villas; she and her brother Gaev will not listen; on the day of the auction the house holds a ball, and Lopakhin buys the cherry orchard; in October the family disperses, the "eternal student" Trofimov and the daughter Anya go toward a "new life," Lyubov returns to Paris to the lover who deceived her, the eighty-seven-year-old servant Firs is forgotten in the locked house, and in the distance the sound of an axe cutting down the trees.

| Act | Time/scene/sound | Content | Function |
|---|---|---|---|
| One | May dawn, the "nursery," windows shut, morning frost in the orchard, cherry trees in blossom | Lopakhin falls asleep waiting for the train; memories of "a little peasant"; Yepikhodov's boots creak, he drops a flower, bumps into a chair: "You see, don't you!"; everyone arrives: "The nursery!"; "The auction is in August"; Lopakhin's plan: lease the land for villas, twenty-five thousand; "Cut down all the cherry trees!"; the recipe for dried cherries is "lost"; she tears up the Paris telegram; **Gaev's speech to the hundred-year-old cupboard**; Pishchik eats all the pills: "Lend me two hundred and forty roubles"; a window opens: "The whole orchard is white"; "Mother, who is dead, walks in the orchard" — a little leaning white poplar; Trofimov arrives: "Grisha, my child!" "How ugly you have become, how old!"; a shepherd boy's reed pipe | A day of reunion: the solution is stated in Act One and refused |
| Two | Outdoors, a ruined chapel, a well, gravestones, telegraph poles, the town in the distance; sunset | Charlotta: "I have no passport... Who am I?" eating a cucumber; the revolver in Yepikhodov's pocket, "an enormous spider"; Yasha "pretends he has just been bathing in the river"; Lopakhin: "Just one word: yes or no"; "Villas and summer visitors... how vulgar"; Lyubov's confession (her husband died of champagne, her lover, her son drowned, she wanted to take poison) → she tears up the second telegram → "I seem to hear music in the distance"; "Why not marry Varya?" "Yes! Why not?"; Firs: "Before the emancipation"; Trofimov's speech; **the sound of a snapping string**: "A bucket broke in the mine" "A heron" "An owl" "It was the same before the great disaster"; the tramp recites Nadson, asks for thirty kopecks, and gets a gold rouble; "Ophelia, go to a nunnery"; "All Russia is our garden" "Throw the keys into the well"; the moon rises | The only outdoor act, the only sunset; the prophecy appears here |
| Three | The evening of August 22, the small drawing room and the hall, a Jewish band, a quadrille | Pishchik: "a descendant of Caligula's horse," money falls through the lining of his coat; Varya dances crying; Charlotta's conjuring, ventriloquism, two girls produced from under a blanket; "Madame Lopakhin!" "The eternal student!"; Yepikhodov breaks a billiard cue; Lyubov begs Trofimov: "Talk to me" → the third telegram: "I love him, it is like a stone hanging around my neck" → "Your beard hasn't grown enough" "At your age, not even one mistress" → Petya falls down the stairs → dancing; the stationmaster recites "The Sinner" and is interrupted by a waltz; Yasha: "I wish you would close your eyes for good"; a passer-by in the kitchen says "It's sold"; Varya swings her stick and hits Lopakhin by mistake: "Thank you very much!"; Gaev, with "salted whitefish and herring," hears the billiards and stops crying; **"Sold." "Who bought it?" "I did."** the keys thrown on the floor; Lopakhin's long monologue "Look at Lopakhin swinging the axe in this cherry orchard! Play, music!" "Why didn't you listen to me?"; knocks over a candlestick: "I can pay for everything"; Anya kneels: "We will plant another, new garden" | The ball = the other side of the auction; the disaster is announced by the buyer |
| Four | October, the same set as Act One, curtains and picture frames taken down, furniture piled in a corner | No one drinks the champagne; Yasha: "It isn't real champagne"; "Only forty-six minutes till the train"; looking for galoshes; "Two thousand acres of poppies netted forty thousand"; "I am a free man... I stand in the front rank"; **the axe in the distance**; "Has Firs been sent to the hospital?" "Yes" (he has not); a crushed hatbox: "You see, don't you!"; Yasha refuses to see his mother; Charlotta carries a bundle like a baby: "Give me another job"; Pishchik repays the money: "white clay" "There was once a Simeonov-Pishchik"; **the proposal scene: "I have no thermometer" — "Yermolai!" — "I'm coming!"**; "What?" [Pause. "Well, then, let's go!"; "Double into the middle pocket... I won't say a word"; "I was only six... sitting on this windowsill watching my father go to church"; Varya pulls out her umbrella and Lopakhin defends himself; "Farewell, my house!" "Hello, new life!"; doors are locked one after another, the carriage drives away; **Firs**: "Locked, they've all gone... they've forgotten me... life passes so quickly... you good-for-nothing!"; the string; the axe | The same setting as Act One, in the opposite direction |

**Example: The Cupboard Speech (Act One)**
```
GAEV: ... My dear, my respected cupboard! For more than a hundred years you have been advancing toward the lofty goals of justice and happiness, oh, you! I salute you...
[Pause.
LOPAKHIN: Yes...
LYUBOV: You haven't changed at all, Lenya.
GAEV: (A little embarrassed.) White into the corner pocket, red into the middle!
```

**Example: The String (Act Two)**
```
[Everyone sits motionless, each thinking his own thoughts, a dead silence. Only Firs is heard muttering. Suddenly, in the distance, as if from the sky, comes a sound like a snapping string, mournful and faint, and dies away.
LYUBOV: What is that?
LOPAKHIN: I don't know. Perhaps a bucket broke in a mine somewhere. But it's very far away.
GAEV: Perhaps it is some kind of bird... a heron, maybe.
TROFIMOV: Or an owl...
LYUBOV: (Shuddering.) That sound is rather frightening!
[Pause.
FIRS: It was just the same before the great disaster: the owl hooted, and the samovar kept hissing.
GAEV: Before what disaster?
FIRS: Before the emancipation.
```

**Example: Sold (Act Three)**
```
LYUBOV: Has the cherry orchard been sold?
LOPAKHIN: It has.
LYUBOV: Who bought it?
LOPAKHIN: I did.
[Pause.
[Lyubov Andreyevna is overcome; she would have fallen if she had not caught hold of a table and an armchair. Varya takes the bunch of keys from her belt, throws it on the floor in the middle of the drawing room, and goes out.
I bought it. Wait a little, don't hurry, my head is spinning, I can't speak...
...
Hey! Musicians, play! I want to hear you play! Come, everyone, and see Lopakhin swing the axe in this cherry orchard! See the trees fall one after another!... Play, music!
[The band plays. Lyubov Andreyevna sinks into a chair and weeps bitterly.
(Reproachfully.) Why didn't you listen to me? My poor, good Lyubov Andreyevna! It is too late now. (With tears in his eyes.) Oh, if only all this could be over soon!
```

**Example: The Proposal That Does Not Happen (Act Four)**
```
LOPAKHIN: Last year at this time it had already snowed, if you remember. But now look: it's clear and sunny everywhere. Only it's a little colder... it's already three degrees below freezing.
VARYA: I don't have a thermometer.
[Pause.
And the thermometer is broken too...
[Pause.
[A voice from the yard outside: "Yermolai Alekseyevich!"
LOPAKHIN: (As though he had been waiting for just such a call.) I'm coming! (Goes out quickly.)
[Varya sits on the floor, lays her head on the bundle of clothes, and sobs quietly. The door opens; Lyubov Andreyevna enters cautiously.
LYUBOV: What?
[Pause.
Well, then, let's go!
```

**Example: The Ending**
```
[They all go out; the stage is empty. The sound of doors being locked one after another is heard, then the carriage driving away. A silence. In the silence, the dull sound of an axe striking a tree, mournful and sad.
[Footsteps. Firs appears at the right door. He is still in his tailcoat and white waistcoat, but he wears slippers. He is ill.
FIRS: (Goes to the left door, turns the handle.) Locked, they've all gone... (Sits on the sofa.) They've forgotten me... It doesn't matter... I'll sit here and wait. Leonid Andreyevich has probably forgotten his fur coat again; he must have gone in his light overcoat... (Sighs, worried.) I didn't see to that!... The young master! (Mutters something unintelligible.) Life passes so quickly, as though I had never lived a single day... (Lies down.) I'll lie down... There's no strength left in you at all! Everything is over, everything is over... Ah, you... you good-for-nothing!... (Lies motionless.)
[In the distance, as if from the sky, comes a sound like a snapping string, mournful and faint, and dies away. Silence again. The only thing that breaks the silence is the sound of an axe striking a tree in the distance.
—Curtain
```
