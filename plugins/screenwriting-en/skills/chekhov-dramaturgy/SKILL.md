# Chekhov Dramaturgy (契诃夫戏剧法)

> **Output language = the language of the question**; always anchor terminology back to the original terms — see [sw-workflow/terms.md](../sw-workflow/terms.md); do not coin your own translations.

This skill distills an act-by-act analysis of the four-volume Complete Plays of Chekhov (Shanghai Translation Publishing House; translations by Jiao Juyin, Li Jianwu, and Tong Daoming): 《没有父亲的人》 (The Fatherless One, Platonov), 《伊凡诺夫》 (Ivanov), 《林妖》 (The Wood Demon) and its rewrite 《万尼亚舅舅》 (Uncle Vanya), 《海鸥》 (The Seagull), 《三姊妹》 (Three Sisters), 《樱桃园》 (The Cherry Orchard), and the nine one-act plays, together with Chekhov's letters, the rehearsal notes of Stanislavsky and Nemirovich-Danchenko, and the criticism of Jiao Juyin and Tong Daoming.

Core creed (Chekhov's own words): **"Start strongly, end weakly. Break every rule of drama. Write it like a novel." "Neither an angel nor a devil."** Conflict is not between one person and another but between a group of people and the environment that surrounds them.

For the act-by-act structural tables and original-text examples for each play, see [reference.md](reference.md) in this directory.

## 0. The Boundaries of the Medium: Which Layers Can Be Moved and Which Cannot

This is a method for the **stage play**. When it is invoked for a film or series project, take it layer by layer:

| Layer | Can it be transferred to film/series? |
|---|---|
| **Structural layer** (no central climax, an even number of acts, events off-stage, the announced event that does not happen, the correct answer stated in Act One and then refused, the three-layer ending) | **Yes.** But "act" here is a theatrical unit: in film it becomes a sequence, in series a segment within an episode — **do not treat "four acts" as the four acts of a film or the four acts of a series** |
| **Character layer** (neither angel nor devil, conflict between a group and the environment, character = a composite of two or three real people) | **Yes**, directly usable |
| **Dialogue layer** (pauses, answers that miss the question, theme lines interrupted by daily life, the same speech spoken at the beginning and the end) | **Yes**, directly usable |
| **Stage directions and sound discipline** (one set of sourced ambient sounds per act, "—Curtain," "[Pause." on its own line) | **Do not copy the conventions.** The use of ambient sound travels; the written format is theatrical. For film and series formats see `sw-format-adaptation` |
| **One-act method (nine types)** | Valid only for stage one-acts and short films; not applicable to features or series |

**In one sentence**: transfer the pattern, not the unit, and not the conventions.

## I. The Seven Full-Length Plays: Comparison and Evolution

| Play | Year | Self-described genre | Time span | Central event (off-stage/on-stage) | Act ending | Final line |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| The Fatherless One | ~1880 | untitled early work | one summer | Sofya shoots and kills Platonov (on-stage) | event | "Give him three roubles!" "Life is a kopeck!" |
| Ivanov | 1887 | drama | Acts Three–Four a year apart | Ivanov shoots himself at his wedding (on-stage) | discovery / truth-telling / gunshot | "My old self has roused itself! (Pulls out a revolver.)" |
| The Wood Demon | 1889 | comedy | one summer + two weeks | Voynitsky shoots himself (off-stage); general reconciliation | courtship / "not allowed to play" / gunshot / applause | "This is wonderful! This is wonderful!" |
| The Seagull | 1896 | comedy | Acts Three–Four two years apart | Treplev shoots himself (off-stage, concealed by "the ether exploded") | confession / monologue / kiss / lie | "Find some way to get Irina away. Konstantin has just shot himself..." |
| Uncle Vanya | 1896 | Scenes from Country Life | late summer → autumn | Vanya fires twice and misses (on-stage); everything returns to normal | courtship / refusal / gunshot / accounts | "Ah, rest!" |
| Three Sisters | 1900 | drama | three and a half years (each act about a year apart) | the baron dies in a duel (off-stage, a distant shot) | proposal witnessed / off to Moscow / marry him / military band | "We long to understand!" "It makes no difference." |
| The Cherry Orchard | 1903 | comedy | May → October | the cherry orchard is auctioned (off-stage, the buyer announces it himself) | spring / moon / sold / axe | "You good-for-nothing!" + snapped string + axe |

**Evolution** (when writing, follow the later practice):
- Events move from on-stage to off-stage (gunshot → distant shot → axe).
- Act endings change from explosions to pauses ("the arrow on the bowstring" → "not allowed to play" → "he refuses" → "What? Well, then, let's go").
- The protagonist shifts from one person to a group.
- The genre label increasingly insists on "comedy."

Chekhov's self-assessment of Ivanov — "The plan was on the right track, but it was not written at all well" — every act ends with an event (a discovery, a truth-telling, a gunshot); from The Seagull onward he changed to weak endings.

## II. Ten Reusable Patterns at the Structural Layer

### S1 Four acts, an even number, no central climax
- The European tradition uses an odd number of acts (three or five) so that the climax can sit at the center; all of Chekhov's multi-act plays have four acts, the aim being "to make the dramatic events on stage ordinary and lifelike" (Tong Daoming).
- Jiao Juyin: Chekhov **does not start from the story when dividing acts but first looks for the right mood** — Act One: the irritating spring, dawn, the family reunion, the dream of an ideal; Act Two: idleness, empty talk, sentimentality, semi-hysteria, unrest and contradiction; Act Three: the desolate night, each person carrying their own melancholy, selfishness, the impossibility of spiritual communication; Act Four: collapse, despair, parting.
- **Instruction:** give each act an emotional keyword first, then decide which events go into that act. Put the events inside the mood, not the mood on top of the events.

### S2 Skip the big events between acts
- Two years pass between Acts Three and Four of The Seagull (Nina's ruin happens off-stage); a year in Ivanov (Anna's death); each act of Three Sisters is a year apart (marriage, childbirth, mortgaging).
- The beginning of an act catches up through gossip: "The professor means to stay till the end of the world"; "He hasn't paid the rent for eight months"; "Father has been dead a whole year now."
- **Instruction:** the largest change happens between acts; the first line of the act establishes the time scale (anniversary, date, name day).

### S3 Move the central event off-stage
- The gunshot is backstage (The Seagull, Three Sisters, The Wood Demon); the auction is in town; the duel is in the forest; the news of death is whispered or announced by the most indifferent person (Dorn, Chebutykin).
- A gun fired on-stage must miss (Vanya: "Ah, bang! Missed? Missed again?!").
- The Seagull: a shot → "It's nothing. Something must have exploded in my medicine chest" → the doctor goes out and comes back → hums → turns the pages of a magazine and talks about a letter from America → (in a low voice) "Find some way to get Irina away. Konstantin has just shot himself..." — **the most important event is concealed by one character with a lie; the truth is passed in a whisper to another character in the final line; the audience hears it, the mother does not.**
- Three Sisters: the duel is first avoided by everyone ("What happened yesterday near the theatre?" "Nothing much") → when it happens it is only "a faint shot in the distance," no one reacts, Masha goes on reciting poetry → finally the doctor whispers the announcement: "The baron has just been killed in the duel."
- **Instruction:** death is first avoided by everyone, then occurs as a single shot in the background, and is finally announced in words by the most indifferent person. If violence happens on-stage, it has no consequences; the scene afterward is the cleanup of the weapon (hiding the gun, begging for morphine).

### S4 The same setting / same speech at the beginning and end
- The nursery in The Cherry Orchard is full in Act One (windows shut, trees in blossom, everyone enters) and emptied in Act Four (curtains and picture frames taken down, furniture piled in a corner, everyone leaves) — the reduction of objects replaces narration.
- In Uncle Vanya the nurse's "Have some tea" and "Or maybe a little vodka" occur once at the beginning and once at the end; the only difference is that the protagonist accepts the vodka the second time.
- The Seagull's play-within-a-play monologue "Men, lions, eagles, and partridges" is spoken twice: in Act One, while Nina speaks, the audience discusses the smell of sulphur; in Act Four, after her ruin, she speaks it again and Treplev is silent — "a tragedy that one must have tasted tragedy to understand" (Jiao Juyin).
- **Instruction:** have the same text spoken once at the beginning and once at the end, with the character's entire ruin in between; or have the same room fill up and then empty.

### S5 The correct answer is stated in Act One and refused
- Lopakhin gives the answer in Act One — "lease the land for villas, twenty-five thousand" — repeats it in Act Two — "just one word: yes or no" — buys the estate in Act Three, and in Act Four says "the weather is just right for building"; Lyubov's response moves from "You don't understand this at all" to "How vulgar" to silence.
- Lebedev: "The ceiling is white, the boots are black, the sugar is sweet"; Sonya: "There is as much work as you want to do."
- **Instruction:** put the correct answer on stage early and repeat it once per act; the drama lies in why the characters cannot hear it.

### S6 Set off ensemble scenes with mechanical beats
- Card-game calls (The Seagull: "Twenty-two!" "Three!" "Eighty-one!" / Ivanov: "Two of spades" "Pass"), the abacus (Vanya: "Fifteen... twenty-five" / the anniversary: "One... zero... zero... three"), prayers and chorus (Tatyana Repina), naval commands (The Wedding: "Haul in the mainsheet, furl the topsail!"), quadrille calls (The Cherry Orchard), billiard terms (Gaev).
- **Instruction:** in the final scene or an ensemble scene, use mechanical counting as a metronome; let the major event (a gunshot, an entrance or exit, news of a death) fall between two numbers.

### S7 The announced event does not happen at the end
- The whole play talks about the match between Lopakhin and Varya; in Act Four the two are alone and talk only about luggage, the thermometer, and the weather: "I don't have a thermometer." "And the thermometer is broken too..." A voice calls from the yard, "I'm coming!" ("as though he had been waiting for just such a call"). Lyubov enters: "What?" [Pause. "Well, then, let's go!"
- Ivanov does not get married; in On the High Road the axe is raised and does not fall: "Did I kill her, or didn't I?"; in Uncle Vanya everything returns to normal.
- **Instruction:** the one event announced throughout the play does not happen at the end; handle it with a single "What?" and a pause. The ending of a tragic one-act is "it did not happen."

### S8 A three-layer ending: a monologue of hope + one line from a nihilist + a household action
- Sonya: "We shall rest" + the watchman's tapping, Telegin's guitar, the grandmother's annotations, the nurse's knitting.
- Three Sisters: "We shall live" "We long to understand" + Chebutykin reading the newspaper "It makes no difference, it makes no difference" + Kulygin fetching his hat, Andrei pushing the pram, the military band fading.
- Anya: "Hello, new life" + Firs: "You good-for-nothing" (his habitual curse for others, now aimed at himself) + the snapped string + the axe.
- **Instruction:** place three layers side by side at the end — the hopeful monologue of the young or the believer, one line from the nihilist, and a household action in the stage directions; give the last line to the least important character, the one everyone assumes has been taken care of.

### S9 A ball / banquet / birthday set against disaster
- A ball on the night of the auction (dancing, conjuring, recitation, borrowing money, interspersed with "the auction surely didn't go through" and "a passer-by said it was sold," until the buyer himself brings back "Sold." "Who bought it?" "I did."); a wedding turns into a debate (Ivanov); thirteen people at the table for a name day; a birthday breakfast announces "They have forgotten us."
- **Instruction:** schedule the festive occasion at the same time as the disaster; let the disaster enter as rumor and finally be brought back by the person involved; let the winner speak the loser's elegy (Lopakhin's monologue is at once ecstasy, shame, reproach, and exhaustion; he knocks over a candlestick: "I can pay for everything").

### S10 A One-Night Carousel (走马灯, zǒumǎdēng — a revolving lantern: one fixed place, one night, characters passing in turn)
- The Fatherless One, one night on the railway embankment: Osip → Sasha → Platonov → young Vengerovich → Anna (hoofbeats) → Triletsky climbing through the window → a drunk → Sasha at the window → Osip peeping → the letter delivered → Voynitsev drunk → a shot → Sasha lying down on the rails → the train.
- Uncle Vanya, a stormy night: every pair tries to come close once and fails; Three Sisters, the night of the fire = the family's night of confession, each person tells the truth between the screen and the firelight.
- **Instruction:** one fixed location, a whole night, characters passing in turn, each leaving behind a decision or a confession.

## III. Ten Reusable Patterns at the Character Layer

### C1 No angels, no devils; the conflict is between people and the environment
- There is no villain. Natasha's victory is written through the room-by-room change of ownership (the green sash corrected → she carries a candle to check the fire → "Irina's room will do nicely for the baby" → "I won't have you sitting in front of me" → "Cut down the fir trees" "Why is this fork lying about"), without a single malicious line.
- The professor's sin is being "a famous nonentity"; Lopakhin weeps when he buys the orchard.
- **Instruction:** write the villain's victory through commands over space and objects.

### C2 Each supporting character holds one simple explanation of the protagonist; the protagonist himself "does not know"
- Ivanov: Lvov = Tartuffe / a scoundrel; Zyuzyushka = married a Jewish woman for money; Kosykh = plays cards like a shoemaker; Sasha = a second Hamlet. Ivanov himself: "I don't understand you, you don't understand me, and we don't understand ourselves."
- Platonov is defined as "the best embodiment of modern uncertainty... the hero of a modern novel that has not yet been written"; Chebutykin: "I don't remember."
- **Instruction:** let every supporting character hold one simple explanation of the protagonist, while the protagonist's own explanation is "I don't know." The protagonist's action is only a response to visitors — send everyone to his door.

### C3 The righteous man as persecutor
- Lvov announces his own righteousness every time he enters, and is mocked by three people from three angles ("Make way for the honest man" "It is impolite to talk to women about your virtues" "Whatever violent thing you do, you always believe you are being honorable"); his monologue has a fixed structure: he misses his chance to speak → "this cursed weakness of mine."
- **Instruction:** write a morally irreproachable but repellent character; create the disgust through "he is never wrong."

### C4 One personal object and one eternal catchphrase per character
- Kosykh's cards ("Ace of diamonds, king, queen..." ×4), Solyony's bottle of perfume, Chebutykin's newspaper, Gaev's sweets and billiard jargon, Pishchik's "Well, just look at that!", Telegin's "How splendid!", Chubukov's "and what have you" (more than thirty times), Shipuchin's "as sure as my name is Shipuchin," Yepikhodov's "You see, don't you!", Firs's "You good-for-nothing," Sorin's "and so forth, and so on," Ferapont's "What?"
- The catchphrase is turned against the speaker at the end (Firs) or misquoted (Masha's oak tree: "A cat, green leaves all around... I've got it wrong"); the personal object changes meaning across four acts (a camera burned, a moustache shaved off and a false one put on, the mother's clock smashed).
- **Instruction:** give each character one personal object and one phrase unrelated to the situation, use it at the moment of greatest emotion; at the end, reverse the catchphrase or have it misquoted.

### C5 The lucid man lists himself and the protagonist as the same kind of failure
- Astrov: "There were only two decent, cultured people in this district, you and I, but in only ten years we have both sunk day by day into this damned vulgar life." "I am as dissatisfied with life as your Uncle Vanya."
- Dorn and Chebutykin are the same. The doctor's self-diagnosis: "When I am drunk I promise to perform the most difficult operations... and believe that I really am a great benefactor of mankind!"
- **Instruction:** let the most lucid character (often a doctor) list himself and the protagonist as the same kind of failure, and write the outsider's influence as the contagion of a disease ("idleness and slackness are contagious").

### C6 The marginal character speaks the theme
- Kuzma the beadle: "This afternoon we buried a gentleman, just now we came from a wedding, tomorrow morning we shall come for a name day. And so it goes on without end." The prompter, the nurse Marina ("Let the turkeys gobble and fight"), the deaf Ferapont, Firs.
- **Instruction:** let the theme be spoken by the person who sweeps, the person who cannot hear, the person who has been forgotten. Let the protagonist tell the truth only to someone who cannot hear (Andrei to the deaf man: "If you could really hear clearly, perhaps I wouldn't be telling you"), and let the listener answer with an absurd rumor ("A merchant in Moscow ate pancakes and died" "It was two hundred degrees below zero in Petersburg").

### C7 The person of unknown identity is in charge of performance
- Charlotta: "I have no passport, I don't know my real age... Who am I?" — eating a cucumber; conjuring, ventriloquism, carrying a bundle like a baby: "Give me another job." Shamrayev tells irrelevant old jokes precisely when others are most tense (before the departure: "The horses are harnessed... once... 'We've fallen for a wily chicken!'" — 中了奸鸡, zhòng le jiān jī, a pun on 中了奸计, "fell for a wicked scheme"). Sorin's "L'homme qui a voulu" ("the man who wanted to").
- **Instruction:** put a person of unknown identity in the ensemble and let performance replace confession; put in someone whose job is to tell inappropriate jokes.

### C8 The one-way love chain
- Medvedenko → Masha → Treplev → Nina → Trigorin ⇄ Arkadina; Sonya → Astrov → Yelena ← Vanya; Solyony → Irina ← Tuzenbakh. Not one pair is mutual.
- The Fatherless One: the same desire is summoned through four media (Sasha's food / Anna's horses / Sofya's telegram-like letter / Grekova's summons).
- **Instruction:** draw the love chain and make sure every arrow is one-way; the pursued person's denial is an admission ("So you don't dislike me after all; you are only afraid," and in the next scene she writes, "I have come back to life").

### C9 Character is a composite of real people + actor feedback
- Trigorin = Potapenko + Chekhov; Charlotta = the English governess next door; Yepikhodov "became exactly what Moskvin played"; Firs grew out of Artyom's fishing routine.
- The Cherry Orchard began with a single bodily action by an actor (fishing, splashing water in the bath) and gradually grew rooms, characters, and relationships; in the end Chekhov kept the atmosphere and catchphrases (the window and flowering branch, the old servant, the mistress who borrows money, the billiards fanatic, "I've put on the wrong trousers again") and discarded the gags (the one arm, the fishing, the bathhouse).
- **Instruction:** a character = a composite of two or three real people; allow the actor's improvisation to flow back into the text; start from a bodily action.

### C10 A character's dignity is part of the theme
- Chekhov objected to provincial productions that played Vanya as a down-at-heel landowner in tarred boots: "A landowner dresses better than you or I" "He wears a very fine tie" — "The point is not the tie but the main idea of the play: the gifted Astrov and the gentle Uncle Vanya sink into a remote backwater, while the professor, this fool, enjoys himself in Petersburg."
- **Instruction:** do not write ruin as slovenliness; give the idealist a pair of old galoshes (the "bald-headed gentleman" Trofimov falls down the stairs).

## IV. Twelve Reusable Patterns at the Dialogue Layer

### D1 The first line is the theme, immediately pressed down by trivia
- "Why do you always wear black?" "I am in mourning for my life. I am unhappy." → "I earn only twenty-three roubles a month." "Father has been dead a whole year now" → the clock strikes twelve. "How long have we known each other?" "Eleven years."
- **Instruction:** the first line is the character's overall judgment on their life; immediately press it down with money, a clock, or a number of years.

### D2 Answer the wrong question, shift half a step
- Jiao Juyin: "What A asks is A's own self, and what B answers is B's own self... any dialogue determined by logical form is not realistic drama." While everyone is reminiscing, Charlotta says, "My little dog eats walnuts"; when a serious question is being discussed, Gaev says, "Red into the middle pocket!"
- Take up only one word: Dorn talks about the crowd in Genoa and the world soul → "Where is Zarechnaya?" Fedya talks about the owl and is asked, "What are you talking about?" and answers only, "The owl."
- **Instruction:** the response always shifts half a step — catch one word from the previous line, not its meaning.

### D3 Philosophy followed by a physical need or a newspaper item
- Vershinin's long speech about "two or three hundred years from now" → "I am thirsty. I would very much like some tea"; Chebutykin inserts "Balzac was married in Berdichev"; the forest monologue → vodka: "In short... (Drinks.) I ought to go. Of course, none of this really matters."
- **Instruction:** after every stretch of philosophy, add a physical need or a newspaper item; let a character's belief be relayed by an admirer, then half-spoken by the character, interrupted by an object, and immediately belittled by the character himself.

### D4 The deepest pain uses the fewest words or the most irrelevant ones
- "When did he say that?" (Anna, hearing "You are going to die"); "I don't have a thermometer"; "The owl"; "A wife is just a wife" (妻子不过是妻子罢了, qīzi bùguò shì qīzi bàle — nine characters, replacing two pages of monologue); "Madam, just a madam!"
- Jiao Juyin: "The great majority of people, when expressing the most painful and unbearable feelings, often say the fewest words, or even something entirely irrelevant." Chekhov to an actor: "The broad masses... when they are in too much pain, only whistle."
- **Instruction:** the injured person's reaction is a technical question; in a tragic scene, let one person whistle.

### D5 A long monologue talks only about professional obsession or a woman from the past
- Trigorin's monologue is entirely the autobiography of a writing machine (clouds like a grand piano, the money plant classified as "a flower admired by widows," "not as good as Turgenev"), and love appears only at the end in the form of "taking notes"; Svetlovidov: "Throw away the stage!"; Astrov's three maps; Vanya: "Ten years ago she was seventeen and I was thirty-seven."
- **Instruction:** let a long monologue talk about professional obsession, not love; turn the theme into a visible prop (a map / a chart), let the character explain it, and let the listener be uninterested ("I can see from your face that this doesn't interest you in the least").

### D6 The listener to a confession refuses to listen
- Masha: "I love Vershinin" → Olga goes behind the screen: "I won't listen, whatever you say," and Masha finishes the confession as usual; Lyubov covers her ears; Vanya: "Leave me in peace"; Anna: "Pray for me."
- **Instruction:** the listener to a confession hides and refuses to hear, and the confession is completed as usual.

### D7 When embarrassment peaks, talk about the weather
- Vanya, holding roses, catches the doctor kissing Yelena; the doctor: "The weather is lovely today. It was a little overcast this morning... To tell the truth, the autumn weather has been splendid this year... only the days are getting shorter... (Exits.)"; Lopakhin: "Three degrees below freezing"; the tramp: "Lovely weather"; "Just the weather for hanging yourself."
- **Instruction:** after a discovery, talk about the weather; the last weather line ("the days are getting shorter") carries the real meaning; the person who makes the discovery is holding a gift meant for the person discovered.

### D8 A quarrel begins with the gentlest action, escalates into identity labels, and ends in shared tears
- The Seagull, mother and son: changing the bandage (childhood memory) → "You don't understand him" → "Jealousy!" → "Decadent!" "Miser!" "Ragamuffin!" "Good-for-nothing!" → he weeps silently → she weeps too → "Forgive your bad mother."
- The Bear: "Bear! Wild bear! Bourbon!" → duel → "First you must teach me how to shoot" → kiss.
- **Instruction:** begin the quarrel with the gentlest action, escalate to mutual identity labels, and land on simultaneous tears or a kiss; complete the reconciliation with a shared object (drinking from the same glass), then have the two speak opposite lines and repeat them ("How happy I am!" "How unhappy I am.").

### D9 Quotation replaces confession
- Shakespearean fragments (Swan Song uses King Lear, Hamlet, and Othello to prove he has genius, then "at best I am fit to be Fortinbras's follower"), Pushkin's oak tree (Masha), Lermontov (Solyony), Hamlet ("Hamlet is afraid of dreams; I am afraid of life"; Lopakhin's "Ophelia, go to a nunnery"), Latin (Kulygin's ut consecutivum, the professor's manet omnes una nox).
- **Instruction:** let characters name each other after classical roles, and let the named person refuse; use quotation to replace confession, and immediately deny it after proving oneself.

### D10 A cold aside after a passionate declaration
- Arkadina kneels and clings to Trigorin's knees: "You are Russia's only hope" → he surrenders: "I have no will of my own" → she (aside): "Now I've got him" → casually: "This, if you like, you may stay."
- Yasha "pretends he has just been bathing in the river."
- **Instruction:** immediately after a passionate declaration, give a cold aside that reveals the performance.

### D11 The last line of a farewell is a household request
- Tuzenbakh going to the duel: "Say something to me" "What should I say?" "Anything" → they talk about the tree: "This tree is dead, but it still sways in the wind like the others" → "I haven't had my coffee yet today. Tell them to get me some."
- Platonov before dying: "Give him three roubles!" (to pay for Marko's tea); Popova: "Toby won't get his buckwheat today" (the signal of changed attitude at the beginning and end); "Fetch my coat."
- **Instruction:** at a farewell, the character asks the other to speak and the other cannot; the departing person's last line is a household request or a small debt; leave the debt between the protagonist and the most marginal character to be paid in the last line.

### D12 Whistles, pauses, half-swallowed sentences
- "(Whistles)" is Masha's language; "[Pause." is the most frequent stage direction ("The closer you come to life, the more you understand that in the reality of life the deepest and most powerful thing is a pause" — Stanislavsky); "If she knew... never mind... I'll tell you later"; at the door Sonya takes back her consent: "No, it's better to leave it unclear..." "What did you say?" "Nothing, nothing. (Exits.)"
- Lovers whisper in meaningless syllables: "Lom—ton—ton!" "Ton—ton!" "La—ta—ta?" "La—ta—ta!"
- **Instruction:** before an exit, take back at the door the consent just given; the other person does not hear clearly, and fate proceeds as before; the betrayed husband expresses what he knows through repeated self-affirmation (Kulygin: "I am content, I am content, I am content").

## V. Stage Directions and Sound Discipline

- Write only what can be seen and heard, not psychology. Jiao Juyin: "If we delete one or two words from the stage directions in Chekhov's plays, several of his characters die."
- **One set of sourced ambient sounds per act**: the watchman's clapper, an owl, an accordion, a cello, a guitar, a waltz, a Jewish band, a fire bell, a military band, a snapping string, a door lock, horse bells, an axe. In the final act only two remain (a lock, an axe).
- An unexplained sound appears twice: the first time everyone explains it ("The bucket broke in the mine" "A heron" "An owl" "It was just like this before the great disaster"), the second time no one is present.
- Time is marked by clocks and train schedules: Lopakhin looks at his watch more than five times ("The four-thirty train" "The twenty-second of August" "Only forty-six minutes till the train"); "Your clock is seven minutes fast"; the doctor's "The first, second, and fifth batteries will start at exactly one."
- Props carry the theme: three maps (fifty years ago / twenty-five years ago / now — "a chart of degeneration"), the map of Africa (the stage direction notes it is "obviously of no use," and it is discussed at the heaviest moment: "Isn't it still terribly hot in Africa at this time of year?"), the stuffed seagull ("I don't remember!"), the hundred-year-old cupboard (Gaev makes a speech to the furniture that should have been made to people), the smashed clock, the mother's photograph, the gold locket, the bunch of keys (Varya throws it on the floor).
- Anti-sound-effect: "I must write another play. It will begin like this: 'How wonderful, how quiet! No birds, no dogs, no cuckoos...'"
- Format (Jiao's translation): character name + space + line; action in parentheses; a standalone direction begins with "["; a pause stands on its own line, "[Pause."; the end of an act is "—Curtain."

## VI. The One-Act Method (Nine Types)

| Type | Single action | Ending | Technique |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tragic type, 《大路上》 (*On the High Road*) | A ruined landowner begs for vodka in a roadside inn on a rainy night | The tramp raises his axe and does not strike: "Did I kill her, or didn't I?" | The pledged objects descend in value (a cap, "it's a sieve" → a coat, "you're naked" → a gold locket, "don't let your fingers touch that face"); a third party (the coachman) tells the protagonist's story; coincidence (the carriage spring breaks, the wife enters the inn) |
| Monologue type, 《论烟草有害》 (*On the Harmful Effects of Tobacco*) | A henpecked husband is ordered to give a lecture | The wife appears from behind the stage and he resumes the lecture tone | The public task is constantly interrupted by private life (the thirteenth, seven daughters, a boarding-school advertisement, a pamphlet for sixty kopecks); 1886→1902 the same line "thirty-three years is the best age" turns from truth to a reluctant lie; he takes off his dress coat, stamps on it, and puts it back on |
| Tragic type, 《天鹅之歌》 (*Swan Song*) | An old actor wakes at night in an empty theatre | He reads Shakespeare with the prompter: "I am not a genius... let's go" | The empty theatre = a grave; "I have no home" ×3; quotation replaces monologue, and after proving himself he denies it; two people, one stage |
| Farce, 《熊》 (*The Bear*) | Debt collection → duel → proposal | "Toby won't get his buckwheat today" | Three-part structure; the chair is clutched and torn twice; misogyny and misandry mirror each other with statistics; the servant bursts in with an axe and a rake and sees the kiss |
| Farce, 《求婚》 (*The Proposal*) | Proposal → quarrel over the meadow → quarrel over the dog | "Good!" "Bad!" "Champagne!" | The proposal is interrupted three times; physical symptoms escalate (heartbeat, numb left foot, twitching eyebrow, seeing stars) as beats; catchphrase counting; the subject of the quarrel has nothing to do with its purpose |
| Tragic type, 《塔杰雅娜·雷宾娜》 (*Tatyana Repina*) | An entire church wedding on stage | The woman in black takes poison: "They must all take poison!" | A public ritual is transferred to the stage intact; private collapse can only happen in the cracks; vocal layers: prayers / chorus / crowd / best man / "a moan" ×7; the theme is spoken by the beadle; the one who takes poison is a bystander |
| Farce, 《一位做不了主的悲剧人物》 (*A Tragic Role*, or *The Tragedian in Spite of Himself*) | A husband at his dacha borrows a revolver | "I want blood! Blood!" | A list becomes dialogue (a lampshade, a pound of pork, castor oil, twenty bottles of beer, a belly band No. 82); the sympathizer immediately adds more (to bring a sewing machine and a canary cage) |
| Farce, 《结婚》 (*The Wedding*) | A hired "general" at a wedding feast talks about seamanship | The best man: "On such a festive occasion..." interrupted three times | Ensemble = each person has a self-absorbed vocabulary (naval commands / telegraph tapping / the Greek's "everything is here"); the hired dignitary discovers the truth: "Where is the door?" |
| Farce, 《周年纪念》 (*The Anniversary*) | A bank director rushing a report is besieged by his wife and an old lady | While the delegate reads the panegyric "to be or not to be," the two women moan | Three unrelated lines stacked in one room; mistaken identity (the bookkeeper drives out the wife thinking she is the old lady); two people faint at the same time; the panegyric and the misery are juxtaposed |

Across all nine: a single space in real time; one object runs through (the locket, buckwheat, the meadow/dog, the crown, the sewing machine, the abacus); physical symptoms as beats; catchphrase counting; the three-part farce structure (task → digressive quarrel → physical collapse → accidental reconciliation or explosion); the tragic ending is "it did not happen"; ensemble scenes use ritual/counting as a skeleton; the marginal character speaks the theme.

## VII. "Making the Bad Good": The Wood Demon → Uncle Vanya

| Change | The Wood Demon (1889) | Uncle Vanya (1896) | Principle |
|---|---|---|---|
| Characters | The Zheltukhin brother and sister, the Orlovsky father and son | Cut | Cut characters who exist only to pair off |
| Central event | Voynitsky kills himself at the end of Act Three | Two shots miss: "Ah, bang! Missed?" | Violence has no consequences |
| Ending | Three couples pair off and one couple reunites; the characters hide and peep and applaud "Bravo! Bravo!" | Everything returns to normal; Vanya goes back to the ledger: "February the second, oil, twenty pounds..." | Do not dissolve the tragedy of people and environment too easily |
| Forest monologue | Delivered in full through "help God create the world" | Sonya relays it first, the doctor gets halfway before vodka interrupts him, and he belittles it: "perhaps none of this matters much" | Belief is interrupted |
| The professor | Complains, sells the estate | Adds "He has not left a single page of writing, he is a zero! A soap bubble!" | Strengthen "youth sacrificed to an idol for nothing" |
| The doctor | In love with Sonya, and his love is finally returned | Knows Sonya cares but does not respond: "I can only make her understand that I cannot love her" | One-way love chain |
| Act ending | "Not allowed to play!" | "He refuses." | The small wish that is vetoed is relayed by a third person — retained as is |

Other rewrite cases:
- The Seagull's Act One ending was originally a "shock ending" (while Masha and Dorn are alone, it is suddenly revealed that Masha is the doctor's daughter, and no later act mentions it). Chekhov argued, "The audience likes to see the arrow on the bowstring at the end of each act"; Nemirovich-Danchenko: "Quite right, but such an ending must be placed at the back, not in the middle!" It was changed to Masha's confession "I love Konstantin." **The suspense at an act ending must have a follow-up; an explosion with no follow-up must be cut.**
- In Three Sisters, Andrei's two-page monologue to Ferapont was cut to nine characters: "A wife is just a wife!"
- A director suggested cutting an entire scene at the end of Act Two of The Cherry Orchard; "he turned pale, thought for a moment... 'Then cut it!'"
- The sound shift in the title: not the profitable "cherry orchard" (вишневый, vishnevy) but the self-regarding "cherry orchard" (вишнёвый, vishnyóvy) — one sound in the title changes the whole meaning.

## IX. Workflow: Writing a Chekhovian Script

1. **First fix the theme (the pulse of the age), then grow the characters, and only then divide into acts** (Jiao Juyin: Chekhov used thick notebooks to collect material for his characters, and did not divide into acts until there was enough for a character).
2. **Give each of the four acts an emotional keyword** (reunion/longing → idleness/unrest → desolate night/each with their own melancholy → collapse/parting), and fix each act's season, time of day, and ambient sound.
3. **Fix the central event and move it out of the frame**; decide who conceals it with a lie and who finally whispers the announcement.
4. **State the correct answer in Act One** and have the characters refuse it; repeat it once per act.
5. **Draw the one-way love chain**; give each character one personal object, one catchphrase, and one real-person prototype.
6. **Arrange a righteous persecutor, a lucid doctor, a performer of unknown identity, a man who tells the wrong joke, and a deaf man or servant who speaks the theme.**
7. **End each act with a pause, a refusal ("not allowed to play," "he refuses"), or a monologue**, not an event; if you use "the arrow on the bowstring," you must catch it in a later act.
8. **Write a one-night carousel** (a stormy night / the night of a fire / a night on the railway) as Act Two or Three.
9. **Set the festive scene against disaster**: a ball / wedding / name day at the same time as the auction / news of death.
10. **Announce one thing and let it not happen at the end** (a proposal, a wedding, the axe falling).
11. **A three-layer ending**: hope monologue + one line from a nihilist + a household action; give the last line to the forgotten person; reverse the catchphrase.
12. **Dialogue pass**: the first line is the theme pressed down by trivia; responses shift half a step; philosophy is followed by tea or a newspaper; the most painful line is the shortest; the listener to a confession refuses to hear; after a discovery, talk about the weather; a quarrel goes from a gentle action to labels to shared tears; the last line of a farewell is a household request.
13. **Cut**: two pages of monologue can be nine characters; an entire scene can be cut; one sound in the title can change the meaning.
14. **Insist that it is a comedy.**

## X. The "Chekhov-ize" Checklist

1. Is the number of acts even, and does each act have a mood keyword?
2. Is the largest event off-stage? Does the gun fired on-stage miss?
3. Is the act ending a pause / refusal / monologue rather than an explosion?
4. Does each character have one personal object and one catchphrase? Is the catchphrase reversed at the end?
5. Is there a "righteous persecutor," or at least no villain?
6. Is the correct answer stated in Act One and refused?
7. Does the dialogue shift half a step? Is philosophy followed by tea / vodka / a newspaper?
8. Is the most painful line the shortest? Does someone whistle?
9. Does each act's ambient sound have a source? Are only two left in the final act?
10. Does the one event announced throughout the play fail to happen at the end?
11. Is the ending "hope monologue + one line from a nihilist + a household action"?
12. Do you insist that it is a comedy?
