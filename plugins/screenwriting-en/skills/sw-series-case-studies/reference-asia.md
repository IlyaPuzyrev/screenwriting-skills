## IX. 坂元裕二 (Sakamoto Yuji), 《花束般的恋爱》 (We Made a Beautiful Bouquet): Scene Statistics and a Five-Year Timeline

> **Medium note: this is a feature screenplay** (released January 29, 2021; director 土井裕泰 (Doi Nobuhiro); starring 菅田将晖 (Suda Masaki) and 有村架纯 (Arimura Kasumi); translated by 蕾克 (Lei Ke); Guangxi Normal University Press · Yīyè folio, December 2022 edition; 35,000 characters of main text). **There are no episodes, no acts, and no act outs**, so it cannot serve as a model for series structure. It is included here because its author is one of Japan's most important television-series writers — 《东京爱情故事》 (Tokyo Love Story), 《Mother》, 《最高の離婚》 (The Best Divorce), 《カルテット》 (Quartet), 《大豆田とわ子と三人の元夫》 (My Dear Exes), 《anone》, and 《初恋的恶魔》 (The Devil of First Love) — and because this is his most concentrated, most dissectable sample of a "dialogue-driven relationship drama." The "invisible three acts" below are **an inference reverse-engineered from length allocation**.

Format: scene number (1–159) + scene heading (location / time / "flashback") + action lines + dialogue. The years are divided into chapters by title cards reading "2015" (handwritten text), with 7 title-card scenes in all: 1, 3, 76, 92, 112, 140, and 153. Monologues are written as `绢（独白）` / `麦（独白）` — that is, `Kinu (monologue)` / `Mugi (monologue)` (voice-over). `*` is an in-scene section divider.

### 9.1 Total Volume and Long-Scene Ranking

**Totals**: 159 scenes; **1,822** effective lines of main text (action lines and dialogue, excluding blank lines); 35,000 characters; roughly 124 minutes on screen — **an average of 11.5 lines per scene**. **Short scenes dominate: 72 scenes (45%) have ≤5 lines of main text**; only 2 scenes in the whole film exceed 100 lines.

Conclusion: **all the weight rests on four or five long scenes (meeting, confession, first quarrel, big fight, breakup); the other hundred-odd scenes are 3–10-line "diary entries"**; the gaps between long scenes grow wider as the film proceeds (28→52→103→121→147).

### 9.4 Three Audio Tracks and Location Reuse

- `X (monologue)` = the voice-over diary track, **151 entries** in the film (Kinu 77 / Mugi 74).
- `X's voice` = a real off-screen sound in the same scene (lovemaking under the covers, a phone call shown to only one party, a replay in a dream).
- **Sound-bridge cross-cutting**: scenes 133–137, the two protagonists' respective "advisors" (Nana, Kaji) interweave by voice, and five short scenes weave a double piece of advice.

| Location | Appearances | Meaning reversal from first to last |
|---|---|---|
| Tama River apartment · their room | **25** | 66 balcony "Wow" → 139 dawn "an unspeakable loneliness and emptiness" → 150 living together after the breakup → 152 taking down the curtains |
| Chofu apartment · Mugi's room | 13 | 15 alone at the kotatsu → 34 grilled rice balls → 54 three nights in a row → 65 "Let's live together" |

`*` dividers total **32, across 17 scenes**; scene 28 uses 7 of them to split one night into 8 sections. **Dialogue budget**: the two protagonists account for **83%** of all dialogue (Mugi 295 / Kinu 266); the supporting characters with the most dialogue in the film are **the "mirror couple" who appear only in the final scene (36 lines)**, seven times as many as the "romantic rival" Kaji (5 lines).

### 9.5 Examples (from the Chinese Translation)

**Example 1 (scene 2): parallel dialogue — two tables of people saying one passage together**

```
Mugi: Love can't be split half and half.
Kinu: Love is one for each person.
Mugi: One for each. Those two really don't get it. Maybe I should go over and teach them.
  Mugi and Kinu stand up at the same time.
  They notice each other, their eyes meet, and in the end they sit back down.
Akane: Never mind them. Mugi, did I leave my earring at your place last night? It might be in your bed.
Mugi: I didn't see it... (a little distracted)
Tomoki: Kinu, my parents are really a pain. They keep wanting me to bring you over to meet them. What am I supposed to do?
Kinu: Mm, yeah, I wonder... (a little distracted)
  Kinu and Mugi sit facing their respective companions, but their hearts have flown elsewhere.
```

Note: The two characters pick up each other's sentences and continue them; the new partners' lines are all "practical matters" (an earring, meeting the parents), which sets off the two protagonists' "cultural matters."

**Example 2 (scenes 47–52, excerpt): alternating countdown dual monologues**

```
Mugi (V.O.): Someone once said that if you've eaten three meals together and still haven't confessed, you can only be ordinary friends.
Kinu (V.O.): I'm starting to get anxious.
...
Kinu (V.O.): Eight hours left until the last train.
Mugi (V.O.): From now on I have to change the mood.
...
Kinu (V.O.): Why does the conversation keep drifting further away?
Mugi (V.O.): Three hours until the last train.
...
Mugi (V.O.): No, no! It's drifting further and further.
Kinu (V.O.): Two hours left.
```

Note: The two monologue tracks share a single clock; the suspense comes from "both are counting down, and neither knows the other is counting down."

**Example 3 (scene 28): a list of nouns as a heartbeat**

```
  They each take a paperback from their bag and shyly exchange them.
Kinu: (opening Mugi's paperback) I've read almost all of Homura Hiroshi's books.
Mugi: (opening Kinu's paperback) I've read almost all of Nagashima Yū's books too. I had no money, so I always waited for the paperback edition.
Kinu: Me too. Or I borrowed them from the library.
Mugi: Which other writers do you like?
Kinu: My taste is pretty ordinary. Ishii Shinji, Horie Toshiyuki, Shibasaki Tomoka, Oyamada Hiroko, Imamura Natsuko, Enjō Tō.
    And of course Ogawa Yōko, Tawada Yōko, Maijō Ōtarō, Satō Aki.
  Mugi listens and nods. Suddenly he notices a used movie-ticket stub tucked into Kinu's paperback.
  The movie is 《自由之丘》 (Hill of Freedom).
Mugi: Ah, Hachiya-san, you...
  The ticket stub Kinu finds is for 《穿裘皮的维纳斯》 (Venus in Fur).
Kinu: Yamane-san, so you're a ticket-stub bookmark person too.
Mugi: Yeah. I'm a ticket-stub bookmark person.
```

Note: The script explains none of the names; "my taste is pretty ordinary" followed by ten writers is both self-mockery and self-definition; it lands finally on an object (the ticket stub), which is more powerful than the list.

**Example 4 (scenes 13 → 31 → 150): the same line, three years, three meanings**

```
[January 2015, scene 13]
Mugi (V.O.): Rock beats scissors, scissors beats paper — I understand those two. But why does paper beat rock? Paper wrapped around rock would obviously tear.
[Same month, scene 31]
Kinu: How could paper possibly beat rock? It would obviously tear.
Mugi (V.O.): Now I know there is one person who thinks the same way.
Mugi: (delighted) You're right, too!
[May 2019, after the breakup, scene 150]
  Kinu and Mugi play rock-paper-scissors. Kinu throws rock; Mugi throws paper.
Kinu: Why did you throw paper!
Mugi: (smiling) Because I'm a mature adult.
```

Note: The first time is loneliness, the second is harmony, the third is "I have accepted the rules" — without any explanation, the audience completes the meaning itself. A similar device is "returning the original line": in 2015, scene 65, Mugi curses out Kinu's interviewer for her — "Even if he's high up, a man like that wouldn't be moved even after reading Imamura Natsuko's 《野餐》 (Picnic)"; in 2018, scene 121, Kinu says the same line back to Mugi, word for word, and Mugi can only answer, "I don't feel anything anymore."

**Example 5 (scene 147): the debate at the heart of the breakup — "lowering the floor"**

```
Kinu: You only think that because today was a happy day. Things will soon go back to the way they were.
Mugi: It's fine if they go back to the way they were.
  Kinu shakes her head.
Mugi: Isn't that what couples in the world are like? Even if the feeling of love disappears... (realizes he has said the wrong thing)
Kinu: ...
Mugi: (but he keeps going) A lot of people still stay married, right? ... Kinu and I could...
Kinu: You still want to lower the floor?
Mugi: ...
Kinu: Constantly lowering the floor of your feelings, living together, thinking everything is just like that — do you really think that's good?
Mugi: Yes.
Kinu: Really?
Mugi: If my feelings cool, doesn't that just show that I'm ready to enter a stable married life?
```

Note: The longest scene in the film (162 lines) has only one argument — whether or not to lower the floor of feeling; neither of them apologizes, and there is no reconciliation scene. In the same scene, the dialogue of the 21-year-old couple at the next table (Rin and Wataru) interrupts as a mirror: "We finally meet. / We finally meet." — "Kinu and Mugi across from them watch a past they can never return to."

**Example 6 (scenes 11 → 149): the closing verdict completed by the other**

```
[January 2015, scene 11, Kinu's V.O.]
Kinu (V.O.): In the 2014 World Cup, the host Brazil lost to Germany and was scored on seven times...
        No matter how bad things get for me, I'm still better off than the Brazilians were then. Yeah, I'm much happier, much happier.
[February 2019, the night of the breakup, scene 149]
Kinu: I always think that no matter how bad things get, they can't be worse than Brazil then.
Mugi: Ah... do you know what Brazil's captain, Júlio César, said in an interview after the loss?
Kinu: Ah, no.
Mugi: After the unprecedented defeat, César said in the interview: "The journey we have traveled so far has been wonderful; we were only one step away."
  The two of them savor the line, start walking again, and a quiet smile appears on their faces.
```

Note: The heroine's private consolation gets its second half from the hero four years later; the film's judgment on the relationship is delivered by a single quotation, and the characters do not say it themselves.

**Example 7 (scenes 155, 159): the ending — an invisible synchronization**

```
[Scene 155, outside the café]
  Kinu and Tomoki come out of the café. Mugi and Akane. They walk in opposite directions.
  Kinu quietly raises her hand, puts it behind her back, and waves.
  Mugi quietly raises his hand, puts it behind his back, and waves.
  Neither sees the other's gesture; they just keep waving and walk away.
[Scene 159, Waseda apartment · Mugi's room]
Mugi (V.O.): ...Because I wanted to eat the fried-noodle bread there, I searched for it, and after six years, the miracle happened again.
  On the computer screen is Street View. Near the Tama River apartment, on an ordinary residential street,
  a man and a woman are so clearly visible, holding a bouquet and a roll of toilet paper.
  Their faces are blurred, but you can tell it is Kinu and Mugi.
  The two of them standing side by side gradually turn into Mugi's illustration.
  The End
```

Note: The ending gives no dialogue at all; two gestures "only the audience can see" (waving behind the back, the Street View photo) complete the emotional settlement; the title object (the bouquet) appears only at the very last moment.

**The turning points are not events but considerate actions** (the author's departure from convention): scene 85, "while blow-drying her hair, he says he's thinking of looking for a job"; scene 107, "he puts on his earphones and says, play with the sound turned up." The film has **no antagonist and no third party who ever becomes involved** (the rival Kaji has only 5 lines; the affair never happens); all the pressure comes from each of their good intentions; **death is not a turning point** (Kaito dies in scene 125; the decision to break up happens at a **wedding**, scene 142); **neither of the two quarrels has an apology scene**.

## X. 卢熙京 (Noh Hee-kyung), 《世间最美丽的离别》 (The Most Beautiful Goodbye): Seven-Chapter Structure Table, Four-Episode Mapping (Inferred), and Family Conflict Web

> **Note on the text's identity**: the text at hand is a **novelization** of the eponymous drama by the Korean television writer 卢熙京 (Noh Hee-kyung, 노희경), whose credits include 《没关系，是爱情啊》 (It's Okay, That's Love) and 《他们生活的世界》 (Worlds Within); adapted by 李圣淑 (Lee Seong-suk), translated by 刘悦 (Liu Yue), CITIC Press, April 2016 edition, 244 pages of main text; copyright page "Copyright 2010 by NOH HEE KYONG". **It is not a script**: no scene headings, no S# numbers, no action lines; it is a third-person omniscient novel. **The book never mentions the broadcaster, the year, or the episode count**, so any "corresponds to which episodes" is inference.

### 10.2 The Chapter-Opening Epigraph Device (a Cold-Open Method Ready to Reuse)

Before each chapter's main text there is a full page **printed with dialogue only, no narration** (p0008 / p0045 / p0079 / p0111 / p0139 / p0173 / p0219); every epigraph is **a passage of dialogue that will appear in full later in that chapter**.

| Chapter | Epigraph page | Epigraph (excerpt) | Scene quoted |
|---|---|---|---|
| One | p0008 | "I want to go home with you after I'm done today" "Why?" "This urinary trouble is always bad..." "Just take some medicine. Why do you have to come to the hospital?" | p0014 |
| Two | p0045 | "They can't operate anymore." "Why?" "It's too late." "Is that something a doctor should say, you brat! Do the surgery. ... She hasn't said it hurts yet." "It hurts; she's just enduring it. It clearly hurts." | p0066–p0068 |
| Three | p0079 | "So, where is the problem?" "Everywhere." "She isn't even uncomfortable, so how can everything be a problem?" "It's uterine cancer." ... "Doesn't it hurt?" "So what if it hurts — will you hurt for me?" | p0092–p0094 |
| Four | p0111 | "Yeon-su, your mother doesn't have much time left. She's going to die." "What are you saying? She already had the surgery — why would she die?" "...The surgery wasn't done." "Then how long can she live?" | p0127–p0128 |
| Five | p0139 | "Father, I can't just give up on Mother. ... I want to do my duty as a child at least once. ... Father, I'm begging you..." | p0165 |
| Six | p0173 | "Mother, die together with me! How will you live after I die? ..." "I shouldn't say this, but... Mother, when you're lucid, even if you have to bite your tongue, come to me." | p0215 / p0218 |
| Seven | p0219 | "Jeong-su, you... even if you forget me, forget Mother's face... don't forget that Mother gave birth to you." "Yeon-su, Mother loves you, you know? You are my..." "You, come to me quickly. Don't leave me alone and bored. Honey, if you think I'm pretty, kiss me." "In-hee, thank you, truly." | p0230–p0238 |

The pattern: ① The epigraph is **always dialogue, always two or more people, always a refusal or an announcement**; not one is lyrical. ② It gives the chapter's **value statement**, not a plot hook — the audience already knows the ending, and the tension comes from "how this line will be said." ③ Chapter Seven's epigraph is **four farewells to different people in parallel**, which amounts to announcing the four-scene structure of the final episode in advance. ④ Converted into a series device: in the first 30 seconds of each episode, show only a passage of dialogue from the later part of that episode (white text on black or a flash-forward), with no image.

Time and season markers (usable directly as an episode clock): the opening is **November** (p0057, "Although it's already November"); the goal of the whole drama is "**to move into the new house in Ilsan before the harsh winter arrives**" (p0019); the in-story time from diagnosis to death is about **five to six weeks** (the announcement "one month... two months" p0128 → discharge → **first snow** p0170, "the first snow, a little later than usual" → death on a sunny morning p0239, "bright sunlight filled the bedroom"). One more pivot: **the patient learns the truth without being told** — p0170–p0172 Mother infers the conclusion herself from **vomiting blood and the mold on the soy crock** (folk omens).

**Three things that must move during the mapping (still inference)**: ① **Chapter Six is too long, so one or two units must be pushed into EP3** — move "Father finds a job at the public health center" (p0194–p0196) and "father and daughter arrange the new home" (p0198–p0202) to the end of EP3, leaving EP4 with nothing but "goodbye"; ② **Grandmother's "nailing the door" and "smothering" scenes must be separated into two episodes** (both in Chapter Six, p0206–p0209 / p0212–p0215); in the same episode they cancel each other out — put the door-nailing at the end of EP3 (the peak of Father's loss of control), the smothering at the start of EP4 (the peak of Mother's transgression); ③ **Yeon-su's affair line must end in EP3**, otherwise any desire of her own in EP4 will dilute the goodbye (this is exactly how the book handles it, breaking up at p0168–p0170).

### 10.5 Family Conflict Web

The configuration method in one sentence: **give every family member a trouble that has nothing to do with "Mother's illness" and is already burning; then let the illness set all six of them alight at the same time.**

At the start, the six people each have an independent predicament, **not one of which is caused by Mother's illness**: Father is at odds with the young hospital director and about to retire (p0013); Yeon-su has reunited with a married man (p0020); Jeong-su is waiting for his university admission results after three years of retaking the exam (p0012); Geun-deok has gambled away the money for a car and beats his wife (p0033, p0056); Grandmother has dementia and a stroke (p0011); Mother herself just wants to move into the Ilsan house before winter (p0019). **The illness's function is not to create conflict but to push every existing conflict to the point where it must be resolved**: Father is fired (p0141), Yeon-su breaks up (p0168), Jeong-su makes an impossible wish (p0163), Geun-deok starts driving a taxi (p0217), Grandmother is nailed into her room and then smothered (p0206, p0212), Mother turns from the one cared for into the one presiding (p0176–p0188).

### 10.6 The Allocation Method of "Goodbye" (Chapter Seven, p0216–p0239)

| Order | Recipient | Form | Length | Core action / prop |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Grandmother** (moved up to the end of Chapter Six, p0216–p0218) | In the bathroom, door locked, the family knocking outside | Long | Washing her mother-in-law's body with her own hands, changing her into new clothes; "Mother, I'm going one step ahead; you come quickly too." |
| 2 | Grandmother (again, p0225–p0226) | Holding hands before leaving the house, almost no dialogue | Very short | "I'm going now." "Yes... go." — the two women look at each other, "thirty years of grudges and affection slowly vanishing like soap bubbles" |
| 3 | Sister-in-law (p0228) | At the door, entrusting | Very short | The bag of walnut cakes; "Take good care of the old lady." |
| 4 | **Jeong-su** (p0230–p0231) | In the car, back seat | Medium | The ring; "Even if you forget Mother's face... don't forget that Mother gave birth to you." |
| 5 | **Yeon-su** (p0232) | In the car, embracing the driver's seat from behind | Medium | A kiss on the cheek; "You... are me; Mother is Yeon-su." |
| 6 | Geun-deok (**absent**, p0227) | **No meeting**; he sobs over the steering wheel at the mouth of the alley | Short | Walnut cakes; "Sister... my In-hee sister... goodbye..." |
| 7 | **Husband** (p0233–p0238) | One night in the new home: porridge → bath → bed | **The longest** | The gold-leaf coffee cup; the list answering "Will you miss me?" "Everything."; "If you think I'm pretty, kiss me"; "In-hee, thank you, truly" |

The five rules of the allocation method: ① **the longest scene goes to the most estranged person** (the wife who has not been cared for in thirty years and the husband who does not know how to care); ② **the shortest scene goes to the closest person** (Grandmother's "I'm going now" and "Yes... go," only two lines); ③ **one person must be absent** (Geun-deok), and the absent scene is the biggest cry of all; ④ **every scene must hand over an object**: a ring (son), a kiss (daughter), new clothes (mother-in-law), an entrustment (sister-in-law), walnut cakes (brother, handed over in reverse), a "thank you" (husband); ⑤ **the last line is not last words but thanks, and it is said by the husband to the wife** ("In-hee, thank you, truly," p0238) — the right to speak at the deathbed is given to the one who stays, not the one who goes. Death itself takes only five lines (p0239).

### 10.7 Examples (from the Chinese Translation)

**Example 1 (p0038–p0039): the doctor tells the husband — not in the office, not in the corridor, but in the small park beside the parking lot**

```
Dr. Yoon pulled Father, without a word, to a small park near the parking lot. They drank coffee bought from a vending machine. A long time passed, and Dr. Yoon still had not spoken.
"You've been quiet this long — what is it you want to say? You're an old maid by now, so it can't be anything about your heart fluttering, and there's no medical malpractice..."
"Your sister's test results are out."
"And so?"
"It's a malignant tumor."
"What?!"
"The trouble urinating is caused by the tumor. Because the tumor is on top of the uterus, it has grown and pressed on the bladder. This is only the first diagnosis, but the tumor has already grown to the point of blooming."
"What are you saying now?"
"Cancer cells have been found in other organs too."
"...Where are the films?"
"The key. Bring it! ... The key to your office, bring it!"
```

Note: The bad news is deliberately placed in the least suitable place (beside the parking lot, coffee from a vending machine); first let him make a joke, then cut in with the announcement; his reaction is not dialogue but an action — demanding the key to look at the films.

**Example 2 (p0092–p0094): the husband tells the wife — in the yard, one cigarette**

```
"Say it. Hurry up. Where exactly is the problem?"
"...Everywhere."
"I don't feel any pain anywhere, so how can there be a problem everywhere?"
"It's cancer."
"Cancer?" ... "Yes." "What kind of cancer?"
"...Uterine cancer."
"Early stage? I don't feel any pain, so it should be early stage, right?" ... "Are they going to remove the uterus?" ... "Then remove it."
"Stop smoking."
"The uterus is useless now anyway, so just take it out. Why are you so upset? Soon-ja from Gupabal, the gyehoe (private savings-club) friends from Pyeongchang-dong, they've all had it removed too... This is just as well; I've been anxious about it all along. At this age I don't need to have children, and I've gone through menopause. I'm not afraid at all. Living is scary enough; what is there to be afraid of in this?"
"Go inside now. Don't look so gloomy. It's so cold... I didn't feel any pain, so how did I get this disease?"
"Doesn't it hurt?"
"If it hurts, will you hurt for me?"
```

Note: The protagonist's attitude toward death is fixed **in the first minute of knowing her condition** and never changes through the drama — what changes is the other five people. "If it hurts, will you hurt for me?" is her only formula for refusing pity. **No character in the book ever uses the word "cancer" to refer to her own illness** ("to this day she has never once said the word 'cancer' with her own mouth").

**Example 3 (p0152–p0155): the anticancer drug is swapped for a nutritional supplement**

```
"How strange — why are there two red pills missing?" ... "This doesn't look like my medicine."
"That's right, it's yours."
"I'm going to change it. This isn't my medicine."
"Sit down! Where are you going!" ... "This is your medicine. Just take this. I'm a doctor; you have to believe what I say. Take it."
"I told you it isn't. Nurses are busy and give the wrong medicine all the time... I know my own medicine best."
"I asked for it. ... Didn't you say you were exhausted after taking that medicine? So I told them not to give it to you."
"Are you crazy, you old man? ... No matter how tired, a sick person has to take medicine to get better."
(Nurses' station) "Don't you have that medicine? The red capsules, the anticancer drug. Give me two of those."
"The medicine has been cancelled."
"I know, it's because my old man... he was afraid it would be too hard on me, so he had it cancelled. But I don't feel tired, so give me that medicine, hm?"
(Head nurse brings two red capsules) "That's right, isn't it?" "Yes, thank you."
(Mother hurries back to the ward with the medicine)
The head nurse smiles bitterly: "It's a nutritional supplement."
```

Note: The information-gap ladder (who knows first, who is kept in the dark, who pretends not to know) generates tension by itself; the final cruelty is delivered by a bystander's half-sentence, and the protagonist does not know she has just been deceived a second time.

**Example 4 (p0167–p0168, p0169): two wardrobes overlap, and then the breakup**

```
(The first time she sees her mother's wardrobe, Yeon-su feels a dazzling shock)
(Father's white shirts are folded neatly layer upon layer; his suits hang straight; in every compartment are Father's underwear, socks, handkerchiefs)
(On the door hang six or seven ties side by side, several of them already tied in round knots. Seeing this, Yeon-su drops to the floor)
—"So this is what a man and a woman, no, a husband and a wife, are like. A man, a husband, is built little by little by his wife's hands."
—"He is merely his wife's work."
— That night, by the Han River —
"We should stop seeing each other from now on."
"Can you eat every meal with me? Can you wear the tie I bought you openly? Apart from the dim emergency exit, can you hold me anywhere else? Can you stand beside me instead of looking around in a crowd? Can you?"
"Only today did I learn that love has its laws. A girl should only meet a boy; a married man's heart belongs only to his family."
"Today I saw your wife's likeness in my mother... I will live well, meet a good man, and live kindly like my mother and your wife..."
(Yeon-su is glad she can look at the river, glad that the two of them do not have to say goodbye face to face)
```

Note: The reason for the breakup is a discovery, not a wound — the mother's wardrobe overlaps with the mistress's situation (a few days earlier, at her lover's home, she saw the same memo, the same underwear folded to the size of a palm, the same seven ties tied in round knots, p0084–p0087); the breakup dialogue is four parallel "can you?" questions plus a concrete list of a future fantasy; the staging itself is emotion (standing side by side looking at the river, not sitting face to face).

**Example 5 (p0230–p0232): in the car, saying goodbye to the children one by one**

```
"Jeong-su, who am I?"
"Mother."
"Call me again."
"Mo—ther."
"Jeong-su, you... even if you forget me, forget Mother's face, forget Mother's smile... don't forget that Mother gave birth to you."
(Mother takes the ring off her finger and gives it to her son) "This, later... give it to your wife."
"I gave it to you because I was afraid you'd forget me. No matter how hard I look, this is all I have to give you. I'm sorry."
(Jeong-su gets out of the car)
"Yeon-su, Mother seems about to lose consciousness soon. I'm always drowsy."
"Mother loves you very much, you know?"
"Yes, I... love... Mother... very much too."
"Yes, I love you. I love you very, very much."
"You... are me; Mother is Yeon-su."
(Mother puts her arms around Yeon-su's neck from behind) "My kind daughter..."
(She kisses Yeon-su's cheek with lips wet with tears)
```

Note: The two goodbyes are completed back-to-back in the same car, with extremely economical staging — the son faces her from the back seat, the daughter sits in the driver's seat **with her back to Mother** (the daughter's face never has to be filmed); what the son receives is an object (a ring) plus a last testament in negative form; what the daughter receives is a kiss plus an assertion of identity; the son's dialogue is compressed into the repetition of a single word, **the strongest emotion given the fewest words**.

**Example 6 (p0237–p0239): one night in Ilsan — the last conversation and the death**

```
"You... will you be all right without me?" ... "No one to nag you anymore — that's good, isn't it?"
"Will you miss me?"
(Father can no longer look Mother in the eye; he only nods)
"When? In what situations?"
"Everything."
"When is everything?"
"When I tie my tie before going to work in the morning." "And?" "When I drink the bad soybean-paste soup." "And?" "When I drink the good soybean-paste soup." "And?"
"When I drink, when I sober up, when I sleep, when I want to hear nagging, when Mother gets confused, when Yeon-su gets married, when Jeong-su enters university, when he graduates, when we fry pancakes at New Year, when we make songpyeon at Chuseok, when I'm sick, when I'm lonely..."
"Come to me quickly. Don't leave me alone and bored."
"Honey, if you think I'm pretty, kiss me."
(Father cups Mother's cheeks in both hands and kisses her deeply)
"In-hee, thank you, truly..."
— The next morning — "Honey." (The right arm he is holding has already lost all warmth)
"Honey..." "In-hee!"
```

Note: The question-and-answer loop "Will you miss me?" → "Everything" → four times "And?" forces the declaration from one word into a twelve-item list; **every item is housework or a seasonal custom, and not one of them is abstract love**; the joking repetition "when I drink the bad soybean-paste soup / when I drink the good soybean-paste soup" closes the drama's soy-crock line; the last words are not "I love you" but "thank you," and they are said by the one who stays; death is given only one sentence, "the right arm he is holding has already lost all warmth" — no breathing, no heartbeat, no doctor, no crying. The last image in the book is "even at the corners of Mother's eyes, sunk in deep sleep, cold moisture had condensed" (p0239): **do not write death; write temperature and humidity**.

The four statements in the afterword that serve as creative principles (all from the author's afterword, p0240–p0253):

- **Principle A**: Both of her parents died of cancer; "death is not something terrible, but the last chance to become beautiful."
- **Principle B**: At the end, Mother "left very peacefully, without a smile and without a painful struggle"; "**Our parting was not beautiful, and it was not sad.**"; "I am not grateful that you told me you loved me before you died, because I 'always' knew how much you loved your youngest daughter, Hee-kyung."
- **Principle C**: "Before my mother died, I never thought she would become my regret. At that time, I was her regret."
- **Principle E**: The author put her mother's "birth story" into the afterword, and then her eldest sister told her it was not true; the conclusion is "**we simply cannot fully understand each other's minds**" — which directly explains the drama's way of having the whole family hide things from one another.
