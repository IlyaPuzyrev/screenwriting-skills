# Dialogue: Seven Scene-by-Scene Beat Analyses and Rewrite Comparisons

> Use with SKILL.md. Beat format: #number "line summary" — action/reaction (gerund) + key subtext points + value change.

---

## I. Balanced Conflict: 《黑道家族》 (The Sopranos) S5E1, Tony vs. Dr. Melfi

- Desire/opposition/intention/strategy: Tony wants Melfi's love; the opposition is that she cannot see his good side; the scene intention is seduction; the strategy is to prove he is a good man. Melfi wants to help him in therapy; the opposition is his wish to turn the relationship personal; the intention is to help the therapy; the strategy is to tell him the facts directly. **Super-intention**: Tony needs to control the only person who stands above him (the Romeo mask conceals the will to power); beneath Melfi's characterization of "opposing danger" is the true character of "seducing danger."
- Three pairs of values: the doctor–patient relationship together/apart; Tony self-deceived/self-aware; Melfi in danger/safe.
- 13 beats: #1 turn on the charm / prepare to face trouble; #2 hands her a Bermuda ticket, "a friend's, he doesn't need it" — makes a demand / laughs at him for being a fool (she uses the rhetorical question "I turned down dinner — you still think I'll go with you?" to avoid direct conflict); #3 "So what am I supposed to do — throw it away?" — claims innocence / avoids conflict (she is silent; "silence is sometimes more powerful than speech"); #4 "You make me sound like half a groupie" — seeks sympathy / blames him; #5 "It's not just that psychiatrist-ethics stuff, is it?" — looks for a way out / gives him a way out (but she offers the painful path of self-awareness); #6 "I want you!" — confesses / stalls for time; #7 "Make me understand what's going on" — makes trouble / crosses the line; #8 "Our values are very different" — backs him into a corner / disparages him; #9 "You don't like my values?" — "You want the truth?" — (pause) "No." — challenges / denies his value; #10 "OK... for example?" — looks at her watch — asks gently / gives him another way out; #11 "No, no, no, no, no, it's not OK" — "You're dishonest. You don't respect women. You don't respect people." — wants to hear the worst / throws a punch; #12 "Am I incapable of love?" — "You always take what you want by brute force or threats" — self-doubt / fires the first shot; #13 "I can't witness violence" — "Fuck you... you bitch" — slams the door — fires the second shot / kills her with words (the draft had established a dozen group-therapy patients as witnesses, so he uses only the most venomous words).
- Contrast in content/length/rhythm: Tony's monosyllables do not form sentences ("ticket..." "Bermuda..." "go, go"); half are questions, the rest lies or pleas; Melfi's three- and four-syllable nouns and twenty-five-word sentences have subject, verb, and object; Tony repeats "OK" five times, rising like waves against the shore. The one who controls the rhythm wins.

## II. Comic Conflict: 《欢乐一家亲》 (Frasier) S1, "Author, Author"

Frasier and Niles put off writing until the deadline and have not begun. #1 "Niles, why don't we admit we can't work together" — "With that attitude there'll never be a book": wants him to accept failure / blames the failure; #2 "Even the fat lady sings" — "Mr. Big Shot Radio Host": calls him an idiot / calls him arrogant; #3 "You're jealous of my fame?" — "By the time I catch up, there's always only chewed meat left": calls him a difficult child / calls him a scene-stealer ("chewed meat" — the incongruity of a mother chewing meat for her baby triggers the laugh and pushes time back to the diaper years); #4 calls him a masochist / calls her a sadist; #5 "I'm the one on the board of the psychology association... but it's your big fat face on the bus ad": calls him a crybaby / calls him a show-off (vulgarizing the noble); #6 "I do not have a big fat face" — "How long are you going to store those winter walnuts?": defends the face / attacks the face (subtext rises to text); #7 "Spindle face — fat face — spindle face — fat face": calls him ugly / calls her ugly; #8 clenches fists / clenches fists; #9 "I'll force you" — "I don't see you forcing me"; #10 pulls chest hair; #11 jumps on his back and grabs his head — "We're psychologists, not wrestlers": attacks / cajoles (targets vanity); #12 surrenders / attacks; #13 "You crawled into my crib and jumped on me": wants to kill / recalls a horror; #14 "You stole my mommy!" runs: kills his brother / flees the scene of the murder. Spiral: personality → appearance → emotion → violence. Each beat lands on a punch word (radio big shot / chewed meat / side of the bus / big fat face / spindle face / wrestler / my mommy); the only exception is walnuts — if rewritten as "Winter's coming and I keep wondering how long you'll have to store those walnuts," the laugh might be bigger.

## III. Asymmetrical Conflict: 《日光下的葡萄干》 (A Raisin in the Sun), Act I, Scene 1

Walter wants Ruth to ask Lena for the entire ten thousand dollars to open a liquor store; Ruth has been pregnant for two months and is considering an abortion; her intention is to avoid. #1 "You know what I was thinking this morning in the bathroom?" — "No": invites her to talk / dismisses him; #2 he calls her attitude bad / she calls their lot bitter; #3 he insists she listen / dismisses him; #4 he ignores her / she mocks him; #5 "When Charlie Atkins wanted me to go into the dry-cleaning business with him, I was a loudmouthed good-for-nothing too, right?" — head drops to his arms: blames / hides guilt (**planting**: the reason for her depression is understood only after the pregnancy is revealed; the actor must not hold her belly); #6 he calls her selfish / she yields; #7 the coffee small-talk plan, "Two more sips don't seem to matter much to you" — "Bobo?": draws her out / senses a trap (planting the sexism that will pay off at the end); #8 he acts the businessman / she foresees disaster; #9 he proves his worldly wisdom / she refuses to play along; #10 "Eat your eggs, they're getting cold" — "A man says 'I'm suffocating, baby' and his woman says your eggs are getting cold!": soothes him / accuses her of not supporting him; #11 "Walter, that's not our money" — raises the moral hammer / faces failure (**first turning point**); #12 "I'm 35, married eight years, and my son sleeps in the living room" — "Eat your eggs": asks for sympathy / ignores him; #13 he accuses her of not loving herself / she accuses him of living in fantasy; #14 "That's exactly what's wrong with colored women in this world" — "Some colored men have done something too": blames her for the failure / blames him for his failure (**second turning point**); #15 he clings to a bad excuse / she mocks his self-deception; #16 he comforts his wounded ego / she retreats into fear. The gerunds show the asymmetry: inviting, insisting, blaming, drawing out, proving, accusing vs. ignoring, hiding, yielding, soothing, retreating. **Three ways to destroy a masterpiece**: open with the turning point; put the turning point very late and pad it with repetition; use no turning point at all.

## IV. Indirect Conflict: 《了不起的盖茨比》 (The Great Gatsby), Chapter 1 Dinner

#1 "You must know Gatsby" — "Gatsby? What Gatsby?": Baker reveals / Daisy hides her surprise (launches the novel's inciting incident); #2 walking toward the dinner table / Daisy plans to humiliate Tom (lets Nick and Baker carry "the marriage is in trouble" back to Gatsby); #3 snuffs out the candle: destroys her husband's romantic gesture / Tom hides his displeasure; #4 the summer solstice topic, asking and answering herself: starts a topic / turns the topic back to herself; #5 "Look, I hurt myself," little finger: makes everyone look / draws attention to herself; #6 "It's all you, Tom": accuses / hides in silence; #7 "That's what you get for marrying a brute" — "I don't like the word 'hulking'": openly insults / orders her to stop insulting; #8 "Hulking and clumsy too": attacks again / retreats into silence. Of 123 words, 107 are monosyllables; no character uses metaphor; figurative language is reserved for description. #4 and #5 seem not to advance but actually serve the spine of action — the narcissist turns the topic toward herself: "Gatsby's obsession with Daisy, and Daisy's obsession with Daisy."

## V. Reflexive Conflict: 《埃尔泽小姐》 (Fräulein Else) and 《纯真博物馆》 (The Museum of Innocence)

- 《埃尔泽小姐》 (Fräulein Else): "This hotel is so big. Like a strange, dark magic castle. Everything is so huge. The mountains too. Frighteningly big. They have never been so dark. The moon is not out yet. It will wait until there is a performance before it comes out... Oh, Fräulein Else, why are you making such a fuss? You were ready to be the mistress of strange men, one after another. And yet you are afraid of the little request van Dorsday is about to make of you? You would have sold yourself for a string of pearls, pretty clothes, a house by the sea — is your father's life worth less than these?" — the first seven sentences average 4.1 words (projected fear); after the critical self takes command, the next seven average 14.5 words; "damned if I do / damned if I don't": hypocrite → slut → coward → slut → ingrate.
- 《纯真博物馆》 (The Museum of Innocence): the lecturer Kemal tells the visitor about the romantic Kemal of the past; 325 words in 11 sentences, averaging 29.5 words, with subordinate clauses and mini-climaxes; the repeated "would" adds softness and uncertainty; the romantic Kemal is quoted only once: "Today, yes, she is coming, now."

## VI. Minimal Conflict: 《迷失东京》 (Lost in Translation), First Conversation at the Bar

#1 Bob gossips to the bartender: charms / pretends to be interested (−); #2 the bartender pulls up a stool: invites him to sit / Charlotte tries to fit in (−); #3 "Uh... not sure": greets / tests whether she is welcome (−); #4 "Suntory time!" — "Vodka tonic": puts her at ease / joins in / approves (+); #5 "A break from my wife, forgetting my son's birthday, earning two million for endorsing whiskey, giving up the stage play" — "Oh": opens a topic / confesses three failures / hides surprise (loss −); #6 "The good news is the whiskey works," she laughs: soothes / laughs sympathetically (+); #7 "My husband is a photographer... I'm not doing anything, just tagging along": invites her confession / admits emptiness (−); #8 "How long have you been married?" — "Two years" — "Twenty-five long years": prepares to pursue / takes the bait / pays court (−); #9 "Have you bought a Porsche yet?": blocks the pursuit / compliments her wit (+); #10 "Marriage loses eight years and leaves only sixteen... there will still be the occasional car crash": gives hope / complains / approves (−); #11 "Philosophy" — "You can make a lot of money in that line": asks for a story / admits he has no faith in the future (−); #12 "You'll figure something out": false hope / laughs it off; #13 "I hope your Porsche plan comes true": also offers false hope / understands (++/−−); #14 "Cheers": celebrates self-deception (+++); #15 a long silence, "I wish I could sleep" — "Me too": admits loss / says he feels the same (loss −−−/intimacy ++++). Three levels of progression: bad / worse / worst; loneliness (having something no one to share it with) vs. loss (having nothing to share). Charlotte's vocabulary: passive verbs, general nouns, no adjectives ("I hope," "I'm not sure" ×2); Bob's lines sound like any tough guy he plays.

## VII. Rewrite Comparisons

- **Hard exposition → weapon**: "We've known and loved each other for over twenty years, since the college fraternity party, when we invited your women's socialist club... my little Trotskyite" → let the character throw the information like a weapon to force the other person, place it in a seductive scene, or hide the information so the behavior becomes credible.
- **On-the-nose → subtext** (《战略高手》 (Out of Sight)): "I'm afraid you'll rape me — Never, all I want is a romantic evening" → "Remember when we talked about Faye Dunaway in 《秃鹰 72 小时》 (Three Days of the Condor)... before they went to bed she accused him of getting rough, he said, 'What, have I ever raped you?' and she said, 'The night is young.'"
- **Telling everything → the third thing** (《杯酒人生》 (Sideways)): "I'm a hard man to know, thin-skinned and temperamental... I can't pursue you, you have to pursue me" → "Pinot can only live in a few special little corners of the world and needs a lot of care... it won't come to you, you have to go to it"; "Please sleep with me" → "A bottle of wine is like a life that has grown and changed and reached its peak, like your 1961, and then steadily and inevitably declines. It tastes so good."
- **Banal → image** (《裘力斯·凯撒》 (Julius Caesar)): "Senator A is so popular and powerful that compared with him we are all little men" → "He bestrides this narrow world like a Colossus, and we petty men walk under his huge legs and peep about to find ourselves dishonorable graves."
- **Repeated beats → progression**: "I need to talk to you — Stay away from me — It's important, you have to listen — Let me go — You have to listen — Shut up and go away" (three pleas/refusals) → "Just standing there you annoy me — I'm not bothering you, I just want to talk — I've heard enough — You haven't heard a word — Because I'm sick of your nonsense — It's not nonsense, it's the truth."
- **Subtext instead of reaction** (《终结者 2》 (Terminator 2)): "Give me your clothes and motorcycle" — "You're crazy, no way" is too shallow → "You forgot to say please."
- **Parenthetical → counter-performance** (《迷雾追魂》 (Play Misty for Me)): "(angrily, face livid) You want the key? Come and get it here" → the actor says it radiantly, proudly, innocently, adorably; calm beyond the normal exposes the pathology.
- **Small talk → flirtation**: "Hi, Debbie" / "Hi, Tom" → "That dress is sexy!" — "You like it?" — "I love it!"
- **Silence** (《黑道家族》 (The Sopranos)): Carmela takes off her bracelet and necklace, stops at the ring; Tony just stares at her; "These aren't stolen, are they?" He keeps staring; she sighs and takes off the ring — at five cents a word, Tony could have said a whole speech: "I'm sorry, the fact is your ring really was stolen."

## VIII. Egri: A Model of Escalating Dialogue

《群鬼》 (Ghosts): Manders vs. Mrs. Alving: "This is all the result of your reading bad books" (attack) → "My good pastor, you are wrong. You were the very one who first set me thinking, and I am deeply grateful to you" (a stronger counterattack) → "Grateful to me?!" → "You forced me to submit to duty and obey my obligations... one knot was untied and the whole thing came loose" → "And this is the result of the hardest struggle of my life?" → "Rather say it was your greatest defeat" → "It was my greatest victory, my victory over myself" → "It was a crime against both of us."

《玩偶之家》 (A Doll's House): after Helmer reads the letter: "Don't put on an act for me (locks the door). Do you know what you've done?" (attack) — "Yes, now I understand completely" (not a counterattack but a declaration of war — the first sign of awakening) → "Hypocrite... criminal" (Nora says nothing and only stares at him — silence speaks more than Shakespeare's lines) → "You've inherited every one of your father's bad traits" — "Yes, and this is how I repay you" (the question is confirmed: she has discovered that eight years have been a nightmare).

## IX. 陆军 (Lu Jun): Models of the Three Requirements of Dialogue

- **Action** (《雷雨》 (Thunderstorm), forcing the medicine): 周朴园 (Zhou Puyuan): "Pour it." — 繁漪 (Fanyi): "I don't want to drink this bitter stuff." — "(to 四凤 (Sifeng), loudly) Pour it." — 周冲 (Zhou Chong): "Dad, Mother doesn't want it, why must you force her?" — "Neither you nor your mother knows where your illness lies." — "(suddenly stern) Drink it. Don't be willful, in front of a child this old." — "Chong, take the medicine to your mother." — "Say 'please drink it, Mother.'" — 周萍 (Zhou Ping): "Do as Father says." — "Ping, persuade your mother to drink it." — "Dad! I..." — "Kneel and persuade your mother." — "(loudly) Kneel!" — 繁漪 (Fanyi), looking at Ping, hurriedly, before he can kneel: "I'll drink it — I'll drink it now!"
- **Characterization** (《相亲》 (Blind Date), two old people): "Oh, 老燕 (Lǎo Yān — Old Yan), you've got me completely unprepared with this business of yours." "Don't think about useless things. Prepared for what? Give me a straight answer — if it's yes, say yes; if it's no, forget it." "What's the hurry?" "You're sweating — give me an answer!" "You've been under your parents' thumb since you were little, then you switched and fell under your daughter's thumb. When are you ever going to contract out a stretch of your own life and call the shots yourself?" "That's just my fate!" "You keep believing in fate — that won't do!"
- **Subtext** (《祖母》 (The Grandmother)): "Kurt, why do you ask me whether that story happened that way?" "I just want to get it straight, Grandma." "You just want to get it straight, but there must be a reason you ask that question." "I'm just interested." "Interested — but why are you interested?" ... (Silence. The young man picks up a book; the grandmother keeps looking at him.) "Kurt, you must tell me now what the reason really is..."
