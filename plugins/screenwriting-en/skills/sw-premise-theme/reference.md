## I. Egri's Premise Checklist and the Premises of Famous Plays

**Premises of famous plays**: 《罗密欧与朱丽叶》 (Romeo and Juliet) = great love can overcome everything, even death; 《李尔王》 (King Lear) = blind trust leads to destruction; 《麦克白》 (Macbeth) = cold ambition leads to self-destruction; 《奥赛罗》 (Othello) = jealousy destroys itself and the one it loves; 《群鬼》 (Ghosts) = the sins of the fathers are visited on the children; 《玩偶之家》 (A Doll's House) = inequality between the sexes in marriage breeds unhappiness; 《死角》 (Dead End) = poverty drives people to crime; 《甜蜜的青春小鸟》 (Sweet Bird of Youth) = ruthless ambition leads to destruction; 《朱诺与孔雀》 (Juno and the Paycock) = shiftlessness leads to ruin (but the son's death has nothing to do with the premise, and Act Two stalls); 《影子与实体》 (Shadow and Substance) = faith conquers pride (actually two premises); 《伪君子》 (Tartuffe) = he who digs a pit for others falls into it himself; 《项链》 (The Necklace) = escaping reality eventually exacts a price; 《狮子上街》 (The Lion Takes to the Streets) = cold ambition leads to self-destruction; 《海军的骄傲》 (The Pride of the Navy) = devotion in love conquers despair; 《人间与天国》 (Between Earth and Heaven) = narrow-mindedness leads to isolation.

**Usable premises** (what is missing is your belief): suffering leads to easy contentment / foolish generosity leads to poverty / honesty defeats deceit / heedlessness destroys friendship / a hot temper leads to isolation / bragging leads to humiliation / craftiness digs its own grave / dissipation leads to self-destruction / egotism leads to the loss of friends / frugality leads to waste.

**Derivation examples**
- Crime play: a perfect crime; a friend finds it boring → no premise → the character "my only motive is your command" → reveal why (the process of a daughter killing her mother) → choose ambition as the murder motive → why is the victim steadfast? → cold ambition carries the seed of self-destruction → 《麦克白》 (Macbeth).
- Teenage robbery: why rob → the father is an alcoholic → the Great Depression → "poverty drives people to crime" → Johnny, 16, with a personal premise "as long as I am smart enough I can get away with anything" against the law's "the net of justice is wide"; if you see only the brutality and not the cause, you will misapply "a lack of policing drives people to crime" — you must explain why the millionaire's son does not steal bread.
- Aunt Clara: debauchery → the rival commits suicide → the husband leaves → religious fanaticism → interfering in others' lives → "extreme behavior leads to destruction"; the ending must be caused by her actions, not an accident.
- The causes of jealousy (frivolity / inferiority / familiarity / boredom / the lover / selling herself to save her husband / misunderstanding) each correspond to a different premise; "only one driving force can produce jealousy."
- 《伪君子》 (Tartuffe), first third: Madame Pernelle, "If he were in charge of the house it would be good for the whole family" (the first hint of entrusting the property); Dorine, "out of jealousy of the mistress" (set-up); Orgon, "And what about Tartuffe? — he can eat a pair of quails and half a leg of mutton — poor man" — four exchanges.

## III. Cron: Building the Ruby Case

- **Flash**: Jennie, "the woman who hates dogs." **Why care**: love and death are one step apart. **Theme**: "Love is worth any price; having loved and lost is better than never having loved" (cliché is fine).
- **Three drafts of the what-if question**: abstract → add the dog → "a woman who considers herself successful and keeps love at a distance is on the verge of losing everything… stealing a dog for an hour or two to prove she is mentally stable… when she cannot leave the dog, she is forced to accept that it is precisely the human contact she has avoided that allows her to bear loss."
- **Who**: Ruby is the "silent partner" in a TV series, Henry's partner for ten years, deeply in love but refusing to live together or marry, "believing she is not at risk of a broken heart."
- **Why**: want — to sit side by side writing with Henry all day; why — the "free pass" to the room of love; misbelief — sincere involvement will hurt you (it corrodes the self → makes you vulnerable → creates an imbalance of power).
- **Origin scene** (after six drafts): the father of Ruby's best friend Beth dies; at the soccer game Beth kneels before an empty chair and weeps → Ruby concludes "Thank God I will never feel such grief"; the spaghetti night, "Ruby is writing a play," imagining her own father's death "a trace of relief," begging Beth to kick the ball, "please."
- **Three turning-point scenes**: age 13, the skating rink — to win Beth back she plays with a boy, really falls for a boy, is kissed, "suddenly and for no reason feels she has lost something" → pulls away; meets Henry and falls in love → realizes dependence → convinces herself it is not love → breaks up; after ten years as partners he proposes → she reluctantly agrees → the wedding approaches and she falls ill → returns to the old pattern "proving she can harvest love without full commitment."
- **Primary external problem** (passes the external three columns and the internal two questions): Henry takes a solo film job and announces the last season → the producer demands the ending be rewritten in ten days → argument → Henry has a motorcycle accident, life or death uncertain → Ruby faces the deadline alone. Stealing the dog cannot be listed — that is plot, not story.
- **Ticking-clock tracking**: co-writing disagreement → the studio demands a rewrite → argument and accident → less than a week → a female fan's script as a substitute → **the sixth chime**: Nora, the veterinarian sister, forces her to move in → she decides to "borrow" a dog.
- **Scene card one**: alpha point = Ruby is about to start writing the script; what happens = Sharon's phone call + Nora's visit; consequence = she finishes the rewrite but first soothes Nora; importance = after losing her beloved, the only thing she can control is the final say over the play; realization = the biggest obstacle is Nora; therefore → find a way to distract Nora.
- **Moment of epiphany**: the star dog Rufus and his owner Tony; "people love dogs because dogs don't hurt you," "they will die someday"; Ruby designs an ending for Tony; "I dodged pain, but I sacrificed joy" → let Juliet live.
- The plot point's triple test (on what grounds can Nora throw Ruby out → Occam's razor: Nora owns the house; from the inner contradiction: when their parents died, Nora wanted to rebuild the connection, and letting Ruby live in the house was her only family warmth).

## IV. Hanson's Premise Statement Template and Examples

- Theme (one emotional noun + ≤2 qualifiers) → basic action (present-progressive transitive verb) + purpose clause (protagonist's name + what he wants + despite antagonist's name + opposition).
- 《哈姆雷特》 (Hamlet): justice → murder; Hamlet wants to kill Claudius. 《E.T.》 (E.T.): separation → seeking help; E.T. wants a boy to help him get home. 《窈窕淑女》 (My Fair Lady): acceptance → professor; Higgins wants to teach Eliza to speak properly and be accepted by society. 《公民凯恩》 (Citizen Kane): success → investigation; the reporter wants to find the meaning of "Rosebud" although Kane left no clue when he died. 《西雅图夜未眠》 (Sleepless in Seattle): the woman dies; father and son are estranged; the child writes to a radio station to find a partner for his father; the father refuses to meet the woman who replied; the child persuades the father to go.
- Avoid: two actions (murder → kill); intransitive / existential verbs ("Eliza wants to be happy"); one-way verbs ("learn"); template blanks: "The emotion that drives (protagonist) to act is (theme)"; "The need for (theme) drives (protagonist) to (transitive verb)"; "(protagonist) wants (desire), although (antagonist) (opposes)."
- The three sentences of a dramatic premise (disturbing event / Act One hook / Act Two hook, each with its core question): 《哈姆雷特》 (Hamlet) meets his father's ghost, who says his uncle is the murderer (will he kill the king?); hires actors to perform a murder in front of the king (same question); Claudius feels guilty and Hamlet prepares revenge → yes. 《天使之城》 (City of Angels): Seth falls in love with Maggie (can they be together?); appears before her and she refuses; learns he can fall to earth → no. 《女人香》 (Scent of a Woman): a student takes care of a blind officer (can he help him face reality?); a crazy journey; the student gets into trouble, which pushes the officer to help him → yes.

## V. Hoxter's Theme Test Bench

The same material (Little Jimmy, a widowed father "carried off" by a space captain — really a love story) run through different themes: growth / love (the captain's niece teaches him about dating in space) / sacrifice / obsession / family (the alien crew = a new family) / friendship (a father can also be a friend) / revenge (the family are refugees) / justice (bring the culprit to justice) — see which theme makes the characters most alive. Four-way interaction: a naive friend vs. a cynical friend plus each one's lover "contains the value of the entire script."

Logline formula: "When his partner is killed by an evil alien (inciting incident), tenacious Brooklyn beat cop Lauren Brown (protagonist) must find the aliens' home planet (challenge) to avenge his partner (theme)"; 《冬天的骨头》 (Winter's Bone): "A teenage girl (protagonist), at the risk of violent reprisal (stakes), breaks the taboo of a drug-dealing family (conflict), searches for her fugitive father (challenge), and saves her home (theme)" + a second sentence on character; 《异形》 (Alien): ensemble protagonist + inciting incident + conflict + adversary, the hook = a haunted house in a spaceship.

## VI. Bork: Eight Story Problems and Eight Levels of Risk

**Eight story problems**: ① I (we) face a life-threatening danger ② my life is probably a mess ③ a once-in-a-lifetime chance to change my self-image permanently ④ I must save someone from a super-terrible doom ⑤ I must achieve a distant but life-changing, seemingly impossible goal ⑥ I must defeat a bully who threatens innocents ⑦ I must escape a terrible state that prevents freedom and happiness ⑧ I must find the best partner but the obstacles are enormous.

**Risk from high to low**: life (all humanity or personal) → justice and prevention → freedom and democracy (escape / release) → preserving family and personal life (coming home / a cherished life threatened) → joining the best partner (a huge external obstacle resolved only at the end) → a better career and prospects (you must put everything you have at risk and be morally superior yet disadvantaged) → a life-changing award (the meaning it carries is what matters) → the chance for happiness (must be made concrete).

**Risks that do not qualify**: an achievement that does not change one's fate; success at work; learning something new or changing an inner attitude; vague military struggle; happiness and health problems that bring no specific huge change; a lone "must make a decision" moment; a person without compassion transforming into a good person.

**Three elements of a logline**: who the protagonist is and his connection / the catalyst event / the nature, mission, difficulty, and importance of the challenge. Example, 《辛德勒的名单》 (Schindler's List): a cunning industrialist makes a fortune from the war but is shocked by the Jews' fate, employs them, and opposes the friendly but perverted Nazi commandant.

## VII. Snyder: The One-Line Story and the Genres

- Ironic hooks: "A cop comes to Los Angeles to visit his estranged wife, and the building where she works is taken over by terrorists"; "A businessman falls in love with a prostitute he hired to pose as his girlfriend"; "She is the perfect woman — as long as she doesn't drink."
- Enhanced version: "A meticulous schoolteacher… an overprotective policeman brother-in-law… a journey through hell."
- Titles: 《律政俏佳人》 (Legally Blonde) is good; 《为爱还是为钱》 (For Love or Money) is the worst; 《核子家庭》 (Nuclear Family) — a pun that sold for $1 million.
- Six creative games: the comedy version / the serious version / the fish-out-of-water FBI / ___ school / versus!!! / my ___ is a serial killer.
- Ten genres at a glance: Monster in the House (monster + enclosed space + a sin); Golden Fleece (a journey to find X but finding yourself); Out of the Bottle (Cinderella gets magic → learns to be an ordinary person); Dude with a Problem (an ordinary person + an extraordinary situation); Rites of Passage (the attacker is invisible "life"); Buddy Love (a love story in disguise); Whydunit (the point is why); Fool Triumphant (the underdog against the system); Institutionalized (a group story, one escapee); Superhero (an extraordinary being in an ordinary world; give him pain).
- Hollywood's secret: 《惊爆点》 (Point Break) = 《速度与激情》 (The Fast and the Furious); 《黑客帝国》 (The Matrix) = 《怪兽电力公司》 (Monsters, Inc.); 《兔子罗杰》 (Who Framed Roger Rabbit) = 《唐人街》 (Chinatown).

## VIII. 陆军 (Lu Jun): Examples of Thematic Digging and 戏核 (xìhé — the core of the play)

**Three revisions of 《妇女代表》 (The Women's Representative)**: strengthen the transformation of the midwife to show the sanitation campaign (narrow) → emphasize the old-vs-new thinking of mother-in-law and daughter-in-law (stale; Mother Wang would become the mother-in-law from 《小姑贤》 (The Virtuous Sister-in-Law)) → connect reforming the family with reforming society; 张桂蓉 (Zhang Guirong) takes out the land deed: "Of the two land deeds, one is mine; of the three rooms, one end is mine… I'll take my land deed, and if I can't live in the house I'll tear it down"; 牛大婶 (Aunt Niu) goes to the district to learn the new midwifery method, runs a health station, and only after tasting the benefits does she turn to Zhang.

**Three endings for 《雨夜访贫》 (Visiting the Poor on a Rainy Night)**: ① the granddaughter begs a stranger to pretend to be the county magistrate → the grandfather discovers the truth → the thatched hut suddenly stops leaking, and the new county magistrate brings the township head and village head to plug the leak on the roof (a political fairy tale); ② the grandfather knows the truth and only blames himself for being old; the hut stops leaking but who repaired it is not explained; ③ the young woman in the raincoat is the brick-and-tile magnate; the county magistrate is sitting in her car being taken to solve the brick-and-tile factory's disaster relief — "a Communist county magistrate cannot know only how to visit the poor and ask about hardship; he is not always cut from one mold"; at the end the county magistrate still has not come.

**Sources of the 戏核**: everyday details (a husband fined; a Hangzhou auntie repaying a peddler's debt); the mysteries of a character's psychology (in 《北京的空气》 (The Air of Beijing), the master steals the servant's cigarettes); the imagined world (in 《獾审》 (The Badger Trial), a badger is brought to court; 《威尼斯商人》 (The Merchant of Venice), a pound of flesh).

**Four revisions of 《名誉妻子》 (The Honored Wife)** (when the plot is not believable, first stand the characters up): make the reason for the marriage sufficient (the mother is seriously ill; the uncle "buys" a poor girl from the countryside; he knows only that she is wounded, not that the wound is fatal; on the wedding night he truthfully tells her and begs her to endure for a while); 肖 (Xiao) has a lover in the countryside and gives herself to him on the night before parting; she becomes pregnant after the marriage; 应虎 (Yinghu) urges a divorce, and the mother and uncle are baffled; Yinghu falls ill, takes poison, and on his deathbed entrusts his wife to the family; Xiao is willing to stay, care for the mother-in-law, and hold the family together.

**Eight cases of conflict**: 《金砖风波》 (The Gold Brick Storm) has no social meaning; the "wounded soldier breaks off the engagement" model is false; 《认婆》 (Acknowledging the Mother-in-Law) "swallows a doddering fly"; the foreign businessman "testing" the factory director is inaccurate; 《旧情人》 (The Old Lover) has three hundred lines of antiphonal singing, a debate of opinions running in place; 《红杏》 (Red Apricot) and 《相思有几多》 (How Much Is Yearning) rely on homophone / judgment / association misunderstandings; in 《羚羊木雕》 (The Antelope Carving), the carving should have been given to A's father and everyone would have been happy (dodging the heavy for the light); a demobilized soldier leaves and comes back full of guilt (a feint); 《洞房之夜》 (The Wedding Night) changes the direction of the conflict midway (lack of tension).
