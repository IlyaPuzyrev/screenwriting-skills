## §12 The Full Table of Forty Prohibitions (41 Rows) · Example Faults in Composing a Qu

**Source**: Wu Mei (吴梅), *General Treatise on Qu Studies* (《曲学通论》), Chapter 11, "Ten Knowledges," the tenth knowledge; scanned pages p0095–p0099 (printed page = scanned page − 20; notes `wu-mei-quxue-tonglun/11-tonglun-yongzi-zuoqu-zhiji.md`, section 10).

**Wu Mei's prefatory note (must be quoted together with the table)**:
> "I have read Wang Boliang's (Wang Jide, 王骥德) *Rules of Prosody* (《曲律》), which contains forty prohibitions on qu. Some of the items listed there do not fully apply to qu; **I have therefore annotated and explained them**. If a student can keep these prohibitions, that is excellent, **but the law is extremely harsh.**" (p0095)
> "The forty prohibitions above: **a master with true understanding will naturally not suffer from these faults. If one cannot keep them all, one must examine oneself and remove the worst, so that they do not offend the eye**; otherwise, one will never be a true professional." (p0099)

→ **The items are Wang Jide's; the explanations are Wu Mei's**; distinguish the two when quoting. **The forty prohibitions are a health-check sheet, not an admission threshold**; they cannot be used to reject a song that stands up on stage. If you cannot run all of them, run at least six: **repeated rhyme, rhyme intrusion, throat-twisting, excessive filler words, piled-up erudition, and chaotic use of modes** (宫调, gōngdiào — the modal system).

**Attribution column**: `qupai` = the phonological layer, belonging to this skill (items 1–16, 35–40); `sw-dialogue` = the purely verbal layer, belonging to `sw-dialogue` (items 17–34). The "(exempt)" row after item 12 is an exception stated in Wu Mei's original book, set out as its own row, so the whole table has 41 rows.

### 12a Chapter-End Appendix: Additional Prohibitions for the Object-Description and Humorous Modes (Applicable to Dialogue Writing Generally)

**Source**: the same chapter, p0099–p0100.

- **Object description (咏物, yǒngwù)**: "**In writing on an object, do not betray the title; the object must be recognizable the moment you open your mouth. Do not prize describing its body; prize describing its use. Neither too close nor too far, capture its charm and verve**, so that it is like a lamp-image in a mirror: clear to the eye, yet impossible to seize. Only then is one a master."
  - Example: Wang Heqing's (王和卿) 〔咏大蝴蝶〕 ("On the Great Butterfly"): "It bursts the dream of Zhuang Zhou; on two wings it rides the east wind; through three hundred famous gardens, one sip empties each... lightly it stirs, and fans the flower seller across the bridge to the east." — "**The opening line alone tells you it is a great butterfly; thereafter it comes down like a split bamboo, and not one line is not a fine phrase.**"
  - Another example: the old song 〔咏柳·白练序〕 ("On the Willow, to 'Bailian Xu'"): "It peeps with blue eyes"; the moment you open your mouth, you know it is a willow. The later lines turn instead to vermilion gates, feathered carriages, painted bridges, and pleasure boats — "**that is where its richness of tone comes from**."
  - **Self-check**: remove every word in the passage that directly names the object; can you still tell what is being described? If yes, you wrote its "use"; if no, you only described its shape.
- **Humor (诙谐, huīxié)**: "Without the most brilliant endowment and the finest brush, exercised with the most supple responsiveness, it is hard to shine. **Not one over-literary word may be allowed, nor one doggerel phrase. Make the vulgar refined; a line that, once spoken, makes people fall over laughing—that is excellence.**" Examples: the Yuan writer's 〈秃指甲〉 ("Bald Fingernails") — "ten fingers like withered bamboo shoots"; Xu Wei's (徐渭) 〈嘲歪嘴妓·黄莺儿〉 ("Mocking the Crooked-Mouthed Courtesan, to 'Huangying'er'"). **"Still, it is not the proper path; doing it once in a while is permissible."**

### 12b Example Faults in Composition (A Complete Negative Case at the Level of Structure)

**Source**: the same chapter, part (2), "Composition" (章法), p0082–p0085. Wu Mei dissects line by line a suite, 〈暗想当年〉 ("Secretly Thinking of That Year"), that "the whole world calls a peerless fine piece," and finds six faults:

| # | Fault | What the original text does |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The first song, 〔步步娇〕, should have only seven characters in its first line; "暗想当年罗帕上曾把新诗写" ("secretly thinking of that year, on a silk handkerchief I once wrote a new poem") **uses five filler characters in a row, already against the rule** | — |
| 2 | The third line by the pattern should have only five characters; the original "心猿乖意马劣" **is changed into a line broken at the waist** (折腰句, zhéyāo jù) | — |
| 3 | Lines four and five, "软玉温香翠拥红遮" ("soft jade and warm fragrance, emerald embraces and red covers"), **have no flow of tone and nothing to rest on** | — |
| 4 | The last two lines, "琴瑟不协，不觉花影转过梧桐月" ("zither and lute do not harmonize; unknowingly flower shadows have crossed the wutong moon"), **repeat the sense and do not connect** | — |
| 5 | The second song, 〔沉醉东风〕, **starts a new idea** and does not connect with the first song | — |
| 6 | The whole suite **repeats rhymes to the point of absurdity** | "In one suite he rhymes two 柳 (liǔ) words, four 愁 (chóu), five 秋 (qiū), two 收 (shōu), three 酒 (jiǔ), two 头 (tóu), three 九 (jiǔ), two 瘦 (shòu)—**a laughable jumble**" |

**The six-point diagnosis (p0085, usable directly as a self-check list)**: "In sum, **the two songs show no scholarship in the craft of lyric, one; no broad vision, two; no ingenious thought, three; no fine phrases, four; no sequence, five; no through-line, six.** Therefore the lyric writer must sweep away all stuffing and superficial language."

**The positive standard (p0082, transcribed whole)**:
> "The songs of a suite — the Yuan people called them yuefu — share the same mechanism as the rhapsody and the fu. **They have a beginning and an end, an opening and a closing. One must first fix the frame, establish the governing idea, arrange the tunes, and then select lines to form the movement. Avoid patchwork; avoid making do.** It should be like the snake of Mount Chang, head and tail responding; or like the mermaid's brocade, without a single flaw. **The idea fresh, the language fine; the characters resonant, the tune round. Not one tune may be added or removed; not one tune may be inverted.** There are rules and measures, color and sound. All beauties are present, yet its excellence lies not within the tune but beyond the lines. It must also be misty and vast, its posture overflowing... **Imitate joy and it makes the spirit sway; write grief and it breaks the heart. It is not meant to please people but to move them.** This is what is called fēngshén (风神 — wind-spirit, verve), this is what is called biāoyùn (标韵 — distinction and tone)."
→ **The sequence is fixed**: fix the frame → establish the idea → arrange the tunes → select lines to form the movement; reverse it and you get "patchwork" and "making do."

**The sixteen-character antitheses at the syntax layer (p0086)**: "Prefer the winding and reject the direct; prefer the ornate and reject the withered; prefer the flowing and bright, not the difficult and halting; prefer the light and fine, not the heavy and clotted; prefer the fresh, not the stale; prefer release, not piling; prefer warm elegance, not vehemence; prefer delicacy, not coarseness; prefer fragrant moisture, not harshness."
**The double test of new and familiar (p0086)**: "I write a new phrase, yet people hearing it think it an old line — that means the mechanism of language is ripe. I write an unfamiliar song, yet people find it easy to sing — that means the tune is right." Every line polished, **let there be no street-market language, no language that cheats the throat.**

**Two rules on rhyme (p0088, discussing the linking songs)**: **one rhyme to the end is the excellent way**; frequent changes of rhyme "after all betray a shortage of talent," and **one must not use "Worshipping the Moon" from *The Pipa* (《琵琶记》) as an excuse**; **repeated rhymes need not be strictly avoided** (all old plays are like this; "to avoid them by force is not as good as to repeat them steadily and harmoniously"), **but a suite like 〈人别后〉 ("After We Parted") that repeats a rhyme four or five times is absolutely impermissible.**
**The three faults of falling rhyme (p0088, *The Pipa* is named)**: ① **inverting a character to force the rhyme** — "手指上血痕尚在" ("bloodstains still on the fingers") writes "衣麻将麻衣," inverting the two characters 衣 and 麻; ② **rhyming by ellipsis** — "红拂鬓云撩" has no 乱 (luàn) after it, so the expected word of the compound is left unsaid; ③ **saying it again after saying it** — 〔饶饶令〕 "但愿岁岁年年人常在，父母共夫妻相劝酬" ("I only wish year after year the people were always here, parents and husband and wife urging each other on") is followed by "夫妻长厮守，父母愿长久" ("husband and wife long keeping each other, parents wishing for long life"); ④ the coda's "惟有快活三是良谋" **is simply doggerel**.

### 12c The Three-Way Division of Responsibility (A Guide to Correction)

**Source**: the same chapter, part (1), "Meaning of Characters" (字义), p0080–p0082. The same error must be distinguished as **the author's error**, **the singer's error**, or **the author correct but the performer wrong** — the remedy differs for each.

| Responsible party | Case | Remedy |
|---|---|---|
| **Author's error** | The character 〔衩〕: in Liang Bolong's (梁伯龙; Liang Chenyu, 梁辰鱼) *The Washing of the Silk · Golden Well, Red Flower* (《浣纱记·金井水红花》), "湿裙衩娇羞谁讶" ("damp skirt slit, shy and bashful—who is surprised?") — the metre here requires a level tone, but 衩 in "clothes' slit" is departing tone; changing it to 靫 (chā), a level tone, fits the metre, but 靫 means "quiver" and cannot be put in a woman's mouth. The root of the fault begins with Chen Dasheng (陈大声); Bolong followed him. In Tang Xianzu's (汤显祖) *Return of the Soul · Seeking the Dream* (《还魂·寻梦》), 〔懒画眉〕 "睡荼蘼抓住裙衩线" ("sleeping by the trailing rose, she catches the thread of her skirt slit") is sung with the departing tone, "completely at odds with the tune pattern of 〔懒画眉〕." Also the character 〔倩〕: in *The Washing of the Silk*, "还娇倩胜江南万马千兵" ("her delicate beauty surpasses the thousands of horses and soldiers south of the Yangtze") should be pronounced like 茜 (qiàn), but is rhymed into the Gēngqīng (庚青) category | **Change the words** |
| **Singer correct but author wrong** | The character 〔车〕 has two readings: in *Worshipping the Moon · Yufurong* (《拜月·玉芙蓉》), "胸中书富五车，笔下高千古" ("in my breast are five cartloads of books; beneath my brush, a thousand ages stand high") — the two lines each carry a rhyme; with the lower line "高千古," the upper must be read "jū" (居). But there is no principle by which the world calls "five cartloads of books" jū; today's singers all follow the reading chē (尺遮切), preferring the rhyme not to fit | **Change the words** (the fault is the author's) |
| **Author correct but performer wrong** | The character 〔叹〕 has two readings: in *The Pipa · Going to the Examination* (《琵琶·赴选》), "离别何足叹" ("parting is not worth sighing over") — this 叹 is level tone, rhyming with the preceding "颜"; later in 〔玉交枝〕 "别离休叹" ("do not sigh over parting"), this 叹 should be departing tone, rhyming with the following line. **Today's actors all sing it in departing tone** | **Do not change the words; add a four-tone note to the script** |

**The concluding sentence**: "I have given a few examples to show how much error has been passed down in the world. Then can filling in lyrics and setting tunes **be done without textual scholarship**?" (p0082)
**What happens if you do not**: confound the three kinds of error, and a problem solvable by annotation becomes a cut-and-rewrite.

### 13c Four Questions and Answers (How a Modern Newly Written Qupai-System Play Uses Qupai, with Page Numbers)

**Question 1: Is it a suite? — No suite; the linked-qupai suite is abandoned completely.**
- The play has 8 scenes, each using 1–8 tunes, with **no modal relationship between tunes**: Scene 3 【幽冥钟】→【刮骨令】→【孝顺歌】 (p0025–0026), Scene 5 【月儿高】→【香罗带】→【华秋儿】 (p0044–0047), Scene 4 【七句半】→【昆曲】→【红衲袄】→【飞梆子】→【新水令】 (p0029–0039). **There is not a single mode marking anywhere in the play.**
- **The same tune is repeated across scenes and characters**: 【红衲袄】 for Blacksmith Yuan (p0010), Sun Yutian (p0019), and Nichang (p0030) — hero, villain, and favorite concubine share one tune; 【飞梆子】 for Meng Dengke (p0033) and Heng Bao (p0055). The chuanqi rules all cease to apply: one mode per chu (出 — a chuanqi act), one suite to the end, and sheng (生 — male lead) and dan (旦 — female lead) do not share a tune.
- The only surviving trace of suite linkage is **linked singing** (接唱, jiēchàng) (#3/#4, #5/#6, #29/#30), occupying the position of hétóu (合头 — chorus tag), but used only for duet combat.
- → **Parallel with Weng Ouhong** (翁偶虹): Weng Ouhong said that full-length Kunqu plays "are too constrained by suites" and switched to "scattered gold and broken jade" — selecting tunes in a cross-cutting way. Wei Minglun, in Sichuan-opera gāoqiāng, arrives at the same step and goes further — he does not even write the note "re-determine the tune, re-set the qupai," but simply marks the tune name and uses it. **Two genres, two authors, two eras, independently reaching the same conclusion.**

**Question 2: Is it "scattered gold and broken jade" tune selection? — Yes; the basis is emotion and the scale of the passage, not mode.** See the table in 13b.

**Question 3: Are the qupai's rules of wording and line count observed? — Half: "one rhyme to the end per passage" is observed; character count and level/oblique tones are not.**
- **Observed**: ① each tune **runs one rhyme to the end without changing rhyme** — 【红衲袄】 uses the Zhōngdōng (中东) rhyme (用／虫／梦／风／涌／峰, p0010); 【香罗带】 has 24 lines all rhyming in Yóuqiú (由求) (秀／愁／就／头／走／丢／咒／头／酒／飕／口／秋／手／牛／受／头／手／流／首／求, p0046–0047); 【月儿高】 has 22 lines all rhyming in Gūsū (姑苏) (釜／书／股／腹／路／书／苦／枯／赋／徒／户／颅／悟／除／腐／涂／聩／骨／府／目／固／夫, p0044–0045). ② each tune has a **roughly fixed scale of line count** — 【红衲袄】 6 lines, 【飞梆子】 4, 【七句半】 4, 【一枝花】 4, 【梭梭岗】 6.
- **Not observed**: ① **character count within the same tune is free** — 【新水令】 mixes four-character repeated lines ("Open the net on one side, / open the net on one side"), four sets of four-character parallelism ("Grand Chengdu, towering Sichuan, / every yamen, every case"), seven-character lines, and ten-character lines ("It is not the Han border and Chu river, dragon and tiger fighting"); 【一枝花】 is three-three-four ("Three cups of wine, three cups of wine, every cup hastens death"). ② **Three-character heads** (三字头, sān zì tóu) are used profusely ("Wait till I— / go to Chengdu, / enter the examination shed," "Startled, I turn my head," "In vain it is left behind," "When I think back"), a bǎnqiāng (板腔体 — the banqiang system) habit rather than qupai metre. ③ **Line count can be extended indefinitely** — 【流水腔】 28 lines, 【香罗带】 24, 【月儿高】 22, far beyond the original tune's capacity. ④ Level and oblique tones (平仄, píngzè) are not required; colloquial speech, Sichuan dialect, idioms, and xiēhòu sayings (歇后语, xiēhòuyǔ — two-part allegorical sayings) enter the song directly.
- → **One-sentence conclusion**: **tune name → emotion and scale; rhyme → strict; character count and tones → ignored.** This directly conflicts with Wu Mei's and Wang Jilie's (王季烈) "filling in lyrics must follow the score." **The ancient and modern lines stand side by side; one cannot take only one.**

**Question 4: Are there self-composed tune names? — No new tune names are invented, but the "tune-name column" is expanded.**
**No tune in the play is newly made by the author** (all 11 gāoqiāng tunes are existing Sichuan-opera qupai; 【新水令】【一枝花】【月儿高】 are old Northern tunes). But in the parentheses where a tune name should go, four kinds of non-qupai appear:

| Type | How written | Instances and pages |
|---|---|---|
| **Vocal-system name as tune name** | "(sings 'Kunqu')" — the Sichuan-opera notation of "five vocal systems used together" (五腔并用, wǔqiāng bìngyòng); **the author marks in the tune-name column "sing this passage in Kunqu style"**, leaving the specific Kunqu qupai to the composer | 4 places (p0029 Heng Bao, p0030 the singers, p0050 offstage accompanying chorus, p0074 behind the scenes) |
| **Another genre's metrical pattern as tune name** | "(sings Peking opera 'Xīpí Èrliù')" — **the vocal system here is a character label**: Sichuan characters sing gāoqiāng, the prince from Beijing sings Peking opera, and the Imperial Commissioner speaks Beijing colloquial throughout | 1 place (p0054) |
| **Recitation method as tune name** | "(recites 'rhymed speech')" | 1 place (p0049) |
| **Vocal-form category / loose tune** | 【笛引】 (prelude), 【七句半】, 【流水腔】 — less qupai than categories of gāoqiāng vocal form | — |

→ **In a modern newly written qupai-system script, the tune-name column is in practice a "singing-method instruction column"**; it can be filled with a qupai name, a vocal-system name, another genre's metrical pattern name, or a recitation-method name. This is much broader than "one must fill in a metrically correct qupai," and is one of the technical reasons the qupai system survives in the modern era.

**One point beyond the four questions: whole Tang poems enter the song.** In Scene 2 Nichang sings the whole of Li Shangyin's "Written on a Rainy Night to the North" (p0020); in Scene 4 the singers chorus the whole of Du Fu's "To Lord Hua" (p0030), **not one character changed, and neither is given a tune name.** Both do double duty: in "Written on a Rainy Night," "night rain on Bashan" hints at Nichang's native place (the next line has a Manchu guard report, "The Bashan magistrate has fired a cannon in emergency"; Nichang's voice fails: "Bashan?"); "To Lord Hua" originally satirizes usurpation, and at the governor's victory feast it is ready-made irony. **The qupai system already had the "Tang-assembled" (集唐, jítáng) tradition — all 55 exit poems (下场诗, xiàchǎngshī) of *The Peony Pavilion* (《牡丹亭》) are assembled from Tang lines — and this play moves it from the exit poem into the aria.**

**The four functions of bāngqiāng**: ① **narration** — stating facts the character cannot conveniently or cannot say (#2); ② **commentary** — standing outside the character to judge, expose, settle accounts (#1 #6 #8 #9); ③ **speaking for / participating** — speaking for the character, even deciding for him (#5); ④ **emotional amplification** — expanding one person into a crowd, a low voice into a full stage (#4 #7). There are also two purely structural uses, **starting the song** and **taking up the lower line** (#3 #4).

**The three formal rules of bāngqiāng**:
- **Extremely short**: 6 of the 9 instances are only 3–7 characters; the longest is 3 lines. **Bāngqiāng never carries extended lyricism** — extended lyricism is always left to the characters.
- **Always placed where "the character cannot keep singing"**: the sentence is cut off (#6), the upper line hangs waiting for the lower (#4 #1 #9), the character is in a state of lost consciousness or lost judgment (#2 #5).
- **It can change the qupai**: #4 bāngqiāng starts 【刮骨令】 anew, #5 bāngqiāng "turns to 【孝顺歌】 hétóng" — **the act of changing tune is performed by the bāngqiāng**, and the characters follow. This is a structural power unique to bāngqiāng in the qupai system.
- **The boundary with "accompanying chorus / chorus"**: the script also has offstage **accompanying chorus** (two places in Scene 5, the repeated last line in Scene 8) and **chorus** (the coda of Scene 8, the singers in Scene 4). Accompanying chorus and chorus are offstage and handle announcements and theme; bāngqiāng clings to the aria passage and handles the four functions above. **The layout also differs**: bāngqiāng is listed in the same column as character names; accompanying chorus is marked "offstage" or "behind the scenes."

**Four transcribed examples**

(1) Bāngqiāng makes a decision for the characters, and they thank it on the spot (Scene 3, p0026) — the unique case of bāngqiāng as "present participant"
```
Meng Dengke   (sings) My wife!
Meng's Wife   (sings) My husband!
Bāngqiāng     (turns to 【孝顺歌】 hétóng) Husband, do not grieve; wife, do not weep,
                                          take your wife to Chengdu!
Meng Dengke   (suddenly enlightened) Hey, that bāngqiāng helped well!
Meng's Wife   Right—the man does not leave his wife,
Meng Dengke   the steelyard does not leave its bowl.
Both          The two of us—let's go!
```

(2) Bāngqiāng magnifies one man into three thousand (Scene 3, p0026)
```
Meng Dengke   (sings) I sigh that tonight, on the road to the Yellow Springs, there is no inn—
Bāngqiāng     (sings 【刮骨令】) Where will they lodge, the three thousand wronged souls of Bashan?
```

(3) Bāngqiāng's cold commentary (Scene 5, p0048)
```
Meng's Wife   (sings) If you lodge the complaint, I will hang myself!
Bāngqiāng     One more life lost in the official's house!
```

(4) Bāngqiāng exposes the surface behavior (Scene 4, p0030)
```
Nichang     (sings 【红衲袄】) Nichang the woman, singing softly—
Bāngqiāng                      Put on a smiling face!
Nichang     (back-turned song) I turn my back, a whole heart of homesickness mourning the old garden...
```

### 14f Praise and Blame across the Five Full Corpora (Must Be Read Together When Quoting)

| Full play | Praise | Blame |
|---|---|---|
| *The Peony Pavilion* | "Its brilliance lies in the border of life and death"; five acts from life to death, five from death to life, "**it searches out the root of spirit and overturns the cave of feeling, beyond what any lyric writer's heart or teeth had reached**"; "**in the Four Dreams every coda is excellent**"; the interface from speech to song in "Waking from a Dream" is a model | Too many filler words to punctuate; "The Netherworld Vow" departs from mode; the páichǎng of all Four Dreams lacks judgment; entering-tone characters mixed among level, rising, and departing rhymes |
| *The Peach Blossom Fan* | "**The layout of the whole is beyond attack**"; it breaks the sheng-dan reunion yet "the páichǎng is still not cold"; "**every phrase can serve as reliable history**"; "even the parallel couplets in the stage directions and spoken dialogue are all beautiful" | Level and oblique tones often off, filler words not well placed; "Transmitting the Song" has wrong syntax; "Lamenting at the Altar" uses the Southern 〔朝天子〕 for the Northern, "entirely losing the sense of a North-South mixed suite"; the whole play lacks arias worth repeated listening; "fine lyrics but no fine tune" |
| *The Palace of Eternal Life* | "Diction, structure, and páichǎng all excel, and moreover the modes are in tune and the spoken dialogue polished, **all beauties complete, not one thing to criticize**" (Wang Jilie); "**it gathers into one play all the songs of past and present that are worth singing and worth acting**... even the little songs of the jing and chou transitions fit perfectly"; "The Dance Plate" and "Reunion" create new tunes rather than sticking to the old *Jiugong* patterns | "The Dance Plate" uses Xiānlǚ with Zhōnglǚ tunes mixed in, "cannot sound beautiful"; "The Hunt" misidentifies tune names; the second volume "is fabricated and unfounded" |
| *The Story of the Pipa* | Native and plain, yet "**never without craftsmanship in ornament**"; the preludes "are all excellent, opening the door to the mountain—truly Dongjia's (东嘉 — Gao Ming) unique step"; the water-milled tune was set from its beat patterns, "all score-makers take *The Pipa* as their model" | Zhīshí and Yúmó not distinguished, Gēluó and Jiāmá used together; inverted characters to make rhymes, rhyming by ellipsis, saying it again after saying it, doggerel in the coda; **the last eight acts are not the original**; and, together with *The Pavilion for Worshipping the Moon*, it is a source of Southern qu's "not seeking modes or counting tunes" |
| *The Romance of the Western Chamber* | "Ancestor of prosody"; *Xu Xixiang* "its fine phrases are not inferior to Wang Shifu" (王实甫) | "It makes polished density its business—**this is a separate army in lyric, not the proper work of a qu master**"; the first line's yin-yang does not fit; whole lines of classics and histories enter the song; *The Southern Romance of the Western Chamber* "the suite form most not to be observed," judged by Li Yu "first in merit, chief in crime" |
| *The Golden Thread Pool* | The only one of the three plays with an act where "song outweighs speech" (act 1, 1:0.7, the act that establishes the character's status) | **Act 4 has no coda** |

**Two overall judgments, set side by side (not adjudicated)**:
- Wu Mei: "**Before and through the Qianlong era, there were plays and songs; between Jiaqing and Daoguang, there were songs but no plays; after Xianfeng and Tongzhi, in truth there were neither plays nor songs.**" — "play" (páichǎng / guànmù, 关目) and "song" (writing / metre) are assessed separately.
- Wu Mei on *The Peach Blossom Fan* and *The Palace of Eternal Life*: "**Judging only the writing, Kong seems superior to Hong; but in the disposition of páichǎng and the allocation of modes, his earnest thought far surpasses Dongtang (东塘 — Hong Sheng).**" — the same pair of works, two yardsticks giving opposite rankings.
